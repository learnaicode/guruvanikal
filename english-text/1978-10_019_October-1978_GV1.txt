[GV1:P0361] October 1978

[GV1:P0362] Question: Why was it stated in two different ways?

[GV1:P0363] Answer: Ordinary people sometimes face afflictions from ghosts and evil spirits. If it is under the control of such a fallen yogi (*Yogabhrashta*), it becomes difficult to remove. If the person is a ritualist or a devotee, it will lead the ritualist astray in his rituals. For a devotee, it will first invoke the deity worshipped by the fallen yogi, or manifest a mixed form of the deity the devotee believes in, ultimately bringing the devotee to the position of the fallen yogi. Thus, after passing many ages, if any great souls are born, when these spirits go there to cause trouble, they might be liberated. Alternatively, with the birth of a Guru belonging to the lineage of Manu, such souls might attain liberation. If they still do not get it, that soul might wander in the atmosphere until the end of the eon (*Kalpa*).

[GV1:P0364] Question: How do you determine this period of Manu?

[GV1:P0365] Answer: The period of one Manu is seen in the Puranas and Vedas as seventy-one Chaturyugas. During these Chaturyugas, when Gurus belonging to the lineage of Manu are born here and there, they liberate such souls either because the spirits troubled them or because the Gurus themselves became aware of them. Only those belonging to the lineage of Manu have the authority to do this. Gurus who worship deities, or those who come to guide people to the righteous path for two thousand or three thousand years, do not have this authority.

[GV1:P0366] Question: In which eras do such Gurus get born?

[GV1:P0367] Answer: Such Gurus are born in all eras.

[GV1:P0368] Question: How do humans understand this, and how do they deal with it in the world?

[GV1:P0369] Answer: Gurus born in the *Satyayuga* only have the task of convincing the people, through writing and speaking, about the knowledge of how the world should function in specific ways. It is a time when people truly see and respect the deities. Nothing stands in conflict with each other. That is due to the uniqueness of the era. That is why it is named *Satyayuga*. As the period of *Tretayuga* begins, certain deviations start appearing among the people themselves.

[GV1:P0370] Question: What is meant by saying "Mother, Father, Guru, God"?

[GV1:P0371] Answer: The first Guru is the mother. We do not know the father. From pointing out and introducing the father, that process takes place through the mother to a certain extent. Then, to an extent, the father leads, up to the limit of his knowledge. Beyond that, the father points out the Guru. That Guru provides the experience of God. Thus, the Guru as mother, the Guru as father, the Guru who knows God, and God as Guru—in this way, from birth, one must climb the steps of knowledge, further knowledge, and ultimate knowledge to seek peace. That itself is the goal. That itself is liberation, and human birth is precisely for that.

[GV1:P0372] Question: I wished to know your opinion about accepting *Sannyasa* (renunciation).

[GV1:P0373] Answer: *Sannyasa* is not a ritual like tying the nuptial thread in a wedding. It has been written in some places in what we call our scriptures that it is so. Such things are distortions that crept into our true lives over time, like adding water to milk. *Sannyasa* is not retreating to some corner to escape from actions, like closing one's eyes to make it dark. The body is meant to perform actions. Even the worship of God is an action. Therefore, the duty of a *Sannyasi* is to handle the problems of society. It was like that in the past. In the holy land of Bharat, that was the duty of a *Sannyasi*. From the day hypocrisy started in the name of *Sannyasa*, the downfall of Bharat began.

[GV1:P0374] Question: Previously, you had mentioned about the prayers to favorite deities (*Ishta Devata*) in certain places; it would be a blessing to hear about that.

[GV1:P0375] Answer: I shall tell you. The father might be highly proficient in mantras, and a universal preceptor (*Lokacharya*) in tantra. The favorite deity (*Ishta Devata* or *Paradevata*) would be a fierce male or female entity claiming paternity or maternity over all ghosts and evil spirits. To destroy anyone in the land, they will perform sorcery inside the heads of cats or chickens. They will make a roasted chicken fly. The liquor of a whole tavern won't be enough; they will still call out, "Bring more, bring more." Upon hearing that call, all fourteen worlds will tremble. This is how the glories of one's ancestral position go. Even today, such boasting can be heard in some villages and towns.

[GV1:P0376] Then the second stage. The father dies. There are minor troubles at home. What the astrologer who conducted the *Prashna* (astrological query) said, and what the maternal aunt's second son blurted out while in a trance, are exactly the same. There is not a single difference. The fiercely powerful father must be enshrined. The father will not let go. Thus, to purify the father, another sorcerer just like the father was brought in. Snapping fingers like purifying the *Koduveli* tuber, a chicken was sacrificed, and the father's spirit was invoked into a granite stone and consecrated under the shade of a tree in the compound. A lamp was lit, and prayers were offered. Needs were stated. Through dancing, swaying, and annual rituals, it became a custom. As generations passed, the dead sorcerer-father became a god.

[GV1:P0377] Excluding today's Vedanta preachers who claim everything is Brahman, let me ask a question: where will this worship—which bears witness to the spiritual essence of this man who captured and worshipped all evil spirits, and who did nothing but create notorious rumors in the land—lead the future generations? Is this the worship of God? Is this Indian culture? Is this the spiritual guidance of this holy land?
