[GV1:P1550] October 1985

[GV1:P1551] We are speaking by evaluating the fortune that shines through sacrifice, in light of the joys and sorrows experienced from the mistakes that have occurred in this world to date. How can we implement this? What is being implemented today? Having recognized the difficulties, virtues, and vices we have encountered so far, we must contemplate whether we can change our current habits, or if not, how we can rectify those mistakes if possible. Can we achieve this within the light of time? Many people have tried this in the past. It is not that everything failed completely; some things were successful. But today, we have taken up the complete form of the anomalies that emerged from it. That is to say, these are the individual subjects that have come down through numerous sages (*rishis*), wise men, ascetics, diligent householders, and lifelong celibates (*brahmacharis*). These are matters achieved by the actions of just two or four individuals among crores of people living. That itself will reach everywhere during their lifetime. Even in the present situation, religious scholars, Vedic experts, and scientifically minded individuals live in various parts of India. However, not everyone can internalize and act upon it. Some might be able to. One or two people might succeed through their actions. Even if it reaches us, to what extent can we reflect it? How much can we comprehend? Even if comprehended, how much can be implemented? How much can we ourselves carry forward to completion?

[GV1:P1552] Looking at each of these, we might find that we haven't even been able to acquire knowledge equivalent to a handful of soil taken from a mountain. We cannot summarize such a vast subject. However, we must eliminate the present calamities by making small changes to the mistakes that have occurred over time. How? Everything evolves from the life involving father, mother, children, husband, and wife. Everything is gained from that. You should not even dream that ascetics or solitary Vedic scholars will accomplish this for you. Suppose someone from among you becomes an ascetic, a Vedic scholar, a wise man, or a karma yogi in isolation. Suppose he grows to his full potential through his sacrifice. How can this reach you, who grow without an element of virtue, a spirit of sacrifice, or class consciousness? How can you, being like that, carry this forward? You are ready to overthrow everything with the attitude that you are everything. Are you not living to pass on the origin of a lineage that fosters bad customs, destroys humility, and loses merit? Then can you reach anywhere? It is enough if you bear the responsibility of your home, land, and country. If you perform karma divided by caste or religion, you will only end up fighting each other and sowing the seeds of destruction. That will grow into a tree, bloom, and bear unwanted fruits, spreading further. Consider how much you can strive to prevent that ignorance from reaching you. You must live thinking of the single class called humanity, without caste, religion, or sectarian consciousness. You should be able to do at least that much. You are being given an opportunity to ensure that your life does not cause destruction to your neighbor or neighboring country. Our backward journey has been a review of how you can utilize this opportunity. We reflected on the extent to which the people of the past imparted good and evil; this is an opportunity to ponder whether we, who evolved from behind them, are at the forefront or the rear today. If you lose this opportunity, you will be able to do nothing.

[GV1:P1553] When we think about this earth, which has existed for billions of years, our lifespan is so trivial. How can we act within this time to be useful for this lineage? Why did one come here? What can one do now to accomplish even an atom's worth of work for this world? Without thinking along these lines, it is said that there is anarchy all over the land. Anarchy of what? The discovery is that it is the anarchy of non-existence. What are we doing with what we have? Whether it is related to knowledge, statecraft, or our family life—how much are you utilizing it? Carrying a habit of incapability for anything as your asset, brimming with the attitude that "I am everything," aren't you living in every home acting as the patriarchal head of everything? Without self-cultivating dignity, and without removing these monetary or class-based perversions from among yourselves, how can you preserve your offspring as a lineage? You need to have foresight for this—that is, the householders. It will not suffice if ascetics or Vedic scholars foresee it. Each of your hearts is a center of knowledge that needs to be reclaimed by imparting your purity of character and simplicity to the people. You are draining it away, stuffing it with all sorts of unwanted things, and trying to reap its seeds. Men and women must resolve how to live from now on, understanding that such impurity has been imparted into us that we cannot even realize it is huddled inside the heart.

[GV1:P1554] We do not conduct classes to impart evil; they are meant only to teach the good. However, what everyone tends to learn first is the bad. Recognizing this, even if our capacity to act is minimal, we must execute it with absolute purity. Every one of you possesses intellect. There may be some who are less intellectually endowed, yet everyone holds their own inherent potential. If we can channel that in a positive direction, each of us will become virtuous, and we will be capable of working to our fullest potential. Without this realization, all our endeavors will be in vain. Here, Vedanta is taught, Vedic rituals are taught, and statesmanship is taught. Yet, in all of these, one can observe the personification of selfishness. What is the use of establishing all this if you lack the sensibility of love? This cannot be achieved through your mischief or emotional outbursts. Nor can it be acquired merely by reveling in the tales of our ancestors. We are well aware of our ancestral history. According to Hindu conventions, there are four *varnas*. Among them, the Brahmin, Vaishya, and Kshatriya enjoy absolute freedom. Knowledge, administration, and Vedic rituals are their monopolies. The Shudra, who constitutes the lowest stratum, is forbidden from learning the alphabet. The scriptures dictated that if he were to learn, molten lead should be poured into his ears. Consequently, we are a lineage that has inherited only aberrations, devoid of any real knowledge. Our ancestors exhibited arrogance and a display of power, operating under the delusion of omniscience. Why did they become this way? They are the ones who were once subjected to beatings, lacking even the basic freedom to dress properly. If they went to the homes of the upper castes for food, they had to survive by gulping down water poured from a distance. They were the ones who starved, toiling until dusk for a mere measure of paddy. How could they survive on such a meager amount of paddy? How could they clothe themselves? How could they educate their children? The arrogance seen today is the flip side of that profound suffering. In contrast to the immense hardships endured by our ancestors, we face no such struggles today. It must be surmised that the very consciousness of freedom was entirely lost in those times. It was precisely when we were reduced to such a state of nothingness that adherents of other religions gained the liberty to enter this land. This can only be regarded as an act of divine providence.

[GV1:P1555] Due to a historical inadequacy in the capacity to act in accordance with Dharma across different epochs, the Hindu systems of worship—Shaivism, Shaktism, and Vaishnavism—were subsumed. Instead of instilling the realization that an Advaitic essence encompasses all these traditions, the masses were led to their downfall through the mere preaching of Vedanta without true comprehension. Our lineage inherited the notion that scholars and sages were exceedingly formidable figures. This lineage merely sat in anticipation, much like a hornbill gazing at the sky for rain, devoid of any genuine knowledge. They existed without understanding the fundamental ways of life, the nature of a nation, or the mechanisms of governance. They remained ignorant of the necessities of individual households. Having originated in this profound ignorance, we must now, at the very least, propel ourselves toward true knowledge. Conversely, if we merely presume ourselves to be the repository of all knowledge, we shall remain stagnant. Therefore, as parents, you must lead an exemplary life. For this, faith must be anchored somewhere. To sustain this faith, we have embraced various mediums. Faith must evolve into a state where it is deeply loved, revered, and actively practiced. It is necessary to personify this faith, conceptualize it through various imageries, focus the mind upon it, and progressively impart wisdom from that focal point. This is the methodology adopted by Vedic practitioners and religious scholars. However, we are unaware of the depth to which this has penetrated. We have been existing in this condition for millions of years. Contemplate how such a faith can be transmitted and sustained. If we fail to impart this today, the consequences upon nature itself remain unfathomable. Nature encompasses all forms of destruction and sustenance. Primarily, one must reflect upon our fundamental sense of duty to preserve this nature, and subsequently act upon it. Failure to do so will lead you astray, precisely as it befell our ancestors. We must resolutely determine that such a fate shall not befall us again. Act with conviction, and everything will naturally align.

[GV1:P1556] Guruvani
