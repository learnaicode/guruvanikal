[GV1:P1662] December 1986

[GV1:P1663] Question: In the Varnasrama Dharma of Brahmins, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas, and Shudras, the author of the Gita himself has pointed out that Kali is the era of the Shudra, the fourth stage. To bring them to the path of karma, that is, to unite and uplift the Shudra (the ignorant), what should be done, Guru?

[GV1:P1664] Answer: A people can only be united under a single ideology! And that too, only an ideology in accordance with the Yuga Dharma.

[GV1:P1665] Question: Given that only an ideology mature enough to sever the roots of beliefs deeply embedded in the framework of old habits and rules can sprout a change in the hearts of the people in accordance with the dharma of Kali, who will be the authority of that supremely noble ideology, Guru?

[GV1:P1666] Answer: Haven't you seen that the Treta and Dvapara Yugas have proven that the Yugacharya will be the authority of the Yuga Dharma? Kali also has such authorities.

[GV1:P1667] Question: How can that great soul be found, Guru?

[GV1:P1668] Answer: When karma is increased and merit is attained, that path will become clear.

[GV1:P1669] Question: For those engaged in industry and commerce for their daily livelihood, by what virtuous deed will they attain it?

[GV1:P1670] Answer: When the Western policy of destroying nations and draining wealth through industry and commerce is abandoned, both will be purified.

[GV1:P1671] Question: How can ordinary individuals achieve this?

[GV1:P1672] Answer: First, each individual must maintain purity and moderation in their own thoughts and actions. Goddess Lakshmi and Goddess Saraswati were the sources of wealth. The lineage of acharyas, who had nothing, acquired wealth through their own karma and meditation. Each individual must take a firm vow that we should be able to become a generation capable of passing it on to the next generation. His education and culture began with the acharya. Even his letters began with the acharya's first letter, and he should be able to live a life of dedication.

[GV1:P1673] Man's karma and dharma constitute his greatness. It should be the acharya who makes the people perform karma and gets it done. Even prompting thoughts about the goal of human birth stems from the acharya's adherence to ideals.

[GV1:P1674] The systems of justice conceived by human intellect are not sufficient to sustain human dharma. People do not even think about the necessity of writhing and twisting, claiming it to be devotion. They do not consider the basis for dismissing something by saying, 'This is Kali.' Today's order, discipline, civilization, and intellect are not those of the olden times. Man can only be guided through methods suited to the times. The culture with its ancient heritage is becoming unknown to today's generation. We are the remnants of having lived as slaves due to a lack of wealth and knowledge, devoid of freedom and culture. Many acharyas came and tried to change this. They failed. The Shudra culture extinguished the ignorant people's inclination to embrace the acharya. When wealth and position came, the Shudra became arrogant. Arrogance destroyed the clan. Even the Brahmins of India fell under the spell of the Westerner's whiteness—possessing a Vedic demeanor but the karma of a Shudra.

[GV1:P1675] The people stood in wonder before the demon-dancers, much like a job-seeker before a palmist. Scholars focused only on making shelves, buying books, reading, and filling them up. No one could save the people from this. Only an enlightened Acharya can save them. One can live in accordance with Dharma only when they are able to internalize the Acharya. Only through that can virtuous deeds be performed.

[GV1:P1676] Guru Vani
