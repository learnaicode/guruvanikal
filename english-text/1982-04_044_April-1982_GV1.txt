[GV1:P1171] April 1982

[GV1:P1172] The British ruled with greater diplomacy and through deceptive methods. They introduced their education and wealth in a distinct manner and ruled for a period. By doing so, they established an administrative system among the people. However, they carried away all other resources from here to transform their snow-clad country into a paradise. Considering all these factors, those of us who lived under such regimes lacked the fortune to properly comprehend the true greatness of the Guru.

[GV1:P1173] An example of this is Kabirdas, the incarnation of the previously mentioned Sage Suka. He was an individual without any formal literacy. He accepted Ramananda, who attained enlightenment through the path of devotion rather than mystical powers, as his Guru. However, despite being bestowed with the merit and eminence to serve as a Guru even to Kabirdas, Ramananda remained unaware of his own identity and the purpose of his arrival. Why did God bring Ramananda into this world without revealing this to him? This was a divine decree. Only Kabirdas, who possessed no significant formal education or scriptural knowledge, could accomplish all this and reveal the true nature of the Guru. Kabirdas was innately enlightened from birth due to the wisdom acquired in his previous incarnations. We perceive Kabirdas as a sage who foreknew future events and recognized the true nature of individuals. It was through Kabirdas that the greatness of Ramananda was brought to light. Dismissing him as a Muslim boy by caste, all the Brahmins who had hitherto continuously chanted the Vedas came forward in opposition. However, with the arrival of the Siddha known as Gorakshan, all these Brahmins fled in fear. Even when Kabirdas was merely a young child, he was severely beaten and expelled three or four times. It was only after enduring all these trials that he finally departed from the Gurukula.

[GV1:P1174] After serving the Guru and traversing all other stages, Kabirdas returned. It was Kabirdas who revealed to the Guru his own true identity and the magnitude of his greatness. This Guru (Ramananda) himself was unaware that he was the one destined to perform and orchestrate all these deeds. What is the reason behind this? It is a divine mechanism established by God to prevent arrogance. Kabirdas, the incarnation of Sage Suka, has profoundly stated that even if all the water in the world's oceans were transformed into ink and all the trees were uprooted to serve as pens, it would still be impossible to exhaustively write about the greatness of the Guru.

[GV1:P1175] Then what constitutes the deception of this Guru? How do we truly comprehend this Guru? Simultaneously, although they share the sacred bond of Guru and disciple, this Ramananda repeatedly beat and expelled this disciple. He was banished from the ashram as if he were slain. Yet, Kabirdas could neither disrespect that Guru nor utter a single word of disparagement. This profound restraint stems from a vibrant, living devotion towards the Guru. It is exceedingly rare to find such unblemished disciples. Equally, it is difficult to find Gurus completely devoid of deception. Both these realities coexist.

[GV1:P1176] Suppose we encounter such Gurus; how would we recognize them? How should we embrace these Gurus? Among them, there are 'Kalantara Gurus' (Gurus of an era) and those who manifest every two thousand or five thousand years. It is said that there are only five such 'Kalantara Gurus' in a Yuga. How, then, do we comprehend entities who appear like Sri Krishna, the Prophet, or Sankaracharya? The philosophical essence of what they have all articulated in various languages remains fundamentally identical. We end up elevating such Gurus to the status of deities, merely seeking refuge beneath their divine guise. This is precisely why Sankaracharya was depicted in the divine embrace of 'Tripurasundari'. This represents a distinct logic employed by scholars. Even in their ignorance, they find a certain comfort in documenting such narratives. When scholars recorded this, the common people inherently believed it to be the truth. Only a Guru who directly imparts knowledge to a child can articulate its specific characteristics. How can disciples truly recognize a Guru of such a profound lineage who lived two or three millennia ago? Or how would the Gurus themselves know? It is stated that Agastya lived as the ultimate, exemplary model for all Gurus. What definitive attributes can be ascribed to this Agastya? Or how can one accurately characterize Sankaracharya? What is to be inferred from the assertion that Mahavishnu was kicked—it signifies the decisive act of a sage. Hasn't this been encapsulated in a narrative? Therefore, as I previously mentioned, once a mortal truly ascends to the state of a sage, he transcends deities, capable of even kicking or spitting upon them. Even if regarded as a mere fabricated tale, the underlying principle is vividly evident. A sage exists beyond the Trinity. Beyond that realm is the Sanyasi (ascetic). He is the one who retreats and eventually returns as a Guru.

[GV1:P1177] How can this Guru be comprehended? This is precisely where we fail to grasp the divine deception of the Guru. No intellect born and nurtured within this world can fathom this, because it is a deliberate deception orchestrated by the Divine. Therefore, it remains beyond our inherent knowledge. Hence, individuals in close proximity to the Gurus must possess elevated consciousness; otherwise, peril is inevitable. There is no merit in merely proclaiming that your Guru is supreme and that you are obedient followers. The true benefit is bestowed only upon those who stand close and diligently strive to align themselves in harmony. Thus, if those accompanying these Gurus lack a magnitude, intellect, and capability equal to the Guru, they will inevitably fail to understand the Guru. Only such a profound disciple can respectfully comprehend this Guru. The history of Kabirdas serves as an irrefutable proof. Numerous disciples accompanied Ramananda; countless individuals accepted discipleship and subsequently departed. None of them proved to be genuinely capable. That is precisely why it is stated that there must be a disciple who profoundly knows the Guru. Only he can authentically reveal the Guru to this world.

[GV1:P1178] Do not harbor the assumption that you can independently comprehend and inherently believe in the Guru. Through familiarity, you might marginally incorporate it into your thoughts and foster belief. Consequently, you can also acknowledge the Gurus chronicled on paper. However, if the term 'Guru' embodies a supernatural force appointed by nature, transcending nature itself, you cannot comprehend it through such conventional means. It remains unknowable through written Vedas or poetry. Nothing substantial has been documented about the true Guru in any of those texts to date. This profound reality can only be realized through direct experience. Thus, if a 'Kalantara Guru'—a monumental Guru and great soul who manifests once in two or three thousand years—instructs you to conduct yourself in a specific manner and engage exclusively in prescribed practices, and if you comprehend and act accordingly, you will inevitably experience it as the absolute truth.
