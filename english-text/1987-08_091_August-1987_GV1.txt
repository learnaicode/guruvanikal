[GV1:P1731] August 1987

[GV1:P1732] Ever since the publication of the Santhigiri magazine commenced, it has been customary to include a column titled 'Birthday Message', based on my experiences and perspectives, in the special editions released under the name of an annual issue or a birthday issue. Similarly, a doubt arises in me whether this note, provided in the special edition intended to be released this time, also entails a critical tone. The reason for this is the two facets that emerge from life: one good, the other bad. Or rather, sin and virtue. Sins are of many kinds; virtues are of many kinds. Likewise, when speaking variously of realization, vision, empirical narrative, life memoir, and life struggle, one's own knowledge and erudition are also incorporated. Erudition is sometimes referred to as knowledge and wisdom. However, no one should doubt that true erudition is spoken of in relation to the *Jnanis* (enlightened beings). But these *Jnanis* themselves are of many types: the *Karmi* (man of action), the *Yogi*, the *Jnani*, and the *Bhakta* (devotee) — it is through these four paths that one acquires a tradition of wisdom commensurate with their knowledge.

[GV1:P1733] For example, a believer and devotee realizes one of the chosen deities in the tradition of *Ishta Devata* (tutelary deity), such as *Pey*, *Marutha*, *Mantramurthi*, or *Yogeeshwaran*. What is meant by realization is the truth—encompassing the gross, the subtle, the effect, and the cause—that can be perceived even by his physical eyes. When he presents this courageously, with love, out of an affinity towards humanity, and compassionately to the best of his ability, people use the word *Siddhi* (attainment/supernatural power) to describe it. If necessary, this can be termed a form of wisdom. A little further ahead is the Vedic approach, represented by the Vedantin or the *Karmi*.

[GV1:P1734] The *Karmi*, having realized each deity through *Mantra Siddhi*, also desires to stand at the forefront of public service with this attained charm, employing mantras, tantras, and yantras. On the other hand, the *Yogi* attains miraculous powers in the science of Yoga by testifying to the six *Chakras* (vital centers). It can be seen that Gorakshan, the practitioner of mystical powers who is believed to have reached the pinnacle of *Siddhi* and is immortalized in stories even today, attempted to surpass Vagbhata, the sage of the heart mentioned in Ayurvedic science. However, the Ayurvedic scientist, the sage of the heart, instructed how and where to use everything from cumin, mustard, and turmeric to *Kurunthotti* (Sida cordifolia), *Thamizhaama* (Boerhavia diffusa), banana stem, ash gourd, and *Paalmudukkan* tuber. Without the aid of modern laboratory equipment, maintaining precise weights and measures, he expounded the truth on how single herbs like *Aanachuvadi* are effective for various diseases. He guided how even a young child could prepare herbal decoctions (*Kashayam*) with minimal additives, advising without causing any harm to our physiology, and laying down rules so that these remedies can be utilized as long as humanity exists. Concurrently, the aforementioned Gorakshan is also a *Jnani*.

[GV1:P1735] From this, we understand that there are various types of *Yogis* and *Karmis*. However, let us now try to observe a true *Jnani*. Among the names of such *Jnanis*, we intend to look back at a few—like Shankaracharya. To make us understand, four words have been used for four types of *Jnanis*: *Brahmavit*, *Brahmavaryan*, *Brahmavarishtan*, and *Brahmavareeshtan*. It is impossible to distinctly show in which all states the acquisition of wisdom occurs just through the word *Brahmavit*. Disentangling the terms *Brahmavaryan*, *Brahmavarishtan*, and *Brahmavareeshtan* is indeed impossible. We must respect the words of Kumaran Asan, who was able to honor all these at least to some extent through his erudition. The ability to articulate this must have stemmed from the assurance provided by the proximity of Sree Narayana Guru. Otherwise, one would not be able to say it out of fear. His lines, stating that "Yakshis and demons are gods to them" and "they have long built fortresses with Smritis," alongside the lines "Change the rules yourselves, or else / they will change you," further strengthen this sentiment, clearly demonstrating the agony in his heart.

[GV1:P1736] Because the aforementioned *Varnashrama Dharma*, which emerged from an upper-class mentality, was interpreted erroneously, we must not forget the role played by the literate scholars here, who devoted their logic, intellect, time, and eras—giving their all through their erudite path—to enlighten or uplift the masses.

[GV1:P1737] However, a true *Jnani* sees none of these; yet, they see everything. Let us look at what that *Jnani* attempted to make us do. To impart love when we see each other, they accustomed us to the gesture of *Namaskara* (greeting) with an *Anjali mudra* (folded hands), conveying utmost grace and facial dignity. This is not difficult for any layman or scholar to follow. It can indicate whatever humility one possesses. Even if there is ego, it would seem to disappear somewhere and stand isolated. The karma is realized in such a way that even the most ignorant person can be elevated to the status of a *Jnani*. At the same time, without forgetting the *Jnani*, the scholar, and the *Yogi*, they have been respected without ever losing the purity of the attempt to realize them by assigning each their respective position. Yet, one must doubt whether this *Jnani* views the scholar's application of terms like *Mithya* (illusion) or *Maya* (delusion) in the exact same manner. The reason is that, having reached a state of respecting everything, this *Jnani*, out of love—to use the words of Sree Narayana Guru or Chattampi Swami, "counting one by one, until the meaning is exhausted"—attempts to polish and refine even the fluctuations of the subtle-sighted ones starting from that point; this aspect constitutes the *Yugantara Dharma* (the dharma of the transition of epochs). Included in this are the calculations of the fourteen Manus and the count of the *Jnanis* who are to appear in a *Manvantara*. It is not merely destroying these calculations; the aforementioned scholarly arrogance or ignorance is clearing a path for eternal destruction in a way that destroys our very religious consciousness. As a result, we have become so deteriorated that we cannot recognize ourselves, isolated from each other, and devoid of religious consciousness due to discovering God and subsequently dividing into classes. Therefore, a permanent state of perpetual distancing, preventing us from uniting, has taken deep root in an insurmountable manner. Consequently, the people here have grown day by day, as if on chemical fertilizers, realizing themselves as pests of destruction; with minds fiercer than Bhadrakali and degenerating into vulgarity through alcoholism, they have become enslaved to the art and prowess of Western culture, ready to assume ownership of what is celebrated as education. This has become the domain of a competitive karma that the Brahmin, Vaishya, and Shudra—who claim to be the Vedic scholars and *Jnanis* here—are eager to collectively accept. Having evolved to this extent, we have been able to reclaim ownership of an administration that owes allegiance to no one.

[GV1:P1738] The householders (*Grihasthashramis*) who stand at the forefront of this, through their modernization, scholarship, knowledge, wealth, and authority, have taken all the aforementioned ignorance upon their heads and become the masters of the aforementioned actions. Do we, who are so eager to create everything from the nursery education they have started to postgraduate study centers after graduation, even remember this? It is worth pondering whether the householders belonging to this division of action, or the aforementioned degree holders or postgraduates, have been able to even glance into the lives of the wise sages who sacrificed their lifespans and bodies to serve the people by aptly placing and operating everything in its proper place. Western culture has stepped in, girding its loins with the determination of the previously mentioned Bhadrakali, to keep us as a group of ignorant people trying to bury forever these truths and dharmas envisioned by the sages so that the thoughts, words, and deeds of every Indian could work in harmony. What we see today, right from the nursery level, is a culture that turns children into possessors of an education that makes them ignorant of who their parents are, only to eventually create old-age homes to hide them away in some corner. If the administration falls into his hands, he will protect the people by inventing not just Star Wars but many other things. Is it not essential for the people of India and all the cultural center trainers who try to impart this to think whether they want to raise their children like this? Do not try to make yourselves incompetent! I offer this before you with the request that you give importance to my repeated pleas.

[GV1:P1739] I see that even those who have cooperated with me in daily life for twenty or thirty years still have minor doubts and find themselves in the unfortunate position of being unable to explain this matter to another person. However, among Hindus—from temple dwellers to Shudras—we have a medium adopted to understand God. Using that as a medium, an advertisement was placed in two newspapers at a cost, along with an astrologer's planetary chart. It was explicitly stated in the astrological query (*Prashnaroodha*) itself that this has not been attained by the lineage of Acharyas until today, that liberation is being granted as requested by the deities, and that the impurity of that very astrologer's family temple has been removed. Once again, those belonging to the temple dwellers and temple believers have sent me several letters in a way that shows they have understood nothing. Who knows what they understood about that astrological query! In that advertisement itself, it was specifically written that they should come to the ashram to find out. Instead, they wrote letters. And the others did not understand anything either. It is with all this in mind that I have written such a message. I wish for you to read and understand this patiently.

[GV1:P1740] May this auspicious day bring goodness to you.

[GV1:P1741] Yours, Swami

[GV1:P1742] Guruvani

[GV1:P1743] September 1987

[GV1:P1744] You people of Palakkad do not have a small life. You have a certain dignity. Even today, you are truly dignified. Why is that? Because they work hard and live off their earnings without harming anyone else. Such a large community exists in this region. However, minor ignorances have occurred. That happens, aren't we humans? Except for what has come from holding the flags of today's politics or imitating various other things, you are worthy - more than anyone elseٹ . You possess honesty. These ruined Namboothiris - though they are useless in some aspects, they are good in others. The entire land currently in the hands of the Shudra class was once theirs. Even today, if one of them is called up in a case and asked, they will not lie. If asked why this is not yours, they will simply say that the possession rights belong to the others. But if it were our people, they would try to lie a little. Despite having such honesty, they were ruined. Why did they lose their Vedic and administrative authority? At the same time, despite being very low religiously, the Shudra has come to the forefront. Even after all this, why doesn't your self-awareness emerge?

[GV1:P1745] It is not just the glory of the era. We don't get that understanding. We only get what our parents showed us. Isn't it through them that our life itself moves forward? Did we have Vedic authority or scholarly expertise? Then how did we come forward? Be it through some imitation or anything else, when we get it, do we have the sense to stubbornly develop it? How many great souls were born here for us? The Parayi Petta Panthirukulam, the eighteen Siddhars - they were born among these Dravidians. Buddha explained what this is for, and told us to do so-and-so. We couldn't take up a path that was told to us like this for ourselves - even today we can't! Recently in Kerala itself, Sankaracharya, Sree Narayana Guru, and Chattampi Swami came. What have we done? Haven't we divided ourselves in the name of caste, confined it within the caste, put it inside a fence, and poured water? Then what is the meaning of saying we didn't get it? Whatever we get, we must grab it quickly. Isn't this because we lack the stubbornness and zeal to have it?

[GV1:P1746] Despite having administrative and Vedic authority, how did they lose it? It was not discarded by simply stating it was unwanted, was it? You yourselves are aware that there were eighteen *Agraharams* (Brahmin settlements) here. Why do you not consider how these were lost? The fourth quarter is the era of the Shudras. It is not their era. Why are we unable to claim it? We are standing in a lineage where our tradition has ruined us. That is the reason. For example, when mentioning the Palakkad region, you yourselves are aware. You know that there is a practice of consecrating and worshipping (*Vacharadhana*), do you not? We handle this in various ways. Is there not a system in our land of consecrating and worshipping even *Karinkuttichathan*? If this *Karinkuttichathan* becomes our deity, who will we become? Suppose a sorcerer in our family consecrated this; where will this sorcerer stand when he dies? If this ancestor goes astray, who will we, born in that lineage (*Rashi*), become? Then, is there any doubt that our deity and our object of worship—all these aforementioned things—have led us astray? An example of this is that all these Brahmins have reached their current state today. We never performed more than them, did we! We only did trivial things using all this. At the same time, they would step out of the house only after strictly following the procedures using this. What has become of them today? Some may say it is because they performed it incorrectly. Did everyone perform it incorrectly?

[GV1:P1747] What you call imitation is indeed correct. If it were not for this attire and language of the British today, would we have been able to obtain even a single penny from the government? So, we have no attire, no language; are we merely the ones who came to claim ownership of the attire and language of those who belonged to a wretched system? Despite facing such a predicament, why can we not change away from it?

[GV1:P1748] Guruvani
