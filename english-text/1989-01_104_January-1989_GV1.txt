[GV1:P2317] January 1989

[GV1:P2318] It becomes reality only when ordinary writers present historical truth exactly as it is, without distortion. This will lead to the culmination of knowledge. Before stating this part, one must first speak about them. Subsequently, it should be stated that people living like them face such and such perils. To rectify that, specific actions must be undertaken. Time has yet to evolve this much—that must be articulated. How should it be stated? Great individuals should state it thus: I have witnessed these specific things. I have only been able to reach the extent of my doubts regarding it. Or, it has not been possible to complete it. Although that light enveloped me, my body could not withstand it. Or, the moment it saw me, that light paused thus and vanished. That articulates a significant portion of their knowledge.

[GV1:P2319] Only an *Acharya* (spiritual teacher) can say that the light vanished the moment it was seen. Only an *Acharya* can declare that the light had enveloped them; those are profound subjects. You must be aware of all this while residing in the ashram. If you stay in the ashram for ten days, you develop a sense of dedication. What is that? The ashram must prosper. The people residing in the ashram must improve. The people residing here become bad. Or they become good. I have no concern about that. I must improve myself—one person has such a thought. For another, it is not so—they think, 'these people do this, do that, they are completely worthless, I am the good one.' Thus, a thousand people will think in a thousand different ways. Dedication implies that, with a Guru in mind, we will relinquish much of this. After all, we place our trust in the Guru!

[GV1:P2320] We would not do that in our homes or in our native places. Many things exhibited there to project one's personality will not be displayed here. Thus, from many aspects, we will unknowingly transform ourselves. First, we must learn the practice of changing from all aspects in this manner. Sometimes, it might take ten to twelve years to comprehend this. Consequently, one will gradually change from all aspects and imbibe some goodness in that space. The completion of this acquired goodness will similarly take another twelve years. It is over various periods that this aspect can be brought to fruition. Thus, we can accomplish it only after knowing about twelve states, twelve spiritual practices, or twelve facts over twelve years. The experience of encountering flaws within this process is what we predominantly possess. What is that?

[GV1:P2321] When you are present here, you will observe some individuals engaging in virtuous acts while others partake in misdeeds. Some will go that way, and some will come this way. The ashram and certain individuals operate in accordance with their innate tendencies. This alone is sufficient to undermine the movement. Consequently, the children who observe and learn from this will be corrupted. They will reason: "We were present here, we studied here, and observed specific individuals committing certain acts. Having done all this, they now instruct us to be righteous." Therefore, they will conclude that these instructions were entirely hollow. This is the perception they will form, and consequently, they will never attain righteousness. You must ensure this does not happen to you. To guard against this, you must first engage in profound self-reflection. Those who impart knowledge must initially possess the fundamental capacity for discernment.

[GV1:P2322] Furthermore, after staying here for ten days, if they are householders, they will utilize their highly developed intellect to commence rendering service for a few days; they will perform substantial service, compel others to do so, and bring and expend a significant amount of money—proceeding to do a great deal within a span of ten years. One person might do so within a year. Another within a few months—acting according to their respective knowledge and capacity. If they lack maturity, they will boast about these deeds. Subsequently, their focus shifts to matters of raising their children. They will become miserly, and through such miserliness, they will misrepresent their actions as something entirely different. All of this constitutes their own destruction. No one instructed them to perform these acts. Having executed them out of their own vital interests, when they begin to vocalize it, not only is the merit of the act lost, but all their practical endeavors will also unravel. The householders who frequent this place, as well as every individual residing here, must comprehend this profoundly. Otherwise, anomalies will ensue. After visiting ten or fifteen times, one might feign a disposition of, "I have come, I have gone, I know everything." Yet, the truth remains that they have comprehended nothing. Even if one were to traverse this path a thousand times, they would not know. Even if one were to stay here and study for a thousand days, they would not understand. Such is the nature of this subject.

[GV1:P2323] Guruvani
