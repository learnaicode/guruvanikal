[GV1:P0103] July 1977

[GV1:P0104] The Glory of Sacrifice

[GV1:P0105] Beloved ones,

[GV1:P0106] My aspiration to dedicate my life solely to virtuous deeds commenced during my early childhood. I must have been around seven years old at the time. I exhibited a profound interest in visiting temples for worship. You must not misconstrue my statement. This was an innate desire. Do you not possess similar innate inclinations? It is highly probable. Some individuals are predisposed to malevolent tendencies, ultimately culminating in their ruin. Simply put, I possessed an inherent obstinacy to avoid such a trajectory.

[GV1:P0107] I began attending school. However, my focus was not on academics. I would incessantly ponder subjects related to devotion. A teacher inevitably noticed this characteristic. He inquired into the reasons behind my silence and unusual behavior. I could not refrain from telling him. My singular thought was that I must reach an ashram somehow. My family members raised objections. However, none of that deterred me. That childhood obstinacy ultimately led me to the Aluva Advaita Ashram. My pursuit of knowledge commenced there.

[GV1:P0108] I gained substantial insight into ashram life during my time there. One crucial aspect—I had reached my destined location. That became my educational institution. My learning was to proceed from there. Subsequently, I arrived at Sivagiri. At Sivagiri, I resolved to establish a firm foundation for my endeavors. This is precisely what I intend to discuss. I can encapsulate it in a single word: sacrifice. After completing my own duties, I would fulfill the responsibilities of others as well. I never experienced any reluctance in doing so. This readiness for sacrifice became the defined objective of my life. I am articulating this specifically for the benefit of the younger generation. Cultivate this attitude during your formative years. If you do so, success is undoubtedly assured.

[GV1:P0109] During this period, individuals from various regions would visit the ashram. I would readily offer them the necessary assistance. Consequently, they would naturally feel inclined to offer something in return to a child exhibiting such behavior. However, the moment I sensed they were about to offer monetary compensation, I would flee the premises. They would typically search for me precisely when they were preparing to depart. Yet, they would be unable to locate me anywhere. One could say this served as a form of amusement for me. I derived a sense of satisfaction from it.

[GV1:P0110] At this juncture, I am reminded of another story about the greatness of sacrifice. It happened a long time ago, during the time of Chandragupta Maurya. A famous person from China arrived in India. He desired to meet and converse with everyone here. Accordingly, he got permission to meet Chanakya, the minister of Chandragupta. How would the minister of a great emperor live? The traveler pondered over it. When he reached near the minister's house one evening, he saw Chanakya sitting in a small hut near a small lamp, looking at the files of important problems of the country. He stood amazed. What kind of minister is this? Suddenly, Chanakya extinguished that lamp and lit another one. The prominent traveler could not help but ask why it was done. Chanakya said: "That belongs to the state, and this lamp is my own. Now we can talk."

[GV1:P0111] The traveler could not help but respect the humility and sense of justice of the statesman named Chanakya. He went back to China with a great experience. What do we understand from this? Since those ancient times, our Indians were renowned for their humility, service, and righteousness. Their sacrifice highlighted the greatness of our country. It also provided an inspiration to be noticed all over the world.

[GV1:P0112] Now another matter needs to be pointed out here. Each of us has different paths in life. We move forward accordingly. When seeing someone prosper, many people get jealous. That behavior pattern needs to be changed. It cannot be changed suddenly. For that, good thoughts must spread within us and wash away the impurities inside. Only then can it be said that one has lived as a human being, and only then will there be satisfaction. We have seen many people being dissatisfied and restless. Do you know the reason for that? Excessive greed. First, remove those greedy thoughts. That is why we must control our minds.

[GV1:P0113] The goal of our life is realized when a systematic path is adopted. Some people set out to become monks while they still have all the obligations in life. Such monkhood is of no use to them. Those who are householders must fulfill their respective duties. Children must be raised through the right path. It is an essential factor for the progress of the country. When they grow up, they will find the ways for their employment on their own. Parents should prepare the paths for them to find it. And then, we should become proficient in activities for our peace of mind.

[GV1:P0114] But there are some people. They lament over the future of their children. The pain of not being able to give them anything. Others, however, just earn money for their children. They amass unlimited wealth. I do not think there is a need for that either. If a son or daughter does not come through the path we intend, let them go their own way. There is no use in remembering that and lamenting over it. This life of ours is not for lamenting like that. We must live it completely.

[GV1:P0115] We have to learn this matter by looking at the Westerners. We know how they raise their children and so on. They give the necessary instructions. They encourage them to do any job. There is no objection in that. Anyway, a job is a job. We cannot but respect it. Here, if someone has a little education, they are stubborn that they must get a job commensurate with it. Because of this, we can see countless youths who are jobless and do not do any work. They will experience the problems of life very seriously.

[GV1:P0116] How can those who have thus become lazy by themselves be reformed? One can also see parents lamenting over it. What I have to say is that there is no point in that. Once the children reach a stage to stand on their own feet, the duty is fulfilled. After that, there is no wrong in even accepting monkhood. One's duty has been fulfilled. That is the path of a complete life.

[GV1:P0117] Acquiring knowledge in childhood,

[GV1:P0118] Seeking worldly enjoyments in youth,

[GV1:P0119] Adopting a sage's conduct in old age,

[GV1:P0120] And relinquishing the body in yogic bliss.

[GV1:P0121] It is a necessity to acquire knowledge in childhood. Along with that, one must adhere to righteousness in life. Only then can a purification be built up even in later times. What is needed to maintain that discipline? Guidance! Instructions from a wise and mature Guru! That will provide immense solace. That will nurture the good tendencies within us. With that, a complete understanding of what Vanaprastha is and its full form will emerge within us. The knowledgeable ones say that it is the householders who can engage more in this matter. One can point out countless examples for that. The very fact that all the authors of the Puranas were householders is sufficient proof. That is the complete life. We must truly respect it.

[GV1:P0122] Guru Vani
