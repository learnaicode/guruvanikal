[GV4:P1063] April 2005

[GV4:P1064] As epochs and centuries have passed, various states of existence have emerged. When we speak of these states of existence, everything from a demon (*pishacha*) to a deity (*deva*) represents a particular state of our being. In the context of this specific state, there are individuals who worship a deity and ultimately perish after attaining the state of that deity. Beneath this hierarchy are *yakshis* and malevolent spirits—are there not individuals in our regions who worship entities like Karinkuttichathan? Similarly, entities like Muthappan and various others have existed in diverse forms. Furthermore, there is a tradition of conceptualizing Subrahmanya in this manner. All of this is factual. It is not that these entities are non-existent; they do exist, and they were venerated by our ancestors. How can we transcend this? If we attempt to overcome it through *yoga*, the *yoga* referred to here is not what we contemporarily understand it to be. The *yoga* mentioned is *Rajayoga*. The others are classified as *Hatha Yoga*, *Hamsa Yoga*, *Chandrika Yoga*, and so forth. Those do not involve *pranayama*. In this *Rajayoga*, there is no *pranayama* either; there is no practice of restraining and holding the *prana* (life force). Having heard all these assertions and assuming them to be superior, we have placed our faith in those ritualistic actions (*karma*) demonstrated during an era like the *Treta Yuga*. That is the *Treta Yuga* and

[GV4:P1065] transferred through the *Dvapara Yuga*, and we are still practicing the very same things. This did not require any modification during the *Satya Yuga*. Back then, during the *Satya Yuga*, humans lived as truthfully as possible. Divided into these eras, we currently refer to the four epochs as the *Chaturyuga*. When speaking of these four eras, the shortest is the period of *Kali* (*Kali Yuga*). This consists of 432,000 years. Double of this is the *Dvapara Yuga*, and double of that is the *Treta Yuga*. Consequently, as we traverse through such vast expanses of time like the *Dvapara*, our practical realities and circumstances undergo complete transformation. Because of this, it is necessary for preceptors (*acharyas*) to emerge from time to time. Only they have the capacity to rectify these matters. This transformation does not rely upon a mere *yogi*, a ritualist (*karmi*), or the simplistic belief that a divine manifestation alone is sufficient. The manifestation of a deity, goddess, or sage is no longer adequate. Their respective eras have concluded. What, then, is the current situation? You must understand this. When such matters arise, unless you gradually inquire and learn, merely attending will yield no benefit for you. You will simply fail to comprehend it. Currently, we have attained the state of a demon, internalizing and worshipping it as part of our heritage. We are subjugated by this demonic entity. We have been worshipping it under the misconception that it is a deity. Over various epochs, we have venerated and empowered it. If we claim it does not exist, how can it simply vanish? Therefore, rather than habituating ourselves to deny its existence, we must acknowledge that it is present. However, to what extent, and in what manner? This is precisely what is termed as the differentiation of transitional states (*avasthantharabhedam*). Thus, when discussing these differences in states, what we must determine is how we completed a specific state or stage, and through what means? That constitutes one aspect of it. Accordingly, the *yogi*, the ritualist (*karmi*), the seeker of knowledge (*jnani*), and the devotee (*bhakta*)—it is through these four paths that one reaches a standstill. There was an era traversed by the *yogi*. That era should not be forcibly applied to the present. Why is that? That must be clearly understood. The era of the *yogi* evolved into the human, which transitioned into the deity, then into the sage (*rishi*), subsequently into the ascetic (*sannyasi*), and ultimately forms the path to liberation as a *jnani*. The deity is not at the forefront. Therefore, the human being itself embodies the deity, the sage, and the ascetic. Concerning our modes of worship, we possess a lineage characterized by the veneration of sacred serpent groves (*sarppakavu*) and similar localized deities. It cannot be stated that these do not exist; they are very much real. The question is, to what extent and in what manner should this existing reality be worshipped and brought to fulfillment? Achieving this requires a profound philosophical vision (*darshana*) of that specific state. We are entirely unaware of the forms or variations we have traversed within a single lifetime. Why, then, should we dwell on it? There are multitude of transitional states. How should one intellectualize and discard them? There are those who question the utility of contemplating such matters—namely, the proponents of Non-dualism (*Advaitins*). To speak as an *Advaitin*, one must have successfully transcended these transitional states. It is not a prerogative granted to everyone. Ignorant of this, individuals utilize merely human intellect, capabilities, and skills in their innate endeavors, celebrating material wealth and education, only to pass away without ever comprehending these transitional states. It is uncertain what has been understood from this. What have you grasped from all that has been said thus far? It is insufficient if we merely continue speaking. May you truly comprehend it. Only when you attain a level of understanding comparable to ours can we progress further. Let us simply impart what we know.

[GV4:P1066] The current epoch is indeed the *Kali Yuga*. There is no utility in regressing to the past; those eras have concluded. Upon entering the *dharma* of this *Kali Yuga*, the Divine itself has caused the manifestation of numerous preceptors and prophets within this realm. Therefore, our *dharma* in the *Kali Yuga* must be one that critically evaluates the rights and wrongs traversed across multitude of past lifetimes. That pertains to understanding the true nature of one's own life force (*jiva*). How can we comprehend this? You must understand it. We must experientially realize the evolutionary nature of the soul, which has taken births even in forms as lowly as a worm. What is the methodology for this? We cannot meticulously sift through and analyze all of that. That is not why this is being articulated to you. You need not contemplate it at all. Dwelling upon it will only cause you distress. However, it is also impossible to refrain from thinking about it. Why? The realization that 'I' am the evolutionary product of a soul that originated from such lower depths inherently characterizes human intellect. It is said that becoming truly human is in itself an arduous task. Thus, do we not commonly say, 'He behaves like a bull'? In such instances, the individual is exhibiting aggressive tendencies comparable to that of a bull. Yet, this is a birth taken in human form. It is the consequence of actions (*karma*) lived out akin to an animal. The entity exists as a human, but possesses only the external physical form of a human. It has incarnated in myriad forms. The differentiation of transitional states (*avasthantharabhedam*) is the process through which one incarnates as a human across multiple epochs, gradually progressing to a stage where human karmic potential can achieve liberation. Nevertheless, one has been born as a human. One might question whether all humans are not inherently human. True; in practical terms, all are humans. No soul exists completely devoid of that nature. Suppose we have taken a birth now. We engage in various acts of worship, perform malevolent deeds, benevolent deeds, unnecessary deeds, and eventually pass away. What becomes of this soul? It will go and merge. Consequently, the lineage of worship that began with demonic entities remains ingrained within us. Will not the latent tendencies (*vasanas*) of that soul manifest? The only tendency derived from birth is that of being human. The soul (*Atman*), however, remains immutable; it is one and the same. It is merely a process of undergoing transformation.
