[GV1:P1298] July 1983

[GV1:P1299] Some people will come here and ask if they can do something according to their own ideas. I will say, do it if you can. The Guru should only think that much. They themselves must observe and state that it has such and such flaws. God has bestowed them with intelligence as well. They should reflect on it a little. They must become capable of correcting it themselves. That is why they are made to do this. Is it enough to simply sit with folded hands claiming one is obeying words? If someone is told to go and dig, they won't be instructed to dig only in this specific square or at this specific mark. They must clear the area and cultivate it themselves. If something is lacking, they will only be advised occasionally to do it in a certain way. They must do it themselves and gain experience. Consider any profession in the same way. Suppose someone is studying medicine now. A procedure will be demonstrated once. Then they have to perform it using their own reasoning. Look at weaving itself. Each person must rectify their own shortcomings. Similarly with cooking. They will learn to prepare another dish on their own. All these are mastered through practice over time. Likewise, when we take up something new, our own karma (action) must come into play. We must observe where the trajectory of karma, undertaken with the four aspects of karma, yoga, jnana, and bhakti, is applicable in the gross, the subtle, the effect, and the cause. This exists even in the demonic realm. If a sanyasi insists that he will only travel in pure places or sleep only in odorless places, he will attain none of this. While in the avadhuta state, sanyasis wander and travel in this manner. They will sleep wherever they find a spot. Sometimes it will be in a cemetery. Sometimes in a drain. It could be on a shop verandah, on the seashore, or under a tree. Sometimes in various temples. Resting here and there, he is taking upon himself his karma. It is by completing such a course of karma that they return as a yogi.

[GV1:P1300] That course of karma is necessary for both men and women. It is not sufficient if only one person completes it. Thus, a two-fold course of karma—a course of karma where man and woman can work together—must be completed. The course of karma from a small child to one hundred and twenty-five years of age, completed in each lifetime, is entirely discovered through the subtle power of the Guru; by studying its nature and understanding in what manner they should be guided to perform karma, conversely, each individual must be corrected by assigning them different kinds of karma. But one may ask, aren't they all human beings? What one person does is not what another does. Thus, it is pervasive in the total karma of the world. It is infused as vasana (latent tendency) like oil in sesame seeds. To activate this—the dryness of a sesame seed is not that of a coconut, the dryness of a coconut is not that of a marotti seed; thus, if you observe the nature of the oil, there are manifold kinds of karma within it. That oil must be extracted, right? Then one must know how to apply it. You must be able to integrate such things into your life through karma. At the same time, it can be washed and purified through that karma. The completion of karma and washing it pure can occur simultaneously. We ourselves lay the foundation for what is past, what exists today, and what is yet to come. Humans should repeatedly perform such a course of karma. Only then can we offer it as our own. That is why it is advised not to falter when you do something. Those with profound vision have understood this. Jesus is the greatest figure in terms of sacrifice. Not in terms of state. Of all who have descended to this earth to date, Jesus is the one observed with the most profound sacrifice and love.

[GV1:P1309] ways. Until now, it has not been possible for various Mahatmas to arrive and establish such a Guru lineage.

[GV1:P1311] incorrectly?

[GV1:P1312] Answer: No.

[GV1:P1313] Question: Approximately how many Gurus will appear?

[GV1:P1314] Answer: Thousands of Gurus have been seen.

[GV1:P1315] Guru: Not merely one or two Gurus. For us, the existence of the Devas is not just a myth.

[GV1:P1316] Crores of Devas can be witnessed. What do we comprehend from this? (To an experienced householder disciple) Tell us what you witnessed when you visited Guruvayur. (He was previously a non-believer).

[GV1:P1317] Householder Disciple: Standing in Guruvayur gazing at the idol, during the worship

[GV1:P1318] I saw Guruvayurappan in the form of the Guru.

[GV1:P1319] Guru: You saw the Guru there, didn't you? If you see the Guru in Krishna's idol,

[GV1:P1320] what would you infer? Or if you perceive Krishna in the Guru's heart, what should you infer?

[GV1:P1321] Householder Disciple: I was able to perceive that divine consciousness, worshipped even by Krishna, in the form of the Guru.

[GV1:P1322] Guru: Arjuna had lived with Krishna for fifty years. Sometimes, Arjuna was even convinced that he and the Guru had taken numerous births together prior to this. Yet, he was unable to act with firm resolve until then. He faltered at the moment of the war. How could he kill all of them, bound by human reasoning? "I cannot kill!" he said. But Krishna insisted that he must. Despite being told repeatedly, he refused to comply. Then, he was asked to reflect and comprehend it. Such a directive can only be given to someone who already possessed vision. He was asked to look into the purpose of his arrival. He looked. Then, he grasped the reason for his existence. He asked no further questions. He immediately bowed in reverence and commenced the war. Despite possessing so much familiarity and knowledge, he was unable to understand and act initially. Living as contemporaries carries such a limitation. It can happen to anyone. After all, they eat, sleep, play, and laugh together. Consequently, the true nature of this was understood only later. Once he understood, he acted. Meanwhile, the *Gita* is narrated through Sanjaya. Because Dhritarashtra was blind, Sanjaya described it to him through divine vision. It is said that Sage Agastya himself went and advised him, "You must not do this. Do not send them to war; everything will be destroyed." When he did not comply, he was told, "See for yourself—I shall grant you vision." This implies bestowing wisdom. "Acquire it. You can witness your own birth, Krishna's birth, Duryodhana's birth, and so on. Upon seeing that, appoint them for this," he said. However, Dhritarashtra declined it. "Then listen to this; this young boy will tell you," Krishna stated. Thus, with Sanjaya seated beside him, the events of the battlefield were narrated. This is what the *Gita* refers to as *Sanjaya Uvacha* (Sanjaya said). It is only after all these events unfolded that the purpose of Krishna's arrival, the arrival of the hundred men (Kauravas), and Arjuna's arrival were understood. But there is no point in discussing it after the fact. If the people had understood it then, this war and these events would not have been necessary. Why was such a situation created? It is for us to study history. Causing such a great soul to be born among the Shudras was intended to bring the era of the Shudra to the forefront. Is it not evident that this endeavor has been ongoing since the time of Krishna? Even before the onset of the Kali Yuga, a Guru was brought forth and provided. This recurring history was explained in detail through two individuals. By causing Krishna and Arjuna to be born as mediums, that entire karmic process was brought to light. There is an instance where Arjuna, an individual capable of anything, ultimately failed. Arjuna inquired of Krishna why that was so. It marked the beginning of Kali. Stating, "Let us now embrace renunciation (*Sannyasa*)," Krishna and his lineage renounced the world. This is history. The trajectory of karma up to that point was demonstrated. Once Kali begins, neither the deity nor the Guru of the previous eras holds a place. That is the essence of *Sambhavami Yuge Yuge* (I manifest from age to age). To bring this karmic trajectory to the forefront, every aspect concerning Krishna will be visible until the end of this Kali era. It has been demonstrated how Krishna arrived and functioned, and how the Guru arrived and functioned. Yet, the tragedy is that we fail to understand. Ultimately, didn't Krishna himself have to instruct him to realize who he was? It is by guiding us in this manner that everything has been accomplished. Kali began. It has been proven once again that whatever actions one performs in any birth, there are consequences to be faced. Krishna, seated beneath a tree in the forest, was shot and killed by a hunter's arrow. Whosoever it may be, the accumulated course of karma must be experienced and exhausted. It was proven that no matter how many births one takes, this consequence will follow. It is said that his body was consumed in the fire of yoga (*Yogagni*). Even such a great soul could not alter his own karmic trajectory. What multitude of such karmic trajectories must reside within you! This is why it is advised not to apply your mere intellect to this; if the intellect is not aligned with the good and applied, it will lead to errors. You must not employ your own logic or the logic of the state. This is highly perilous. The democracy seen today will plunge you into chaos. It is neither of these; rather, by attributing supreme greatness to the Guru's word, you must harmonize yourself with that greatness, act, and transform into it. That is the profound familiarity known as Guru and disciple.

[GV1:P1324] Birthday Message

[GV1:P1325] September 1983

[GV1:P1326] The world today is engaged in a conflict without a clear adversary. Knowledge battles knowledge, wealth battles wealth, and authority battles authority. It is everyone's belief that this struggle is for the sake of progress. Today, this phenomenon is most prominently observed in India. Gradually, this conflict is escalating to the extent of a father against his son, a son against his father, and a mother against her daughter. The assumption is that this is done with pure intentions.

[GV1:P1327] The *Puranas*, epics, and religious scholars document that this phenomenon is not confined to the present day but existed in previous epochs (*Yugas*) as well. Among religious scholars, both those with spiritual experience and those without have engaged in this equally. Experience, in this context, refers to divine experience. There are also experiences rooted in faith and those rooted in action (*Karma*). Upon comprehensive examination, it becomes apparent that they have acted in a manner that makes everything appear correct. What remains incomprehensible is the ultimate objective they seek to conquer. What is it that they intend to sustain? It can be observed that they have upheld truth, righteousness (*Dharma*), kindness, and compassion to the best of their respective understanding. Those who have operated at the forefront of this endeavor are the Gurus, prophets, and scholars. Following them are the adherents of the four paths: *Yoga*, *Karma*, *Bhakti* (devotion), and *Jnana* (knowledge). There is no one who operates outside these four paradigms. A question may then arise: why specifically highlight the followers of these four paths? The answer is that each one of us is encompassed within these four paths. However, the majority of the populace remains ignorant of the true nature of their actions.

[GV1:P1328] It was mentioned earlier that this dynamic existed in preceding epochs as well. However, the majority have failed to comprehend that these occurrences manifested during the transitional phases—the conclusion of one epoch and the commencement of another. Driven by a universal desire for transformation, they implemented their ideologies. Today, those transformations have fully materialized. It must be deduced that they lacked the foresight to precisely delineate how one ought to function until a specific period. We continue to operate today in the same manner as previous epochs did. Yet, a doubt arises as to whether due consideration has been given to the specific requirements of the current epoch. Indeed, it is no longer a mere doubt but an established reality. There is no need to look far for evidence. Individuals are actively manifesting this within their respective spheres of action and reveling in it.

[GV1:P1329] This is rooted in the premise that India stands at the vanguard of action (*Karma*) and righteousness (*Dharma*). A resolute dedication to action, functioning upon this foundation, has flourished here. Concepts such as religious tolerance, alongside all analogous principles, are encompassed within this. However, a profound ignorance regarding when, where, and how to apply these principles has afflicted all our adherents. Furthermore, it is India that finds itself entirely incapable of eradicating this ignorance. It is not difficult to deduce that other nations have managed to overcome this to some extent, aided by the grace of certain prophets and various astute intellects. This is because, within the framework of the Trinity (*Trimurti*), the *Shakta*, the *Shaiva*, and the *Vaishnava* traditions are each regarded as paramount within Hindu *Dharma*. None is deemed inferior to the others. Furthermore, they have all invariably remembered to strive toward the realization of non-duality (*Advaita*). In this pursuit, both the learned scholars and the unlettered masses demonstrate equal proficiency.

[GV1:P1330] Why does such a situation arise? The adherents of the Gurus from those respective eras constitute the grave underlying context that has plagued India. There is a vociferous, collective demand that this paradigm must be altered. Yet, no one possesses the slightest comprehension of what should replace it. Because each *Shaiva* and *Shakta* faction has introduced its own particular dogmatism, individuals have consequently become entrenched in their respective ideological trenches. The fundamental conviction of those traversing these paths seems to be merely collecting and showcasing the stones, thorns, and sheer disorientation they encounter within those trenches.

[GV1:P1331] However, remaining detached from all of this, without anyone to forge, open, or facilitate a path for them, there exists a group of people who simply wandered arbitrarily along their course. They constitute the Shudra class. For generations, they have been bereft of the knowledge required for their own upliftment. Yet, this Shudra class remains oblivious to the fact that this is an era in which the Divine is bestowing grace upon them. Kerala serves as a definitive illustration of this phenomenon. In Kerala, up until a certain historical juncture, even the monarch belonged to the Shudra class. Historical records attest that the administration of Marthanda Varma and his successors, as well as the preceding reign of Cheraman Perumal, transpired here. Similarly, evidence indicates that individuals belonging to this Shudra class exercised sovereignty across various regions of India. The fisherwoman referenced in the narrative of Sri Ramakrishna has been historically substantiated as a woman of royal lineage. The undeniable truth, however, is that we have failed to unequivocally comprehend and articulate these historical realities.

[GV1:P1332] It is worth reflecting that although many Gurus emerged in India, their followers were unable to practice, sustain, or even preserve a single word of their teachings. Here, the Shaiva, Shakta, and Vaishnava traditions have never failed to shower accusations like a volley of arrows on the Gurus who spoke the truth and on their followers. Caught in this trap, it is usual for these Gurus and their followers to drown over time.

[GV1:P1333] To some extent, it is the Westerners and the Arabs who have escaped from this. However, their time has also passed. It is good that they are also thinking in tune with the times. A great opportunity has arrived for the people of India as a whole to think in accordance with the times. Although we received such opportunities many times in the past, we were unable to bear them. At least now, we must be able to bear it and spread it to the world. This should be our determination. We must reflect deeply on why Buddhism had to be exiled from here and how we should utilize that lesson. Similarly, the Parayi Petta Panthirukulam has proven its steadfastness in action. Basaveshwara, who came in the recent past, was a steadfast man of action despite being a Shaivite. So was Narayana Guru.

[GV1:P1334] All these are being twisted and taken back to the Shakta, Shaiva, and Vaishnava traditions. We are unable to accept any matters suitable for the times. There is evidence that the aforementioned three forms of worship were practiced all over the world. The sacrificial altar which prides itself as self-existent ٹ that is, during the time of Moses, when he saved everyone, helped them cross the river, and taught them everything, among that, when he asked his followers to raise their hands, it became clear that one person could only raise it below the elbow. This was precisely because the face of a bull was held under the armpit. It is seen from the story that he cursed them on that day.

[GV1:P1335] Whatever it may be, what we have gained today from Indian stories is a lot of blessings and curses from gods and goddesses. Today, every person who practices Shakta, Shaiva, and Vaishnava as the right path has become a slave and a yes-man to those who accepted a prophet, yet they have not forgotten to claim that Advaita is supreme. Belonging to a tradition that is unable to think up to the level of cause and effect and take what is needed, and being under the Gurus who act proudly as followers of an assembly of scholars, we are still falling into the same old trench today. Out of lack of prior experience, and because governance and education were far away from them, the Shudras, who flew across freely like birds—neither human nor animal—and walked without digging pits where it lay open, had to accept the slavery of the Western and Eastern intellectuals in the work they did by pretending to be upper caste with what they got. Those eloquent speakers who talk about the universe-embracing Advaita, who belong to both the lower and upper castes and have no hesitation in saying that Dharma has decayed and Adharma is being practiced, are yearning for the dress and language of the white man.

[GV1:P1336] To transform this situation, let us all forget the discriminatory mindset of being a Shakta, a Shaiva, or a Vaishnava, forget religious hatred, and find out the easiest way to accomplish what we need to do in the Dharma of this age called Kali. For this, let the existing wise men*, scholars, religious leaders, and the intellectuals in today's political rat race, without bargaining with each other, think if there is anything we can do in the Dharma of Kali. I use this auspicious day to wish for an opportunity to think and make others think in a way that is useful to all the people of the world, that this twentieth century or the followers of the Gurus who lived then have contributed, as the first step of this Kalidharma, by combining action and righteousness and bearing witness in that compassionate Brahman.

[GV1:P1337] *Those who have the inner eye of wisdom by the grace of God.

[GV1:P1341] Today, the world is fighting a battle without knowing what it is fighting against. Knowledge fights knowledge, wealth fights wealth, and power fights power. Everyone believes that this is for growth. Today, this is mostly seen in India. Gradually, this conflict is escalating to the point where a father fights his son, a son his father, and a mother her daughter. The assumption is that this is done with pure intentions. Puranas, epics, and religious scholars record that this existed not just today, but in other eras as well. Among religious scholars, both the experienced and the inexperienced have done this equally. Experience here means divine experience. There are also experiences based on faith and karma. When examined comprehensively, it can be seen that actions have been carried out in a way that makes everything seem correct. What is incomprehensible is what they intend to conquer or sustain. It can be observed that truth, righteousness, compassion, and empathy have been upheld by each to the extent of their understanding. Those who have operated at the forefront of this include gurus, prophets, and scholars; followed by yoga, karma, bhakti, and jnana—there is no one who does not fall into these four categories. The question may arise as to why the followers of these four paths are specifically mentioned. Most people are unaware that what each of us does falls into one of these four paths. It was mentioned earlier that this existed in previous eras too. Not many have been able to understand that it existed even at the beginning of those eras. Ideas were put into practice with the mindset that everyone needed change. Today, all of that has come to pass. It must be inferred that until now, it was not possible to precisely prescribe how one should act up to a certain period. Today, we continue to act just as previous eras did. However, it is doubtful whether we have considered what this era actually needs. It is not merely a doubt, but a reality. There is no need to look far. Everyone is manifesting it in their sphere of action. That is, India stands at the forefront of karma and dharma. A dynamic spirit of action that functioned in that capacity is flourishing here. Religious tolerance and all similar subjects are part of this. However, all kinds of our followers have fallen into a state of unfamiliarity regarding when, where, and how to do this. It is India that is completely unable to eradicate this. It is not difficult for us to understand that other nations have somewhat succeeded in this due to the grace of certain prophets and many other intellectuals. Because in the Trimurti system, each is great. No one is inferior. None have forgotten to propagate Advaita. In this, both the scholars and the ignorant are equally adept. Why does this happen? The followers of the respective gurus of each period form the grave backdrop that has affected India. Everyone clamors that this must be changed. But no one knows what it should be replaced with. Because every Saiva and Sakteya sect has brought their own obstinacies, everyone has fallen into their respective trenches. The firm belief of these followers is to pick up and present the stones, thorns, and aimlessness found within. However, keeping away from all this, there are a few who have arbitrarily strayed into an untrodden path without anyone to pave, open, or cut a way for them. They are the Shudra class. For ages, they have lacked the knowledge to rise up. But this Shudra class does not know that this is a time when God is compassionate toward them. Kerala is a clear example of this. In Kerala, until a certain period, even the kings were Shudras. Historical records show that the reign of Marthanda Varma and, before him, the rule of Cheraman Perumal took place here. Similarly, it is evident that people belonging to the Shudra class ruled in many parts of India. It has been proven that the fisherwoman mentioned in the story of Sri Ramakrishna was a royal woman. The truth, however, is that we have not been able to understand or articulate all this unequivocally. The Saiva, Sakteya, and Vaishnava traditions have not refrained from raining down accusations like a shower of arrows on the gurus and followers who spoke of this here. Trapped in their snares, these gurus and followers have customarily sunk into oblivion over time. Those who have escaped this to some extent are the Westerners and the Arabs. Their time had also passed. It is good that they are also thinking in tune with the times. An opportune moment has arrived for the Indian people as a whole to think in accordance with the times. Although it came our way many times before, we could not handle it. At least now, we must be able to bear it and impart it to the world. This must be our resolve. We must thoughtfully consider why Buddhism had to be exiled from here. Similarly, the Parayi Petta Panthirukulam has demonstrated dynamic action. Basaveshwara, who arrived in recent times, was a man of dynamic action, despite being a Saivite. The same applies to Narayana Guru. All these are being twisted and taken back to Saktism, Saivism, and Vaishnavism. We are unable to grasp anything that aligns with the times. There is evidence that the aforementioned three modes of worship were practiced all over the world. The sacrificial stone claiming self-existence—that is, during the time of Moses, when he saved everyone, led them across the river, and taught them everything, it became evident that when he spoke of the followers' hands, one person's hand would only rise below the elbow. This is precisely because the face of the bull was kept under the armpit. Stories suggest that he cursed them then.
