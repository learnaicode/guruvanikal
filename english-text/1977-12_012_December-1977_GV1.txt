[GV1:P0193] December 1977

[GV1:P0194] Question: Why did you differentiate between the great sages (Rishis) and the ascetics (Sanyasis)?

[GV1:P0195] Answer: The great sages are those who attain experiential knowledge and document everything in a manner that is highly beneficial to the common people, while also demonstrating its practical application. Only a few among the ascetics acknowledge this. They remain aloof from everything. They maintain no attachments and possess a sense of complete detachment towards all things. They merely exhibit certain imitative behaviors solely for the purpose of sustenance.

[GV1:P0196] Question: So, is it not about such ascetics that our people have been speaking?

[GV1:P0197] Answer: Yes, people simply amuse themselves by recounting tales of those who practice certain yoga disciplines and survive without even needing dry leaves or plain water. They waste their time unnecessarily discussing these stories without any understanding of how useful this is to the public or how people can attain such states. Some individuals, tempted by the intensity of mystical powers (Siddhis) and erudition, waste their time praising them. A true Jnani is one who respects these various aspects by assigning them their appropriate value. This Jnani evaluates everything, demonstrates the validity of all paths to the people, and attempts to impart knowledge by giving it a distinct character.

[GV1:P0198] Question: What exactly did you mean by the distinct character of knowledge?

[GV1:P0199] Answer: By documenting that specific events occurred in certain periods, they perceive only the unadulterated truth within the nature of the Jnanis, and they eventually convince us that there is only one ultimate truth.

[GV1:P0200] Question: You mentioned the Manus earlier. In what context are you referring to them?

[GV1:P0201] Answer: The Manus are the ones who encompass all the descriptions of the world. Upon closer examination, they are the absolute authorities governing creation, preservation, and destruction. The governance of this world is characterized by fourteen Manus leading up to the great deluge (Pralaya). Such great souls have recorded that the duration of each Manu consists of seventy-one Chaturyugas. If their guidance falters, and if the world strays, it will be plunged into perpetual blindness. Until a specific birth occurs to rectify it, the world will have to continue traveling on that misguided path.

[GV1:P0202] Question: Has any of the Manus ever made such a mistake?

[GV1:P0203] Answer: Yes. We shall discuss that later.

[GV1:P0204] Question: What types of worship practices do you think are most desirable to adopt during this period?

[GV1:P0205] Answer: Four modes of worship practices are considered the most ideal.

[GV1:P0206] Question: Which are they?

[GV1:P0207] Answer: Karma, Yoga, Jnana, and Bhakti. It is difficult for Karma and Yoga to be fully reflected in this era. The Karma Yoga spoken of by an ascetic is different. An ascetic considers any action undertaken as Karma. Ordinary people perform actions adhering to certain rules and regulations. This cannot be effectively mirrored in current times. Similarly, Yoga also poses difficulties for common people. Jnana can be manifested in any era. Even if not manifested, nothing else can affect Jnana. Bhakti holds significance in all times. Nothing can reach completion without devotion. Particularly, Bhakti is the most suitable path in this age.

[GV1:P0208] Question: Since you mentioned that Bhakti is the most beneficial in this era, would you be willing to explain how one should practice this devotion, and how it can remain effective from the beginning, through the middle, and until the end of time?

[GV1:P0209] Answer: Initially, learn to practice all the disciplines of a devotee to the point of turning them into experiential nature. Even if we attain a minor spiritual experience in such a manner, the assistance of a Guru in the absolute nature of devotion is required in every way to gain clarity over it. Regardless of whatever flaws may exist in that Guru's conduct, the one who believes will be able to find a way to derive spiritual satisfaction from it.

[GV1:P0210] Question: What do you mean by stating "regardless of whatever flaws"? Can there be various types of flaws like that?

[GV1:P0211] Answer: There are flaws in many ways. When a person practices devotion, if the knowledge attained is not complete or accompanied by humility, an arrogance will arise regarding the knowledge itself. It is specifically termed as 'Satvik Ahankara' (righteous ego). You are all well aware of the consequences that follow when arrogance takes root!

[GV1:P0212] Guruvani
