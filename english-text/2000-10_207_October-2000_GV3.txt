[GV3:P1049] October 2000

[GV3:P1050] If we find the boundaries of a god or goddess or their retinues, how much have we really seen? In truth, this is nothing at all. At the very least, considering the visionary tradition of the past five thousand years, one should be able to unify a Guru, an Acharya, or a Prophet who came within it and move forward. Not a single religious theorist has been able to do this. We haven't even had the time to think about these things. Even if we had the time, we wouldn't be able to think like that. No one has thought beyond what Mahatmaji did. After that, it was Narayana Guru who thought more. Let us say we adopted a path like that of Sri Ramakrishna, Vivekananda, or Shankaracharya. How would we know which of these is right and which is wrong? Everything is in such disarray that it cannot be known.

[GV3:P1051] A girl we raise becomes a mother. That girl should be able to preserve her own culture and an understanding of the country's progress. Who is working to raise her in that manner? Everyone is like a machine facing west. Other than singing what we learned and passing exams, what do we have of our own? If we are not looking west, we are interested in looking north. According to their logic, the tradition of Hindus lies in the north. Another section belongs to the Dakshinamurti faction. Because things got ruined in this way, we could not progress in any way.

[GV3:P1052] Narayana Guru Swami has expressed sorrow over not having a child. The meaning of this is not as uncles say. For example, Nataraja Guru was someone Swami nurtured. Nataraja Guru could not carry forward that idea. Following that, not a single educated person even looked back at it. Everyone went after bookish knowledge by studying Vedanta. Advaita is great, Dvaita is small - if each such argument is made big and small, will the ideas or theories obtained through hardship be correct?

[GV3:P1053] What is meant is that the successors could not do it. Nobody could adapt to and act according to that idea. There is no other field that acted as wrongly as the successors of Narayana Guru. The householders of India have never engaged in a karma of thinking about, growing in, and nurturing their swadharma as householders. One section completed it by speaking of Varnasrama dharma. Another section completed it by speaking of the Vedic religion. This is the logic of the Hindu. The logic of Christians and Muslims is no different. There has not been a single logic that is equally useful to all people. How can we overcome this shortcoming?

[GV3:P1054] We are starting from where all the Acharyas who came and went had stopped. In the last five thousand years, about twenty-five Acharyas like Jesus and Moses came. Over two thousand Gurus came among the Hindus. Around two hundred and fifty individual Gurus, like the Parayi petta panthirukulam and the eighteen Siddhars, came. Their contemporaries did not allow anyone to do anything. Sikh Gurus were killed by being thrown into roasting sand. Sri Krishna was not even allowed to stay in this land. How did this destined karma come upon all of them, making them unable to execute the Divine Will? You must consider whether their actions stood in goodness or went astray.

[GV3:P1055] Whatever kinds of defects there might have been, India is a land enriched by the birth of sages. How did such a land of India end up with such a misguided karmic path? All the Acharyas were those who endured sacrifices to improve humanity in every way. But how much goodness could we extract from their sacrifices? From what states did they operate? What were the transitions they went through? What were they able to achieve? What did they do before leaving? Only those with wisdom can evaluate their actions as having set the stage for a great sorrow.

[GV3:P1056] Kali Yuga is the shortest period among the yugas. Various people speak of the length of the Kali Yuga in different ways. Some say five thousand years, others say four lakh thirty-two thousand years. There are also those who state a length between these two. Whatever the different time calculations regarding the length may be, the scenario presented to convince people that Kali is not good is not true. Is Kali really bad? When we look at all the different types of freedoms and rights humanity has gained in this yuga, Kali is never bad; it is the best. The reason Kali is called bad is because the dharma of Kali is not being practiced.

[GV3:P1057] If the respective dharmas were practiced in Treta Yuga and Dvapara Yuga, and similarly if its dharma was practiced in Satya Yuga, shouldn't its dharma be practiced in Kali Yuga? It is because people did not know how to practice it that they declared Kali as destructive. Countless chaturyugas, more than one can count in a lifetime, have passed without practicing Kali. Our lifespan is such a short period that we cannot determine a chaturyuga in any way. Therefore, we are not capable of fulfilling the Yuga Dharma. Due to this reason, the weakness that has come upon us for a long time continues to afflict us. This affects not only India but the entire world.

[GV3:P1058] Now let us look at the births of great souls. It was mentioned earlier that there are about twenty-five notable births in Islam and Christianity. How many sacrifices they have made for humanity! When we look at how many succeeded in implementing the Divine Will among them, it is not difficult for us to see that there is no one. The truth remains that the Yadava clan could not reap the fortune brought by the birth of Sri Krishna. Therefore, despite having around two thousand sages, yogis, and ascetics in India following Sri Krishna's birth, what all harms have befallen India! In Kerala alone, two or three great figures including Shankaracharya, and in other parts Jain and Buddha came, and they too got caught up in the extended stories of scholars. Because of all this, we suffered massive defeats. The reason for those defeats is that we did not become strong enough to internalize and act upon the teachings of these great ones.

[GV3:P1059] We must start only from where these great souls stopped. Otherwise, we will just keep counting these defeats one by one and lie there. What purpose do we achieve by counting like that? Nothing. We must rely on the Guru who points out these mistakes. We must believe in such a Guru. If a thousand people believe like that, a true visionary aspect will be useful for only four or five people. Thus, out of a lakh people who might benefit from the vision, there will not be more than five who can be selected. Through these five, it will be revealed what we can achieve through this Guru in the light of all the Gurus who have come so far. That Guru is the seat of permanent peace for humanity.

[GV3:P1060] A Vyazhavattam (Jovian cycle) is twelve years. This is the calculation of the rotation of the Earth, Sun, Moon - all nine planets. We can never take up the karmic path that has spun like this through ages. However, the experience here is that if the Guru is entirely capable, the vision of the great predecessors can be obtained through that Guru. These great souls will come in the light of this Guru and show the path. This is the complete proof that this Guru is entirely capable. How will such a Guru be useful in our lives? Even if useful, how will we accept it? Reach the path of this Guru in a way that gives liberation to everything that was worshipped ancestrally. This is success in life. After granting liberation, the Guru’s dharma is to take the karmic path that is mixed as gross, subtle, causal, and effect, and correct it for the disciple. This is the dharma of the Guru. Only the Guru who falls within that dharma is eternal peace. Completion of Vedanta, logical arguments, and debates are not going to be useful to us. We are not going to gain anything either.

[GV3:P1061] There is vision in all religions. We also have a karmic path to perceive in what ways it exists. However, what we need to know is what weaknesses exist in the paths where predecessors were accepted in vision, yoga, devotion, and the path of action. The Guru who points out the remedy for that weakness to the disciple will make the disciple perform this remedy himself. It is not the Guru who should perform the remedy. Even if the Guru does it, it is only to correct the mistakes that might have occurred in the disciple's karma. Otherwise, the Guru must not do the action that the disciple is capable of doing. Because, if the Guru does it, that action will cease with the Guru's time. The karmic path lived out by the disciple for that purpose is the perfection of sacrifice. Learning and teaching the glories of that sacrifice is the true Guru-disciple relationship. The biggest matter is how much we have achieved in such a Guru-disciple relationship. Simply repeating, learning, and teaching what the Guru showed does not make a true Guru-disciple relationship. Bringing a disciple to this level is the Guru's sense of duty. This era, which has been fully granted to this lineage for this sense of duty, is the best indeed.

[GV3:P1062] Guruvani
