[GV1:P0172] November 1977

[GV1:P0173] Question: So, what should be understood from this is that the Gurus you speak of themselves exist in numerous natures like this, and their states themselves exist in various ways, right?

[GV1:P0174] Answer: Yes.

[GV1:P0175] Question: If so, what kind of Gurus should actually lead this?

[GV1:P0176] Answer: Gurus of all kinds can show the reality. But that is solely dependent on their growth.

[GV1:P0177] Question: Are you referring to the growth in the path of each one, or the overall growth?

[GV1:P0178] Answer: There are Gurus who reach reality through the growth of each one. There are also Gurus who reach reality by seeing it as a whole.

[GV1:P0179] Question: You said that there are Gurus who reach through the growth of each one. How is that?

[GV1:P0180] Answer: A devotee is a traveler of a path. A yogi is a traveler of a path. A karma yogi is a traveler of a path. The Jnani, who grows by bringing all these together, is a visionary. There are also Gurus born at the beginning and end of yugas (epochs). The names of the Gurus concerning the yugas are Avatara Purushas (incarnations). They will also have some special characteristics.

[GV1:P0181] Question: What would those special characteristics be?

[GV1:P0182] Answer: We have seen some historical records, haven't we? Sree Rama, Sree Krishna, and Balarama were all born in different eras. Among them, Sree Rama was born in the Treta Yuga and showed many special characteristics. But none of the previously mentioned ones were able to show such great special characteristics as Sree Krishna.

[GV1:P0183] Question: What could be the reason for that?

[GV1:P0184] Answer: When changes in the epochs (yugas) occur, certain specific changes will also be seen in the prominent figures (Yugapurushas) born in those respective epochs. A Chaturyuga is completed by four yugas. Therefore, the gurus born at the beginning and end of that epoch will possess greater capabilities. The authorities of this Chaturyuga are the Manus.

[GV1:P0185] Question: What is your opinion regarding the authority of individuals like Sri Krishna in this context?

[GV1:P0186] Answer: Sri Krishna is the final guru of the Dwapara Yuga and the first guru of the Kali Yuga.

[GV1:P0187] Question: What is the reason for you to state both together?

[GV1:P0188] Answer: Sri Krishna has exhibited many independent actions. His glory will continue to be reflected to some extent until the final phase of this Kali Yuga. That is what stems from that independence. It is not mere independence; rather, whatever is necessary emerges appropriately at that very moment. If one examines the lives of other incarnations (Avataras), the concept of acting 'according to command' can be observed. However, Sri Krishna operated entirely independently. The names of the gurus and incarnations who have appeared thus far continue to be repeated. Yet, in practical application and in the enthusiasm of the people, is it not Sri Krishna who is seen at the forefront?

[GV1:P0189] Question: What is the reason for this independence being reflected in Sri Krishna?

[GV1:P0190] Answer: When referring to Kali, does it not evoke the impression of doing anything with an independent nature? Yes, it does. If one closely observes Sri Krishna, it becomes evident that, like a primordial trait, he reinforced that independence and the outdated historical records right at its inception. Similarly, he led his life in a manner that suggested the impending future would possess distinctive characteristics. Next is the nature of the visionaries belonging to the lineage of the aforementioned Manus. They acquired knowledge over long periods and documented certain teachings and guidelines. Through this, profound foresight is also seen to flourish. However, the sages (Rishis) who evolve within the lineage of the incarnations continue certain hymns and traditions of service. They demonstrate various practices of mystical powers (Siddhis). The realized souls (Jnanis) who grow in the lineage of Manu merely advise and disseminate knowledge suitable for their respective times. There are Siddhas and Jnanis who fail to recognize this distinction. They often criticize various matters, offer advice, and depart. It is merely akin to certain Vedantins attempting to portray themselves as Jnanis by quoting the words of the realized ones without possessing any experiential knowledge. The majority of the Jnanis we encounter on this earth are those who flaunt their erudition by solely consolidating the maxim, 'Brahma Satyam, Jagan Mithya' (Brahman is the truth, the world is an illusion).

[GV1:P0191] For a true Jnani, knowledge means self-knowledge (Atma Vidya). It is that which self-illuminates within one's own soul. Such Jnanis are the true assets we possess. For instance, consider the science of Ayurveda. Similarly, Mantra Vidya, Tantra Vidya, and Shastra Vidya. If one were to examine each of these individually, they would appear as the ultimate truth. It is through these disciplines that we practically comprehend the documented experiences of the great sages and a select few ascetics.

[GV1:P0192] Guruvani
