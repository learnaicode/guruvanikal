Gurukula Brahmaniveditham

The ceremony performed by Brahmins lasts for nine and eleven days. This ceremony involves seating boys and girls, reading out a summary of the Puranas and epics, and explaining them both materially and spiritually. A summary of the six Shastras and the four Vedas is read over ten days (six plus four). The eleventh day is the wedding. They also perform a specific ritual during that wedding. Although not everyone does it at home now, it is still practiced in the homes of chief Tantris. In other homes, the father, mother, and closest relatives sit together, fill a large vessel with water, catch a fish, and while chanting Vedic mantras, make the bride and groom scoop it up. This symbolizes the birth of Vyasa.

However, the ignorance they imposed on the Shudra class includes Kettukalyanam and Pulikudi. This was done to prevent Shudras from attaining Brahmajnana (knowledge of Brahman). To announce that a girl has come of age, relatives and friends are invited. An ada (a type of pancake) is made using nine types of grains. The girl who has come of age is seated on a mat placed over this ada, and everyone gathers around and showers flowers on her back. This ada is then given to the Nair of the Illam. The remaining adas are cut and distributed to all relatives and friends. The name of this ada is Poriyuda. Whoever eats this Poriyuda will never attain Brahmavidya.

The ritual we have initiated to correct this misguided practice is Gurukula Brahmaniveditham. Brahmavidya is the knowledge acquired through the practices instructed by the Guru. For this, the girl must be protected as a Brahmacharini. Children worthy of Brahmajnana will be born to such a Brahmacharini after marriage. These children are called noble offspring (Salsanthanam). Such a ceremony did not exist among us before. Therefore, God has revealed this ceremony for us. It also signifies informing the parents and the community that the girl has reached the age to acquire knowledge. By following this path of instruction, one can at least reach the state of Saraswati, Parvati, or Lakshmi. Knowing at least this much, we must raise our girls and boys in a way that benefits the world. They can proceed as Brahmacharis and Brahmacharinis, as householders (Grihasthashramis), or through the stages of Brahmacharya, Kaumara, Vanaprastha, and Sannyasa. This is the knowledge we have received. This lineage in particular, and everyone else, must know this. The position these children attain is also acquired by their parents. The purity of the Gurukula Brahmaniveditham lineage, as that soul takes birth again, is intended to make this lineage universal. This is the purity of purpose behind this ritual. What they see through the vision they receive is the culmination of millions of years of sacrifice by not just gods and goddesses, but the entire lineage of prophets. This is why it is said that they should at least attain the position of Saraswati, Parvati, or Lakshmi through this vision. Even without making these sacrifices, children here who have the vision are attaining this state. This ritual is meant to bring that into our children through action. However, not all children may receive this blessing. For the lineage of children whose souls do not have this right, this ritual helps them acquire it through action. The Brahmacharis, Brahmacharinis, and householders here are proof of this.

What have the authors of the Puranas in the Vedic religious tradition done? The Puranas and epics are crafted in a way that does not equip children to distinguish between good and evil, nor does it make them worthy of the Guru's karma. Through the Guru's effort, while the Guru is present, we all receive this blessing. But what when the Guru is not there? Even while the Guru is alive, through disciples or visionary figures, all forms of karmic proficiency in the Guru-disciple relationship are attained by the disciples through the Guru's grace. This karmic proficiency in nature

It keeps it dissolved. One should consider whether the right to such a great ceremony is needed while the Guru is alive. All this must be made a right in Karma, included in Dharma, and given to the world through the Guru Parampara. Moreover, the goal of this ceremony called Gurukala Brahma Niveditham is to sort out some of the flaws committed according to customs included in the Vedic religion and to complete them in the Dharma of realization.

Through this karma, we are completing the karma of Chaturvarnya to serve the complete knowledge of the Satya Yuga. For this, parents, guardians, men, and women, without distinction, make the Brahma resolution the cause of world creation, and this Guru Parampara is the foundation for it.
