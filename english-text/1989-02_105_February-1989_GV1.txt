[GV1:P2324] February 1989

[GV1:P2325] We bear the moral transgressions (*dharmadosham*) that have descended through the karmic actions accumulated by our ancestors. Eradicating these moral transgressions is attainable solely through another resolute action. If we fail to comprehend how the elements termed karmic afflictions and moral transgressions have reached us, and how we harbor them, we will endure the absolute misfortune of being entirely incapable of imparting this knowledge to our children. Therefore, comprehending how this has befallen us is paramount; it is not merely a matter of preventing its recurrence, but by retrieving and examining these past occurrences, we must definitively determine how to resolve these incurred afflictions through our lineage—this is not a solitary issue. In its stead, we must accumulate merit (*punya*) of equal or double magnitude, and portray our very lives as a paradigm of a karmic resolution framework. To manifest such a life, we require profound knowledge.

[GV1:P2326] Among us, within the societal divisions of Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, and Shudra, it is noted that there exist one hundred and eighty distinct castes even among those who were Vaishyas and Shudras; and these one hundred and eighty castes have conducted themselves with one hundred and eighty divergent dispositions. Consequently, not only has the sense of unity amongst us dissipated, but we have also invited every conceivable indiscretion, corrupting one another in every possible manner. Furthermore, we have arrogated the ownership of karmic actions that even our children cannot approach. We are striving to establish a way of life wherein at least our children might possess the capability to eradicate this karmic burden.

[GV1:P2327] Irrespective of our endeavors, a certain degree of deficiency and injustice inevitably permeates our actions. What must constitute our shared endeavors to rectify this deficiency? There are two pathways to achieve this: through the dutiful actions that must be fulfilled by the responsibilities of a householder; and through the rightful merits we have accumulated. Having traversed the paths of a yogi, a seeker of knowledge (*jnani*), a devotee (*bhakta*), and a practitioner of action (*karmi*), the action that ultimately attains liberation in that karma must also emerge from the foundational bedrock of the actions we perform today. This is not achieved through our independent intellect. Rather, by nurturing within us that profound conviction termed divine will or the will of *Brahman*, we constitute the foundational stone that must impart this wisdom to our young children or to the great souls yet to be born. In this endeavor, there is no distinction between male and female, or young and old. There is no caste, no religion, and no class.

[GV1:P2328] It is fallacious to proclaim that this *Kali Yuga* is worthless! Why? The circumstance exists today wherein a Shudra can enter and operate in any sphere. Even after fully acquiring the rights of a Shudra in this manner, without genuinely comprehending the essence of a Shudra, and by continually aspiring upwards to govern with ambitions of emperorship, the entire Shudra demographic has veered toward the trajectory of ignorance. Consequently, it is not wisdom that has reached their hands. By propagating perversion, passion, caste, and pervasive subdivisions of caste, this ignorance has proliferated to an extent where it can never be wholly eradicated. How must this be resolved? For that, divine determination is imperative. To become a participant in the divine will, we must inevitably adopt either a preceptor's tradition or a prophetic tradition. A definitive path for this has been expounded to us. This path is paramount. The world has hitherto never been presented with such a path. Furthermore, no alternate path can emerge except in the subsequent epoch. We have transmitted such a profound ideology. All preceptors and all prophets, abiding as inner controllers (*antaryamis*), have been safeguarding this ideology on our behalf. We have orchestrated various karmic actions toward this purpose. Henceforth, the actions you perform must adhere to a strict discipline, order, and methodology. It must be a pathway that is traditional to you. In accordance with this prescribed methodology, a bylaw will be drafted.

[GV1:P2329] This is not a task that just anyone can accomplish. Why? There may exist a religious bylaw or a caste-based bylaw. However, this bylaw serves as a minor, yet profound, amendment to all existing bylaws. Whether one is a Hindu, a Christian, a Muslim, a Buddhist, or a Jain, no one can refute its validity. We are laying the foundational groundwork for the drafting of such a bylaw. This document is being meticulously formulated for the united functioning of all of you. By cultivating unity among yourselves—devoid of distinctions such as male or female, young or old, superiority or inferiority—we are offering this to the world as our greatest contribution, to the utmost of our abilities. There is no utility in speaking of endeavors one cannot fulfill. We do it to the best of our abilities. It is not restricted to any specific individual. If one can merely pinch and offer a grain of soil, that is sufficient. This objective must be realized. It is for this very purpose that you have been bestowed with this knowledge.

[GV1:P2330] How can you impart this knowledge moving forward? I am communicating to you the directive I have received: that this must be undertaken collectively. Are you capable of undertaking it or not? We have arrived in this land. It is this land that has been fortunate enough to receive this. If one asks how, the answer remains unknown. Perhaps, in some intrinsic state, this land possessed a latent virtue. Or perhaps, it is intended for this wisdom to transition away from this place where an extensively degraded culture currently resides. Whatever the reason may be, such a profound fortune has accrued to this land. It is not merely impending; it has already arrived. If they choose to deny it, whether knowingly or unknowingly, the fault is not theirs; they have, at the very least, acquired the fortune to have it assimilated by their future descendants. As a precursor to this, we are undertaking the initial imparting. By imparting it, we are demonstrating it to them.

[GV1:P2331] Today, a large number of people are visiting Santhigiri. This is not enough. All of them, transcending caste, religion, or class, must eliminate the karmas accumulated over countless births in order to establish a traditional lineage. It remains attached to the soul. It is the complete manifestation of a great and relentless effort that must be undertaken by cultivating love, faith, glory, devotion, and karma as a tradition. No matter how much we work for this, it will not be sufficient. It is an endeavor where no amount of time spent working will ever be enough. I am stating this as an announcement so that you may all develop the strength to undertake this. All of you must unite and embrace this *Brahmanischayam* (divine will), irrespective of differences such as male or female, great or small. We, being of the ignorant *Shudra* class, must undertake this to the best of our abilities and ensure that others do as well. We must establish the right to usher in the *Satyayuga*, bringing transformation not just to the *Brahmin*, *Kshatriya*, and *Vaishya*, but to all living beings. *Kali* (the Kali Yuga) is the era born for us to attain liberation and claim this right. You must undertake this together by taking a solemn vow.

[GV1:P2332] The pledge, recited and submitted in the holy presence of the Guru and the Guru lineage (*Guruparampara*), is appended below:

[GV1:P2333] 'Correcting the mistakes committed by us and our lineage until this day, we solemnly pledge before the truth of the Guru that we shall transition to the righteous path granted to us as divine grace through the *Guruparampara*, and that we shall, to the best of our understanding, acquaint ourselves with and introduce to others that *Brahmanischayam* in accordance with the divine will, handling it appropriately.'
