[GV1:P1189] June 1982

[GV1:P1190] The history of such Gurus is what is narrated here. With what profound depth it has been articulated! How can people possibly comprehend this upon hearing it? I have already stated it—seven Gurus emerge across seven epochs. When he departs, the Divine sets him apart in such a concealed manner that no one knows what became of him, and subsequently, for the sake of future eras, he is reborn into a collective of five Gurus within a single epoch. Following that, he is brought back into the lineage of Manu and is incarnated as Manu. How can you ever conclude writing about this? That is what is referred to as the divine play or illusion of the Guru.

[GV1:P1191] Obtaining the history of such Gurus did not prove beneficial to us. Consequently, we adopted the available deities like Bhadra, Kali, and Madan and worshipped them. We simply proceeded in that manner. Today, the era has changed. We must elevate ourselves in accordance with this time. We can achieve this solely through the glory of the Guru. We possess no inclination to study who such a Guru truly is. We might have studied and acquired a fair amount of knowledge. This Guru has not appeared in any of the positions delineated in that history. Then how shall we accord our respect? Such ignorance is perpetually manifesting among us. Those standing in close proximity must exercise extreme caution. Otherwise, that lifetime will be squandered for them. Why did I state that the lifetime would be squandered? Due to the ignorance of those in close proximity, harm will inevitably occur at some juncture. The mind will become suffocated. Consequently, conceptualizing something within the mind, it might occasionally translate into words and actions. With that, is not that lifetime wasted? I am stating this derived from my own experience. Thus, when such matters emerge among you, you will arrive at a scenario where you orchestrate your own destruction. You will develop arrogance akin to the arrogance that befell the children of Sri Krishna. Or you will inevitably acquire it. If you wish to prevent such an occurrence, must you not discipline yourselves through humility, love, devotion, and righteous action? Who is available for that? It is an absolute certainty that one will refuse to be humble. It is equally certain that true love, characterized as devotion, will not be mutually reciprocated. A profound obstacle will invariably be placed upon it.

[GV1:P1192] Have you not observed certain ascetics (*sannyasins*) who aggressively grab hold of you upon sight? There are such individuals in the lineage of Sivananda. The moment they see you, they will embrace you. Thereafter, they will absolutely not let go. They will address you with every conceivable expression of affection and offer you everything they possibly can. One ends up consuming food beyond the capacity of one's stomach. Once they commence offering, they will not relent until it induces indigestion. They will serve food with such intense earnestness. They will meticulously perform all the requisite hospitalities. They will also exhibit the absolute embodiment of humility.

[GV1:P1193] I was speaking regarding the lineage of a Guru. It is in a similar vein that I mentioned earlier that our lineage suffers from a profound impairment. What is witnessed here are the blunders that have ensued due to the administration and contemporary reforms. As a consequence of the constituent subjects growing up in a degraded state, they are devoid of refinement. Their actions are continually oscillating, merely echoing what their fathers and mothers practiced at their respective times. Because of this, it is profoundly difficult to instantly study and comprehend the Guru. However, if you are fortunate, you can comprehend and study the Guru in a single fleeting moment. If further blessed by fortune, it will eventually culminate into a profound realization.

[GV1:P1194] At first sight, an inner prompting arises. An unknown love towards that person. But it will be an unfamiliar love. By unfamiliar love, it is a love we have never felt before. No one can know how it came about. After that, with further acquaintance, another feeling arises, and again, love. At the same time, what if this person does not grow? There is education, sometimes humility, sometimes love, and sometimes it seems like they have everything. But what if their mind is different? Through this acquaintance, he will be kept as an unjust person who seems old. 'Since my youth, I have drawn water for the Guru, brought him charcoal for brushing teeth, boiled water for him, and provided everything, staying with the Swami until the age of twenty-seven' - then he realized. He studied the Swami.

[GV1:P1195] This is one of the deceptions of the Guru. This is said about the Guru. He stayed with that Guru until the age of twenty-seven. From a single moment - only the disciple can know what increases from that moment. However, during this time, he will clash strongly with the Guru nine times. It is like a lone elephant and a measurer assessing the magnitude of growth. Once it is seen that the step is measured, he will be pulled back by the tail. There is a problem here. The Guru will only stand according to destiny. He does whatever is appropriate for destiny at that time. Then no one has anything to do or say about it. Is it easy for a disciple to understand in this state?

[GV1:P1196] A thousand disciples of a Guru will be seen in front, each asserting their own ego. One who lacks this, whether male or female, will only see one. Thus, for a perfect Guru, there will be seven people. These seven people will perceive in seven different ways. But the Guru is also a great trickster. This speaks of the Guru's deception. Having quarreled with a neighbor, one person will come to the Guru and complain that the other did this and that. The Guru will side with him, and the other will also come and complain. He will find some fault in this one too. Seeing his fault, he will scold him severely and reprimand him. What would you think if you saw this?

[GV1:P1197] Even the most worthless person will come to the Guru. He might be a drunkard and a scoundrel. The Guru will stand before him as well. On the contrary, he will also stand before someone who comes to behead him like a Kapalika. There, it is the disciple who must logically protect him. At the same time, he will also stand before one who lives a moderate life. He will stand in the same way before a woman who is the greatest adulteress. If she comes groomed and adorned, like a murderous rogue elephant (as Sree Narayana Guru said), that Guru will stand before her like a mosquito. Then, how can we understand this Guru?

[GV1:P1198] One person comes and says - Swami, he has encroached on my property. Another says, Swami, I trusted him, gave him everything, and loved him. Here, he has abandoned me and left. I also have three or four children. This is also said before the Guru. At the same time, a madman with a broken head has also arrived. Similarly, someone with nothing and no thoughts will come asking for today's porridge. A few perfect scholars will come together with another subject. An Ayurvedic physician, an astrologer, and a sorcerer will come as another set. Do you see the people coming to the Guru? Then there will be one who, upon seeing him from afar, falls down, prostrates at every step, and falls before him.

[GV1:P1199] How long will it take to see all of them together and understand that all this is acceptable? Then it is assumed that they all have understood the Guru's deception. They will not understand. He will speak peacefully with all of them. He will stand by them as well. But if he does some little work, he will also show anger towards him. He will scold him only for what he has done. Have you seen that? It is said that one could not stand near Sree Narayana Guru Swami. It is very difficult to stand near Gurus. In this state, it is impossible to understand great souls by standing near them. Unless one stands very worthily, it is impossible to know about them.

[GV1:P1200] Guruvani
