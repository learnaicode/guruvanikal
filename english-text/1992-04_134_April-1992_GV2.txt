Malayalam source paragraphs GV2:P0247 to GV2:P0263
English of the whole month, in the translator's own paragraphs.

April 1992

In an ashram without brahmacharis, sacrifice will be less. An ashram can reach its true nature only if it achieves at least ninety percent of complete sacrifice. Those living in such an ashram must first be careful about the weight of good and evil in their own minds.

We have love for our father, mother, and others. But if we observe carefully, we will know that this love is not that great. It can be understood only if observed very carefully. That is why at times we feel love to a greatly increased extent. The tendency to seek to increase one's own happiness through love for others is innate in everyone. Any method will be adopted for that.

As man goes along with the flow of emotions, love is lost. Eventually, a state of uncertainty will arise. What should we do about it? We must place our witness in 'one'. That witness must also be appropriately present before us.

Why do we see flaws in a Guru? Because the general concept of a Guru, or the chosen deity, has different states. A perfect Guru is beyond states. If one does not have the qualification to voluntarily obtain such a Guru, or to find one by searching, or to see perfection in the Guru once found, they will leave at a certain stage. Whatever was done until then will also be ruined. That is what was mentioned earlier -- love will decrease and one will end up in a state of uncertainty. Therefore, whatever the life may be, we must have a certainty in that life. Even a butcher needs that certainty. A householder leading the most worthy life can attain more merit than an ascetic. Whether a householder or an ascetic, the Guru's love will be according to each person's capacity for action. One who is firm in the Guru-disciple relationship will not doubt the Guru. He will not hesitate to perform his duties.

The life of a householder has five parts. Five duties - household duty, duty to gods, duty to ancestors, duty of charity, and duty to the self. Of these, the household duty is exhausted in action. None of the others can be done by knowing them oneself. The fulfillment of the past, present, and future life fulfillments is to be accomplished through the performance of these duties. Only a Guru can do this. The gods have an authority in the five duties. Because two periods - Treta and Dvapara - are the periods of the gods. In the four yugas, one yuga belongs to man. Another is completely for man to change his demonic nature. Gurus who teach these five duties with discrimination are rare.

A Guru's love is not human. Therefore, it is not so easy to understand a Guru's love. The disciple must learn how a Guru is. A Guru will also stand with a thief -- he is his Guru too. Even a thief does his work praying, "Oh God, let no one see this."

Everyone's devotion is of a different kind. There are those who pray, "Give me time to pray after all this work is done." There are those who pray, "Let my husband go blind." After the age of twenty, girls develop a kind of devotion. There is no shortage of those who pray crying for the fulfillment of their needs. That is for the realization of their desires. They will do any service to get things done. If the desire is fulfilled, they will kick it away. Some, however, maintain devotion without desiring anything. One can understand all this only if one knows what dharma really is.

A Guru is like a thief. He will stand with every thief. He has to beat and shake his karma like an arecanut flower. How should we respect such a Guru? If we cannot respect him knowingly, we must believe blindly. But that blindness should not be of an ignorant kind. If it is an all-knowing Guru, one must surrender oneself.

A disciple will be one who cannot tolerate hearing anyone say a fault about the Guru. The disciple's feeling will be, "It is my need that my father should be well." This is perfect faith and love. Anyone can accept a Guru. But how many can become like Vivekananda? When the Guru fell ill, all the disciples inquired. However, only Vivekananda drank the pus kept in the vessel. Similarly, Jesus also had twelve disciples. But there was a flaw in love on each side.

Even among children of the same father, love is expressed in different ways. That is the only way it can be in this world.

Some people may not be able to fully trust the ashram. The reason for this is their inherent tendencies. For this to change even slightly, three elements are required. Three times in each stage, thus a total of nine times, the change will come and become firm. This is how the karmas of the three periods—past, present, and future—are combined to bring the disciple to a steadfast faith that makes him worthy of liberation. To attain this state, the guru's radiance must spread across galaxies.

The Guru will stand by everyone, in order to crush their karma. If opposed, it will not be possible. That is what Jesus did. Jesus knew about the karmic path of Mary Magdalene. Buddha knew about the karmic path of the Kapalika. But Shankaracharya made a mistake. The disciple may not have the sincerity of the Guru. The Guru cannot abandon anyone, can he?

The devotee must be vigilant about how to guide the mind. Purity of action is essential. Suppose one could only plant a mango sapling and water it. But even that much must be pure.

The knowledge to accept a Guru must initially be obtained from the mother. No matter what past-life flaws one has, half of them will be resolved by the mother's goodness. The mother must understand all the habits of the child. Such a mother herself should raise the child. A look of love and seriousness must both be full in the mother. Because of love, the child will not be able to abandon the mother. Seeing the mother's seriousness, the child should develop fear and devotion. Raising such a mother is the most important thing. It is also the most difficult karma. If a mother who hides things from the father raises the child, the child will not turn out well.

We will receive all kinds of blows. At that time, we must have the love and courage not to abandon and leave. That is the removal of our sinful power. Humility can also be acquired in this way. These kinds of things cannot be obtained from anywhere else other than from a Guru or a lineage of Gurus.

This is the first step of ashram dharma. The disciple must carry it and impart it to others in an exemplary manner. Then he should travel extensively and acquire knowledge. Finally, he must return to the ashram itself and lead a peaceful life.

Guruvani
