[GV1:P1445] October 1984

[GV1:P1446] There is an art to Sanyasa (asceticism). What is called the art of Sanyasa is filled with immense merit and love. Most people do not have any idea about it. That is no one's fault either. The past two Yugas were times that highly praised, knew, and proclaimed the era of the Devas. In this Kali Yuga, which has lasted only five thousand years, the things spoken by various great souls and prophets at different times have only partially reached our ears. It may have reached a few people according to their respective merits. There are many who hear and dismiss it. However, this is not a time to dismiss it, but to pay attention. Within these five thousand years, a section of people who were considered the most inexperienced and uncultured have ruled and come forward. But even though efforts were made for the smooth administration of the country or the world, they have not been successful. However, the time for it to be sufficiently successful is now. It is God's decree that it must happen through humans.

[GV1:P1447] Without understanding this divine will, what all are our people still doing today! Worshipping Bhadrakali, their attitude is 'I will show the greatness of my Goddess'. Bringing all kinds of 'absurdities' through these rituals, and intertwining our very lives in them, we have degraded ourselves to such an extent that we do not even desire the survival of the soul. When speaking of the survival of the soul, being born as a human itself is for the achievement of the soul. A soul attains a human birth only after millions of past lives. Human birth is the most supreme among all births. Getting a male birth is even more supreme. A birth as a devotee is even more distinguished than that. Therefore, we can achieve immense fulfillment through a human life. It is the flaw of not evaluating and reflecting upon this that, despite the advent of Mahatmas (great souls) all this time, it has not been of proper use to us. What was revealed through the divine message here is that two thousand four hundred and forty-four Mahatmas were caused to be born in the Kali Yuga itself. Even though so many great souls were born in the Kali Yuga, the shortcomings of the people could not be resolved.

[GV1:P1448] As an example of the aforementioned shortcoming – after we are born, grow up, and lead a moderate householder's life (*Grihasthashrama*), the subsequent period is the time to earn something for the soul. However, if we examine each individual's life, we see that their householder life extends until the end of their life. Once a person leads a householder's life and their children have grown up—regardless of the good or bad that befalls them—the logic of staying back to raise and reform them, their children, and their children's children, instead of seeking the path to spiritual liberation (*Jivanmukti*), is incomprehensible. Their entire lifetime is spent raising children, and then they depart. They have neither seen anything beyond this nor do they possess any other thought. Those who pass their time thinking, "If I could just get this youngest daughter married off, I could have taken *Sannyasa*," will not feel like stepping away even after getting that youngest daughter's daughter married. Then they realize the elder son's daughter's child is ready for marriage. The thought would be, "It would not be a bad idea to die after seeing her married off as well." In this manner, getting continuously entangled, they have absolutely no concern for their own souls. We need nothing else for our downfall in this state—we ourselves become the cause of it. This is the meaning behind the saying that one will lie bound in the cycle of birth and death like a cartwheel.

[GV1:P1449] Saying this does not mean that you should not rejoice upon seeing your own children. There are lessons these children must learn over time by observing the lives of this grandfather, grandmother, father, and mother. When that happens, they will reflect, "The same status that my predecessor lived and demonstrated is sufficient for me." Thus, this is a good method to prevent bequeathing ruin to the world.

[GV1:P1450] You led a comfortable life. Yet, for the comfort of your children and their children, you live with the attitude of "making money even by stabbing another to death." You have no time to reflect on what purpose this serves. Whenever one looks, there are only some forms of money-making schemes and debts. Beyond this, you are unable to earn anything else. How many generations backward will the progeny of one who hoards excessively in this manner regress? Will not the entire karmic consequence of what was acquired through wicked means be transmitted to them as well? We should not allow ourselves to be ruined in this manner. This does not mean wealth is unnecessary. While alive, one must not stretch out one's hand to beg from any human being. For that, one must certainly work hard through a righteous path, eat properly, and earn wealth. Only then will our children also turn out the same way.

[GV1:P1451] Today, the children of householders who return from Persia (the Gulf) have a certain way of walking about for a few days. They are seen in a state where they have no idea how to drape their clothes or how to walk. It is mostly seen in the Varkala region. Chirayinkeezhu taluk is the most prominent for it. They do not even know how the wealth they earn should be utilized. If handled in this manner, what will become of their next generation! Such impoverished children will eventually disappear from sight. They will commit any kind of robbery. Having acquired a little education as well, they will completely ruin the land. They will turn into such vile individuals. Therefore, such householders must be extremely cautious. All this stems from their ignorance. This is because they have not lived in the correct manner. Therefore, when they get a few pennies, they show this arrogance, thinking they will not be considered smart unless they create a commotion over it. A nature that rectifies all this ignorance must emerge through our lineage itself.

[GV1:P1452] Guruvani
