[GV1:P1855] January 1988

[GV1:P1856] When we desire a method of worship, it is customary to do something or the other. It is as a result of worshipping in forests, hills, and various places that these tribal dances and other such practices emerged in the wilderness. After this happened, many great men came to India again. Along with that, they also imparted some knowledge to the Westerners. Within these four thousand years, first Moses, and then about two thousand years later, Jesus—having come like this, they suffered greatly regarding this, and as a result of following the words of that prophet, they gained quite a few followers from among the lower classes. That happened here as well.

[GV1:P1857] But not a single one who rigidly held onto the label of 'Hindu' here improved. Some became Christians, some Muslims, some Buddhists. Even after such decay, quite a large number still remains. During this time, they fought in the name of freedom. Thus, they obtained something. All this is true. But what do you intend to achieve with this life? Do we not merely possess the knowledge that passes by like this?

[GV1:P1858] What is your interest in a human being living and dying? If each of you were to think—what could it be? You are householders (Grihasthashramis). That is what is being asked. When you are householders, you stand in the karma of upholding dharma by living as two beings in the capacity of husband and wife. So, what is this dharma? What is karma? What is the order of life? What is the special truth within it?

[GV1:P1859] When one becomes a sannyasin (ascetic), there is no need to ask this. When he becomes a sannyasin, let him walk, come, or go! If he gets something, he gets it; otherwise, he doesn't. Let it be whatever it is. He has no responsible duties to perform here. He is either performing penance for himself or for the welfare of the world. And if he does that, he does it; if not, he doesn't.

[GV1:P1860] As for you, what is your karma? What is your duty? Suppose you are in the position of a Brahmachari (celibate student) when you are a student. How should we carry forward the condition of the karma that involves teaching that student, learning, making them capable of ruling, and making them capable of leading a family life? In such a position, who are you, and how have the other conditions of the household been managed? How far have you reached in your karma? What is the weight of your sins and merits?

[GV1:P1861] You should have the knowledge to think at least about these things yourself. This is karma. Without the knowledge to reflect upon this, whatever you may accumulate will slip out of your hands. Not only that, but it will also cause certain calamities. I have not told you to become ascetics. Nor have I said that you should change your customs. How does karma reside within your trajectory of dharma? Have you ever thought that it is a punishment from God or something similar? It is not so. Then how did it come about? You are people who have reached an age where your external life is almost coming to an end.* Therefore, you must be able to articulate that. You are the ones who have practiced the administration of the householder's life. Statecraft is another matter. You are well aware of the mistakes that occur in life.

[GV1:P1862] Did it happen because we ourselves desired to become bad? Or did it happen because of our intermittent mistakes? It happened due to mistakes. Mistakes might have occurred for some time. Yet, even knowing that this is a mistake, why do you not know how to change it? You, who independently perform the dharma of a householder, experience complete freedom. It is a system where, with full awareness, you can act to make things better or worse. What is the flaw? Why are you unable to change from that flaw? When someone came with you out of desire, why do you later say that it fell apart? If you can lead even a small life, there should be some prosperity derived from it.

[GV1:P1863] Guruvani
