[GV1:P1240] December 1982

[GV1:P1241] Various great personages emerged and established a way of life intrinsically aligned with the environment and natural conditions of the specific regions they inhabited, structuring it in a manner profoundly beneficial to humanity. The Occidentals developed a lifestyle and culture meticulously adapted to their indigenous environment. The Orientals accomplished the exact same. The Occidentals conceptualized and structured their civilization so effectively that it would remain relevant and endure for a millennium beyond their own lifetimes. Contemplate the fact that our own lineage has evolved through a similar process. This is a legacy meant to survive for hundreds of thousands of years. Accordingly, we must forge a robust cultural heritage for our lives and our progeny. An individual living a hundred thousand years from now must still possess a memory of us and retain our cultural excellence. They must not exhibit the aforementioned perversities. If such moral degradation were to occur, all of these legacies would ultimately be rendered utterly useless to us.

[GV1:P1242] For what purpose were we born here during this specific epoch? This is the critical juncture when we must deeply contemplate and act upon what we ought to contribute to our homes, our society, our nation, and the world as we mature. We must determine what benefits those who once embraced and loved us should derive from us, and similarly, what utility we should offer to those who both criticized and assisted us. Today, we may live a healthy life for perhaps ten or sixty years. Within this finite span, we encounter a multitude of life's triumphs and tribulations. We experience various degrees of social stratification and inequality. Furthermore, we often exist in complete obscurity, unknown to anyone.

[GV1:P1243] Can we ever reclaim a precious moment of life that was squandered due to a perverse and degraded existence? Let each individual reflect upon the fact that it is the "I"—an ego incapable of even resolving that its mind should not inflict pain upon another or become entirely useless to them—that parades this arrogance! When we critically examine each of these aspects, we must ask: Who are we? For what purpose are we living? What is the ultimate trajectory of our existence? What intrinsic moral value do I possess to transmit to our future lineage tomorrow? Or, is there any possibility of preventing inherent flaws within myself? If such a possibility exists, from where must it be imbibed? Even if it is imbibed, what definitively ordained principles do we possess to ensure its enduring survival? Who must we aspire to become? How must that paradigm be transferred to our lineage? What relevance does this have to my own life? How far removed am I, then, from this ideal? It is imperative that each and every one of us engages in this profound introspection.

[GV1:P1244] Somewhere along the line, we are hopelessly groping in profound darkness, entangled in anomalies. It is akin to a blind man aimlessly searching in the void. In such a state, the environment is hostile, and vision is entirely absent. The paramount question is how we can possibly elevate ourselves from an existence utterly devoid of alternatives. Here, the primary favorable condition is the indispensable acquisition of vision. Subsequently, the natural environment must become conducive. Just like this, despite being born as a human being into both a worthy environment and favorable circumstances, what can be done if you deliberately refuse to perceive reality? If your cognitive framework is merely oriented toward living in such a degraded state, how will you ever achieve intellectual or spiritual elevation? God has created you with every necessary qualification to assimilate all these fortuitous advantages and implement them in your life. While you ought to conduct yourself with the utmost care, ensuring that this magnificent gift of creation bestowed upon you is never tarnished, you instead eagerly hasten to engender a lineage that rapidly degenerates into a vile disposition. Who are you, unable to tolerate the sight of others living peacefully? For what purpose were you born into this world? Have you ever deeply contemplated the nature of this world or your own existence?

[GV1:P1245] The sheer extent of behavioral perversity prevalent among today's school-going children becomes blatantly evident upon observing their attire and demeanor. Their parents are evidently not rearing them in accordance with our indigenous natural environment. Infants are paraded about clad in heavy gloves and socks. Our geographical environment is fundamentally distinct from that of the Occident. Our land allows for comfortable and elegant locomotion. Conversely, Occidental nations are often blanketed in thick ice and snow. They don such protective garments exclusively to withstand their harsh climatic conditions, as exposing hands and feet would precipitate frostbite. Residing in a highly favorable tropical climate, we merely observe these foreign practices and relegate ourselves to being the servile appendages of their culture. We have conspicuously failed to traverse the noble path illuminated by our ancestors through their lives of profound sacrifice, nor have we succeeded in assimilating that intrinsic goodness into our own lives. Indeed, we do not even exert the effort to do so. We are currently basking in the radiant light born of their timeless struggles and hardships. How we must internalize all of this, what substantial contributions we must make for the sake of impending generations, and what precise legacy we must forge for our lineage—these are the profound questions we must continuously ponder throughout our lives.

[GV1:P1246] Guruvani
