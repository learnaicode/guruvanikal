[GV1:P1283] May 1983

[GV1:P1284] Our present endeavor is for the negative actions and positive actions to come together, leading to the destruction of the negative actions, and reflecting as positive actions through divine grace. For this endeavor, it is essential that we know certain things. Weighing the rights and wrongs that have come and gone here over time, we must consider whatever we have practiced, how that practice will reflect within us in the future, and how that reflection should benefit the land and the nation. We must first practice and understand that beneficial manner ourselves, and only after acquiring the capability to execute it without flaws will the world be able to accept it. Only if we conduct ourselves with this conviction can we bring it to reality. For this, we must believe that a foundational sense of duty, which ought to have been instilled in us, has indeed been instilled within us. The reason for this is that over many past periods, spiritual experiences occurred here without our knowledge. Through them, certain words and actions were imparted, and it is in the light of those words and actions that we conduct ourselves today. We received that word only after the complete operational capacity of this world was revealed to us. After revealing it, it was shown over several periods how rights and wrongs occurred in this manner, and what else remains for us to do. First, one thing is shown. Then, it will be shown in another aspect. Only after being shown in this manner multiple times have we been able to comprehend it in a systematic order.

[GV1:P1285] That systematic order, to our knowledge, is the Trinity (Trimurti) system—the one that exists among us and is known to us. In this Trinity system, what was first shown were the flaws that occurred in every action performed by the Shakta, Shaiva, and Vaishnava sects. We did not understand why these flawed aspects were being shown. Later, the positive aspects were also shown. It was shown in which era the application of this virtue was required. How did it come into operation? Why did it go wrong? At that time, a distant vision also emerged. It was the lineage of Manu that created the primordial operational capacity for all of this. Thus, it was realized that this lineage had been able to manage and bring it forward properly. Therefore, the lineage appears to have descended in a very worthy manner. It has come like a lineage that we cannot even imagine today. First, the birth of a Guru belonging to this lineage of Manu.

[GV1:P1286] The knowledge of it that has been imparted to us is that nine spheres were created through the power of that penance, and upon those nine spheres, twelve zodiac signs were created, and subsequently, through twenty-seven constellations, its karmic results were manifested. How far-sightedly it was managed for this purpose! What tremendous fruits of effort those great souls must have experienced for this! Today, there is no need for us to dwell on any of this. We are born in the result of that Karma. However, due to our own actions, certain flaws have befallen us.

[GV1:P1287] The flaw being referred to is that, initially, an unorganized path emerged in the lineage of Manu. The beginning implies that this occurred during the third Chaturyuga (four-age cycle) of the seventh Manu. The seventh Manu—according to the knowledge obtained from the Bhagavata and other sources—is named Vaivasvata Manu. It was in his third Chaturyuga that this error occurred. If one contemplates them, it becomes evident that everything happened through them. The wise men of that era, wherever they looked, could see that karma originated through them. Wherever one looks, only the lineage of Manu is visible. Manu himself has transformed into God. There was deep contemplation regarding this. Upon observation, it is seen that even the repetitive capacity of this sphere has manifested through them.

[GV1:P1288] What is that? They are the ones who performed the karma (actions). Thus, they possess authority over it. As they became the authorities of that karma, an arrogance inadvertently grew within them. It is something that happens to us as well. If we accomplish any small task, we tend to feel that we have achieved something monumental. This feeling can manifest in anyone—whether they are householders, ascetics, workers, yogis, wise men, or devotees.

[GV1:P1289] A devotee—when goodness is perceived in his perfected nature, he may think that he has attained near completeness. What might have occurred would merely be the manifestation of some minor deity or goddess. However, the matter he envisioned materialized. He could not see beyond that. We possess the familiarity of assuming that if a deity is manifested, everything is achieved. If, behind this, a situation arises involving Vedic authority or a state where even learning alphabets is forbidden, it leads further into wilderness. We have encountered many such experiences. Yet, no one knows the reason for pointing out the flaw in this lineage of Manu. That is why we remain ignorant about Manu. It has even been asked why others could not attain this knowledge. The reason is that over many periods and many births—when we say many periods and births, one birth might occur four or five times in a single yuga. Thus, they were born, raised, and died across countless yugas. It is said that the karmic potential within this remained confined to this soul. The prolonged activity of this soul was in a state of doing what is instructed and doing nothing beyond that. There are many such great souls. But they are brought forth only as needed. By divine will, that soul has been set aside and kept at a distance. They were given what was necessary. That falls under God's necessity and duty. Whether we call it God or Brahman, assign it any name. If it brings forth such a specific creation, there is a forethought to it. That forethought is what has transpired. This is one such soul within it. It was stated that it has been brought forth according to my intention, and it will function solely according to my intention. Therefore, the karma occurring according to this intention is not ours, but God's. So, this was merely explained to us. It did not come through our own realization. It was indeed completed over several eras. When asked in which eras this error occurred, it was in Manu's third Chaturyuga—the third Chaturyuga of Vaivasvata Manu—that this error emerged. They became arrogant with the notion of 'I'. Why did such arrogance arise? That course of karma is observed in these spheres. That course of karma is seen in the zodiac signs. That course of karma is evident in the twenty-seven stars. No human can be born outside those stars. Not even a blade of grass can sprout. Then this karma becomes theirs. The consequences of the karmic power seen in it, whether good or bad, will be seen as their own. Then whom should they respect?

[GV1:P1290] This is how an ascetic felt the urge to proclaim "Aham Brahmasmi" (I am Brahman). The capability to assert "I am Brahman" spontaneously developed within them. However, it is flawed; one should not declare oneself to be Brahman. From that point, this lineage of Manu shifted entirely. Yet, they forgot the matters concerning Manu himself. It is said that having forgotten, three more Chaturyugas merely passed in such narrations—the people existed aimlessly. Again, in the seventh Chaturyuga, the Trimurti system emerged. They too examined these texts, completely altered the lineage of Manu, and established the Trimurti system by strengthening it. Thus, all calculations were converted into those of Brahma, Vishnu, and Maheshwara. By only changing the calculations, they established that all creation occurs through this. However, there is a divine resolve for this to happen. When this emerged, their preference shifted toward the state of comfort. The position of a deity is exceedingly comfortable. The Trimurti system is celebrated by them with great joy. But it exists only up to the realm of the Devas (Devaloka). This group did not, nor do they, make an effort capable of proceeding beyond that to the realm of Brahman (Brahmaloka). This was fulfilled by this Trimurti system itself. Or rather, reaching the divine presence was accomplished by the eleventh Chaturyuga. It is stated that a Chaturyuga comprises twelve thousand divine years. How many of our years would these twelve thousand divine years amount to? That is, one divine year equals three hundred and sixty-five of our years. Such twelve thousand divine years make one Chaturyuga. Thus, it was completed from the seventh to the eleventh Chaturyuga. That is to say, this Trimurti system—observe the extent of preparations made during this period, fulfilling the lineage of Manu or the lineage of the Trimurtis according to that Brahmic resolve. Consequently, their specific course of karma emerged; the karmic course of the Trimurtis.
