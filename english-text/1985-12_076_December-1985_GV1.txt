[GV1:P1568] December 1985

[GV1:P1569] In our midst, if we mention terms like Karma, Yoga, Sanyasa, or Jnana, it cannot be said that we possess significant knowledge about them. Perhaps it might be found in a few isolated individuals. This is primarily due to the historical shortcomings of our environment. Firstly—the state. We lack the familiarity to even examine the nature of statecraft that has existed in governance up to this day. Statecraft requires comprehensive knowledge on both a national and global scale. The governance system must guide and rectify what needs to happen in the world. We have not had the opportunity to acquire such knowledge. By 'we', I mean the Shudra class. The reason the Shudra class did not attain it was because, according to the laws of Manu, it was written that they had no right to rule. It is no longer possible to examine whether this was right or wrong. Therefore, if necessary, they had to be made Kshatriyas in order to rule. You must be aware of the political tales behind conferring Kshatriya status. Or haven't you seen the futile efforts made to elevate even Sree Chithira Thirunal to Kshatriya status? We know this without having to ask anyone. It is impossible for such knowledge to reach the Shudra class. The denial of literacy is the primary reason for this. In an illiterate community, we cannot expect to find this aforementioned statecraft—or authoritative religious texts like the Puranas, Upanishads, or Vedas. If we pass long periods without knowing any of this, won't we become incapable of knowing who the ruler of this nation is or how to function within the foundational systems of the world? Then, we would just eat whatever we get and celebrate whatever we see. We had no knowledge beyond that. Due to the grace of time, we are now in a position to attain this. There is a decree that what happened in the Treta and Dvapara Yugas should not happen in the Kali Yuga. For that purpose, the divine will had taken precautionary measures back then. Because we cannot understand this, it still seems like an insurmountable mountain to us. The reason for this inability to understand is what was mentioned above. Let us examine it within a short timeframe.

[GV1:P1570] Since the time of Sree Krishna, it was indeed the divine will that intended to instill something into the Shudra. God has shown great effort so that it would come to us in a very peculiar and unembellished manner. The biography of Sree Krishna, who demonstrated what one's own life is, serves as complete proof that God attempted to introduce this into us by bringing it through nature. Such a Sree Krishna is depicted before us as the husband of sixteen thousand and eight women. Other Vedic authorities and scholars received the light of knowledge to view him either as an eternal celibate or as the author of the Gita. We must possess the knowledge to look back at this critically. Similarly, the tales of kings are written, read, and praised. When one is busy praising the king, there is no time left for anything else. Not everyone is allowed to do that. We are not even permitted to approach the king closely in our capacity as subjects. Such a wretched system was prevalent all over India. What do we know about this spiritual knowledge? In truth, we know absolutely nothing. We only know some sort of behavioral code. An example of this is that despite Sree Krishna making such a monumental effort, we could only comprehend Sree Krishna in this limited manner. Now, many people talk about the Gita. They say the Gita is a good book. It has only been a short time since we started regarding it with respect. So, how much can we possibly know about the Gita in our midst? Nothing at all. Moreover, if the Gita is spoken about anywhere, the difference is almost like that between a bitter gourd and a roasted anchovy for us. Thus, for our lineage, which had been turned into the possessors of such base knowledge, by the light of time in the interim—as I mentioned earlier—God, in order to make us understand, presented us with the history of the cruel Kamsa, birthed a small child right there, and corrected the monumental error that had occurred over time in the possession of this level of supreme knowledge. Sree Krishna is the one who convinced us by words and demonstrated through his life what our duty is and how we ought to live. Sree Krishna did not go anywhere to hide and pray. He was not seen practicing any other penance. What could be the reason for that? He was a Jnani by birth. He also attained it through Karma. That is to say, when we understand Sree Krishna—it is about the correction of errors that had occurred until then and the highlighting of another aspect. We must know what greatness to attribute to such an individual. Different Gurus will appear in every Yuga. A fifth one will come to link all these together. He is the final Guru of the Chatur Yugas, who destroys the karmic errors that occurred in them, while maintaining the foundations of the three Yugas intact. That is the position Sree Krishna holds. Through the mere child's play of a little boy, he brought down Kamsa, the most exalted and powerful among the kings until then. He lined up two sets of people—those who knew Krishna in one set, and the ignorant Gurus, elders, and elites in another set; he stood with one set, gave his entire army to the other side, and made them fight. Before that, he showed Arjuna who all those people actually were, those who had been celebrated by history as great men until then. Even after showing this, a situation arose where Arjuna failed to understand. What was that? Arjuna exhibited a stubbornness to say, 'They are my Gurus, my grandfathers, my uncles,' showcasing a fully Sattvic nature characteristic of a king. He asks that Arjuna again, 'Do you not believe me? Then why don't you listen to what I say?'. When he saw that Arjuna was not convinced despite everything being explained, he played his final card. 'Who are you? Who am I? Why did you and I come here? Think about what your Karma and my Karma are,' he demanded right there on the battlefield. Arjuna sat there and reflected. He realized. Then, without uttering a word, he got ready for the war. It was the greatest slaughter witnessed until then. After making him do all this, standing again as the master of a life, he declares—'Among you, he who worships the ancestors will be in the ancestors, he who worships the deity will be in the deity, and he who worships me will be in me'—a directive and a command. A powerful knowledge was imparted from this.

[GV1:P1571] But we did not get the opportunity to be convinced of any of this. As time passed, when they started recounting the tale of the sixteen thousand and eight women again, God did not forgive it there. An attempt was then made to categorize that distress and direct it towards India itself. First, the era of Moses was brought in even before the creation of Buddha. Because you did not assimilate it, Buddha was brought in in another guise to correct you once again. When it was seen that this too would be uprooted from here, Jesus was brought in before that. Thus, if we examine the matter of this divine will being implemented on a global scale, it will become clear to us what all we are heirs to, and what all we are supposed to witness. Similarly, He later transformed the lineage of Jesus into the possessors of education and science amidst people who had no literacy or anything else. Then, an experience was created where it was made out that we could only learn any science today if we learned their language. It is as if the ownership of such profound ignorance and injustice has been passed down to us as a gift. It is said that Shankaracharya did this, did that, and so on. It is true. But regarding the Dharma of the Hindu, many people have come and spoken about it at various times. What did this give to the people? In reality, the masses remain very disconnected from this subject as well.

[GV1:P1572] As a result, a very strong ignorance is being spread in two or three ways even today. It is spread, firstly, politically. Secondly, it is spread on the basis of class and caste. Both of these obstinately attempt to create distance between us. In the name of this, many organizational masquerades have been introduced here. It is done in such a way that it does not appear as segregation. Thus, by introducing sectarian mindsets, they create an opportunity for mutual conflict and destruction. In the process, by raising the clamours of Hindu arguments and making people fight among themselves, upper-caste hegemony will once again be brought to the top. The ignorant will fight among themselves and perish. The lower-caste (*avarna*) community will become a tent of sinful forces. Then they can bring what they intended to the forefront again. They are intelligently making people perform a great act for this purpose. The truth is that not a single child possesses the knowledge to look closely and understand what this is.

[GV1:P1573] When it was transmitted to Kerala, another ill-effect occurred. The Nairs, assuming themselves to be upper-caste (*savarna*), have started claiming that it belongs to them. Now they are fighting each other from a distance. Everyone thinks they are winning. However, everyone is perishing. You yourselves are creating all the weapons for destruction. You strike with them and destroy yourselves. They have intelligently managed it so as to hand over its ownership to you—you must understand this; that is the first point.

[GV1:P1574] Guruvani
