[GV1:P1208] August 1982

[GV1:P1209] Birthday Message

[GV1:P1210] We achieved independence through a struggle filled with sacrifice. We need to evaluate how far we have advanced through actions in the field for the fulfillment of the desire for which we gained independence. We desire to walk a little faster on the path cleared by someone else previously, whether religiously, economically, or politically. And that too, merely by looking at them, not by looking at ourselves.

[GV1:P1211] In the field of education, we are following the same system created by the English and distributing some degrees. Colleges have turned into slaughterhouses. Religiously, conflicts that do not even exist there are instigated by those who live in their name or the name of that place. In that position, what was the 'Hindu' able to create for India or of India, either in the sense of India's independence for India, or in the realm of secularism as a Hindu nation with secularism as its inherent nature? Or what encouragement could be given to other religions through the Sanatana Dharma of India—through the Hindu Dharma?

[GV1:P1212] We must reflect on what we are doing in accordance with the times. By 'time', I mean this era. This is the Kali Yuga. Which exponent of Dharma has stated that by proclaiming the dharma of the past three yugas, the dharma of Kali can be fulfilled? What we have been able to create in independent India are Congress from A to Z, communist movements of various colors, and political parties based on religion, caste, or narrow nationalism, such as SRP, NDP, Lok Dal, and the Muslim League.

[GV1:P1213] Seeing today's political turbulence (regarding ideals and principles), one feels like saying it is akin to taking a pot of water from the sea and claiming, 'This is the ocean.' Even that remains entangled in the words of Westerners.

[GV1:P1214] Is the independence we desired for all this? Was this the intention of the great souls who tried to build a secular India? Was it for this that thousands sacrificed their lives, forgetting caste and religion, without distinction of class and color? As soon as independence was achieved, didn't we have an eagerness to create religious and caste-based political parties that foster a sectarian mindset by dividing our motherland into two? Our independence is not for paving the way to the complete destruction of humanity by accumulating Western armaments. What we need is not to create class conflict by separating employers and workers into two classes. All this constitutes the face of today's India. Despite all this, we have not awakened.

[GV1:P1215] It is not too late for us to awaken and act. At least now, with the enthusiasm to awaken and act, making the mind balanced so that the three instruments of mind, word, and deed can equally attain truth or prosperity, making our faith one, making our politics one, maintaining the integrity of our motherland, remembering the words of the great Thiruvalluvar who said, "One caste, one lineage, one God," or to reiterate, respecting the words of Sree Narayana Guru in Malayalam, "One caste, one religion, one God for man," determining what sacrifice we can make, religious leaders and administrative leaders joining together and standing firm in one with absolute faith, the desire for love ingrained in each of us involved in the karma of humanity or God's creation becoming one, and for the sake of that undivided 'One', thinking about how much we can act and contain ourselves by reaching the weight of the atom called 'One' in that unbroken entirety, one should take a firm resolve to work by dedicating one's lifetime for the sake of the children to come 'tomorrow'. Thus, I desire that every living being may be fortunate enough to experience the happiness of freedom and the glory brimming with the love of peace.

[GV1:P1216] Guru Vani
