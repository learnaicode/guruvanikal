[GV1:P0621] February 1981

[GV1:P0622] Question: You had mentioned earlier that it could be stated with a directive. I am interested to know what that is.

[GV1:P0623] Guru: During the experiences of the past sixteen years, certain directives have become our own. Moreover, certain past events have come and gone according to the times. We must lay down the directive by truly accepting all of them, not by opposing and defeating them. But the familiarity to understand that must reach the people. The main role in that has come to the ascetics (Sanyasis) themselves. The Dwapara Yuga and Treta Yuga, two completed yugas, were useful for some time in knowing the past, present, and future through direct perceptions and inferences.

[GV1:P0624] Question: You mentioned direct perceptions and inferences. I do not understand the intention behind it.

[GV1:P0625] Guru: We are not speaking of the direct perceptions and inferences spoken by a materialist. Deva, Devi, Guru - when saying Guru, there are four types of Gurus: Brahmavit, Brahmavaryan, Brahmavarishtan, Brahmavareeshtan. When speaking of the era of Devas and Devis, it only means the Treta and Dwapara Yugas. Two: The people who worshipped Devas and Devis performed certain miraculous feats (siddhis) and magical acts. They did all this by satisfying the Devas and Devis with mantras and tantras. The application of siddhis has been carried out in all periods. Even today, some ascetics and yogis continue to do so. One should not assume this is the ultimate truth. We must gain the knowledge that the lives of these Devas and Devis continue to exist with awareness throughout their time.

[GV1:P0626] Question: How can we acquire this knowledge?

[GV1:P0627] Guru: This knowledge is acquired when a Guru transcends the status of Devas and Devis and realizes the glory of the divine presence. A Guru can advise on this only when knowledge about the universe is attained. Under a Deva, there are also Rishis and ascetics. They have a shorter lifespan than this Deva. That being the case, in one sense, they have stated that Shiva and Mahavishnu are complete. They gave a name and showed some calculations. That is their logic.

[GV1:P0628] However, descending from the Trimurti system, all religious followers possess certain attributes conceptualized as sub-deities. Muslims refer to them as Jinns and Christians as angels. The determination of this time period is obtained when tracing the lineage of Manu. The period of a Manu is calculated as seventy-one *Chaturyugas*. One *Chaturyuga* is twelve thousand divine years. A divine year equals three hundred and sixty-five and a quarter human years. One divine year constitutes a single day for a deity. Calculated thus, how many lifetimes would human beings require to comprehend this? Even when a human dies and becomes a *Pitru* (ancestral spirit), our month equates to a single day for them. For *Yakshas*, *Gandharvas*, and *Kinnaras*, the time span is even greater. We must comprehend at least a portion of such calculations through direct inference. It has been stated that the era of a Manu spans seventy-one *Chaturyugas*. When a single *Chaturyuga* comprises twelve thousand divine years, one can deduce the magnitude of seventy-one *Chaturyugas*. We have not yet completed recording the chronological calculation of deities for a single *Chaturyuga*. That in itself is the predicament. How can one chronicle a calculation of such immense duration?

[GV1:P0629] If we fail to evaluate Manu at this juncture, we will be confounded once more. We ourselves have been unable to comprehend the history of the Manus. Consequently, oblivious to the narrative of the Manus, what can we, leading mere century-long lives, possibly comprehend? Our scholarly pursuit has already been diverted from this trajectory to another. The very mathematics has vanished from such matters. Judging by the nature of the calculations detailed in our *Bhagavata*, Europeans still have a considerably long way to go in catching up.

[GV1:P0630] The reason for this lies in their calculation of minutes. To arrive at a single minute, we would have to compute extensively. It is stated that a time measurement is assigned to the fraction of a moment it takes for a robust young man, forcefully thrusting a sharp stylus, to pierce through one tender bael leaf and strike another placed immediately beneath it. I am aware that preceptors in traditional village schools used to teach such calculations. They were referred to as *munthiri* calculations, *maahani* calculations, and the like. It is taught that one thousand *maahani* equals 62.5. I have transcribed such calculations onto palm leaves. In those days, regardless of the complexity of the calculation, one merely had to learn and recite it within five minutes. Today, attempting such calculations without mechanical assistance would require countless iterations of multiplication and division.

[GV1:P0631] When the *Gita* speaks of *pratyakshavaham dharmam*, it means that the deity, goddess, or guru we envision manifests directly to prescribe specific *dharmas* (duties). *Pratyakshavaham dharmam* implies that such manifestation encompasses the past, present, and future timelines of a life. Thus, it should be understood that just as humans have spoken of the three tenses, the calculations pertaining to everything from deities to the Earth, the nine planets, and the twelve zodiac signs can be obtained through this direct perception. If we preserve the knowledge obtained in this manner, it becomes clear to us. The life of Sri Krishna itself further substantiates this example. Arjuna—who stood on the battlefield paralyzed by fear, casting away his bow and arrows, declaring he wished to gain nothing by slaying his uncles, grandfathers, and preceptors—failed to comprehend when Krishna told him, "You kill no one, cause no one to be killed; the soul cannot be destroyed," so the narrative goes. Krishna repeatedly advised him. When that counsel proved fruitless, he summoned Arjuna: "Do you believe in me?" "Yes, I always believe in you." "Then why do you fail to understand despite my repeated explanations?" Arjuna then replied to Krishna, "My only fear concerns the act of killing them." "Very well, do one thing. Try to envision me. What was I prior to this era? Were you alive then or not? If so, who were you? Who were your uncles and preceptors back then? Who were you? Observe what you were doing." Looking thus, with the eyes of wisdom, he saw who they all were and who Krishna was back then. In this manner, God clarifies that we ourselves possess a state providing evidence of our manifold attributes. We have numerous other proofs as well. Great sages have authored treatises—such as Agastya's seven thousand and Bhogar's one thousand. It is said they would look at a plant and ask, "What are you good for?" This signifies that they looked at the plant with divine contemplation. The divine illumination residing there bestowed the knowledge of its medicinal properties. Because they meticulously recorded this, preparing a formulation based on those calculations yields results even today. They did not make discoveries through laboratory testing as is done today. Back then, they discovered everything without the use of machinery. Today, we find it impossible to live without mechanical equipment. Viewed in this light, to this day, no errors have ever originated from these great masters.

[GV1:P0632] A question might naturally arise: how then did this Vedic authority reach its current state? The present era speaks of the deities' calculations. However, the calculations of the Vedas and Upanishads speak solely of illumination. Similarly, we have received various revelations over the past sixteen and a half years. Fully comprehending and applying this knowledge will take ages. When utilized according to these revelations, the present-day Trimurti system—namely the Vaishnava, Shakta, and Shaiva traditions of worship—must attain completion through the path of the Guru and the lineage of Manu. It represents an experiential possibility resulting from the integration of the gross, subtle, effect, and cause states for both men and women, young and old alike.

[GV1:P0633] Guruvani

[GV1:P0634] March 1981

[GV1:P0635] Question: Does "experiential possibility" imply that we must harmonize all past events to foster a specific disposition?

[GV1:P0636] Guru: To speak more precisely, there is no need to state that we must foster it. Everything unfolds according to time. Furthermore, it would be more accurate to say that much gets distorted due to our ignorance. The only flaw herein stems from the deficiencies accumulated because we are unable to act appropriately at the right juncture.

[GV1:P0637] Observe the sheer ignorance prevailing in places we herald as progressive. We encounter numerous newspaper reports indicating a significant belief in witchcraft in contemporary times, to the extent that even in America, there is an apprehension of sorcery. Similarly, amidst us who proclaim our culture to be the greatest, what sort of Vedic behaviors are on display? Disgraceful practices, such as inquiring whether one should demonstrate their prowess while in a state of trance, are taking place here.

[GV1:P0638] Even for individuals who feel ashamed to declare "I am a believer," their own families will not permit them to disavow it. Or if they are permitted, the neighbors will end up ostracizing them; such is the situation. Otherwise, the presence of believers mirrors the epidemic of modern politics. Within a single household, one might be a devotee of Vishnu, another of Shiva, a third of Subrahmanya, and a fourth of Kali—each worshipping their chosen deity under the conviction that theirs is supreme. Hearing them speak, we too might feel they are correct. Is it not correct? It is indeed correct. However, this escalates and transforms into the language of the "ego."

[GV1:P0639] In this world, everything that comes into being and perishes, starting from a single grain of sand, possesses its own distinct completeness. One can perceive that this world is sustained by the support of that completeness. Viewed from this perspective, it is evident that even completeness cannot endure without the support of another completeness. We mutually support each other without even comprehending this completeness, thus feeling a sense of grandeur akin to a frog in a well. While navigating life in this world with this understanding, one fundamentally requires something to stand firm upon.

[GV1:P0640] The truth is that if our actions towards it are unified into one, they encompass everything. Otherwise, it will become so void that we will feel entirely incapable of having accomplished anything. Step aside into it for a moment. Then, the recurring anomalies in actions performed at another time for the sake of wholeness will appear as a substantial aggregation. Our current endeavor can merely reduce its magnitude; however, if anyone believes it can be entirely eradicated from this world, such a notion is akin to nihilism.

[GV1:P0641] Therefore, our only recourse is to act while praying for the wisdom to align our spontaneous intellect and capabilities with the divine will, standing closely behind it. For this, the words that convey this divine decree to us serve as our foundation. Upon deep contemplation, we realize that our existential state lacks even the significance of a mere atom. In this atmosphere, if one gazes into this mirage, countless atoms can be observed flickering in and out of existence within the circumference of a single coin. It suffices to consider ourselves merely as one among such atoms. One must not assume a state of absolute nothingness. Doing so would lead to complete voidness.

[GV1:P0642] When it is understood that billions of such atoms mutually interpenetrate and that this penetrative quality pervades everywhere akin to a gas, one should immediately reflect upon whether the nature of our mind is not analogous to this. In such a gaseous state, the mind will completely permeate our desires, exactly like a gas. We must comprehend that this mechanism remains identical, regardless of whether the desire is virtuous or malevolent.

[GV1:P0643] Certain ascetics serve as exemplars of this phenomenon. Placing their faith in the ritualistic nature of particular deities, they elevate and worship them as the Supreme God. Consequently, this transforms into a form of worship dictated by their individual desires. As the number of adherents engaging in this worship multiplies, some develop an inclination to exhibit their prowess. This leads to the urge to exercise mystical powers (*Siddhis*). The application of such powers may be intended to attract crowds or to serve selfish motives. Regardless of the intent, the ultimate objective remains the fulfillment of desire. Moreover, an individual's desire has the potential to inflict instantaneous harm upon many.

[GV1:P0644] Since the epoch of creation, the world has continually existed as an amalgamation of virtues and vices. Therefore, it is only after thoroughly contemplating and comprehending the true nature of ourselves—who are a composite of good and evil—along with others like us, and the world itself, that one should endeavor to manifest miracles, and indeed, the greatest of miracles. Otherwise, one would be compelled to perpetually perform miracles merely to mitigate the destruction engendered by these very manifestations.

[GV1:P0645] Consequently, the actions performed by both individuals up to that point are rendered futile. It implies that while one created, the other destroyed. Ultimately, neither party is able to achieve anything. This is precisely how those who identify as ascetics act and ultimately face defeat.

[GV1:P0646] Question: Are you referring specifically to mystical powers (*Siddhis*)?

[GV1:P0647] Guru: I am not merely referring to mystical powers, but rather encompassing all aspects of action. None of these ascetics committed these acts with the explicit desire to become wicked. They simply slipped in their actions. If one lacks the innate disposition to act with their mind focused upon the divine presence and aimed at the divine will, this flaw will subtly overtake us without our awareness. It is this specific detriment that afflicts us as we continuously engage in numerous external and internal actions.

[GV1:P0648] Question: In your opinion, what must be done to avert this?

[GV1:P0649] Guru: The directive to avert it is inherently contained within those very words. Did you not notice it? A devotee inherently possesses a distinct qualitative excellence in their disposition. For a yogi, it manifests through the science of yoga. For a person of action (*karmi*), it emerges through their deeds. The seeker of knowledge (*jnani*) possesses it innately. Even though the essence remains the same, the devotee possesses the effortlessness to act with a sense of total surrender; the yogi harbors the mindset that their achievements stem from yogic prowess; whereas the *karmi* holds the attitude of "behold the deed I have accomplished." The *jnani*, however, is capable of recognizing that everyone's actions are correct from their respective standpoints, and can establish that they themselves are the embodiment of that very truth. Experience has proven that only the *jnani* and the devotee can attain this effortlessly.

[GV1:P0650] Question: May we consider this as a self-realized experience?

[GV1:P0651] Guru: You may. However, while considering it as such, one must not deviate from the philosophical discipline by adopting an attitude of "that is their experience, and this is my experience."

[GV1:P0652] Question: What is the reason for saying so?

[GV1:P0653] Guru: Because the devotee, the *jnani*, and the yogi all possess a *sattvic* (pure) nature, they will acknowledge it. Yet, accompanying this is something known as *sattvic* ego. One unknowingly takes pride, assuming that the actions they have performed possess considerable superiority. This tendency has thus emerged and afflicted everyone.

[GV1:P0654] There is evidence that this is ingrained in all of us. There is a tendency in us to say, "It is somewhat acceptable." We also do not say it is completely unacceptable. We use this same expression even for the very best. This flaw primarily occurs in the most intelligent individuals. If it can infiltrate the intellectual, how can we be certain it will not infiltrate us? It will certainly infiltrate us as well. To prevent its entry, the devotee develops an attitude of absolute surrender toward the Guru, recognizing that the previously mentioned path is the sole recourse. The *jnani*, observing the goodness that everything is functioning correctly in its respective place, consistently anchors their sacred actions solely in virtue, and accomplishes them in an ordinary manner without being overwhelmed by the force of that virtue.

[GV1:P0655] Question: I still wish to hear further elucidation on the exact nature of what you are implying through all of this.

[GV1:P0656] Guru: I will have to reiterate what has already been said. Nevertheless, I shall explain it in slightly more detail. In this current *Kali Yuga*, we can only speak by observing its weight and magnitude. Therefore, it can be clarified better only if articulated with that at the forefront. What is your current understanding of the term *Kali*?

[GV1:P0657] Question: It is perceived that *Kali* has deeply entrenched itself within people and everything else. The prevailing belief is that, unable to perform righteous deeds, the innate goodness of humanity will be entirely annihilated.

[GV1:P0658] Guru: That is incorrect. We ourselves assert that we have arrived on this earth in accordance with the results of our past actions (*karma*). If that is the case, does it not imply that the fruits of actions performed incrementally in the *Treta Yuga* and *Dvapara Yuga* will manifest in this *Kali Yuga*?

[GV1:P0659] Question: It will manifest.

[GV1:P0660] Guru: If it is to manifest, shouldn't the virtues cultivated during those eras also manifest?

[GV1:P0661] Question: The virtues must manifest as well.

[GV1:P0662] Guru: If so, isn't acting in the fourth era while evaluating the virtues and vices of the preceding three eras conceived as a state of absorption (*layanathmakam*), where one reaches a state of liberation—devoid of both positive and negative attributes—achieved through the fruits of actions executed with a perfectly balanced understanding of the weight of merits and demerits?

[GV1:P0663] Question: Yes, it is exactly like that. Then, is it your proposition that this era is capable of fully grasping and retaining virtue?

[GV1:P0664] Guru: Yes, certainly. No other three eras offer the opportunity quite like this *Kali Yuga*, where one can strive, perform any action through hardship, and bring it to the forefront. Or even if they do, it is exceedingly delayed. This is precisely why even our ancestral sages desired to be born in the *Kali Yuga* to attain liberation.

[GV1:P0665] Question: Having listened to what you have said thus far, it is to be understood that, by evaluating everything that has transpired until today, an attempt is being made to rectify past anomalies by establishing a system as suited to the times as possible, shaping the forthcoming state of affairs, and bringing it to the forefront, is it not?

[GV1:P0666] Guru: That is precisely the objective. That is also the knowledge we have received. It would require an extensive period to completely implement and consolidate the knowledge we have documented over many days.

[GV1:P0667] In that systemic arrangement, when observing the characteristics of ashrams that have existed until now, our ashram exhibits certain distinctions. Thus far, the practice has been to strictly follow the predecessors and tread the exact path they have shown. All of this needs to be systematized—not partially, but entirely. That is to say, according to Hindu jurisprudence, we have now been entrusted with these three types of worship modalities. To unify the scenario of establishing "one" in the place of all three, one must first comprehend the matter and believe in it. It is only after believing that one will be able to put it into practice.

[GV1:P0668] There are some people for whom outdated methods of worship are ingrained in their flesh, blood, and marrow, clinging tightly to their lives. As soon as faith is first established in this, the concepts of ancestors and the concepts of God they have worshipped thus far should be unified, and with the power they have worshipped, they must be brought into the divine light. Then, in that place, the faith in the Guru must be firmly established again. They must be convinced and believe in what that Guru is. Thus, they must learn to worship with the concept that the Guru is God. Such is the fundamental principle of the Hindu. That is why it begins with 'Gurur Brahma' and concludes with 'Sakshat Para Brahma'. Only if such knowledge is acquired can it be conveyed to the common people.

[GV1:P0669] Therefore, one must know the qualities that this Guru, who is the Supreme Brahman, should possess. He must treat both the wicked and the virtuous equally. He will try to bring the wicked to the right path by afflicting and inflicting pain through their own actions. However, what is shown to the virtuous is also the same. There is only a slight difference. That difference is that even if there are many faults in the actions of the wicked, he will forgive them and let them be. He will not try too hard to correct them. But in the case of the virtuous, he will control them very strictly. If they do not listen when controlled, he will let them go. Why is that? If they deviate even slightly from the path they have ascended, that slight deviation will cause a profound gap within them. If such a gap occurs, it cannot be bridged in that lifetime.

[GV1:P0670] It is keeping that memory in mind that Gurus develop an attitude that even if someone does not become right, it does not matter, let them go. If it is a wicked person, they will think let him fall a bit and then rise up. That is why, in a Guru-disciple relationship understood in this manner, disciples take care not to give room for even a single contradictory word. A Guru lineage that begins to turn in this way will face stages of enduring numerous hardships. Because the course of that lineage seems contrary to what people are accustomed to. For example, if one observes carefully, one can see what kind of revolution occurred when attempts were made to change each existing thing.

[GV1:P0671] If one happens to read at least once the sorrowful truth experienced by the recently emerged Christianity and Buddhism, along with their lineages, it is easy to understand what the people who experienced that pain and their Guru lineages went through. It is because they went through such hardships that their sacrifices have to be valued. Looking at the era we live in today, unless we proceed with a bit more strength and propriety, it will not be possible to bring people to the right path.

[GV1:P0672] Every individual standing involved in the path we intend should assume themselves to be resolute in purpose and that they are the strongest and most appropriate person on this path. Whoever they may be and in whichever path they may live, they must live focusing on that Guru's path. Only if we fully internalize such an ideal can we serve this and create precedents. Otherwise, out of desire or base enmity, one might create a rift and move away.

[GV1:P0673] For that, first truly study this ideal and draw strength from it. Having gained strength, act moderately and come forward through intellectual prowess to advance. It is imperative that every individual possesses all appropriate compromises, outpourings of love, and a wealth of sacrifice. Only such lineages have been able to come forward to date. All those who step into this must be courageous and possess the capable nature to reconcile even those who are estranged through love. If this happens, we can all see a good tomorrow as divinely conceived. This is how our Guru lineage should set its goals.

[GV1:P0674] Especially householders (Grihasthas) should be the ones to become acquainted with this and come forward. Men and women must join together and perform only actions that mutually accommodate each other with love and respect in all endeavors. Thus, the home will attain a place of unity. Through that love, the lineage of offspring will be good, and in imparting the necessary knowledge to the children in that lineage, two or three other conditions will also have to be adopted.

[GV1:P0675] In this regard, one should not consider which caste, which religion, or which class someone belongs to in the Guru lineage. Regardless of how they are, let there be unity. What is achieved through that unity is that they are all like members of one family. Through that, find occupation and create a unified form. Through this, there will be a peace for their life's hardships. The lineage of self-education will become theirs. That culture will be cultivated to a level befitting the lineage. Thus, when their organizational nature becomes one, their sense of unity will also become one. Even if we gather everything for this, there is another nature we must strongly know.

[GV1:P0676] This nature called Guru must be one in whom all the special powers of the divine system have occurred. It is not for us to imitate the path of the gods and goddesses. Without recognizing them and correcting the destruction, one cannot be brought before a Guru. The people must be completely corrected and brought back along the path traditionally taken by our predecessors, the Devas and Rishis themselves. Furthermore, one must have held both the system of Devendra and the position of the lord of the Ganas. They must be ones who have experienced what is called the presence of Ishwara, the presence of God, and the presence of Brahman. Only such a Guru can bring these ideals together.

[GV1:P0677] Question: Hearing what you just said, one must recognize and correct everything from ghosts to Yakshas, Gandharvas, Kinnaras, and the city of Vetalas. What is the way for us to know and correct this here?

[GV1:P0678] Guru: There, it is enough if you accept the word of the Guru exactly as it is. You do not need to correct it. The Guru is one who has borne all of this, right? It is sufficient just not to violate the words of the Guru. Then you can ascend without error.

[GV1:P0679] If one intends to act like the Guru after the Guru's time, one must observe and correct this, learn all of it while the Guru is still present, and stand with wisdom. Otherwise, that movement will stagger for a while after the Guru's time. Then it will cease to exist.
