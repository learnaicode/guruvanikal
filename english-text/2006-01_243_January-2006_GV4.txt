[GV4:P1255] January 2006

[GV4:P1256] Whether it is Hinduism or any other religion, we possess a traditional knowledge that we have worshipped and followed until today. Therefore, whether this knowledge is right or wrong, it is something we have continuously been repeating. We cannot attain a clear, confirmed understanding regarding this knowledge. Because the flaw lies in our own hands. What is it? A competitive spirit among the Shaiva, Shakta, and Vaishnava sects. Everyone holds a competitive spirit without even knowing what a Shaiva, a Shakta, or a Vaishnava truly is. Similarly, this exists in all religious doctrines. However, such an extensively varying doctrine as found among Hindus has never been seen elsewhere. This is not because none of us have thought about the reason for it. We have thought about it; we have thought about it many times. But we have not attained it as true knowledge. I realized that it hasn't been attained based on my own experience. We have heard things like this. "Isn't a single glimpse of the Lord enough? It is everything"—there is still no shortage of such teachings among us. But no matter who learns or teaches in this way, no god or goddess is capable of sufficiently giving us anything. Nowadays, it is not a big deal for you to become a god or a goddess too. Each of you can become one. But when moving in that direction, one must understand one thing. The course and tribulations of this life cannot be resolved by these gods and goddesses. Similarly, there is the mention of Rishis (sages). This Rishi exists both beneath the god and above the god. But those who are above never obeyed the god during the era of the gods. We do not know this fact either. There is also a reason why they do not obey the god. These three sects compete and say - 'We are the greatest, our god is the greatest?' - The people within these groups themselves became divided into lower castes and higher castes, and thus assigned a different god for each caste, keeping a lesser god for a lower caste. Then whose god is the greatest? This is a question we must ask ourselves. Apart from this, there is something we remain without obeying and without knowing today. We have assumed that it is the Sanyasis (ascetics) and someone else who should be handling this. Beyond that, we have materialistic thoughts. We also convince ourselves that this is all we need. Leaving aside the fact that this happens due to our ignorance, ask those who say this: does one emerge as a Sanyasi, a Brahmachari, or a good Vanaprastha from family life? Or do they come from Sanyasa itself? What do you think about it? Since you completely fail to understand that all these arise from family life, you have merely assumed what you heard from someone else. This is not the case; truly, it is through people leading a family life that children beneficial to this land, home, and country are born. Whatever our past visionaries have done for us, if there are any remaining shortcomings in their deeds, such visionaries must explain to us the ultimate truth contained within the doctrine.

[GV4:P1257] So, because we do not know who such seers are, we make many mistakes. Thus, seers will not come in all eras. In this yuga, if we speak of the dharma of another yuga, it will not work. At the beginning of this yuga, Sri Krishna's clan was given royal status. So, at the very place where it was stated that there is no Shudra rule or Veda recitation, they were given ruling authority right at the beginning. That is Kamsa. So, this authority of Kamsa could not be fully understood by him with an attitude of dedication. What is this attitude of dedication? Be it a god, a goddess, a guru, or God, one should handle it only with dedication. However, as far as he was concerned, he did no wrong. What we have heard is about the wicked Kamsa. No. A wicked person cannot hear a divine voice from the sky (Ashariri). He is not wicked. We could not understand this either. How can a wicked person hear an Ashariri? He might receive something from some demon or deity. But because he had the dedication of this manifestation in his mind, a mistake happened. The god and goddess who are seen manifested will bless everything as much as possible. My god blessed me, my god, my goddess ڊ there are millions of people who say this. Each of these people says this believing they are great in their own way. They do not say it thinking it is bad. If so many people speak about this religion, caste, or class in different ways, is God something to be shared among each of them like this? Let us ask ourselves, is such a God supposed to do good for us? We have no question about this either. Then, if someone says something logically, they speak the truth in their minds as well, and go into an action that destroys broad-mindedness. What is it? Rationalism, liberationism - there are only two arguments. But by constantly arguing like this, they will end up paralyzed in their final days. Other than this, what can they gain through arguments? What do they know? What do you know about predestination? Nothing. What has become God's will? Without any such knowledge, as far as we are concerned, apart from making up God by forcefully grasping, we absolutely do not know any karma that imparts goodness from it. The reason for that is we have been taught that the dharma of Kali is very bad. Kali is never bad. If we are among those who believe this, this is what is generally said. Now, if a boy at home commits a mistake, they say, 'Isn't that Kali?' Whether it's a girl or a boy. So what is the fault with Kali? That is not known either. What is it? If there is a yuga dharma set apart for these 4 yugas, we hear a saying that in Satya Yuga we are all the same. Let it be whatever. In Treta Yuga, the worship system changed like this, and it became a bit stronger in Dvapara Yuga, and Satya Yuga is the same for all of us. The dharma of this Kali is to churn and straighten out if any mistakes have occurred in these 3 yugas.

[GV4:P1258] Guruvani
