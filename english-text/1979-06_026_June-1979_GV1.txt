[GV1:P0429] June 1979

[GV1:P0430] Question: Guru, please explain the remaining details regarding the special prayers in this ashram.

[GV1:P0431] Answer: I have reminded you about our prayer once before. It is received from nature as an ethereal voice. Sages often receive it in this manner. However, even in this, it will not be perfected until a certain stage is crossed.

[GV1:P0432] Question: What is this stage that you mentioned?

[GV1:P0433] Answer: There are four stages of demonic entities.

[GV1:P0434] Question: What could be the reason for stating that demonic entities have four stages?

[GV1:P0435] Answer: Pey, Madan, Marutha – two stages. There is a category known as Manthramoorthi—a stage achieved by mastering mantras. One can attain mastery over Madan, Pey, and Marutha through mantras. Similarly, one can attain mastery over Bhadrakali, Mahalakshmi, Shiva, and Vishnu through mantras. At the same time, there is a stage for those who aspire to become enlightened beings. It is referred to as Brahmarakshas. Bhadrakali is also perceived as Brahman. That is Brahmarakshas. Even Lord Shiva is perceived as Brahman. That too becomes Brahmarakshas. Vishnu is also perceived as Brahman. That as well becomes Brahmarakshas. The forces starting from Bhadrakali onwards will cause harm during different phases of human life.

[GV1:P0436] Question: What is meant by phases?

[GV1:P0437] Answer: The phases refer to the era of Manu. You must have heard of something called the Manusmriti, haven't you?

[GV1:P0438] Question: What is Manusmriti?

[GV1:P0439] Answer: One can only understand it by studying it.

[GV1:P0440] Question: How can we study matters that we haven't even heard being spoken about properly?

[GV1:P0441] Answer: We were unable to comprehend this due to the detriments of the Varnashrama Dharma and the mode of worship rooted in the Trimurti system. We could not differentiate between righteous and unrighteous deeds. The Indian tradition—the earliest creations—comprised Rishis (sages) and Sanyasis (ascetics) in every aspect.

[GV1:P0442] Question: What is the reason for you to specifically differentiate and explain the concepts of a Rishi and a Sanyasi?

[GV1:P0443] Answer: There is no significant difference between a Rishi and a Sanyasi. However, the distinction lies only in certain aspects of how they manifest and function. A Rishi is one who harmonizes with everyone and acts in an all-encompassing manner.

[GV1:P0444] Question: I did not understand that. Kindly elaborate.

[GV1:P0445] Answer: That is, a Rishi is one who observes everything—whether right or wrong—without blaming anyone, enduring and forgiving entirely, consoling himself by accepting all faults as his own, and applying this equanimity among the people. A Sanyasi is not like that. He firmly believes that his actions are unequivocally right and desires to be respected by others.
