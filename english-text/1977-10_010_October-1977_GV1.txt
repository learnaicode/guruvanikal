[GV1:P0164] October 1977

[GV1:P0165] Question: Some Vedantins are heard saying this, aren't they? I am not born, I do not die, there are no births and deaths in the world, the universe is an illusion. What is the reason?

[GV1:P0166] Answer: Vedanta is correct. The reason Vedanta came to be is because there was something else, isn't it? Those who said this universe is an illusion were living beings, weren't they? With the help of the five elements, with the help of the five senses, and thus with the help of the koshas (sheaths), the soul, which is enclosed in this body within a form created by God, ranging from the nature of the annamaya kosha to the anandamaya kosha, is brought through the jnanamaya kosha to the vijnanamaya kosha. Thus, the whole world can know only through this. When the knowledge that comes in this way turns one into a subtle thinker, it is often called jnana thought, vijnana thought, and ajnana thought. Based on this, some experience it in the formed, the formless, and the undivided.

[GV1:P0167] Question: These Siddhas are assumed to be Jnanis, aren't they? Then what made you distinguish between Siddhas and Jnanis?

[GV1:P0168] Answer: Siddhi and Jnana are indeed two different things. Siddhi utilizes certain mantras and tantras, worshiping through certain deity systems or Yogishwara systems, experiencing it, and creating some minor experiences among the people - acting like the nature of a yogi or a deity oneself. A Jnani does not do this. Through the path originating from the Guru's teaching, recognizing everything that can come into the light, even realizing what sustenance of life or sustenance of prana is, the Guru's teaching is an instrumental path capable of perceiving even the unique virtue of those koshas in which that prana operates. But a Siddha is not like that; a Siddha proceeds by believing only the words received from the guidance he prefers. There is only a very distant relationship between a Siddha and a Jnani.

[GV1:P0169] Question: You mentioned the Guru's teaching. He comes forward by giving the form of some favorite deity like this, doesn't he?

[GV1:P0170] Answer: That is correct. We can record it here like an administration. The peons standing near the king are also called peons. In courts, every bench clerk has peons. Likewise, there are some in certain companies. There are peons in all offices and schools. Village officers have peons. But the status and privileges enjoyed by a peon who can meet the king and discuss matters may not be found among these peons or superior officers. Similarly, one practices yoga, one practices Guruhood. It exists in many states and many justifications. All this exists through justification as well. Certain shifts in position are the reason for this.
