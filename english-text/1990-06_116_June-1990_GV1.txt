[GV1:P2522] June 1990

[GV1:P2523] Disciple: O Guru, whether it is big or small, isn't ego

[GV1:P2524] a common trait?

[GV1:P2525] Guru: God placed an absurdity to mislead the Iyengars—saying, "You play like that." And to the Iyers, "You play like this." You know how it will be. Isn't this trick called God the thing that makes the player play? "You will play like that, won't you? Then let me put down something like this"—that is how He makes them clash.

[GV1:P2526] Disciple: But, as far as we are concerned, this game is a serious matter, isn't it, Guru!

[GV1:P2527] Guru: Yes, as far as we are concerned, it is a serious matter indeed. Just because it is a matter, shouldn't we reach a state where we have to accomplish it?

[GV1:P2528] One shouldn't just create it and then try to work it out in practice. The cause must be generated. It is that cause which is being questioned today. That is what was said earlier. Through a causal action, the cause was made to happen. What is the cause? Why was this established as a cause here? When questioned like this, what we are providing is the explanation for it. Each one of you must think about how this explanation should be established and how it should not be.

[GV1:P2529] Disciple: In what the Guru just said, the flaw called ego is the cause of this division. Seeing how the division arises, what should be done so that there is no ego in this new path of ours and to make everyone cooperate in a pure manner?

[GV1:P2530] Guru: It can only be done through a small path. The opportunity hasn't arrived to do anything completely. There is a reason for that too. Earlier, we talked about each person's classifications, right—Ugramoorthi (fierce deity), Mantramoorthi, Bhadrakali, and so on. A system embracing the grotesqueness of having a thousand heads, a thousand eyes, and scooping up a thousand snakes. What would be the inner spiritual state (atmabhavam) of having worshipped all those monstrosities? Do you believe that for all these monstrosities to be destroyed, this life itself must perish? We believe that a primordial origin of such karma must exist somewhere.

[GV1:P2531] Disciple - It exists across different lifetimes.

[GV1:P2532] Guru: Yes. Therefore, our archetype is based on faith. That foundation is conceptual. This concept constitutes the mindset of karma. Giving a belief, one conceptualizes—this is my God. Even though we returned from that savage nature, cleared the forests, and established temples, what is enshrined there is still the entity that lay in the forest. We bring the elephant that lived in the forest, bathe and decorate it, sit on its back, perform antics, don costumes, establish the concept of God, and worship it. What do our grand-uncles do? Breaking several coconuts and holding the multi-tiered traditional torch (*theevetti*), assuming this ritual to be their prerogative, they wrap a large dhoti around their prominent bellies, tie a large towel over it, and walk around feeling gratified. In the region where I was born, there is an object made of screw-pine leaves used to protect oneself from the rain. It is called a *kuttil*. In some places, it is also known as a *choodal*. They just drape it over themselves. Even during strong winds and heavy rain, one does not feel cold inside it. The warmth remains trapped inside. We have unfortunately engaged in such practices. The grand-uncles fold a sack and drape it like a *kuttil*. Inside, they hold a lamp. They drape this *kuttil* over the elephant and the goddess as well. It is only after this that they hold the eight-wicked torch. No matter how much his arms ache, the grand-uncle insists on holding the largest torch himself. It would consist of eighteen to twenty-four flames. Standing holding it is a matter of great prestige for the grand-uncle. When the uncle does this, how can the successor abstain? He is compelled to hold it. Thus, we possess certain systems of worship and traditions characterized by an ignorance that has simply been passed down. Exploiting this lack of awareness, the Brahmin cleverly orchestrated everything he desired. Where did this Brahmin originate? To which race does the Brahmin who arrived from the Middle East belong?

[GV1:P2533] Disciple: Aryan.

[GV1:P2534] Guru: Let it be Aryan or whatever. What do you call it? What is your understanding regarding it? Apart from the sacred thread worn by these Brahmins, there is another item, isn't there? An item similar to what police officers wear?

[GV1:P2535] Disciple: Crossbelt.

[GV1:P2536] Guru: Exactly. These Iyengars possess it as well.

[GV1:P2537] Guruvani
