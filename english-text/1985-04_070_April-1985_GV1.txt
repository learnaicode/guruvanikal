[GV1:P1503] April 1985

[GV1:P1504] You have been designated as the custodians of a karma that purifies in this state. However, we require the knowledge to solidify that understanding, to think expansively, and to acquire that karmic competence. That is our ultimate goal. Today, we are only capable of transmitting that knowledge to our successors. One should not assume that anything else can be done. If anything else is attained, it is solely the resolve of Brahman. It belongs neither to you nor to me. You must disseminate knowledge of this nature.

[GV1:P1505] Who will be the progeny capable of grasping this knowledge? It is described as the incarnation of a Guru who has guided millions of people. Descendants fortunate enough to facilitate the birth of such a great soul must be born among us. It cannot be conclusively stated that many have already been born. What we require today is a purity of karma to enable such births. To achieve this, rather than working in isolation and harboring the egoistic notion that 'I am superior' through individual actions, you must internalize the truth that 'I am nothing; I am merely a participant in a collective karmic endeavor.' Only then can you anticipate salvation and prosperity.

[GV1:P1506] This is our path to salvation. True attainment is possible only when actions are performed with the profound dedication that 'we are essentially nothing; nevertheless, let us contribute to the best of our abilities.'

[GV1:P1507] There is another profound reason for depicting the aforementioned Brahmin ancestors in such a manner. For them to be emancipated from the dominion of this deity, their salvation must occur through the position of a Guru. A Guru, therefore, is neither a deity, nor a sage, nor an ascetic. An ascetic attains liberation and departs. A sage transitions into an authority over a particular domain. A wise individual appreciates all these paths. While they may all be considered gurus in a sense, they are not the true Guru described here. Which Guru, then? It is not merely one capable of granting liberation to another sage. That is the essence of its meaning, contrary to common interpretations. The reality is that this concept remains misunderstood by you. Grasping this knowledge can be intellectually demanding. However, acquiring this wisdom is our imperative need today. By imparting it to our descendants, they may eventually offer guidance to our land, our nation, and the world. This is not an avenue for acquiring immediate wealth, nor is it a forum for mundane inquiries such as diagnosing an illness. This is pure knowledge. The crucial question is our stance in relation to this knowledge. In this regard, we have been bestowed with the opportunity to fervently invoke the divine, even in our state of ignorance. Therefore, it is essential that you all invoke the divine collectively and with utmost sincerity.

[GV1:P1508] What we witness in the world today is merely superficial. We are destined to ascend to a significantly higher state. That time will come; an intimation of this has already been received. We represent the primordial lineage of this transformation. According to the divine resolve of Brahman, the lineage of Manu and the lineage of the Trimurti are being unified to initiate the creation of an entirely new lineage. This is our role. It is a nascent sprout of that divine will. We have been entrusted with the foundational path to cultivate this sprout. Understanding this, you must achieve karmic purity devoid of any discriminatory intellect, derive wisdom from it, attain competence through that wisdom, and when fortune aligns, everything will manifest in its rightful place. We must remain vigilant until that opportune moment arrives. One must not succumb to arrogance upon acquiring partial knowledge, nor exhibit insolence upon accumulating material wealth. Such detrimental traits must be entirely eradicated from our character.

[GV1:P1509] As previously articulated, the Shakta, Shaiva, and Vaishnava traditions of the Trimurti lineage converged into a singular entity, transitioning into the wisdom of Manu's lineage. That wisdom is now undergoing a transformation, paving the way for the knowledge that catalyzes a new creation, which is what is being discussed here. The foundational karma for this endeavor is what now rests upon us.

[GV1:P1510] Presently, there may be both theists and atheists among you. Regardless of your stance, remain steadfast in your conviction. One must not oscillate between the two. Adopting faith merely when health and vitality decline is ultimately futile. You must firmly demonstrate your conviction while you possess health and vitality. The responsibility to substantiate the nature of disbelief also rests entirely upon you.

[GV1:P1511] When I first arrived here, there was an elderly gentleman who would visit with a rusted sword tucked under his arm. He would approach me to discourse on Vedanta, often beginning with, 'I had an uncle named Pillai. My Pillai uncle is in a state of Samadhi.' This is not the type of knowledge we ought to propagate. Despite our insignificant stature, we have been presented with profound revelations, including the lineage of the prophets. Their legacy is by no means insignificant; in fact, it is somewhat more extensive than ours. This is because the domain encompassing the Hindu identity was once more vast. However, the reverberations of the virtuous karma associated with the Hindu identity were primarily confined to India or the regions historically integrated with it. There are numerous nations across the globe where Hindu Dharma is virtually non-existent. When evaluated from this global perspective, the reach of the Trimurti system is quite limited.
