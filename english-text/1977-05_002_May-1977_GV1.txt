[GV1:P0024] May 1977

[GV1:P0025] Question: You mentioned that for a true devotee, sorrow is nectarine. So, when a guru experiences sorrows from ordinary people, should the guru grieve over it?

[GV1:P0026] Answer: No. Mothers often cry when their child falls ill. However, they do not throw the child away. There are times when a mother, wife, or husband has to nurse their loved ones through great sorrow. Because of that, no one severs that relationship. Just because a boil appears on the body, one does not discard that body part. All this teaches us that we must endure sorrow to reclaim love. Only such love can transform into something divine.

[GV1:P0027] Question: In what forms do the tests from a guru appear?

[GV1:P0028] Answer: Firstly, the guru will adopt measures to increase their knowledge. If the disciple lacks the understanding to comprehend and act accordingly, it will inevitably create a massive distance. Then, the disciple might perceive it as pain or as a test.

[GV1:P0029] Question: This guru-disciple relationship is one where both the guru and the disciple act with mutual understanding, isn't it? Then why is this gap created?

[GV1:P0030] Answer: In a workshop, school, or college, do all the students pass the exam? No. What was taught to them was not flawed. What was demonstrated was also exactly the same for everyone. Similarly, among the students of Brahma Vidya (divine knowledge) and Sidha Vidya, the teachings may not yield identical results for everyone. That is why this gap is created.

[GV1:P0031] Question: It is recorded in certain Puranas that the guru and the disciple will act with mutual understanding, isn't it? Why is it recorded that way?

[GV1:P0032] Answer: The same explanation given earlier applies to this as well. Just because they have understood the guru, it does not mean everyone will act in the same way.

[GV1:P0033] Question: Suppose a person without intellect or capability develops devotion—what should he do?

[GV1:P0034] Answer: It is much easier for him to sustain his devotion than the people mentioned earlier. Since he lacks capability, he will not try to deeply scrutinize anything. He desires to maintain his devotion and affection by offering service in accordance with what he observes.

[GV1:P0035] Question: Then, will he reach the ultimate reality?

[GV1:P0036] Answer: He does not think about whether he will reach the ultimate reality or not. He only considers whether there is any merit in what he is doing. That will be serving him in accordance with his intellect and experience. So, isn't he the smart one!

[GV1:P0037] Question: Suppose a girl develops such true devotion. How will she fulfill it?

[GV1:P0038] Answer: Do you assume that a girl is not a human being?

[GV1:P0039] Question: It is said that girls are not allowed to participate in such devotional matters during certain times. That is why I asked.

[GV1:P0040] Answer: Is it something you have heard, or something you have read?

[GV1:P0041] Question: That is what is heard and is the widely held belief.

[GV1:P0042] Answer: What you have heard is merely the chatter of ignorant people. The belief also originated in the same way. History records that many sages lived among us Hindus. History proves that most of the sages were accompanied by women. It is only among some yogis that we do not see women residing with them. There is no mention of Shuka getting married or living with women. Neither is there any such mention about Narada. We do not see it concerning Bhogar, the guru of Subramanya, either. But Bhogar is not described as someone with diminished lust; he is a siddha and a wise man.

[GV1:P0043] Kulapathi Speaks
