[GV1:P1864] February 1988

[GV1:P1865] The Blunder of Not Knowing What a Guru Is

[GV1:P1866] (The asset I gained from the dedication to service rendered

[GV1:P1867] over 18 years at Sivagiri and its branches)

[GV1:P1868] Beloved and devout champions of action,

[GV1:P1869] Upon seeing the headlines of the criticisms and hymns of praise from each of you in the newspaper, I felt a sense of love, joy, and a kind of suffocation. Devotion is but one, is it not? There are no two, right? It merely accords with each person's level of knowledge. I do not know whether you have understood Sri Narayana, who attempted to carefully comprehend and convey the state from which our ancient beliefs—that is, the Trinity—originated, and to create a religion of liberation from it.

[GV1:P1870] Some people who believe in Sree Narayana as a Guru, and scholars who merely flaunt their knowledge by attributing to him the greatness of a social reformer—their penmanship is inadequate as far as Sree Narayana is concerned. This is similar to the Yadava clan that set out to destroy Sree Krishna. Ultimately, Krishna had to destroy his own clan. Are not today's devotees of Sree Narayana destroying him through their penmanship and this grand spectacle (Mamankam)? Alas! Do they know about the divine will that enabled him to be called Narayana Guru, having undertaken penance across many births and, as a result of that penance, being born as the son of Madan Asan and Kuttiyamma, and growing up as Nanu Asan? Evaluate that with greater worth. Otherwise, when the time comes, this will become a hypocritical drama you perform before your children. It would be wise to consider whether your act of tying up, whitewashing, and burying Narayana Guru within it, magnifying him out of today's devotional ecstasy, stands any higher than mere court poetry. The public and royal subjects who wrote praises for the king will internalize and read them. But what will the people, who are not even capable of thinking whether the king is truly like that, assume? They might respect it like an official royal seal. All these antics of yours are exactly as Kunchan Nambiar said: "The pillar of light is a great wonder! I too must get my share of money." What prompted me to write something like this is the speech of Sukumar Azhikode and the explicit highlight of it by someone using the pen name Sreelan. It so happened that I wrote this on Sivaratri, the final day of the grand spectacle, the very day it completes a century. Had I not moved away from Sivagiri, I would not have been able to tell you this.

[GV1:P1871] You who are believers of Sahodaran Ayyappan should consider one thing. You celebrate him as a rationalist. On the day of Narayana Guru's Samadhi—

[GV1:P1872] The essence filled with the fear of disease, old age, and death

[GV1:P1873] The Sadguru who attained glorious nirvana!

[GV1:P1874] I do not know how you viewed the emotion that prompted Sahodaran Ayyappan to write thus, or how you viewed Narayana Guru. Thinking further, how did you evaluate Narayana Guru, who performed a hundred and one consecrations from the Kottar Ganapathiyam Kovil consecration all the way to Mangalore? From among all those, you pick out two matters to mention and become both joyous and infuriated.

[GV1:P1875] Are you ready to remove the grinding stone and the Ardhanareeswara consecration placed in front of the highly boasted consecrations in Kalavamkodam and Murukkumpuzha, and honor the words Truth, Dharma, Compassion, and Sympathy in their place?

[GV1:P1876] You clench your fists and punch the air, proclaiming "One Caste, One Religion, One God". Shouldn't that hand be striking your own heart instead? If so, would you have suffered the tragic fate of having to punch the sky? In a place where he said do not make toddy, do not give it, and do not drink it, as it is poison—do you think the obsessive propaganda you cultivate by making it, serving it, drinking it, making others drink it, taking a share of its profits, and showing off a grand spectacle, is meant to nurture Narayana Guru rather than destroy him? Is your grand spectacle about the Narayana Guru who, during the time he performed penance at Aruvippuram, lived on alms received in his palms, washing his only lower garment, sitting inside the forest while it dried, without even a spare cloth to wear?

[GV1:P1877] Have you included in the newspapers at least one of the statements spoken by Madathuvilakathu Kesava Panicker and Nani Asan, who was one of the householder devotees of that time? Have you published the sacrifice of his foremost disciple, Sivalinga Swami, and the fact that he had to suffer beatings? Why do you not highlight the Guru's words advising the boy of that time: "You got beaten, didn't you? It doesn't matter. A time will come for that, it is enough if you come then; do not come anymore and get beaten like this"?

[GV1:P1878] During the time I lived at Aruvippuram, I regularly went and prayed for forty-one days on the rock where the Swami had performed his penance. On the fortieth day, a form that I had never imagined or remembered appeared in the sky. Suddenly, my body shivered and trembled. When I saw that form, there was a halo of light akin to the one seen around the moon. On that very evening at four o'clock, on the way to Neyyattinkara, someone called me into a Potti's shop. When I looked up, I saw that photo and understood who it was. From then on, a visionary nature began in me. However, it continued without any specific order or method. Because I did not know the order and the right path, there was sorrow in me and a compelling urge to find clarity.

[GV1:P1879] Then the lineage of the people who cut down the casuarina tree and chased away Sree Narayana beat me up too. That is unbecoming of a sanyasi and a brahmachari, and is deeply insulting. When I read Sri Ramakrishna's Vachanamrutam that day, I came to know that he had understood the condition as impersonation. I too had that experience. It is not wrong if it happens to ordinary people who have no religious consciousness. But one should consider that actions have consequences. That would be the virtuous thing. The rock on which I stood and prayed that day was chiseled and placed as a pedestal at Kodithookkimala. That was a task carried out by myself. Madathuvilakathu Kesava Panicker met its expenses.

[GV1:P1880] For sixteen years after the consecration of the Sivalinga at Aruvippuram, the Sivalinga, which stood exposed to the sun and rain just like in Tamil Nadu, was worshipped by Raman Pillai, a devotee of the Swami who lived nearby. In your language, a Kosavan (potter). For all this time, it was he who worshipped it by taking water from the river in a pitcher, putting basil leaves in it, and chanting "Siva Siva". Even after sixteen years, the devotees of Sree Narayana could not build a shelter for it. Sree Narayana, who was a mendicant seeking alms then, came every new moon day (Vavu), made them understand the benefits of Vavubali, and the Swami himself made them perform the bali. The Swami would bury a 'chakram' (coin) he received as dakshina near the rock. Whenever he came on new moon days, he would make the youngsters perform a day's service. In those days, the Guru used to sit and perform penance in the inaccessible Sankarankuzhi, Kodithookkimala, and the caves. The rock on which he used to do penance then was leveled, and the Sivalinga was consecrated upon it. Sand has been spread around it.

[GV1:P1881] All of this is the result of the efforts of the youngsters of that era. The formless stone that was kept in the Vavupura before that is the very one placed at Santhiswami's samadhi site today. When you look at that stone, it appears somewhat like a Ganapathi. But it has no distinct form. The Swami also took up the task of teaching cooking to the youngsters of that time by instructing them to bring rice and provisions.

[GV1:P1882] Meanwhile, the Swami's name began to spread to many places. Among those, in a place called Palluruthy Illickal, the Swami's devotees built a temple and another group demolished it. When this happened several times, the Swami went there. The devotees started building the temple once again. A man named Avarachan arrived with a machete along with a group of people. The Swami called only Avarachan close and consoled him: "For how long have those poor people been building it. A few ignorant people come and demolish it. Go, you too go and help them." Immediately, Avarachan threw away his machete, and everyone joined together to build the temple. On the third day itself, the consecration took place.

[GV1:P1883] Thus, a drunkard who learned about the Swami from Illickal came to Aruvippuram. He was an excellent pandal (marquee) maker. For an anniversary that fell two or three years before the completion of the temple construction, the Swami placed a rupee into his hand and said, "Take this, eat something, and build a pandal for this Sivalinga. The shape of that pandal will be the shape of the upcoming temple." Immediately after that anniversary, the foundation was laid in the exact shape of that pandal. It was the chakrams that the Swami used to bury every new moon day that were utilized to cover the expenses for it.

[GV1:P1884] The devoted young men of those days would plant an *ethavazha* (a variety of banana) sapling each and water it. Thus, every tree there was planted by the youth. The construction of the upper structure was completed with the money earned from selling the banana bunches. Only the roofing material, which is a combination of bricks, lime, tiles, and palm jaggery seen above, was procured through the collection of funds from the locals. Raman Pillai, who had been performing pooja for all these sixteen years, was a self-sacrificing man who showed great diligence in all the activities there. Once the temple was established, the Swami found it impossible to move anywhere due to the heavy rush of people. Raman Pillai was exhausted from continuously cooking and serving food. There would be nothing left for him to eat. This continued for three days without food. On the third day, troubled by the lack of rice and provisions and overwhelmed by intense hunger, he thought of chopping the stalks of wild taro, cooking it, and eating it. He collapsed right where he was chopping it.

[GV1:P1885] When Swami came to know about this, he quickly ran over and sprinkled water on him. When he regained consciousness, Swami held him in his arms and laid him down. Asking, "Has such poverty struck here?", he asked them to dig a spot. People assumed that there was a treasure buried there. But on that very day, a bullock cart full of rice and provisions arrived there. My understanding is that, following such hardships, Raman Pillai suffered from dysentery multiple times and had to sacrifice his life. It is a pity that these office-bearers did not even try to utter or make someone utter a single word about such a great man who made such an immense sacrifice. Educating the youth about such matters is far more noble than exaggerating sleight-of-hand-like miracles performed elsewhere. This could have at least sharpened the value of their sacrifice.

[GV1:P1886] If you had described the reason why Swami stopped the *Kettukalyanam* (a traditional mock marriage ceremony), the pooja room at the house of Judge Adambil Vasudevan, the story of him going among the fishermen and helping them hold the nets, and his statement that "temples are enough, what we need now are educational centers", there would have been no need to put on this grand spectacle. I have heard that a joke shared between Chattampi Swami and Narayana Guru, who were contemporaries, when they visited the house of Pannisseri Nanu Pillai, is being used out of ignorance with a different meaning to belittle someone. The reply "there is work (*pravrithi*), but no one (*aarilla*)" to the question "What, have you taken up the village officer's (*pravrithiyaarude*) job too?" is just a joke. There is no need to exaggerate it. At the same time, there was an incident where, upon visiting the house of some Nair landlords, Chattampi Swami was given a mat to sit on, while Narayana Guru was given a low wooden stool (*korandi*). Chattampi Swami immediately called Narayana Guru and walked out. Didn't you realize that this Nair supremacy turned out to be an utter failure?

[GV1:P1887] If you remember what was done by stopping Swami's car when it was travelling on the highway northwards from Vaikom, shouldn't you reveal why it was done? He walked back from there, arrived at the Vaikom Satyagraha Ashram, expressed his sorrow, boarded a boat, and alighted at Muhamma. When there was a drizzle, he took shelter in a government guesthouse. The watchman there, who belonged to the Potti community, suddenly slammed the door shut. Saying with forbearance, "We didn't know. We entered unknowingly," he walked out getting drenched in that rain. Shouldn't we also recall how the Nair landlord known as Kattikkattu Sivaramapanicker, upon coming to know about this, prostrated before him and pleaded, "Swami, I have committed an act that will bring harm to the land and its people, please forgive me"?

[GV1:P1888] You must remember how Swami braided the coconut leaves with his own hands when the Aruvippuram building was constructed but the kitchen area was left unthatched. Does anyone remember how, when the northern building tilted in the rain because the person who built it did not construct it properly, he gave a command saying that the building should not fall until Govindan Vaidyan arrives?

[GV1:P1889] Swami stayed in Aluva for six months to establish the Aluva Advaita Ashram. He ordered Chaitanya Swami to clear the sacred serpent grove (*sarpakkavu*) and gather all the timber. There is a new house in Chandiroor. This house belonged to the most aristocratic Nair family of those times. There were serpents everywhere you looked in that house. If you placed a hand on the beam, there was a serpent. If you accidentally stepped somewhere, there was a serpent. After Narasimha Swami, who attained *samadhi* in Eroor, and Chaitanya Swami together gathered some soil from the four corners of that compound, sealed it in a pot, and took it away, there has been no serpent affliction (*sarpadosham*) there. Some timber was cut from there. Because they did not respect this and take it away, that family perished. Eventually, Aroor Sreedharan Jyothsyar bought that place and started living there. The locals know how the relatives of Kattikkattu Sivaramapanicker perished because they did not accept this. Do not forget the truth that only his children somehow managed to survive.

[GV1:P1890] When Swami was in Aluva, an amount of three hundred and sixty-four rupees was owed from Sivagiri to Mahani, a prominent merchant in Varkala. Unable to procure goods, he came and cried. Then Achuthananda Swami, who later became the *Mathadhipathi* (head of the monastery), quickly reached Aluva and informed the Guru about the matter. A priest called Sankaran Paradesi, the paternal uncle of P.R. Shastri, was a dependent of the Guru at that time. Achuthananda Swami and Sankaran Paradesi reached Sivagiri, and even after selling the cows they had at that time and borrowing from many people, they could only gather three hundred rupees. That was given. The remaining amount was somehow settled by Sankarananda Swami, who later became the *Mathadhipathi* and was serving Swami at that time, and Sankaran Paradesi together.

[GV1:P1891] After that, Swami started a Sanskrit school in Aluva. Among the six Harijan students there, two of them, Kunhiraman, who became the City Mayor, and Damodaran from Kottarakkara, had to suffer beatings. One day, while returning from school, these Harijan students laughed loudly on the road. There was a Nair household in front of the Advaita Ashram. The landlord there did not appreciate the laughter. The people of that house beat the boys, exhausted them, and threw them on the road. You must remember that incident. Saying, "Do not file any case. With that money, they can be treated and cured," Swami brought them to Sivagiri, summoned the uncle of Kunhiraman Vaidyan from Kollam, and got them treated and healed. After that, expressing his sorrow that "When we are no more, these children will be left without food," he told the householders visiting there: "You should conduct a Guru Pooja here on the birth star of your children or the death anniversary of your relatives." Even after calling all the Ezhava landlords of Travancore in that manner, only one hundred and fifty Guru Poojas materialized. Finally, it was Krishnanandan, Guruprasad, and Bodhanandan from Malabar who completed the three hundred and sixty-five Guru Poojas. In those days, the pooja would be performed with the Guru seated. It was the custom that the Guru would eat from it, and the rest would be given as *prasad* to those who performed the Guru Pooja. This is noted down here, considering it fit to be told to the reformative theorists who say about such a Swami that "he wasn't too bad" and ask "did the Guru ask to worship the Guru?". It is better for these reformative theorists to read the *Guru Gita*.

[GV1:P1892] Thus, the Sarada Math was built using the funds remaining from the Guru Pooja. In those days, the mother-in-law of Kulathoor Kunhiraman (Adarshanandan) and women of her age used to carry the stones. If they carried stones for two *chakrams*, they would only accept one and a half *chakrams* as wages. The rest was dedicated as service. When Swami Santhalingam, who was the secretary of the Sivagiri Mutt at the time, was carefully overseeing these matters, Kumaran Asan, the General Secretary, asked for the accounts and caused him distress. Should it not be revealed to the community that Swami Santhalingam and Swami Achyuthanandan consequently became upset, left Sivagiri, and went to reside in Thiruvannamalai for six years? After six years, Swami Achyuthanandan returned. Swami Santhalingam did not return. Falling ill, he went to Swami Ramanandan in Thrissur and attained *samadhi* there itself. It must be remembered that most of the prominent and capable disciples of that time, from Sri Sivalinga Swami to Anandatheerthar, were offspring belonging to the upper-caste communities. Recently, even at the age of eighty-two, Anandatheerthar was beaten up at Guruvayur for the sake of the downtrodden. Although the community did not accept Anandatheerthar, who discarded his sacred thread to become an ideal, hardworking disciple of Sree Narayana Guru, he sacrificed his entire life for that ideology. It is good for the activists to reflect that if all this had been revealed, the community hatred seen today could have been eliminated at least to some extent.

[GV1:P1893] When Swami Atmananda, a member of the Ganaka community who was the headmaster of Aluva School, was harassed by community members, he declared that he would no longer step foot in Kerala and went to live with Swami Govindananda at the Sree Narayana Sevashram in Kanchipuram. It was Swami Atmananda who brought the Sevashram to its present stature.

[GV1:P1894] During the time Sree Narayana Guru resided in Varkala, he would go to the houses of the neighboring Paraya community, call the little children, give them four *chakrams*, and send some to buy rice, oil, and other provisions. He would make the other children scrub and wash the pots and pans and dry them in the sun. The Swami himself would chop the vegetables, cook the rice, and serve the food. When their parents, who had gone to the forest to chop firewood and cut grass, returned in the evening, the children would say that some 'Thampuran' (lord) came and did all this. That was all they knew how to say. Out of them, a fair-skinned boy named Raghavan was taken to the drain, bathed, and made the Swami's cook. Should we not remind the people and enlighten them about the fact that this Raghavan would serve the remaining food as *prasadam* to others after the Swami had finished eating, just as a Brahmin priest distributes temple *prasadam*?

[GV1:P1895] Varkala Plavila Kesavan Mesthiri was a prominent figure of that time. He was a railway contractor who cleared roads. The knowledge shared by his son, Kunjukrishnan (the uncle of today's Gurukulam Sankaran), is noted below. This devotee of the Swami had a flaw. Whenever Raghavan started giving the *prasadam*, he would slip away. He would suddenly disappear like receding floodwaters. Ayyappan Pillai, known as Sathyavrathan, found a solution for this. He had Raghavan mix milk, bananas, sugar, ghee, honey, and flattened rice by hand. The contractor usually slipped away when the serving began. To counter this, Swami Sathyavrathan himself took the *prasadam* in his hand and sternly said, "Take this, Mesthiri!" Out of fear, he extended both hands and accepted it; this incident should also be brought to memory.

[GV1:P1896] The Swami went to Aluva and wrote a power of attorney for Chaitanya Swami (Ayyappan Pillai). When the community leaders created a dispute by getting Kumaran Asan on their side, the Swami called Chaitanyan and told him, "You do not need to listen to this quarrel, go and oversee the construction of the Thalassery Jagannath Temple," and sent him away. Following that, when the construction of the temple in Kannur and the Sreekanteswara Temple in Kozhikode was completed, Chaitanya Swami attained such greatness that the local people would tremble like a banyan leaf upon seeing him, and he attained *samadhi* in that very land.

[GV1:P1897] Even though Sree Narayana Guru tried to destroy caste in this manner until the time of his *samadhi*, if you had considered the mental agony that prompted that great ascetic in his final days to write, "I am resigning from the SNDP in thought, word, and deed," you would not show interest in the spectacle seen today. It would have become something that causes pain to all community members in Kerala. He had to cry in pain hearing the words of these very Malayalis. During the period when that name reached at least throughout Kerala, the land where the Swami was born and raised—that is, on the day of the Chathayam celebration in 1125 (Malayalam Era) at Chempazhanthy—I was on my way to Chempazhanthy. At that time, the Medical College road was not in good condition. Therefore, two mothers were talking to each other, standing on either side of the Kattachakonam road, named Kesavadasapuram, which goes east from Ulloor.

[GV1:P1898] "Why did a bunch of idlers go by today, wrapped in yellow?"

[GV1:P1899] "Didn't you know? Today is the anniversary of the one who died falling from a coconut tree."

[GV1:P1900] "Who is that?"

[GV1:P1901] "The anniversary of Nanu Asan, who was killed by hitting a coconut on his head under the pretext of *samadhi*."

[GV1:P1902] Today, a memorial for Narayana Guru has been built in that area. After crying for many days upon hearing this conversation, I realized that it was not their fault. It is the consequence of the knowledge imparted by their ancestors, who were indoctrinated to believe they were upper-caste, without realizing they were lower-caste. They do not know that the community in which Sree Krishna was born is Harijan. Those who stand proudly as Yadavas belong to the Harijans. I understood that this is the flaw of the established governance, the rulers, and courtly poetry. We know that this was a place ruled by a Pulaya because 'Pulayanarkotta' still exists today. Those who read the story of Cheraman Perumal will also know this without a doubt. It would be good to reflect that if the backward communities, who constitute eighty percent of India, had understood this collectively, every home today would have been able to transform our country into a heaven and raise children who claim the right to liberation.

[GV1:P1903] When Sree Narayana Guru saw that his ideologies would not be implemented through the SNDP, a year before his *samadhi*, he formed a group of fourteen people at Koorkenchery in Thrissur and registered it under the name 'Sree Narayana Dharma Sangham'. There is a photograph taken on that day showing Swami Sankarananda, the last head of the Math, holding a board with 'Sree Narayana Dharma Sangham' written in chalk. Are you doing all this while keeping that inside the Sivagiri office?

[GV1:P1904] When the Swami returned after receiving treatment from Dr. Pandalai in Madras, all the Ezhava leaders gathered at the Kollam boat jetty to receive him and brought him to the place where Neela Hotel stands today. The area seen on the western side of the Neela Hotel was a parade ground (Peeranki Maidanam) at that time. At night, the Swami used to sit in that area and meditate. It is there that the college and the Sarada Math are situated today. At that time, the Guru had told Sanku Bhaktan, "This area is a suitable place to build an institution. One day it will become so."

[GV1:P1905] There, the Ezhava leaders brought community matters to the Guru's attention. Saying, "Alright, let it be, I am coming to Sivagiri," the Swami sent them back. After reaching Sivagiri, three days before the Swami's *samadhi*, the community leaders arrived again and spread open a will for him to sign. In those days, the Swami, whose illness was severe, had to be supported to sit up. He suddenly sprang up and asked, "I built this through chanting mantras and practicing medicine—won't you even let me lie down in peace?" With that, the community leaders departed.

[GV1:P1906] It is good to think about the tragic fate that Sri Narayana devotees themselves managed to bring a stay order to prevent the fortieth-day bathing ceremony (Nalpathukuli) of Sri Narayana Guru. You should also remember the plight where devotees had to throw away the pooja items brought from Madurai at the railway station and leave. Shouldn't we highlight the personal ethics of an officer named Kasim, who asked the then Yogam secretary, "Sir, isn't he someone born in your own community - couldn't you have brought the stay order after this one day was over?"

[GV1:P1907] After that, a case went on between the Sree Narayana Dharma Sangham and the SNDP for ten years. Finally, to destroy the record that the Dharma Sangham was established, two almirahs were lifted from Sivagiri, brought to a rock at the place where the watcher Kunhiraman lived on the southern side of the Sivagiri school, and burnt. Is it the monk order that won by presenting a copy signed by the Swami stating "I have resigned from the SNDP in thought, word, and deed" at Sankarasubbayyar's bench, that is showing all this today? Shouldn't we remember the incident where Ayurvedic acharya Bhargavan Vaidyar was beaten and his keys were snatched away, alleging that he took the side of the monks back then? He was someone who was adopted, raised, and taught Ayurveda by the Swami.

[GV1:P1908] There is clear evidence as to how you view Narayana Guru. Worldly householders might lack that knowledge. It brings sorrow that the monks have lost it. The act of praising the ideal to the skies and installing the Shivalinga in the heart is something even a worldly devotee cannot conceive. Narayana Guru did not think of installing this Shivalinga inside. It was to increase the greatness of sacrifice and lead human life to salvation. This has become an action plan that ties one down even below ordinary people, rather than that of an ascetic. This is not suitable for a ritualist, a devotee, a wise man, or even a modern devotee. Who with a perfect mind wouldn't think that devotion, if it degrades, shouldn't stoop this low!

[GV1:P1909] Vairavan Shanthi, a man from Vakkom, became Shanthi Swami and attained Samadhi at Aruvippuram after living up to the age of one hundred and fourteen. You must have known about that ascetic. Once Narayana Guru lay down to sleep, he would lie across the door of that room. When the Swami got up at night without him knowing and crossed over, a man as tall as Kuttichathan would go with him. One day, upon reaching near the neem tree on the northern side of the temple—I don't know if that neem tree is still there today—a globe of light was conversing with the Swami. The Swami was enveloped in light. Seeing this, the Shanthi fell to the ground trembling with fear, and the Swami lifted him up, gave him water, and asked, "Do you have to come with me everywhere?" If you remembered at least this incident, you wouldn't have been able to show such a heart-linga. It would have been enough if you had at least respected what was written about Chidambaram.

[GV1:P1916] cchidamruthamennu thelinju dheeranayi

[GV1:P1919] If you had only thought about the meaning of that 'mridu' (soft) and the meaning of 'Sat Om', you wouldn't have drawn this farce.

[GV1:P1920] If I explain further, it might hurt some people. I doubt they will like it. I happily accept such Sri Narayana devotees proclaiming that I am against Sri Narayana. But I am sure that I will not face the heartbroken opposition of that ascetic. Your jubilation seems to once again pour sorrow into life. Can there be a greater ignorance shown towards Narayana Guru than this? If you only advertised that it was people among them who brought a stay order to prevent the Swami's fortieth-day bathing ceremony, all the people today would have seen the Swami as venerable. I am completely confident that there would have been no need for the fate we see today—calling the community members, building platforms, and making them clap for meaningless political speeches as Sukumar Azhikode reminded us.

[GV1:P1921] Likewise, I am convinced that none of those who were in Sivagiri in 1130 [Malayalam Era] can say that I am against Sivagiri.
