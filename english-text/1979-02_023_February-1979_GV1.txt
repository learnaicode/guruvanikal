[GV1:P0405] February 1979

[GV1:P0406] Question: Guru, kindly elaborate on the remainder of what you explained last time regarding the specific benefits of the Pournami prayer.

[GV1:P0407] Answer: Although the spiritual and the material are similar, whichever we give more weight to is what will grow. The mother of a child born on Pournami must be conscious. She must realize that this is for the world and that it has been ordained by God. A father, whenever a child is born, would ask whether the child is a boy or a girl. Nine girls were born. When the ninth child was also born a girl, the father sat with his head in his hands and said, "Lord, enough of girls." The tenth born was a boy. The father died during the boy's childhood itself. If the pain experienced in a single moment can have such an immense impact, it is impossible to articulate the consequences of the actions we perform in pain over time, and the results of prayers offered in agony.

[GV1:P0408] Question: Guru, could you please explain about the special prayers in this ashram?

[GV1:P0409] Answer: This ashram has a specific purpose. It is not the mainstream convention that is followed here, but our own equivalent tradition. Our mode of worship is the most advanced among all those that have existed until now. One must understand what it is—its true value. There have been Gurus and lineages in the past as well. They all provide the guidance of their predecessors. Here, there are no predecessors. All of that is integrated into this. This is how one studies about a Guru. No one realizes that we are not merely clinging to a path shown by someone else. In ancient times, it was said that divine messengers received words. They called it an ethereal voice (Ashariri).

[GV1:P0410] Words are received from the light. We implement them while we are still alive. Previously, such knowledge was kept secret and preserved, only to be revealed through a disciple after the master's time. This is the difference. We did not create this. There is a profound greatness to it.
