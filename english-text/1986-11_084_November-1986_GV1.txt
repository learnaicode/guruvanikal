[GV1:P1651] November 1986

[GV1:P1652] Initially, there was only a very small hut in Santhigiri. A hut just about my height. Only if one stood exactly in the middle would their head not hit the roof. In every stage of growth, we must be able to reflect from the very beginning. What myriad hardships were overcome since those days to reach this point? Within a year, we managed to build a small prayer room in place of that little hut. It was only after seven years and four months that people began arriving, recognizing it as a place of worship. Today, it has reached this state. You must realize how much hardship brimmed with the profound response of goodness within this journey. You must understand that whenever goodness is about to occur, it is preceded by an element of evil. From that time until today, this has been my experience. If the impending goodness is monumental, it will be preceded by a situation where people collectively criticize or speak ill. If it is minor, it will be some other situation that causes mental anguish. If it is even smaller, at least a little child will cause us some pain. Something of that nature must occur. That is the reaction of goodness. However, ordinary people believe that sorrow should never befall them. We even have a system for that—offerings (*nercha*). If they hear that going to a particular place will ward off sorrow, they will rush there. Yet, we do not visit such sacred places merely to prevent sorrow. It is to inquire and understand our joys and sorrows, to discover what solutions exist for them, and to explore the possibilities within our lives to alter the state of our soul. It is to recognize the potential disruptions that may arise from our course of action, to work through them, and to attain the opportunity to become the true masters of our own *karma*.

[GV1:P1653] In the karma of gods and goddesses, they revel in numerous joys and sorrows, immerse themselves in it, and are then forced to take birth again. However, this is not what is said about human beings. But it is the singing of praises and rituals of the gods and goddesses that you have learned. You sing praises to the gods and consider it to be everything. This is similar to some emotional people who love someone deeply and then stand ready to hang themselves. If asked why, even they would not know. Similarly, when these special praises, with their repetitive power, reach our karma, it becomes equivalent to hanging our lives. However, they have stated in one word—in the Satya Yuga, penance is required; in the Treta and Dvapara Yugas, manifesting the deity and offering praise; and in the Kali Yuga, chanting of names. They haven't specified whose name. Therefore, even in the Kali Yuga, we merely chant the root mantras (moola mantras). Scholars have written that salvation (Sayoojyam) can be attained through this. By bestowing all forms of mastery upon the gods and making us worship them, they are kept pleased. The enlightened ones stand above this and speak of the Upanishads. There are Upanishads written by scholars. In them, depicting such descriptions, those who consumed liquor (Sura) from the enlightened kings of that time were preserved as Devas (gods). Thus, those who consumed the liquor and meat offered to the gods became Suras. Those who did not became Asuras. The people who are categorized as Asuras belong to the Shudra class. This Shudra class constitutes about eighty percent. All of them might turn into Asuras. You yourself should ponder the reason for this.

[GV1:P1654] This Shudra class encountered a peril in the beginning. It can be untied if one tries, but as long as it remains untied, it continues to be an affliction. This is expressed in a verse in the Bhagavata.

[GV1:P1655] Karma acts as bonds for a person.

[GV1:P1656] Liberation certainly comes only when the bonds are severed ٹ

[GV1:P1657] So, even if you have wondered what the method to untie the knots is, you have not been able to untie them so far. That is the greatest of the perils that befell the Shudra class. Praises were written in such a way that it seemed violence and mischief were the virtues of the Kshatriyas, thus instilling cruelty in them and making them great. All forms of growth have been denied to the Shudra class. If any mistake occurs in that, it is enough to give alms to the Brahmin. Furthermore, they taught that if a Brahmin curses someone to death, that too is a blessing. Cursing meant beating to death. How many such stories do we have? Initially, when I said this, those who portrayed it as deliberately spoken to malign the Brahmins are with me today. Today, they are somewhat convinced. I did not say this after studying history. I said it after observing and understanding the ancestors of each class and caste. It is upon seeing that I called it an affliction that one does not know how to untie.

[GV1:P1658] There were no Brahmins in India. Those who were here were the Konkanis. They are still docile people. (I do not forget the fact that some are ready to become violent under the RSS). They were meritorious people who treated snakes and cows alike and lived without hurting anyone. You must understand that a lineage with such merit perished in this manner. The Aryans drove away this Brahmin community, which maintained honesty, from here by copying various intellectual systems. Then they created their own language, their own order of worship, and became gods themselves. That is the trick of the Aryan.

[GV1:P1659] Some of those who were driven away returned. They can be seen in many parts of Kerala. But today, they have no hold anywhere. If anything is fabricated anywhere, it is the tantric knowledge of the old Aryans. Realizing that this fate befell them because they accepted Tantra and Vedic rituals, they have not yet been able to retreat from it. Although an acharya named Madhwa came among them, he led them back to the old ways. You should not assume it is dishonesty. It is honesty. Only by asking that very power of light what went wrong with us will there be a correction for it. That was also difficult to get at that time. Because Madhwacharya had some scholarship, he did not move in that direction. Thus, Madhwa is the acharya who wrote Dvaita as Advaita and asserted that one can attain Advaita from this Dvaita. Consequently, that lineage reached a state where there was neither Dvaita nor Advaita. Worship reverted to those old tantras. Thus, that lineage declined.

[GV1:P1660] We too must be careful not to make a similar mistake. I am not speaking of Advaita here. I am speaking about the purity of karma. You should not eliminate karma by conflating it with Advaita.
