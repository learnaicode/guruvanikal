[GV1:P1366] January & February 1984

[GV1:P1367] In the primordial era of ignorance, humanity acted in a beastly and demonic manner, appropriate to those times. That state has evolved, and today it has reached the visible position of *Dharma* (righteousness). The current condition in *Kali Yuga* is one where an individual contemplates the nature of this luminous entity rooted in philosophical discipline, and wonders how to integrate oneself into it. This state is replete with all attributes. The world functions perpetually in such a way that both good and evil are thoroughly churned and clarified, enabling us to extract its ultimate essence.

[GV1:P1368] The endeavor is to imbue ourselves with this functional capacity, impart it to our children, and elevate it traditionally through generations. God reveals this to us so that it may operate in the most optimal manner. How does this occur? For instance, various imaginations and perceptions spontaneously arise in our minds without our conscious knowledge. When terms like *Bhakti* (devotion), *Yoga*, *Jnana* (knowledge), or *Karma* (action) are mentioned, a corresponding imagery forms in the mind. Little children have a specific perception when they see their father, whereas adults have a different one. The same applies when seeing a mother. Thus, the perception towards individual persons varies uniquely among different people. Similarly, a painter and a singer possess distinct forms of imagination. How did this imagination emerge in the human mind? The imagery that arises corresponds to each individual's inherent tendencies (*vasana*). It is the intrinsic inclination of one's own life-force. What happens is the continuous amplification of this tendency. It is a profound secret residing within us, often without our awareness.

[GV1:P1369] Consequently, various forms of perceptions arise—similarly, a specific disposition is developed towards God. After contemplating various matters in the mind, the perception that ultimately reaches us is an amalgamation of beauty, humility, truth, tolerance, and merit. It emerges spontaneously from within ourselves. This is an assimilation intrinsic to the evolutionary nature of the mind. It can only be realized through experience and cannot be adequately expressed through words.

[GV1:P1370] *Bhakti* (devotion) is a dimension akin to a magnificent mountain of love. It is fundamentally a path of love. That love is the perception of a profound glory in everything one beholds. It is a state of mind where an involuntary reverence for all things emerges from within us. Children naturally develop an affinity for toys or tasty items. As age advances, this affection undergoes a twofold transformation: one aligns with earnestness and dutiful action (*karma*), and the other with emotionality. This is something each of you has experientially realized. However, it is impossible to articulate exactly how this transpires.

[GV1:P1371] Through these continuous transformations, it is only when we encounter a great soul—or a philosophical thought associated with them—in whom the underlying realities of humility, truth, and sacrifice function in perfect unison, that we comprehend the true weight of this devotion. It is an exceedingly magnificent glory. We are currently situated within it. Standing in this position, our reflection should be: 'I must integrate this into my life-force, cultivate it incrementally, and act in a manner that benefits my colleagues, home, society, and nation.' Some individuals observe, understand, and merely move on. Others are different; they endure any sacrifice and exert relentless effort for the welfare of others. Everyone is human. For some, merely witnessing all this provokes anger. For others, there is an element of selfishness in their affection. Or alternatively, they possess an ignorance that prevents them from recognizing and respecting whatever actions others undertake. Thus, we have deficiencies in every aspect. True cultural refinement (*samskara*) can only be achieved by rectifying all of these flaws. To accomplish this, we must step into a public sphere, carefully observe every action there, discern the inherent good and evil within them, and ultimately arrive at the path where further sacrifices must be made. Ascend in this manner and set forth upon the sacrifice of instilling the excellence of this greatness into every individual among us. It is through such selfless individuals that we have gained so much familiarity and knowledge today.

[GV1:P1372] How shall this be acquired hereafter? Through whom shall it be acquired? We all possess knowledge and the desire to acquire all this. However, when it comes to the point of execution, there are numerous obstacles—we may find ourselves incapable, or if we attempt it, the actions may lack mutual coherence, rendering us unable to accomplish the task successfully. We must eliminate these impediments and forge ahead. We should advance in a specific direction only after meticulously consolidating our thoughts on what must be done to achieve this.

[GV1:P1373] For instance, Hindus initially establish a *Bhajana Matham*—a designated place for devotional worship. Following that, a Temple is built, and subsequently, an *Ashram*. Similarly, for Christians, it begins with a chapel for the month of devotion, then a Church, and finally the establishment of a Metropolitanate. For Muslims, the progression follows the order of *Thaikkavu* (prayer hall), Mosque, and finally Mecca. This is the progressive structure all these wise predecessors have established. Through this, they have been transmitting distinct levels of knowledge. It has been integrated with each respective religion in this manner. What is the significance of this integration? Through lived experience, they have harmoniously blended spiritual and material aspects, linking them together like a chain. Otherwise, the individual would stray from the path.

[GV1:P1374] This, however, is distinct from that. Here, both discipline and salvation are surrendered at a single focal point, and from there, each aspect is gradually reclaimed. That is the fundamental nature of love inherent in the Guru-disciple relationship. My savior and my disciplinarian are one and the same entity. In a way, it is akin to a monarchy; however, it is intrinsically different. It is a process of learning through life itself. In every individual's life, the Guru prompts them to perform specific actions, and through the disciple's affectionate reverence and loving respect, this essence is gathered purely, cleansed with humility, and imbued with an indescribable, fortuitous love. Consequently, there is no state unacquainted with glory in that realm. It is by embodying this profound glory that each of us must perform our actions.

[GV1:P1375] The sanctity of the titles of father, mother, and siblings exists only because we bestow it upon them. The status that we have instituted for God is quite similar. When we take up water to drink, we often close our eyes for a brief moment. Some individuals do this when they lift a spade to dig, pick up their tools of trade, or take a pen in hand. It is not that they revere these objects as God. However, unconsciously, there exists a certain sanctity associated with them. It is merely a demonstration of this inherent sanctity.

[GV1:P1376] There is a general assumption that drivers, boatmen, and mahouts possess no reverence for anything. Yet, a driver will close his eyes for a moment to display that reverence right before taking hold of the steering wheel. As for the mahout, he too pauses for a moment before picking up the goad. Why is this so? He knows the beast could trample him to death or gore him. Therefore, upon taking up the goad, he inwardly prays that the animal remains docile at its sight. The same applies to a boatman. A mere glimpse of dark clouds gathering in the sky is enough to evoke intense anxiety within him. A person soaring through the heavens may mistakenly consider himself as free as a bird. However, once on board, it is solely his unwavering focus that ultimately guides him to his destination. Observing these instances, one must recognize where such mindfulness is required in life. The primary duty of a Guru is to cultivate this profound sense of mindfulness within an individual. You must comprehend all these aspects. Irrespective of our path, it is impossible to navigate life without this fundamental attentiveness.

[GV1:P1377] We are presently standing on a narrow path of knowledge. We are merely beginning to enact one part of it. Therefore, the first lesson to learn is how to cultivate innate human love. We are accustomed to perceiving love both with and without an exclusionary mindset. We must diligently learn how this love should manifest in various contexts. Regardless of whether we are scholars or unlettered individuals, we can only progress in this world by imparting this love. By perceiving the sanctity of that love in everything, we must stand firm and begin to study it. Through that study, we must examine each subject and bring it to the forefront of our lives. This remains our ultimate objective.

[GV1:P1378] Guruvani

[GV1:P1379] March 1984

[GV1:P1380] Navigating our life is, in a way, relatively effortless. To illustrate, when a first-grade student observes a fourth-grade student studying, it appears as an overwhelming burden to the former. Nothing seems clear to him at that stage. However, upon reaching the fourth grade himself, he learns playfully. This is because he has already grasped the most effective methods to facilitate his learning process. We approach every subject in a similar manner. Once we acquire that intuitive aptitude for learning, everything else becomes trivial. Conversely, if an individual lacks any exposure to such concepts and has never even attempted to seek that knowledge, they will initially comprehend nothing. Furthermore, when they finally begin to understand, the mind feels heavily burdened, causing an urge to retreat from it entirely. Many among us suffer from this tendency to flee when confronted with something inherently good. This stems from our lack of habituation in witnessing, listening to, and familiarizing ourselves with virtuous matters.

[GV1:P1381] A system known as *Shudranyayam* (the logic of the Shudras) once emerged amidst us. There was an era when a Shudra was prohibited from doing practically anything. They were not allowed to touch, listen, or approach others; if they were to perform any task, they were expected to complete it and immediately run away—this was what they had taught us. Such an oppressive culture prevailed in our society during that period. Subsequently, an opportunity presented itself to the Shudra. This chance materialized in the fourth phase.

[GV1:P1382] It has been 5000 years since the birth of Sree Krishna. During these 5000 years, we have not been able to articulate and realize the true role of the Shudra. By bringing forth great souls into this world, God continuously educates us through their exemplary lives. However, to closely observe and learn from their lives, we require a certain degree of wisdom. It is perhaps due to a deficiency in this wisdom that we have failed to accomplish this.

[GV1:P1383] The Vaishya can conduct himself rather gracefully. Nevertheless, he has been unable to internalize and act upon this higher wisdom. The Marwaris and Gujaratis of Northern India serve as a prime example of this. They are at the forefront not only in generating wealth and sustaining their lives but also in undertaking philanthropic endeavors for various causes. They often execute these acts of charity with immense grandeur. Such behavior is rarely observed among us, as we are unaccustomed to it. When they offer *Gurudakshina*, it is of the highest standard. They continually desire to contribute as much as possible. However, they are equally ruthless in accumulating wealth, harboring no hesitation in adopting any means necessary to acquire it. Ultimately, they gather these riches, only to give them away charitably with the other hand.

[GV1:P1384] Even though kings belonging to the Shudra caste ruled here, it proved futile for us. During the reign of a Shudra, we ought to have attained complete freedom. It was either by divine intervention or a stroke of fortune that administrative power fell into the hands of the Shudras. However, instead of utilizing it for the greater good of the populace, they manipulated it in a manner that exclusively benefited their own kin. They segregated society into various classes and castes, eventually leading to a situation where one Shudra could not even walk in the territory ruled by another Shudra. Consequently, the ruling Shudra began oppressing the other Shudras under the guise of caste divisions. Armed with the scepter of authority, they were prepared to crush anyone who stood in their way. As a direct result of this, they now stand utterly ruined and dismantled. We have failed to perceive this reality. The rest of the Shudras degenerated into a marginalized class devoid of even the right to walk on public roads. It was a dark era when they lacked the fundamental rights to adequate food and clothing. Their garments had to remain strictly above the knee; such was the draconian law. If an official arrived to collect taxes while the head of the household was having a meal, the homeowner had to shout out from his place, stating, "Your humble servant is sitting here drinking a little rice gruel." They were compelled to speak in such a subservient manner. According to the law, when girls attained puberty, four *chakrams* had to be paid to the authorities. Similarly, women were strictly prohibited from covering their breasts. A breast tax, known as *Mulakkaram*, was imposed upon them for this very reason. Failure to pay this tax would result in the authorities confiscating all the decent household goods. Once, when officials visited a house to commit such atrocities, a woman residing there was overcome with unbearable anguish. She placed a fresh plantain leaf before the official, severed the very body parts upon which the tax was levied, and offered them on it. This horrific incident took place in the Cherthala taluk. Even today, recounting this event evokes a profound sense of disgust and horror.

[GV1:P1385] It is only after enduring such an oppressive era that we have arrived at our present state. Thus, the administration of that specific class of Shudras—who subjected us to myriad forms of suffering and actively sought to demean us—has finally been eradicated from this land. Today, even their own descendants cannot justify those past actions. They have evolved to a significantly higher moral standing. That is indeed a blessing. Consequently, we can console ourselves with the hope that some goodness will prevail when they ascend to positions of power.
