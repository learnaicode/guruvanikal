[GV1:P1513] May 1985

[GV1:P1514] Today, adherents of all religions and individuals from all strata of society have migrated into India. Yet, when evaluated collectively, we constitute a very minuscule fraction of the population. You remain oblivious to this reality. Unwittingly, you carry a narrative, instilled by others, that falsely amplifies your own greatness. This Earth is the dwelling place of countless nations. A comprehensive census of global populations remains elusive, rendering other demographic comparisons moot. It is insufficient to merely assert and convince ourselves of our own grandeur; one must also critically examine the realities illuminated by practical experience.

[GV1:P1515] Therefore, in this context, we are akin to individuals attempting to lasso a mountain of grandeur with a mere cotton thread—not even a resilient one. Yet, with this fragile thread, we aim to conquer this mountain. This is because the prevailing current is favorable to us. Aligned with the trajectory of this current, we are merely proceeding while holding onto this thread, as if hauling a conquered mountain. We must interpret our situation with precisely this understanding, acknowledging our limited capabilities. However, present circumstances are advantageous to us. Advantageous to whom? To the uninformed masses scattered across the globe. The term 'Shudra' signifies the ignorant. Kali Yuga is the epoch of the Shudra. You possess tangible evidence of this within yourselves. The educational opportunities available here were not benevolently apportioned to us by the traditional Brahmin and Kshatriya elites. Rather, we gained access to them through the inevitable progression of time. This is what is meant by the current being favorable to us. We are immersed in this current; essentially, we are fortunate to have been born in this specific era.

[GV1:P1516] Today, we are in a position where everything we touch yields fruit. The oppressive practices of untouchability, unapproachability, and social exclusion have been eradicated. Presently, there is universal freedom in these matters. Therein lies our triumph. We have now acquired the capacity to govern and to administer. What remains is the acquisition of the profound knowledge discussed earlier. Subsequently, through rigorous contemplation and the purity of our actions, we must integrate this wisdom as our own.

[GV1:P1517] There are certain things that women in our homes must be very careful about. There is no mother in this world who does not think, in the name of some belief, that 'my daughter should get this,' 'my husband should get that,' or 'my children should have all these things.' Therefore, when you perform every action (karma), it should be with the thought that it must bring goodness to the world and to your progeny. Similarly, if it is a father, he should not think, "I am unable to perform karma even for the two children in this house." Instead, the thought should be, "May they have an existence in every way, to provide for ten people, to eat, and to wear." Whether one is a cart driver, a boatman, or anyone performing any task, if he is a father, he must possess this knowledge. One must not embody laziness. We can perform any karma; you should not assume that it is merely for feeding the family. It might be a small thing. However, the knowledge, experience, and tradition that your progeny acquires through your actions must stand as the foundation for the world and for this lineage. First, you must acquire that knowledge. Then, impart it to your descendants. This is our goal.

[GV1:P1518] Therefore, all these ancestors (Pitrus) mentioned to us for this purpose, the previously mentioned Brahmin ancestors, the reason they are called so is because they are the progeny of those with knowledge. Due to that knowledge, they attained the position of a Deva. They are unable to descend from this Deva state. A Deva also does not have the authority to grant liberation (Mukti). This is because he does not possess karma. He has a subtle body (sookshma sareera). Then how can he achieve it? He cannot. That is why they appeal to be born into a lineage that possesses purity of karma (karma shuddhi).

[GV1:P1519] Once their merits (punya) are exhausted, they fall back into the human world. Their desire is that this fall should be into this very lineage. Then, starting from scratch, they achieve completeness through karma and attain liberation through birth. Thus, whether you are male or female, you must make a firm resolution: "I, who am to be the master of karma of this standard, shall not tread the path of error anymore." You must muster this courage: "An opportunity to see, hear, and understand this has come to me; I shall reclaim it."

[GV1:P1520] Without understanding this, tomorrow a sorcerer (mantravadi) or an astrologer might come, scratch his head, and say, "I see the influence of Gulikan in this." If asked for a solution, this ritualist will say, "No problem, just call a priest (Tirumeni) there, perform Tilahavanam, Shuddhihavanam, and Kshetrapindam, and grant purity to this ancestor." He does not know who this Gulikan is or how this Gulikan can be controlled. We perform these acts without knowing the position of this Gulikan, Deva, or Devi, or what the qualification of this karma is. This is where we fail. That is a science created during the time of the Devas to reveal the meaning of the Deva lineage.

[GV1:P1521] However, if you examine the Siddha Panchangam, it prescribes that we can have such karma performed by a person akin to a sage (Jnani), it can be performed in any condition, and there is no Rahukalam for him. You must have it done with this understanding. This is said so that the magnitude of what is being done can be understood.

[GV1:P1522] Guruvani
