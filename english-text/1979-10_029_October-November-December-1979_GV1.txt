[GV1:P0474] October, November, December 1979

[GV1:P0475] Disciple: What is the necessity for having firmness in faith?1

[GV1:P0476] Guru: An explanation for this was given in the Santhigiri magazine here, haven't you read it?

[GV1:P0477] Disciple:2 Even though we read it from time to time, our doubts have still not been cleared.

[GV1:P0478] Guru: It is difficult to instantly convince you about what faith is, as it is hard for a heart lacking inherent tendencies (*Vasana*) to grasp it merely through words. *Vasana* refers to the resultant fruits of the merits and demerits (*Punya* and *Papa*) that you have accumulated at the time of your birth. Merits and demerits imply the characteristic qualities present in our soul prior to our birth.

[GV1:P0479] Disciple: How is that so? What do you mean by the characteristic qualities present before birth?

[GV1:P0480] Guru: Our Hinduism or Sanatana Dharma (*Sanatana* refers to the culture of the Gurus of respective eras; meaning that which is eternally applicable). We neither had the opportunity to study that culture beforehand, nor do those currently present possess the knowledge to impart it. As an obstruction and a stumbling block to this, it was deliberately manipulated so that it would not be accessible to anyone other than the Brahmins. The Trimurti system and the Vedic system subdued the Varnashrama Dharma, which was considered superior, and reduced it to mere class distinctions. However, it did not benefit the aforementioned Brahmins either. The reason it was of no use was that the Vedic rituals advanced among the Brahmins and reduced their duties to mere Tantric practices.

[GV1:P0481] The Trimurti system was a framework designed to manage not just life, but also governance through these duties. When governance and mythological epics were thus integrated and elevated in a way that made them appear as the visible gods themselves, and as they prepared to study and teach it, the memory of Manu, the primordial Guru, faded away from among us. Through this, no true knowledge could be attained either. At the same time, extremely degraded modes of worship were prescribed for castes from the Nairs downwards.

[GV1:P0482] 1. This Guru-Disciple dialogue was given the title *Human Community—Yesterday, Today, and Tomorrow*. Later, it became a part of the book under the title *Humanity—Yesterday, Today, and Tomorrow*.

[GV1:P0483] 2. The disciple's name is printed as Vellayani Appukuttan Nair in the magazine.

[GV1:P0484] Through that worship, not only were we unable to comprehend the path forward, but it also became impossible for them to provide us even a hint—or for themselves to attain—the knowledge of what was required for us in different eras. In this context, the matter of faith that you asked about remains merely in name, aimlessly circling like an enigma.

[GV1:P0485] Only those who have experienced and known faith can completely articulate its form. If such experiential matters are spoken to anyone other than those approaching that experience, it will become an object of ridicule. Thus, it can only be explained to you through simpler examples. Faith is love. The love towards one's father and mother. The love between siblings, the love between husband and wife, the love for relatives, the love for friends, the love for things that are dear, and among those dear things, the love towards the medium called God. What needs to be specifically understood is how this medium called God is utilized.

[GV1:P0486] That love or medium called God is truth. Our ancestors have reminded us that it is love, glory, and lightness. Truth, righteousness (*Dharma*), compassion, love, and empathy are all noble terms associated with this. The Guru is the one who harmonizes and actualizes all these together. When one acts with faith in the words of the Guru, it is experienced as truth and as God. Thus, it became evident that the Guru is the supreme path to realizing the concept of God. It is in this era that the Guru illuminates before us in an absolute and complete form.

[GV1:P0487] Disciple: What is the reason for giving such prominence to the Guru in this era? There were Gurus in the past as well, weren't there?

[GV1:P0488] Guru: In the past, that is, before the time of Krishna, actions were in accordance with the *Yugadharma* (the duty of the age). Except for a few shortcomings, things were progressing according to the *Yugadharma*. *Yugadharma* encompasses the *Krita Yuga*, *Treta Yuga*, *Dvapara Yuga*, and *Kali Yuga*. This is how the four ages (*Chaturyuga*) are established. Among these, during the *Satyayuga* (*Krita Yuga*), all of us human beings lived truthfully together, without any discrimination. However, when the age changed, that form of light which was perceived as truth, reality, and love, disappeared. While the bewildered humans remained confused for a long time without knowing what it was, some intelligent and curious individuals began to spend their time in penance and prayer deep inside the forests, on riverbanks, in desolate places, and on seashores. After prolonged efforts, depending on the extent of their spiritual elevation, certain forms and lights began to briefly appear and vanish here and there. After a long time passed in this manner, some began to draw what they had seen in the forest on trees using green leaves. Those on the seashore began to record the nature of the light and the sunbeams they saw on the horizon.

[GV1:P0489] Thus, as time progressed, various forms and expressions were drawn and recorded, adapting to the times, as if it were a creation. Subsequently, a desire emerged to assign words to these. When they prayed for this, they began to receive words from the atmosphere, both through light and otherwise. They named this *Ashariri* (a disembodied voice).

[GV1:P0490] Later, when letters were invented, scholars attempted to inform us by recording all these through writing. Thus, the term *Mantra* originated. They started praying by assigning a specific mantra to each form. Mantras, *tantras*, and *homas* (fire rituals) were uniquely prescribed for deities of different forms, establishing a formal system of worship. In this way, they evolved into various distinctive rules and injunctions.

[GV1:P0491] Thus, mantras and tantras transformed into prescribed rules, and with the advent of the *Dvapara Yuga*, they became manifestations. Consequently, the deities of the *Treta Yuga* and the *Dvapara* period became manifest deities. Beyond that, when neither we nor our lineage were able to utilize the words of the Guru, which carry an illuminating commanding power, we had to remain content with the mantras and tantras used for worshipping the deity as a manifest god. Furthermore, these tantric and mantric practices were handled in such a way as to imply that even Vaishnavism and Shaivism were unsuitable for the Dravidians.

[GV1:P0492] Over time, the Dravidian practice of finding satisfaction in worshipping through the remaining *Shakteya* (Goddess-centric) traditions became distorted, turning into absolute madness and frenzy. Thus, *Yakshis*, *Madans*, and *Mantramurthis* became gods. In this manner, the glory of God was reduced to mere swaying and delirious dancing. Among these frenzied people, who lacked any literacy or other informative knowledge, even if one or two good individuals emerged within that large community, they were left unable to do anything, forced to stand by in utter bewilderment, not knowing what ought to be done.

[GV1:P0493] This situation gradually accumulated and began to manifest as claims of 'our caste' and 'our religion'. Adequate guidance to grasp the deeper meaning of any of this was largely unavailable. However, after a long time had passed in this manner, the concept of freedom emerged from the evolving thoughtful nature among them. During the period when people were enamored with systems of imitation, they suffered without freedom, facing immense hardships, and were even denied the right to education. Human beings grew like wild plants, completely unable to leap toward true knowledge.

[GV1:P0494] Thus, the desire for freedom occasionally surged among the people who had been trampled upon and kept like mere grass for a long time by the combined forces of mighty rulers and cunning *tantriks*. Then times changed. By that time, the Westerners had discarded their obsolete forms of worship and adopted new ones, a process that began with Moses and was completed during the time of Jesus. The Arabian people, adhering to the words of the Prophet, renewed their worship protocols and learned to live systematically. Through their prophethood, which served as the words of a Guru, they were able to pursue a creative life in a manner suitable for their times. Thus, they strove to make their nations whole and perfect through art and prosperity.

[GV1:P0495] In this way, even if their isolated islands were geographically inhospitable, they inherently possessed the intelligence and freedom to live and sustain life in that environment. Thus, as their lifestyles were repeatedly renewed over time, and as education and intellectual enlightenment flourished, they set out to investigate whether any other human societies were living in a similar manner. That is how they discovered this land. Though their initial intentions might have been rooted in goodness, selfishness is, after all, an inherent human trait. Arriving here in various capacities, engaging in trade and other crafts, those intelligent individuals who understood the cultural values, as well as the highs and lows of the local populace, discovered the vulnerabilities to exploit. Using their financial power, they thoroughly grasped the deep-rooted inequalities and divisions among the impoverished people—divided by caste upon caste. They assisted the upper classes with wealth and helped the lower classes by providing food and clothing. By winning them over, they utilized their self-serving interests to seize and consolidate political power. They subsequently implemented their willful propaganda and their version of religious freedom.

[GV1:P0496] As a result, when education, wealth, and enlightenment grew among some of these poor people, those who previously had no religious freedom or knowledge about religion grew under a different shade; some attained the goal of religious freedom and remained content with it. Thus, it became undeniable to say that everything fell into their hands. However, they also acted in a way that imparted a level of civilization to us, aligning with their own standards of civilization. If we carefully observe their actions, we can understand the true motive behind all of this.

[GV1:P0497] The foremost among these is that a great soul named Krishna was born in a downtrodden community known as the Yadava clan. They were categorized as the Krishna group and brought under control. By feeding them with tantras and mantras, they were manipulated and kept in their marginalized state. The words of the preceptor failed to uplift them. Following this was the advent of Buddha. Although previously born among the downtrodden, he was then born among the Kshatriyas. Just as what could not be completed during the time of Moses was fulfilled through Christ, God orchestrated various maneuvers here, back and forth, to bring it to completion.

[GV1:P0498] What that means is, among the Westerners during the time of Moses, the question asked was, 'Oh, are you the Son of God? Is this going to happen through you?' To fulfill this, God blessed by making a son with no claimed paternity into a prophet. Thus, leaving a people who refused to accept him—despite being given noble lineage, capability, and simplicity—to wander on an ignorant path for two thousand years, he was eventually placed in the womb of a carpenter's daughter, who had no profound knowledge, capability, or wisdom, and was born in a manger, transforming him into a prophet. Over here, however, even before that, Krishna was born among downtrodden humans, suited to the times. Over two thousand years later, since that did not take full effect, Buddha was born. He advised setting aside the conventional modes of worship practiced up to that point, suggesting that the *Panchashila* (Five Precepts) was sufficient, aiming to instill a greater sense of freedom. Since the authority of kings and other freedoms existed, it was accepted and practiced to some extent. But because the *Varnashrama Dharma* (the caste system), the *Trimurti* tradition, and a certain degree of Brahminical hegemony persisted, it became impossible to render the path—which operated on the ideology of religious freedom among ignorant people—completely pure and holy.

[GV1:P0499] During that period, this somewhat aged concept of freedom drove away the Guru tradition, firmly re-establishing the *Varnashrama* and the *Trimurti* system in its place. In such an era, with the intention of imparting a bit more knowledge to humanity, by the same divine fortune that brought forth Jesus, Buddha, and the Prophet, Shankaracharya was born in another place. In order to convince the people that their understanding was flawed, he spoke accordingly, established four *Mathas* (monasteries) to put this into practice, and founded *Advaita* (non-dualism) with the intent that a contemporary system of worship should emerge among you.

[GV1:P0500] By integrating this into the aforementioned Trimurti tradition, and deliberately withholding the philosophical insights of Vedanta, it was amalgamated with the Puranas and the Trimurti system, thereby institutionalizing absolute Vedic hegemony. Simultaneously, former Buddhist monasteries (*viharas*) were appropriated and transformed into Devi temples. Furthermore, they inculcated in these ignorant masses a depraved mode of worship characterized by animal sacrifice, arson, and abusive rituals.

[GV1:P0501] Consequently, *yakshis* (demi-goddesses) and ghouls once again became their deities. It was perhaps upon careful observation of these developments that the poet Kumaran Asan was prompted to remark that it was "fortified by the Smritis in the past." Although all the great spiritual luminaries articulate this, the process of rectification necessitates substituting an existing paradigm with a new one in order to dismantle the former; it is precisely this principle that figures like the Buddha, Sankaracharya, and Sree Narayana Guru elucidated through their teachings.

[GV1:P0502] However, the implementation of such rectification is obstructed by an impregnable systemic framework, firmly entrenched by the aforementioned political regimes and religious adherents. Prior to dismantling this structure, one must possess the requisite discernment to evaluate the stature and contributions of the preceptors who have emerged thus far. Naturally, such knowledge can only be gleaned through their foundational texts, followers, and direct disciples. Consequently, by appropriating every spiritual master who has appeared to date and obstinately asserting that salvation or liberation can exclusively be attained through their specific lineage, individuals become ensnared in an intellectual gymnasium; they unwittingly devolve into automated entities, ultimately succumbing to subjugation once more.
