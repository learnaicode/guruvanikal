[GV1:P0213] January 1978

[GV1:P0214] Question: To prevent that, how does one acquire and reach a state of flawless devotion by understanding the right aspects?

[GV1:P0215] Answer: When a person who earnestly desires fulfillment seeks it, there will be a sense of completion in whatever they reach. In that state, they will discover it themselves and accept such a Guru. If it is a devotion lacking this quality, the Bhagavata details the concept of twenty-four Gurus. Even at that level, one who reveres devotion will be able to attain the goal.

[GV1:P0216] Question: When you say that, does it not attract certain other flaws? Is this not the Kali Yuga? There are indeed certain defects in adopting such conditions. How can that be rectified?

[GV1:P0217] Answer: That is precisely why it was stated that in this era, the Guru must be someone who has attained perfection in devotion and is currently living.

[GV1:P0218] Question: What should be done if we lack the ability to visually perceive and act upon it?

[GV1:P0219] Answer: When one becomes a devotee, even without knowledge, an inquiring nature is inherent to him. In the course of such inquiry, one will find a person who is free from exaggeration, does not have a nature that forcefully attracts through magical feats, does not have the nature of soothing the eyes and pleasing the mind with costumes and ornaments, and lacks the knowledge that mesmerizes by reciting learned scholarship. Once such a person is found, one may feel satisfaction in him. When satisfaction is felt, we will naturally be compelled to spend some time there to get to know him better. From that, the skillful action of the wise one (*Jnani*) who behaves with a complete nature will occur in him and in those who desire like him, in a divine manner, even without the knowledge of the one called the wise one.

[GV1:P0220] Question: Even if it happens, aren't most people unaware of it?

[GV1:P0221] Answer: Yes, it happens so partly due to the deficiency in the course of their karma. Even so, by understanding it in that manner and trying somewhat, they will realize it as the truth and reach a path where they cannot find fault with it.

[GV1:P0222] Question: Suppose one is actually living with a Guru. What would be his characteristics and capabilities?

[GV1:P0223] Answer: First, the locals and those close to him will feel that there is a goodness in him greater than anything else. A spontaneous affection will be felt towards him without his knowledge. Secondly, if one observes his actions carefully, it will be evident that there is greater weight and depth in all his deeds. Thirdly, in his manner of interacting with people, even if he lacks formal education, he will habitually act with the knowledge characteristic of someone possessing education and wealth. One will mostly see from him actions that please a majority of people. Even if someone speaks disapprovingly, he will not stand to strongly oppose and argue with them. Or if he does oppose, it will only be to contradict for the sake of the matter at that moment and then move on.

[GV1:P0224] It is difficult to sustain any harsh, strong, or permanent opposition in him. Fourthly, actions imbued with empathy, kindness, and compassion, sweetness of words, humility upon seeing virtuous people, loving them more than one's own kin, not violating the words of the Guru, not feeling satisfied upon hearing words that disrespect Gurus, opposing and defeating such words if possible, being able to do good in that position, always pursuing the love of God in any sorrow, happiness, danger, or affection—thus, the knowledge to completely understand the Guru will be inherently vested in him.

[GV1:P0225] Now, the second type of devotee will only exhibit all these aforementioned qualities to half their extent. In certain matters, he will feel the need to prioritize his own actions. To celebrate and show off its merits, some mischievousness will emerge in him. Even if he has love for the Guru, he tries to highlight and maintain certain things. However, even if there is a feeling of complete love, there will be circumstances where he is unable to love completely.

[GV1:P0226] The third type of devotee will accept everything and continue to work. However, if there is a situation where he lacks freedom or if the Guru himself reprimands him, he will just walk away as if that were the final straw. When the fourth type acts, he will do everything well. He will not show stinginess in spending money or in other matters. If something he dislikes is said, he will constantly show an opposing nature in his actions. He will constantly display his greatness born of wealth and splendor. He will absolutely not have the nature of giving what is useful in his hand to others.

[GV1:P0227] If opposed, he will behave worse than a wicked person and will be inclined to destroy. Even if it is the Guru, he will push them into a dangerous zone. If necessary, it will even lead to destruction. It may also completely hinder the growth of the movement. Amidst all this, the Guru will continue to work with all of them in the same field. Primarily, according to the fortune of the Guru, there is a convention of having the most perfect disciple. With the attitude that there is no counter-word to a word, filled with sacrifice, humility, love, and richness in every action, it will seem to us as though a devotion entirely absorbed in God and the Guru overflows in him.

[GV1:P0228] His final stage will be seen as akin to the Guru or magnified tenfold. The world will make its judgment. It is the service of such individuals that benefits the world forever.

[GV1:P0229] Guruvani
