[GV1:P1931] April 1988

[GV1:P1932] A sannyasin achieves fruition through his diligence and sacrifice. This is something that not only Hindus but all human beings should know. When a person with such sacrifice, or someone belonging to a guru lineage, steps into a place, a house, or a compound, he feels that whatever needs to happen there, a scene involving his sacrifice should be created for them. Only a renunciate (thyagi) will feel that way. Others will not feel so. They will only try to extract something from wherever they go. In general, human understanding is like that. It is not so for a renunciate. He will not have any desire to earn something for himself or any desire other than the merit of his own soul. Having lived for a long time like this, taking many births, all these powers of penance will unite to attain supreme knowledge (jnana). It is after attaining this knowledge that one emerges as a Guru. When a wise person (jnani) is born, he is born knowing nothing about the path of wisdom. But once he grows up, he will have established a movement or something else.

[GV1:P1933] From there, the work begins again. It is this work that has been pre-ordained by the Divine for the welfare of the world. Such a worker, or his guru lineage, wherever they go, whether they walk on the street or anywhere else, will constantly observe how the houses and the land are situated. As a thinker or a writer walks from one end to another, he will grasp the pattern of that place. What does a sannyasin do when he becomes a jnani? He does not look at the pattern. How was the ancient period here? What have the people adopted as its medium? What is the character of this land? What has happened to the country? What is going to happen in the future? He contemplates all this. Then, if anything can be done there, he will do it through his divine resolve (sankalpa). Such gurus come only once in a long while. Such great souls arrive only when the land faces destruction or when the whole world is on the verge of ruin. They will continuously work from one end to the other. They will act both through people and independently.

[GV1:P1934] Five such gurus will come for a Chaturyuga (a cycle of four yugas). It is through such gurus that this world gains stability. If the nature of this work is not explained to the land, the home, and every individual, the welfare of the land will not endure. While they make a divine resolve, the actual action lies in the hands of the people in the world. The Guru will reform them in whatever way is necessary—by instilling a little fear, offering some love, using some willpower, and imparting some enthusiasm. What happens otherwise? The evil tendencies of the land will act and advance. That is why it is said that a country without virtuous people is ruined.

[GV1:P1935] Good people (Sadhus) means righteous people. It is from them that we and the country receive blessedness. If the knowledgeable ones among the contemporaries are acting as part of a guru lineage, their desire will be a prayer to the Almighty to make them work in a way that brings everlasting peace and harmony to that land and its people. Others are not like that. They will be acting only to show off their grandeur. The operational capacity based on grandeur will not last. However, because of faith, something might happen. But it will not endure. Just like the renunciate, those standing closest to him will also continue to work.

[GV1:P1936] When working, some people may incur certain faults. How this happens is that while they are working, either a word they dislike will be directed at them, or as they progress in their work, they might say something that hurts someone else. That word will have absolutely no connection to the knowledge they have spoken of until then. They might be speaking very profound words. It will seem as though they possess all the knowledge in this world. But in the very next moment, they might utter a word that hurts someone.

[GV1:P1937] Every worker must realize that everyone has a character flaw. God has given us an opportunity to eradicate this. The firm resolve that 'I will not waste this opportunity' should be undertaken willingly, just like a sacrifice. Those who do not like it cannot do it. I will resolve this character flaw of mine and thereby earn spiritual merit. One should not become insignificant after resolving it. If performed with the sense of dedication that I should be made to perform an action which earns merit and resolves the evils afflicting my home, my land, and my country, it will be completely eradicated in three years. What remains is that we can work for that goodness. After working for goodness, its greatness will manifest after death. Even the maternal ancestors will be purified.

[GV1:P1938] Guruvani
