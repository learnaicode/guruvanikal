[GV1:P0798] September 1981

[GV1:P0799] Birthday Message

[GV1:P0800] Disciple: What prompted you to take up ashram life?

[GV1:P0801] Guru: An inherent inclination that unknowingly existed before merely found an opportunity to manifest itself more clearly, that is all.

[GV1:P0802] Disciple: What was that opportunity?

[GV1:P0803] Guru: It was the ignorance within the household. What I mean by ignorance is—my stepfather, the absence of a father—because of that, we were at the place where my mother was married a second time. There, my stepfather would drink, and quarrels surrounding that were a regular occurrence. This was the cause for the motivation.

[GV1:P0804] Disciple: You considered this as an opportunity?

[GV1:P0805] Guru: Yes, this came as an opportunity.

[GV1:P0806] Disciple: After this opportunity arose, towards what did that initial inclination of yours turn?

[GV1:P0807] Guru: This inclination further sharpened the aspect of devotion. Thus, although the desire for an ashram life was within me for two years, since I was in an environment where I did not know how to go about it, I felt like going to Palani. For that purpose, I undertook vows and walked around with a garland around my neck like others do. After two years passed like this, everyone started asking why I was not going. I replied that I would go when the time comes, and that extending the duration of the vows would not cause any harm.

[GV1:P0808] Disciple: Was it after going to Palani that you reached this state?

[GV1:P0809] Guru: I did not go to Palani. I went to the ashram itself. One day, due to a quarrel with my stepfather, I spent that night in a bhajan mutt there. The next morning, I went to the guru who had taught me the alphabet and requested him to take me and enroll me in some ashram. He fulfilled that desire.

[GV1:P0810] Disciple: In which ashram did he enroll you?

[GV1:P0811] Guru: Initially, it was in Kalady. Since things were not right there, I came straight to Aluva and reached the Aluva Advaita Ashram.

[GV1:P0812] Disciple: What was your age at that time?

[GV1:P0813] Guru: Fourteen years old.

[GV1:P0814] Disciple: At that age, when you raised such a request to him, he took you straight to an ashram without saying anything else, didn't he!

[GV1:P0815] Guru: He already knew more information about me beforehand. It was with this understanding that he did this.

[GV1:P0816] Disciple: His name?

[GV1:P0817] Guru: Kumara Pillai.

[GV1:P0818] Disciple: Whose was the ashram where he first took you?

[GV1:P0819] Guru: Agamananda Swami's - Sri Ramakrishna Mission.

[GV1:P0820] Disciple: Was it with the intention of leading a monastic life that you started the ashram life even then?

[GV1:P0821] Guru: Certainly.

[GV1:P0822] Disciple: In that regard, I have a desire to know about each experiential realm of the life you have had since then, that is, about each experience of the monastic life.

[GV1:P0823] Guru: I came to the Aluva Advaita Ashram and spent two years there. After that, I was invited to Sivagiri. I came to Sivagiri and spent quite some time in the branches of Sivagiri. After spending four continuous years in Sivagiri itself, they sent me to various institutions. I also spent a considerable time taking charge of those institutions. Altogether, seventeen years were spent in Sivagiri and its affiliated branches.

[GV1:P0824] Disciple: In spiritual matters, there is a tradition of initiating one into the monastic life, that is, giving spiritual instruction (upadesha). Did you receive that from Sivagiri itself?

[GV1:P0825] Guru: No. There is such a practice in Sivagiri. They usually do it at the time of bestowing the saffron robes. Until then, many live there under the boundary of being a brahmachari. However, I had felt earlier that an instruction devoid of spiritual experience was unnecessary. Therefore, I did not receive instruction from them.

[GV1:P0826] Disciple: You deliberately refrained from receiving it.

[GV1:P0827] Guru: Yes.

[GV1:P0828] Disciple: Then, during which period and from whom did you receive the instruction?

[GV1:P0829] Guru: During my time spent at Sivagiri and its branches, while at Aruvippuram, I developed a strong, indescribable desire to find a guru possessing true experiential merit. While I was pondering how to attain this, a person named Vijayan (from Karinkulam), who was a disciple of a wandering ascetic known as Pathani Swami, spent a night with me. The next morning, saying, "You are quite good, I will show you a Swami," he took me along. We went towards the beach. Reaching the beach, we walked from Vallakkadavu to Poonthura. While returning and arriving amidst the thickets on the western side of Beemapally, he was there.

[GV1:P0830] He did not have the appearance I had imagined. A very large, dark figure. With a very long beard and wide, staring eyes, his physique evoked a formidable disposition. He was wearing a piece of a sack. It was getting close to dusk. Therefore, I did not approach him. When Vijayan went and sat to speak with him, I heard him say in Tamil that he did not know me. Saying "Alright, let it be morning," he let it go. That night, we slept at Beemapally. The next day, very early in the morning, while I was bathing, he came down that way. Seeing me walking like that, he called out and imparted the instruction to me right there and then.

[GV1:P0831] Disciple: You mentioned that you received the instruction; within how much time after that did you have the spiritual experience?

[GV1:P0832] Guru: It was two and a half years after receiving the instruction that this realization occurred. Prior to that, I happened to receive a vision. In a very clear manner.

[GV1:P0833] Disciple: What was thatٹ

[GV1:P0834] Guru: That was—during the time I resided at Aruvippuram, there was an occasion when I was mentally and physically exhausted. The mental exhaustion was partly due to certain difficulties from the administration. Another was the distress of not attaining this, of seeking for the sake of spiritual matters and not attaining it; the body wearing out with daily fever. Waking up at four o'clock every day was a routine. One day when I woke up, I felt dizzy and fell, hitting the wall. It was six o'clock when I managed to get up from there.

[GV1:P0835] That day, there was such an exhaustion of the mind. The body gave out too. And the purpose for which I came was not fulfilled. What would this life be? Thinking like this, I felt that I did not come desiring any of this. If the body goes, let it go. What is there to think more about it? When I felt that way, a sort of courage came over me, and I quickly performed the daily routines. There is a full-length portrait of Narayana Guru Swami in a room of that southern building. After sitting there and performing prayers and pujas, I immediately felt that I should go to the place where he meditated. Going there, I made a firm resolve that day to climb this hill for forty-one days and see what happens, and thus I kept going for forty-one days. On the fortieth day, upon reaching there and looking up from the rock, I suddenly saw a halo, much like the halo of the moon. And within it, a face. That face was unfamiliar to me. Since I was unfamiliar with what it was, a sudden shudder went through my body. And I climbed down.

[GV1:P0836] That evening, I went to Neyyattinkara to fetch oil for the temple. While there, another acquaintance invited me into a coffee shop. As I was drinking coffee, I looked up and saw a portrait of the very figure I had seen earlier. Upon inquiring whose portrait it was, I was told that it was Rishi Sai Baba. I resolved then that if I ever were to build a humble dwelling, I would enshrine this portrait there first.

[GV1:P0837] My experience with my Guru was thus—without my speaking another word, and without his saying anything further, I received his spiritual initiation.

[GV1:P0838] Disciple: I fail to comprehend that. What does it mean to receive initiation without either party exchanging a word?

[GV1:P0839] Guru: The purpose of my visit was stated in a single word. He also posed a question in a few words: 'For what purpose have you come?' I replied, 'For whatever may bring peace to my soul.' These were the only words exchanged. Embracing me with tears, he declared, 'You are the rightful heir.' He then wrote down the initiation. Without uttering another syllable, he instructed, 'Very well, take this and continue to utilize it in this manner.' They were two mantras. 'Within three months of utilizing this mantra, two signs will manifest. They will appear in a dream. You must return here then.' Accordingly, the signs appeared, and I returned. The Guru stated that everything was present, yet there would be a considerable delay. It was only after approximately two and a half years that a continuous series of experiences commenced.

[GV1:P0840] Disciple: Could you perhaps elaborate on those experiences in a manner that we might comprehend?

[GV1:P0841] Guru: Merely recounting the experience to you will not render it comprehensible. This experience entails the manifestation before us of a light or form that we had not anticipated. It must not be a projection of our intentions. That is its underlying truth. If it were anticipated, would it not merely be a materialization aligned with one's own thoughts?

[GV1:P0842] Disciple: Indeed, it would imply that it arose in accordance with one's imagination.

[GV1:P0843] Guru: Is it not correct to say it manifested from his imagination? Did he not possess such an imagination? He certainly did—regarding God,ٹ he will only receive what is conducive to his mental transformation at that particular moment. However, that is not the case. From its primordial form, it must manifest beginning from whatever he initially experienced, in accordance with the evolution of the soul that has been instrumental throughout his successive incarnations.

[GV1:P0844] Disciple: It must emerge from the earliest stage, that is, from the very inception of the soul.

[GV1:P0845] Guru: Affirmative. It must manifest in accordance with the meritorious deeds of the soul's primal incarnation. Therefore, it ought not to manifest in this manner. It should not appear in the form currently venerated, nor in forms that have passed, nor in those anticipated in the future; rather, it dawns within him in accordance with the destiny of that specific incarnation. It is utterly impossible to articulate this in words. One can only rely upon what has been seen and heard.

[GV1:P0846] Disciple: Indeed.

[GV1:P0847] Guru: Consequently, what will you comprehend from this? You shall comprehend nothing. I might claim to have seen Shiva, to have seen Vishnu, to have witnessed myriad other entities, to have beheld the entirety of nature, and to have observed numerous forms that have manifested within nature unto this day. However, none of this will foster any true comprehension. Merely uttering the words jaggery, sugar, clarified butter, and honey, and attempting to taste them—will that yield any sweetness?

[GV1:P0848] Disciple: No.

[GV1:P0849] Guru: Even licking the written words will impart no sweetness. It must be properly cultivated and refined. Such refinement can only be achieved by each individual themselves. It cannot be cultivated through any other means whatsoever.

[GV1:P0850] Disciple: We earnestly desire to hear through which stages and by overcoming what obstacles you have attained your current state, presented in a manner that provides us with an understanding of the unique characteristics inherent to each realm of these experiences.

[GV1:P0851] Guru: As I have previously recounted to you, a form of which I had neither heard nor possessed prior knowledge manifested before me. Prior to that, for seventeen years, I offered worship at the samadhi of Narayana Guru and at the site of his consecration. I derived no such spiritual experience from that practice. That was not what materialized before my vision. If you inquire as to how this occurred, what am I to say? Even if I were to explain, it would invariably appear as a paradox.

[GV1:P0852] Disciple: Indeed, many individuals who visit here genuinely report such experiences. The populace generally perceives it as deeply wondrous when these individuals claim to have beheld Shiva, or to have witnessed other divine forms. Conversely, however, their entire lives frequently appear to be an accumulation of profound sorrows. We are eager to comprehend the underlying reasons and dynamics behind this paradox.

[GV1:P0853] Guru: Let us address that matter. I have stated previously that the deities, ascetics, and sages depicted in our Puranas today, as well as the adherents of the four subsequent paths—namely Karma, Yoga, Jnana, and Bhakti—have manifested in accordance with their past innate tendencies. Nevertheless, they have recorded that they beheld Shiva, or their accounts align with the words conveyed by the authors of the Puranas in accordance with our Hindu Dharma. We have had the occasion to observe this phenomenon here as well. Do we not hear individuals here, ranging from young children to those fifty years of age, claiming to have seen various specific divine figures?

[GV1:P0854] Disciple: Indeed.

[GV1:P0855] Guru: All of this must be witnessed. It is indeed necessary to behold such things. However, it is incongruous with the present era.

[GV1:P0856] Disciple: Thus, you have elucidated the nature of these experiences, noting that they manifest in specific individuals. Yet, following this explanation, you declared that they are unsuited for the current epoch. However, is it not within this very epoch that children and individuals of all ages are experiencing these phenomena?

[GV1:P0857] Guru: Affirmative.

[GV1:P0858] Disciple: What, then, is meant to transpire in this present epoch? What are its defining characteristics? How ought one to approach it? We possess an earnest desire to comprehend this as well.

[GV1:P0859] Guru: The culmination of a Chaturyuga is constituted by four distinct Yugas.

[GV1:P0860] Disciple: So the scriptures decree.

[GV1:P0861] Guru: Herein, we can observe this progression through the lineage of Manu. This framework serves to distinguish the epochs of the deities from those of mankind. The eras dominated by the deities are the Treta Yuga and the Dvapara Yuga. As the Dvapara Yuga approached its twilight, Krishna emerged as the venerable figure who unequivocally demonstrated a more complete manifestation of human liberty. It is evident that upon his advent, Krishna accomplished numerous feats demanding far greater contemplation than those of the deities who had presided up to that juncture.

[GV1:P0862] Disciple: Indeed.

[GV1:P0863] Guru: Among those clearly discernible feats, the most significant pertains to the profound trepidation experienced by Arjuna, and the subsequent eradication of that very fear.

[GV1:P0864] Disciple: Indeed.

[GV1:P0865] Guru: Until then, Arjuna held the belief that the deities, ascetics, and sages as per the scriptures, as well as the associated gurus and uncles who were their followers, were all to be respected. Arjuna had within him something capable of removing all of this in an instant to reveal his inherent nature, yet he remained in a state where he could not understand or act upon it. The merit of a guru lies in correcting this. History says that Arjuna was with Krishna for about fifty years. Yet, it is said that Arjuna still doubted Krishna and questioned him again. Finally, sitting on that battlefield, he asked - "Do you believe me?" "I certainly believe you," said Arjuna. "Then why are you not doing what I say?" "I will not do such things. It is not in accordance with Dharma." - How can one say "I believe you" and "it is not in accordance with Dharma" at the same time? "I certainly believe you. But citing this moral condition, it is not right to kill uncles, others, and gurus. Therefore, I will not do it," he said.

[GV1:P0866] "Let that be, you know me." "I know." "Why did I come? See why you have come now." This means he is speaking of his wisdom. We call it vision (Darshan) here. Arjuna could also receive it, just like these children receive it. Sitting there, he looked. He showed that he had come to reclaim the state of this era. "Alright, do you believe that?" "I believe."

[GV1:P0867] "Alright, then we must see in what all stages we both have taken this human birth since the beginning." He looked - and then within half an hour, he received a detailed understanding of why they had come in all stages, how they had lived together, whether they had a guru-disciple relationship, etc. Only after that did he stand up without questioning, immediately prostrate at his feet, take up his bow and arrows, and kill everyone. This is the incident, isn't it? That is a proof.

[GV1:P0868] Prior to that, no guru who had come until then had ever orchestrated an event in this world where uncles and gurus were killed in this manner. No one other than Krishna was there to prove this as a first step to demonstrate that this is a complete epoch of man. After that, there is a part where Krishna himself says - "Kali has begun. It is no longer our time" - which part is that?

[GV1:P0869] Disciple: It is said that after the war, in connection with the ascension to heaven, it is the period to strive for liberation (Moksha). That is the occasion.

[GV1:P0870] Guru: There was an occasion back then when Arjuna lost in a battle. Which one is that?

[GV1:P0871] Disciple: It is Vyasa who speaks more about that. After Krishna's ascension to heaven, asuras came to Dwaraka and started abducting the women. Arjuna came to protect them. But Arjuna found himself so devoid of strength that he could not even lift his bow and arrows. He had to stand there unable to fight. And the asuras abducted all those women.

[GV1:P0872] On this occasion, when Arjuna prepares to commit suicide with the intention of ending his life, Vyasa advises him. This is now the era to seek liberation. At the beginning of Kali Yuga, the devas (deities) have no power. He says that this happened not because of your incompetence, but because if you use these weapons acquired from them, it is a time when they have no power, and therefore the effect of those mantras has also been lost. He also tells him to strive for liberation.

[GV1:P0873] Guru: Alright, so that is clear to you. The time has come for man to strive for liberation. The Kali Yuga is for humans. It is understood that even if a man becomes a deva, he has no power. So, the meaning is that one must progress from there. What do we get from these words? That the position of a human-god is not enough, isn't it?

[GV1:P0874] Disciple: Yes.

[GV1:P0875] Guru: Even today, we worship deities. Our Puranas have been fabricated in such a way that we worship not only gods and goddesses but even spirits like Madan, and our Vedic scholars taught us to continue the accompanying traditions and exactly what those predecessors showed us. Only they should perform Vaishnava pooja. Another person can perform Shaktheya pooja. Yet another can do Shaiva pooja. Thus, some people mix everything together. If you keep doing things in this manner, where will you achieve anything in this era? These are some matters that you have missed without realizing. That is why it was said that there is no benefit in this during this era. There will be no benefit from the devas.

[GV1:P0876] Disciple: But still, today it is universally practiced with deities, and people believe there are some minor results, and they make some offerings accordingly, don't they? That is the prevailing system of worship today, isn't it?

[GV1:P0877] Guru: Yes, all that will be there. I didn't say it wouldn't be there. But what is the use of this for you, what benefit does your soul get from this?

[GV1:P0878] Disciple: That is correct. People universally know that this is an era where one must strive for liberation. In a way, chanting names (Namasmarana) is enough. It is said that this is the era where chanting names brings liberation. But how will these people, who do not know what liberation is or where to find the paths for it, strive for it today? In this era, whose name will bring liberation? For liberation to be attained through chanting names, liberation is conceived or implied as the soul merging into Brahman, isn't it?

[GV1:P0879] Guru: Yes.

[GV1:P0880] Disciple: So if that is the case, it can be said that the desired subject is purely a spiritual matter.

[GV1:P0881] Guru: Yes.

[GV1:P0882] Disciple: Then, it is not recorded anywhere in the Puranas that anyone received this from the devas anywhere.

[GV1:P0883] Guru: No.

[GV1:P0884] Disciple: Moreover, when the devas themselves were asked about this subject, they too said to seek a Guru for it.

[GV1:P0885] Guru: Yes.

[GV1:P0886] Disciple: So an appropriate Guru is required for that today. Then what is the way for these people to attain this through such a Guru? How should one search for it? Shouldn't there be such a Guru?

[GV1:P0887] Guru: Yes, we have received a notification about that here. That is, through celestial voices (Ashariri) and otherwise. So such words are proof for us. We can take that as a foundation stone. From the time of Krishna, a different nature has emerged. Krishna is the last guru of Dvapara Yuga. In our experience, we received all visions in the nature of Darshan from that Krishna himself, and experienced all the difficulties from this. It is hard to express the difficulty of that experience. In short, all the gods and goddesses, yogis, sages, and ascetics till date appear in their vision and instruct them like this. They will indeed speak the truthful words. After moving forward like this for a while, their words will falter. We won't experience that word.

[GV1:P0888] Disciple: The words spoken by these devas and sages?

[GV1:P0889] Guru: Yes, even we do not know why that is. We searched but could not grasp it. Such experiences happened for about seven and a half years, and various kinds of changes occurred. Each of these deities would come and say something. It would be correct at first. Then it would not be correct. Like that, it changed rapidly. All kinds of sages have come.

[GV1:P0890] Disciple: You mentioned that the preceptors of all religions and all sages have come, haven't you?

[GV1:P0891] Guru: Yes.

[GV1:P0892] Disciple: Will all the great sages come like that?

[GV1:P0893] Guru: They will come.

[GV1:P0894] Disciple: If they come?

[GV1:P0895] Guru: When they come, they give their vision; others will tell some things.

[GV1:P0896] Disciple: The preceptors of all religions - Christ, Prophet, Guru Nanak - won't all those who are mentioned like this come?

[GV1:P0897] Guru: Everyone will come, no one will be left out. Their souls will come and shed tears for this - that is out of joy - and they will instruct us. They will say this by calling out, "Children."

[GV1:P0898] Disciple: It was said that all the religious teachers would come and instruct us. What will they say? What will they ask?

[GV1:P0899] Guru: When these religious teachers arrive, they will display some joy. While displaying that joy, they will ask through the vision they receive, "Do you have anything to give us? Tell us what we should do." Then they themselves will tell us: "You do not have much to gain from us. All of this is already embodied in your Guru. You cannot demand and receive anything from us that is not available from that Guru. We shall impart certain things to you. You must not deviate from them. You only need to follow this specific discipline, without violating that word, the word of the Guru. You will attain everything from it" — this is what was instructed then.

[GV1:P0900] Disciple: If it is stated that all the religious teachers have come and they have nothing specific of their own to offer, and that everything is encompassed within your Guru, it seems this mission is intended for the emergence of a universal religion. What do you have to say regarding this?

[GV1:P0901] Guru: There is no reason to doubt that at all. Because, apart from what these teachers stated, everything we have received since then up to this day has already been proven.

[GV1:P0902] Disciple: One more thing, the teachers who have arrived are individuals who founded respective religions.

[GV1:P0903] Guru: Yes.

[GV1:P0904] Disciple: However, the spiritual teachers who have come to the world are exceedingly numerous. Why is it that, instead of all of them, only a very few individuals are present here in a manner worthy of being mentioned by name?

[GV1:P0905] Guru: That depends on the era. Krishna arrived during a specific epoch. He appeared in the final phase of the third Yuga within a Chaturyuga cycle. A transitional stage where another Yuga was commencing. That is, Krishna appeared on a path that specifically proved through God himself that freedom had been attained by humanity. Following that period, most of those who have subsequently arrived on this path are Gurus themselves; they are referred to in two different terms as either prophets or Gurus. Apart from that, everything is primarily attributed not as being spoken by deities, but entirely by the Gurus. For instance, it was these great sages (Rishis) who discovered and wove the garments we use today, and who formulated the medicines for our treatment when we are afflicted by illness.

[GV1:P0906] Disciple: It is exactly like that.

[GV1:P0907] Guru: That is what can be observed through history. Therefore, it becomes evident that these were imparted to them. Whatever was required for human utilization during this era was received from God. However, they conveyed it while keeping it concealed. That might be due to the magnitude of their erudition or similar factors. It is also possible that they received it with the instruction that it should only be expressed in such a manner. Otherwise, they would not have done so. They have never been questioned as to why they did it this way.

[GV1:P0908] They say that upon seeing a plant, one can ask it, "Who are you?" Or it will proactively say, "I am suitable for this purpose, you may take me for it." Thus, it is clear that such knowledge was divinely communicated to them. Otherwise, how could they extensively document so many medicinal remedies? How did figures like Bhogar's twelve thousand or seven thousand even emerge? It is impossible to merely discover them. By the time one discovers a medicine and determines its exact weight, dosage, and properties, an entire lifetime would pass. This was not the case; the knowledge of these medicines was systematically imparted to them. That is exactly what they have documented. Otherwise, it would have been impossible to compose and compile so many verses (Shlokas). It is acknowledged that Agastya, Bhogar, and several other profound masters received knowledge in this manner. Their traditions continue on that basis.

[GV1:P0909] However, many great souls have appeared even before this to elevate the world to a certain level. As evidence of this, the most significant among the lineage presented to us are the Manus and the lineage of the Manus. But, apart from their names being briefly mentioned in the Puranas, it is not entirely clear what the Manus accomplished. The reason for this lack of clarity is that we have received a revelation here indicating that a mistake occurred on their part in some manner. That mistake is ego (Ahamkara). The reason for this is that today's ascetics (Sannyasins) use a phrase — 'Aham Brahmasmi' — meaning 'I am Brahman'. Today, almost all of us use that Vedantic phrase. We too develop the egoistic notion that everything belongs to the mind, and everything is driven by the mind. Do we not?

[GV1:P0910] Disciple: Yes, we do.

[GV1:P0911] Guru: The scholars state, "O man, it is your mind." If the ego takes possession of that mind, would not this entire state go awry? This originates from the Brahman. I have merely become an instrument for that divine resolve. I have been blessed with a fortune in it. One can receive this only by remaining in the humble realization, "I must bow down and perceive it." Otherwise, apart from repeatedly chanting the absolute phrase "I am Brahman", one will never truly receive the divine words.

[GV1:P0912] Disciple: Yes.

[GV1:P0913] Guru: Among those who are referred to as ascetics, they talk about this wholeness, saying things like it is the great absolute, the nectar of existence-consciousness-bliss (Satchidananda), and so forth. However, it must be understood that they are speaking without having any profound knowledge about it. Because they can only record the words received from the Brahman in a manner suitable for use across different eras. The reason this wisdom resided only in a select few masters like Vasishtha and Vyasa is because they were entirely devoid of ego. It was received exclusively by those who stood without ego, in a state of humble supplication, thinking, 'I do not know anything; whatever is bestowed upon me, I shall faithfully execute.'

[GV1:P0914] Disciple: Has it not been received by those who are otherwise?

[GV1:P0915] Guru: No. Nor can it be obtained merely by speaking. Similarly, from the knowledge we have received, we can understand that in the lineage of the Manus as well, they were afflicted by some form of ego. Thus, all the great souls who have appeared to date must have made some errors at one point or another. It is among the mistakes that occur due to their intellect or their close familiarities. It has happened to everyone. No one can escape without committing that error. After all, they were born as human beings, were they not? Therefore, if they impart words as their own without deeply beseeching for them, such errors will inherently creep in. Hence, not a single word of one's own should be employed in this matter.

[GV1:P0916] Disciple: In this specific matter?

[GV1:P0917] Guru: It must not be used in this matter. One should not display their intellect or power here. If an individual displays anything as their own, then their true self no longer exists. Everything should be submitted with the attitude: 'We shall perform it in accordance with the resolve of the Brahman. Whatever is Your resolve, that alone is sufficient; there is nothing of my own.'

[GV1:P0918] Disciple: Absolute humility.

[GV1:P0919] Guru: Precisely that. Otherwise, if one proclaims 'I am here', then that divine essence is lost. That is exactly what another Vedanta states—

[GV1:P0920] "Whoever keeps asserting 'I, I'—"

[GV1:P0921] "If sought within, it is not many, but becomes One."

[GV1:P0922] At the same time, something else is mentioned in the '*Harinaamakeerthanam*':
