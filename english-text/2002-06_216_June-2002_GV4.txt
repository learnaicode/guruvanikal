[GV4:P0045] June 2002

[GV4:P0046] To what shall we surrender and pray? We have passed down certain knowledge that came and went millions of years ago. At the same time, prophetic traditions, as well as Sikh, Buddhist, and Jain traditions, have emerged in such a way that it is impossible to know whether what has been passed down is right or wrong. All of these have been spoken in various languages in different parts of India. However, most people in India possess the experience of having worked steadfastly within the Trimurti tradition. Among these, they have also lived according to the doctrines brought by people of other religions. When each of these doctrines presents its own perspective, we may also feel that there is truth in it.

[GV4:P0047] We must understand one thing. How will the karma performed in the Treta Yuga or Dwapara Yuga be useful in the Kali Yuga? If there is a transitional nature today that is shifting towards the Satya Yuga, how can this karma be adapted for the Satya Yuga? India has more experience with this transitional nature of epochs. Although it exists to some extent in other lands, they articulate it in a different manner. There are doctrines in Western and Arabian countries that are propounded in various ways. However, none of these should be taken as exemplary in the Kali Yuga. Why? The Prophet's doctrine is one whose time has not yet ended. It can be carried forward for another ninety years. Yet, within this period, conflicts and quarrels capable of disintegrating it are taking place within it.

[GV4:P0048] Similarly, in the case of Hinduism, the knowledge of a transmission that has been passed down for many years is invoking a God who has been divided for caste and religion in various ways. We possess the ignorance of thinking that by speaking thus of caste and religion, only the God who fits within them is supreme. To remove this ignorance, many great souls came independently even in this Kali Yuga. The prophetic religions that came in the Kali Yuga arrived in such a manner. But we cannot recognize any of this. Therefore, we have proceeded with the attitude that everything is acceptable and good. However, we cannot fault any of this. This is because we have seen karmas that were performed appropriately in their respective times and yielded results.

[GV4:P0049] Are these results deserving of the liberation of our soul? Even if we agree that everything else is correct, all of these have the shortcoming that none of them is a karma entitled to grant liberation. Should we not then develop an independent mindset that claims the right to the liberation of the soul and allows each of us to become a part of the merit-acquiring karma?

[GV4:P0050] We are those who live here for a short time without knowing anything at all and then depart. In the meantime, people manifest in various ways—as those who live as fathers, mothers, children, wives, and husbands, as those who live by embracing asceticism, or, if none of these, as those who live desiring the worship of God. But we are merely unable to know what flaws have occurred in all of this. These karmas are as old as we are, making it impossible to know them.

[GV4:P0051] It has been more than five thousand years since Kali Yuga began. We, who cannot even study the history of these five thousand years, will never be able to study all the Yugadharmas that occurred previously and adopt a model of Yugadharma. Therefore, let us just know what we need to know concerning our lives. If we move on to other matters, we will have to learn a lot of stories and historical wealth. Even after knowing all this, the things necessary for our present existence are not going to yield from them. Or, even if they do yield, we will also bend in accordance with how we try to believe, love, and put it into practice in the coming times. Other than this, we are not going to get any opportunity from such matters to specifically say, know, or think anything about the destruction that is going to befall our lineage.

[GV4:P0052] People might tell us about the great merits they achieved in times past. Whatever that may be, we must be convinced that there is a great blunder before us. Who are we in the karma spoken of by the many thousands of Gurus and more than twenty-five prophets who have come and gone? How will we theorize all these? How much have we been able to accomplish in the same measure as the karma they performed in their times? If we did accomplish it, what did we transform it into? To what extent were we able to merge into it?

[GV4:P0053] If we can ponder this slowly and adopt it over time, the first thing we must understand is that we have a flaw. What is this flaw? In what era did it occur? How did it come about? We stand having been born in an era where these things can be clearly known, and with the karmic capacity to find a solution for it. Having understood this, can it be taken up as a karma; will that dharma be attained through that experience; can this dharma be undertaken in a way that is free from karmic blemish? This is what you should think about. Otherwise, if you go about listening to, thinking about, and saying many things, you will have no foundation in anything. You do not know how to retrieve and preserve anything. Nor do you know what karma is to be preserved the most.

[GV4:P0054] What needs to be preserved the most is our character in life. What has mostly accumulated in life are entanglements. We must see that those entanglements are karmic attributes clinging to our soul. Within that karmic attribute itself, this karma can be prepared and contained. If that karmic attribute is made to repent, what are the good things that can come to us? You should at least have an idea to know this much. Only then will you feel that this is worth taking up. And that too, without rejecting anything.

[GV4:P0055] The guidance that has reached the karmic path of the human world up to this day must have surely reached the extent of the karmic attribute of this entanglement. If there is no doubt about it, one only needs to look at what that aspect is. That is the Shaiva, Shakta, and Vaishnava traditions. For the Shakta, that is supreme; for the Shaiva, that is supreme; for the Vaishnava, that is supreme. Besides, there are thousands of its branches. In this special dharma that we have been practicing, seeing, knowing, and speaking of in these traditions, how much merit-acquiring karma have we been able to perform? It has been lost in such a way that our families, our countrymen, let alone the Vedic religion or any notable prophetic religion, are unable to perform this dharma. If asked how this loss occurred, they will only evade the question.

[GV4:P0056] We can only live in accordance with the dharma and karma of the parents we got. Otherwise, what else can we do but get caught in some karma that the land and its people characterize as good or bad, and take on and foster that karmic blemish. We have a history of karmic blemishes that cannot be exhausted no matter how much is said. But that is not our resolve, is it?

[GV4:P0057] What is it that we require? What constitutes the realm that falls within the divine ordinance? How can we assume this responsibility? The human world requires a disposition that comprehends this. We cannot progress by exhaustively studying the system of the Trinity and the Manu system that prevailed for numerous epochs prior to it. There is only one core subject for this. If one fails to comprehend it, it remains a subject prone to the gravest errors. In what manner have the great souls of the past demonstrated this? One might have heard of deeds encompassing certain extraordinary mystical powers. The common populace cannot achieve anything by merely relying on these mystical abilities. For example, suppose an individual ascended to heaven in their physical body. What do we gain from such an occurrence? Would we derive any tangible benefit by merely recounting this narrative and offering our reverence?

[GV4:P0058] This is what we must comprehend: during our lifetime, we must discern the virtues and vices of bygone eras. We must understand the reasons why these virtues and vices have become a burden upon us. To realize this, we must trace our steps backward. Having been born absorbed in the karmic legacy that our ancestors revered and worshipped, we cannot arbitrarily discard it or abruptly transform it into something entirely different. However, we can deduce one fundamental truth. The Divine cannot manifest in a paradigm that assigns a separate deity to each caste. Do men and women have distinct gods?

[GV4:P0059] We remain ignorant of how the karmic force, which has purportedly manifested as distinct deities for men, women, and various castes, is sustained. Consequently, our entire understanding is restricted to a lineage that chased miracles and exhibited extraordinary mystical powers. We have not comprehended these mystical powers as a systematic discipline of knowledge. Instead, we have misconstrued them as a distinct form of spiritual wisdom. If an individual resides somewhere and offers solace to a few people, society immediately questions whether he is a mystic. We have erroneously equated the term mystic with an enlightened being. We remain completely unaware of the fundamental truth that a mystic is not necessarily an enlightened being.

[GV4:P0060] What is the karmic course that astonishes us by performing Jinn worship and displaying the frenzy of various devotional spectacles, or the karmic course of a Brahmarakshas yearning to merge with Brahman? There is a story told regarding this Brahmarakshas. A Yaksha summoned gods like Agni, Vayu, and Varuna and placed a blade of grass before them. But these gods could not stir or move it. Agni could not burn it, nor could Vayu blow it away. What can we understand about this blade of grass? We evaluate this miracle from within the confines of a karmic potential that has been subjected to the passage of time in mythology. What is the meaning in saying, "I do not respect this"? There is a fact that this was once revered. Do we disrespect it because it does not exist, or because we have too much knowledge, or because we lack knowledge? Therefore, we must comprehend a matter in accordance with our knowledge. Otherwise, you will face great ruin. Let this be understood first.

[GV4:P0061] What constitutes your knowledge? "Yesterday, I went to such-and-such places. Today as well, I went. Now I am returning." Expressed differently, "I went, came back exhausted, lay down, and slept well." How does one know that he slept? Sleeping is a transformation of the life force. The individual himself knows the state of sleeping well. However, what is the cause? We require the karmic capacity to recover and understand what the cause is. If what is seen in the waking state appears in the dream state, and what is seen in the dream state appears in deep sleep, there is a mechanism that grants liberation to the deep sleep state. The term deep sleep is generally understood as a state of sleeping without knowing anything. However, that is not the case. There is a dimension where one sees without physical eyes during deep sleep. So, which is this eye that sees? It is that which perceives within the core of the mind. That is the karmic course we cultivate. How will that dharma reach us?

[GV4:P0062] In the nature of this mind's eye, the karmic course of billions of years is being dragged before us. That was never done by me. It was not done by my ancestors. Then who did this? What we observe here is a karma that remained hidden behind some flawed action. If you do not completely understand this, you will find it difficult to grasp. What you assume is that if you invoke God, you must become wealthy tomorrow. Or you must become the greatest ruler. Or you must attain happiness by roaming freely and living as you please without any illness. Which of these is good? All are indeed good. But you must understand one thing. We possess a certain familiarity that our ancestors believed, knew, and communicated. We must realize where this will lead us. How does this manifest before us in the waking, dream, and deep sleep states? We have been made an instrument for this.

[GV4:P0063] We are gradually observing and comprehending what ripens in the waking, dream, and deep sleep states one by one. What is this waking, dream, and deep sleep? In the waking state, the eyes may be closed, or they may be open. Whether the eyes are closed or open, there is a nature of seeing. Even while sleeping, that special attribute does not leave us. Furthermore, one perceives while remaining in a silent, motionless state where the nature of space and time is indiscernible. This is another facet of deep sleep. It is difficult to comprehend how this comes to us. If one begins to count its seeds, another problem arises. Did the seed come first or the mango? What answer can be given to this? By standing on a sense of dharma that rationalizes certain things which can be dismissed through logic by asking such questions, who can we become? We will become no one.

[GV4:P0064] If you accept a title such as Guru, Deva, Rishi, or Sanyasi, you must understand one thing. What did we gain from the actions of about twenty-five prophets of the past? Similarly, what will you gain by hearing, knowing, and reading about sages who have come and gone across epochs, and about a Guru who emerged as the controller of miraculous deeds by displaying numerous mystic powers and miracles? That is not it; what you need is a center of faith that grasps these past occurrences. What will you observe in this karma that stands capable, having seen and grasped the actions of various epochs? This is what must be seen in the waking, dream, and deep sleep states. When it thus occurs and is experienced in the waking, dream, and deep sleep states, one must be able to recognize what it is. There are some individuals here who are capable of knowing this. So, what should we do? We must attain the karmic capability to grasp the cause, through which we became eligible to attain it. That cause, which guides us to retrieve both the good and the bad, can only instruct us on the capability to attain this path. That must be instructed in accordance with each individual's knowledge. Otherwise, one might be bewildered, assuming that one's own actions are entirely perfect, and love may manifest as a reflection of ego, resulting in nothing being accomplished. The love of God is not this. It is a karmic course that traverses thousands of paths that we have crossed, walked, known, and acted upon. It cannot be acquired merely by hearing, knowing, or speaking about it. It only guides you by explaining that what you see and hear is precisely this, in accordance with your understanding. Before a Guru who imparts such teachings, everything—all the enlightened beings, yogis, gurus, and prophets from all the traditions that have existed hitherto—must assemble. You should only accept such a person as your Guru. Otherwise, you will commit a grave error. You will fall to the ground like someone who loses their grip. How should you perform your karma by incorporating life, having understood at least this much? You will not incur any harm merely because you did not contemplate on God. However, you will not receive its benefits either.

[GV4:P0065] What is meant by faith in God? The ancestral curse of many predecessors, who waited for prosperity and wealth by making vows and offerings, building numerous pavilions, and constructing grand temples, remains within us. How can this curse be removed? You need not wander aimlessly for any of this. No matter where you wander, it will not work. What you need is a Guru who is capable of liberating everything that you and your ancestors have previously worshipped. Whether one is a prophet, or someone who has demonstrated brilliance by recounting various legendary riches while standing in the path of the prophets, or someone who has advanced far in mystical practices and attained the power of levitation, all of them must come before this Guru for the sake of their lives. Unless you surrender and pray to such a Guru, you will not gain any special benefit.
