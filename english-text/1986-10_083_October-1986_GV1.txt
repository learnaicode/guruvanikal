[GV1:P1646] October 1986

[GV1:P1647] Today, the actions we perform concerning the concept of God are typically conceptualized through various forms and attributes. However, some individuals differ, often perceiving this in an illusory manner. Others conceptualize the divine nature as absolute nothingness, without any specific objective, yet omnipresent and pervading everything. Humanity was created by endowing it with form, expression, and action. Consequently, a person's conceptualizations and emotional expressions develop in alignment with their mental maturation. Through such conceptual frameworks, great souls have emerged and departed in accordance with the *Yuga Dharma* (the duty of the epoch). It is customary for us to regard the extraordinary power associated with their concepts as the very concept of God. This subsequently transitions into a form of worship. Initially, we lacked the capacity to worship or even conceive of such a phenomenon. There existed such an era. After all, in the beginning, humanity experienced a primitive state, incapable of cooking food or wearing clothes. Later, through the progressive development of the human intellect, and by imparting expression, form, and love, those actions were cultivated. The spiritual power of such souls has flourished and exists today. This is precisely how every object we currently utilize was conceptualized. Following this conceptualization, humanity learned the appropriate and inappropriate applications of these creations. Thus, through the continuous discernment of the good, we comprehended the nature of life's actions.

[GV1:P1648] Since today is the atomic age, you can understand this in every way. Today, there is a course of action to examine and know what exists on earth, in the sky, and in other celestial bodies. We happened to be born, grow up, and experience this in such an era. If we had lived ten or two hundred years ago, we would not have been able to use or even imagine these motor services and other vehicles that we see today and use daily. Similarly, electricity, as seen today, could not have been conceived in those times. Today, so many things have been invented for human comfort. Whether sitting at home or anywhere else, events happening in every corner of the world are visualized and seen in the same form, expression, and emotion as if they were happening right here. This is the merit of our times. This is *Kali*. Therefore, it is impossible to describe what can be achieved when the *Kali Yuga* progresses further. This demonstrates the magnitude of human invention. At the same time, it was somewhat similar in the ancient times of the Devas, only their methods were different. It is said that they chanted mantras to release weapons and suspended things in the atmosphere through sheer willpower. Such things have occurred since time immemorial. However, we should not assume that this happened to everyone at all times. Back then, things could only happen that way. Consequently, the human mind was directed toward that path, and that path was continuously strengthened. Strengthening through mantras, strengthening through form and expression—all these were brought into action. Through the same imagination, it remains effective in the atmosphere, within us, and in each of the celestial spheres, proportional to the magnitude of that concept. However, as time progressed, we have grown into the knowledge to think, make others think, learn, and teach beyond those ancient methods.

[GV1:P1649] Today, we are situated within the *dharma* and *karma* of the *Kali Yuga*. We have attained things easily today. Those who lived two hundred years ago could not have even dreamed of any of this in reality. We are strengthened in such an action. However, in the *dharma* of *Kali*, there is something we need to attain profoundly. We must realize it through self-actualization. Just like our concepts and actions, a concept and action specific to this *Kali Yuga* must exist in the world. What are they? We must continuously perform virtue-dense actions through conceptual manifestation, eliminating the negative aspects of the past, elevating them to higher planes, and establishing them in the world as a domain of action (*karma kanda*) on our behalf. For this purpose, we have received guidance through divine revelations and spiritual visions. Having been blessed to partake in this monumental task, we must possess the absolute certainty that we can accomplish this—which represents the highest of merits, the greatest of actions, and the pinnacle of wisdom. If we, who are entirely convinced of this truth, treat this matter trivially while being recipients of the era's potential, it would be a profound disrespect toward the Divine. It would also befall us as one of the greatest sins. Therefore, regardless of whether we are sick or healthy, young or old, or whoever we may be, it makes no difference in this regard. This is an action that affords us the opportunity to perceive everything as one. Saying that everything is one does not imply that all human beings are identical. However, in terms of merits and demerits, there will be minor variations according to their individual abilities, tastes, and colors. The eligibility for experience will be more or less the same. Medicine may act quickly for some, while for others, it may not. It will succeed or fail depending on one's inherent karmic tendencies. Similarly, someone may be able to make inventions swiftly, while others can understand things immediately upon hearing them. Someone else might be able to manifest something into action merely by visualizing it from afar. Such variations exist. This is the truth. Therefore, one should not claim, "Only what I have seen is right," or "Only what you have seen is right." What everyone has perceived is indeed correct. It is a state of awareness cultivated by performing actions while rooted in one's respective imagination. However, a shortcoming has occurred among our ancestors. A habit has developed where past eras are pushed back, and only those past times are acknowledged. That is insufficient for us. We must give importance to the era we are living in, highlight past actions, undertake what can be practiced from them, preserve their goodness, discard the evil if it must be discarded, halt what needs to be halted in its proper place, and harmonize the conceptual methodology, action, and wisdom required for this world.

[GV1:P1650] Guru Vani
