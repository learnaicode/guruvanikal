[GV4:P0735] Guruvani January 2003

[GV4:P0736] How can it be acceptable to say that the material world does not exist, only the spiritual exists, and that the material and the spiritual are two distinct entities? If we say that this universe and all lineages are lies, are maya (illusions), are non-existent, and are perishable, how and from where will we look at the heavens beyond this world? Whether it is Mayavada (doctrine of illusion) or any other doctrine, why speak of what exists as non-existent?

[GV4:P0737] Seeker: The material and the spiritual are two sides of the same entity.

[GV4:P0738] Guru: I agree with all that. There is no use in preaching Vedanta. What I want to know is how you will handle it.

[GV4:P0739] A small child—be it a boy or a girl—does not gain the fortunate experiences of its birth through its life. Why doesn't that happen?

[GV4:P0740] Guru: Everything has stages to become complete. First, you need a belief about that. If you do a karma, shouldn't a karma that came with your birth be resolved? That karma must be resolved through your physical body. When karma is resolved in that way, the body will experience hardships and difficulties. How can it be right to say that things should happen without facing this hardship?

[GV4:P0741] Seekers: Karma must be done through the body.

[GV4:P0742] Guru: Yes. For how long, in what manner, and how? Karma is resolved in a thousand ways for a thousand people. How will you view it? The ignorant will be confused here.

[GV4:P0743] From one perspective, we understand that everything is maya (illusion), nothing is real, and shouldn't we leave everything behind and go? The one who preaches this Vedanta is someone with a hundred descendants, including children and grandchildren. The truth is that this Vedantin who says the body is perishable and the soul is imperishable does not know either of these. I would say that the misconception is what he holds, and what you hold by making his words the authority.

[GV4:P0744] Every action has a goal and an attainment. But the goal and attainment of each are different. The goal, the attainment, its nature, time, and the duty (dharma) fitting for that time—this is what you must study.

[GV4:P0745] People prayed to reach the feet of Rama. That was in that era. This is not Rama's era. What is the use of reaching Rama's feet in this era? Do you think that by leaving everything material and dying, you will reach Rama's feet?

[GV4:P0746] What is the duty of the time (Kaladharma)? The duty of the age (Yugadharma). Then what is the duty of the age? Having seen the duties of all four ages (yugas), and having examined the rights and wrongs that have occurred in the four ages so far, isn't it the knowledge of how to correct those mistakes that you need? The problem is that you lack this knowledge.

[GV4:P0747] How did this knowledge cease to exist? Kamsa too suffered the same predicament. You too have suffered the exact same predicament that Kamsa did. "There is no mistake; everything is secure"—suppose you transition into the next moment with this assumption. One must always possess an awareness regarding the source of such a transition, its precise measure, and what can be achieved through that source. One should not move forward without this contemplation.

[GV4:P0748] During the *Treta Yuga* and the *Dvapara Yuga*, attaining a manifestation of either Rama or Krishna was sufficient to achieve everything. However, in the *Kali Yuga*, that holds no such significance. Here, both young children and the elderly receive visions pertaining to epochs far more ancient than those. In their visions, they perceive entities ranging from spirits to deities, as well as sages, ascetics, and the enlightened—essentially, everything that has existed to date. Analyzing the revelations communicated by these entities in such visions indicates that the era in which this world ought to have advanced far beyond its current state has long passed! There must undoubtedly be a reason why these children and adults present here are endowed with such visions; what do you presume that reason to be?

[GV4:P0749] Seeker: Their ancestral or past-life connection with the entities they perceive.

[GV4:P0750] Guru: That certainly exists. However, the ancestral connection is not limited solely to them, is it? There also exist relationships with one's father, mother, children, wife, and husband, among others. As long as you lay claim to these relationships, the duty of a Guru is to elucidate the true nature of each and subsequently detach you from them. While an ancestral connection does exist, it is not the sole truth concerning you. Once that truth manifests in your experience, would it be adequate to remain complacent, believing that the divine vision of the Lord from bygone epochs is sufficient?

[GV4:P0751] What trajectory have we and our ancestors followed since the beginning? Where do we currently stand? Throughout our evolution thus far, what magnitude of boundless compassion from great souls has been bestowed upon us? Having showered you with such compassion, each of them has expressed: "Children, I was not fortunate enough to receive the opportunity that has now been granted to you."

[GV4:P0752] What do you deduce from their manifestations and these subsequent declarations? They were unable to exist and attain fulfillment during the epoch of such a Guru. It is you who have been bestowed with that extraordinary fortune. Having received such a blessing, what is your imperative duty?

[GV4:P0753] You are witnessing the soul of the individual who manifests and communicates in such visions. You must perpetually bear in mind that the profound spiritual system presented before you is one that unifies the disparate disciplines—the paths of *Yoga*, *Karma*, *Jnana*, and *Bhakti*—that were practiced during the era of that individual and the epochs of the venerable sages preceding them. You have empirically witnessed and acknowledged the verity of a system that genuinely unifies these paths. It is based upon this concurrence of yours that I speak.

[GV4:P0754] Irrespective of the grandeur of any given era, you must first comprehend how far you have progressed and where you currently stand within the spiritual path you have discovered through your knowledge and fortune. In the absence of this foundational understanding, no further spiritual endeavor can truly materialize.

[GV4:P0755] Seeker: A competent Guru is indispensable to reveal that, is it not?

[GV4:P0756] Guru: Indeed, such a path of the Guru emerges only after the advent of countless preceding Gurus. None of the predecessors were inadequate. Rather, they unequivocally acknowledged the existence of a reality transcending their own teachings. Among them, the Prophet attached immense significance to doctrine. However, upon transcending mere doctrine, one perceives the prevailing illusions suspended within this spiritual atmosphere. He asserted that everything, from spirits and ghouls to deities, constitutes mere deception and artifice. What one perceives here is not the ultimate truth; there exists a realm beyond. As one ventures further into that beyond, it becomes apparent that billions of *Jinn*, vastly more formidable and potent than anything previously witnessed, are present. None of the Gurus of bygone eras possessed the capability requisite to transcend these profound forces.

[GV4:P0757] Who could possibly deny the existence of the Hindu philosophical traditions, or the profound spiritual endeavors of the prophets, Gurus, and visionaries who have emerged to date? All of these undeniably possess an intrinsic truth. Having observed this, should we merely proceed with the complacent notion that they should remain in their respective domains, or should we advance by critically analyzing their inherent limitations? The essential wisdom you must cultivate is the capacity to comprehend this reality, irrespective of your direct affiliation with it.

[GV4:P0758] The general populace typically perceives the path of the Guru through the lens of mystical phenomena (*siddhis*). Consequently, you harbor a misconception, assuming that merely by the will of a Swami, anything can be effortlessly accomplished. I must elucidate the fallacy inherent in this assumption of yours. Billions of souls across countless epochs have been ensnared by this very delusion. You must cultivate an understanding of the events that transpired within the spiritual paradigms of bygone eras (*Yugantara Dharmas*). If you remain exclusively preoccupied with your immediate concerns—seeking superficial remedies such as, "proceed in this specific manner, combine these particular actions to achieve a desired outcome, or perform a minor ritual and ascertain what else is required to bring it to completion"—and thus confine yourself to purely personal or familial matters, you shall remain wholly incapable of comprehending the fundamental cosmological errors.

[GV4:P0759] Who can possibly refute the existence of the predetermined destiny you brought forth upon your birth? What inherently resides within you is your predestined *karma*. No individual possesses the capability to evade its realization. Let those inevitable experiences manifest in their myriad forms. We need not search elsewhere for the dimensions of our *karma*, the fruits of our accumulated merit (*punya*), or even the vestiges of our envy, animosity, and other intrinsic vices; they are deeply embedded within us. The extent of the spiritual evolution we can achieve in this lifetime, and the subsequent trajectory of our *karma* following this earthly existence—these profound inquiries must remain the perpetual subjects of our contemplation. Conversely, if a soul lacks the requisite fortune to engage in such profound thought and spiritual growth, upon finding a Guru—having cultivated faith, bestowed love, and received guidance—one must, at the very least, engage in a critical evaluation of the spiritual wisdom thus acquired.

[GV4:P0760] Each individual is bound by their distinct *karma*. You are obligated to diligently perform and fulfill the temporal duties (*kaladharma*) pertaining to both the past and the present. Subsequently, you must transition to the ensuing *kaladharma*. We are acutely aware of the existence of a *karma* that has accumulated over time, as well as actions carelessly squandered. It is our imperative duty to integrate these *karmas* into this divinely ordained path, purifying them, and subsequently bequeathing this sanctified *karma* for the benefit and spiritual sustenance of our future generations. You may have undoubtedly labored with immense hardship and sorrow for the sake of your descendants; however, perfection remains elusive. Such is the inevitable consequence of the complex evolutionary dynamics of your soul. You need not be overly preoccupied with that perceived incompleteness.

[GV4:P0761] As previously articulated, each individual possesses a unique *karma*. There are numerous children, as well as adults, present here who have achieved profound evolution concerning spiritual visions. That which they perceive, hear, and articulate is undoubtedly accurate. Yet, upon progressing to a specific juncture, their spiritual advancement stagnates. An insufficiency of requisite *karma* and merit (*punya*) is a primary cause for this. Being born devoid of ancestral purity (*pitru-shuddhi*) constitutes another fundamental reason. The sacred ritual performed here is precisely the spiritual process of purifying those ancestors who procreated in a state of impurity and subsequently elevating them to a transcendent realm. Through the profound grace of the Guru, you shall eventually possess the discerning capacity to ascertain whether they remain in a state of purity or impurity. The ability to directly perceive this reality through visions may not be accessible to all. This unequivocally illustrates the inherent complexities and evolutionary dynamics of the soul. Some possess the capacity for such perception, while others remain oblivious. In certain households, it is conceivable that every member is endowed with this vision; in others, only a select few may possess it; and in yet others, it remains entirely inaccessible to all. This reflects the deeply personal and varied experiences of each of you. Having profoundly realized this experiential truth, and firmly rooted in the sacred bond of the Guru-disciple relationship, it is incumbent upon you to propagate this profound knowledge across your homeland and the wider world, transcending all barriers of caste, religion, class, gender, and age. To accomplish this, you must first internalize this wisdom yourself. If you, who are privileged to exist as contemporaries of the Guru, fail to internalize it, whom else could possibly be guided to comprehend it? It is a reality that not everyone possesses the innate capacity to comprehend these profound truths upon mere instruction. Nevertheless, you must steadfastly press onward, propelled by the intensity of your faith and your unwavering resolve. Your ultimate success or failure in this endeavor will be directly commensurate with the depth and steadfastness of your faith.

[GV4:P0762] You possess inherent imperfections. While an imperfection might appear acceptable to you, it may not be so to others. If your actions are driven by the desire to project an image of perfection to the world, those endeavors will inevitably collapse. Everyone present here has experienced such failures at some point; there are no exceptions. You must not surrender to lethargy, claiming, "We are utterly exhausted by life; do something for us," and remain inactive. Rather, you should reflect: "I may not possess the strength to wield a pestle and pound, yet, somehow, this husk must be removed." Otherwise, you will be forced to consume the outer shell along with the kernel. The tasks assigned to you here are specifically designed to strip away this superficial exterior. Action yields true benefit only when it cultivates a character of profound substance. Merely claiming to have witnessed or experienced something may sound impressive, but it possesses no practical utility.

[GV4:P0763] What defines a true believer? It is analogous to the cultivation of an elephant foot yam (*chena*). There are those who merely observe the yam being planted, those who witness it sprouting, and those who see it fail to sprout. Conversely, there is the one who diligently cultivates the yam and ultimately reaps the harvest. Among these, who represents the genuine believer? A while after planting the yam, the original seed tuber ceases to exist. Why, then, was it cut and planted? To transform its pre-existing state. It was sliced before being sown; thus, what was buried is lost. Reproduction is achieved through planting distinct, severed pieces. Therefore, if one adopts the philosophical stance that "everything is an illusion" (*Maya*), arguing that no matter how many yams are planted, empty space remains, what purpose does that serve? People have observed whole yams being buried, fragments being planted, and yams merely dried for seed preservation. Yet, regardless of how or when you observe it, is it not your misfortune if you cannot grasp its true nature and practical application? Is a mere superficial glance at the yam sufficient? Certainly not.

[GV4:P0764] Faith is undeniably a virtue. However, clinging to a static belief, demanding that faith must always manifest in a specific, predetermined manner, is impractical. If one merely adopts the ancestral notion that a single glimpse of the divine is sufficient and equates to absolute realization, what can be achieved? Such a simplistic approach is inadequate. What benefit do we derive from merely tallying the yams in our own possession against those held by others? It is akin to the proverb, "If the father arrives, there will be jaggery." But what if the father fails to arrive? Therefore, you must diligently account for what is presently in your own grasp. You have initiated the process of claiming your rightful inheritance; you must complete that reckoning. What if you refrain from this accounting, assuming the keys remain safely with the grandfather? Everything exists, situated right where it belongs. However, the grandfather has passed away. This is the crucial reality that must be comprehended.

[GV4:P0765] Each of you possesses potential proportionate to your own capacity. In life, the measures of fortune, virtuous deeds, sin, and merit vary significantly among individuals. A mere vision of the Divine is insufficient. Our very approach to invoking the Divine is fundamentally flawed. This exemplifies the faith of a Shudra. Kamsa serves as the paradigm of this Shudra faith; his spiritual evolution halted at that specific juncture. He witnessed it, placed his faith in it, and sought experience, mystical powers, and knowledge entirely through that limited paradigm, never progressing beyond it. Consequently, he failed to comprehend the true significance of the ethereal voice (*Ashariri*). Therefore, an individual who has ascended only to the level of Kamsa is inherently incapable of distinguishing between *Advaita* (non-dualism) and *Dvaita* (dualism). This is the defining characteristic of the Shudra varna. All the preceptors (*Acharyas*) who have emerged thus far remain stagnant at this level. This realization dawns only upon reaching the ultimate state. One might confess, "This is not it, Lord; I was gravely mistaken. I harbored the conviction that nothing exists apart from You." However, upon arriving there, one discovers a multitude of entities, some equal and many superior to oneself. It is these forces that govern the cosmos. This is the precise predicament of all such preceptors.

[GV4:P0766] If you fail to grasp this profound truth and merely attempt to attain fulfillment by marveling at, singing about, and blindly acting upon the ancient accounts narrated by others, your fundamental misconceptions will never be eradicated. One must advance beyond the stagnation point of Kamsa and these preceptors, progressing to a state where one can comprehensively decipher and resolve the manifested course of karma.

[GV4:P0767] How many billions of people inhabit this world? Within this solar system, there are six billion. If one were to attempt progression by narrating the individual experiences of every single person, advancement would be utterly impossible. The distinction between *Dvaita* and *Advaita* was formulated as a simplified heuristic to address this complexity. The manifestations of *Dvaita* are fundamentally uncountable; they perpetually shift and evade precise categorization.

[GV4:P0768] Yogis adopt various practices—such as *Hamsa Yoga*, *Hatha Yoga*, and *Chandrika Yoga*—to loosen the physical bonds of their limbs and clear somatic blockages like phlegm. Does this physiological mastery imply the attainment of perfect spiritual wisdom? In contemporary times, any celebrated yogi is readily proclaimed a *Hamsa Yogi*. However, allowing this metaphorical swan (*Hamsa*) to flutter about indiscriminately without true realization will not yield the desired ultimate objective.

[GV4:P0769] Guruvani

[GV4:P0770] March 2003

[GV4:P0771] I have frequently explained the essence of *Guru Pooja* to you, tailored to the context of various occasions. Yet, what is your actual comprehension of *Guru Pooja*? Merely worshipping the Guru—nothing beyond that. While *Guru Pooja* is conducted in numerous locations, it is not a universally practiced ritual even across India. In North India, the term *Guru Pooja* is widely used. However, they too have failed to grasp its profound magnitude beyond the superficial act of revering the Guru. At Santhigiri, *Guru Pooja* is performed with a distinct and deeper significance. Nevertheless, even long-standing members of this community remain largely ignorant of its true dimensions. There are fundamental reasons preventing you from achieving that requisite level of familiarity and understanding.

[GV4:P0772] Among the *Brahmin*, *Kshatriya*, *Vaishya*, and *Shudra* categories, the first three possess a rudimentary capacity to understand when instructed. However, when it comes to the *Shudra* varna, there is an absolute void of such spiritual awareness. What precisely is a *Shudra*? It is not the designation of a caste, but rather a *varna* (an occupational and spiritual classification). Historically, they were disenfranchised from undertaking authoritative spiritual practices. It is widely propagated that even the *Manu Smriti* mandates this restriction. Yet, this represents a gross misinterpretation of Manu's injunctions. The error lies in the arbitrary systems imposed by later individuals claiming to interpret Manu. Given this entrenched situation, through what means, in what manner, and at what juncture are we supposed to exert our agency and achieve realization?

[GV4:P0773] The unique essence of *Guru Pooja* at Santhigiri lies in the intimate bond between the Guru and the disciple. The *Shudra* varna is only capable of perceiving this Guru-disciple relationship as a transactional ritual of offering oblations and displaying superficial respect. Presenting offerings and exhibiting reverence does not constitute true *Guru Pooja*. Despite repeatedly hearing, witnessing, and intellectually engaging with this concept in various forms, you fail to internalize it. Even when kept in close proximity and guided through the practices, comprehension remains elusive. The individual lacks even fundamental self-awareness. This exemplifies the profound ignorance inherent in the *Shudra* varna. However, this cannot be entirely attributed as their fault, for a historical decree was imposed explicitly barring them from active participation. This is the root cause. While the *Vaishya* possessed the liberty to engage in labor and commerce, they too were marginalized from the core procedures of worship. Regarding the purity of lineage (*Gotra Shuddhi*), the strictures of the Vedic religion served as their absolute authority as well, consequently depriving them of true lineage purity. Both of these varnas remain utterly ignorant of the protocols required to attain *Gotra Shuddhi* or the profound nuances of true worship. The *Shudra* is omnipresent as a facilitator and servant in all spheres, yet remains entirely excluded from the esoteric core of these matters. Thus, through generations, this varna suffered a catastrophic loss of *Dharma*. *Kali Yuga* is, in fact, the era uniquely designed for reclaiming this monumental loss. Contrastingly, we have been indoctrinated to believe that *Kali* signifies absolute destruction. If *Kali* is merely annihilation, what purpose does human birth serve in this epoch? Alternatively, should we postpone our existence until *Kali* concludes? The mechanics of *Dharma* operate on an entirely different paradigm.

[GV4:P0774] In the chronological progression of the *Yuga Dharma*, *Kali Yuga* constitutes the fourth quarter. Whosoever takes birth during this epoch—whether a *Brahmin*, *Kshatriya*, *Vaishya*, *Shudra*, or even a *Chandala*—possesses an undeniable, fundamental right to liberation (*Mukti*). *Kali Dharma* is the precise mechanism that unequivocally guarantees this right. Recent history provides ample empirical evidence to solidify this assertion. The most paramount example is the very incarnation of Lord Sri Krishna. Krishna manifested within the *Shudra* lineage. Yet, despite being born in a *Shudra* clan and ascending to royal authority, Kamsa remained fundamentally incapable of comprehending this divine reality. Nevertheless, from an alternative perspective, it is inaccurate to assert that Kamsa lacked awareness. On the contrary, Kamsa was a believer of unwavering conviction. He possessed substantial intellect, a capacity for devotion, and profound dedication. He was endowed with the mystical capability for direct esoteric vision. Therefore, he should not be misconstrued as an ignorant fool. What, then, constituted his fatal flaw? He critically lacked the supreme spiritual knowledge (*Atma Vidya*) required both to abide by *Dharma* and to implement it. Furthermore, he was entirely oblivious to the rigorous path necessary to attain that transcendental *Atma Vidya*.

[GV4:P0775] People often ask if the manifestation of the Lord is not enough. What is the purpose of this manifestation? Is this manifestation meant for blessing and destruction? That is how the scrutiny of Dharma is performed at the end of every epoch. But this is not *Atmavidya* (Self-knowledge). We cannot do anything to remove the mistake we have made through this concept of blessing and destruction. Moreover, the ignorance regarding blessing and destruction continues to persist. How can this be changed?

[GV4:P0776] Today, we cannot achieve anything by dividing ourselves along the lines of caste and religion and dancing wildly. There is no point in fighting either. Is there a need to fight for God? If you, living in your own land, have different Gods, shouldn't you just move away from that land? It is because you worship God as separate entities that you have suffered the misfortune of fighting among yourselves. It is because of this blindness of caste and religion that you could not be saved even though Sri Krishna came.

[GV4:P0777] Even though an advance notice was given that the birth of Sri Krishna would happen in a certain way, what all was done by failing to respect it? He was chased to all parts of India without being allowed to stay in one place. Instead of understanding this series of events in that manner, what do you have in your hands other than the ruined traditional actions, celebrating the story of the Pandavas and the tale of whether the wax palace was burnt down or not, treating it all as a mere play and matter of importance? And then, you have been misled into believing that this is *Atmavidya*. This notion is the biggest among the blunders that have occurred to this day.

[GV4:P0778] There is a trick behind the Shudra conception of Sri Krishna. This trick is a puzzle thrown in front of the Shudra. What is it? The imagination of him being the husband of sixteen thousand and eight women. By leading you into a completely unnecessary action, nurturing you in that tendency, and making males and females dance up to the Balagokulam, they have completed the process and turned you into the remnants of ignorance. What can be done about the vileness that has cultivated this ignorance through the worship of God? Is it from this vileness that one should strive to reclaim *Atmavidya*? *Atmavidya* does not have such a nature. And one should never assume that it does. Furthermore, no one should think that they can be saved by climbing to the pinnacle of Vedanta, claiming that this does not exist. Who can be improved by babbling about things that are of no use to the living? It is simply wasting time by blabbering without any significance. This will only serve to push one into actions where the aspect of spiritual merit cannot be illuminated within the Dharma of living as men and women. If the degradation of Dharma that has occurred within just five thousand years is this much, just think how immense the magnitude of the degradation that has come over very long periods would be.

[GV4:P0779] Now let us look at how it has been played out in another way. Who is to be improved by demonstrating the worship method shown in the character of Sri Rama and by ritually enacting the coronation (*Pattabhishekam*) of Sri Rama's time? The coronation of Sri Rama is a trajectory of action that pushes one into destructive deeds performed merely as a play. Because we, our ancestors, and each one of us have done so, living without any certainty about the epochs, we are unable to determine the Dharmas of the times. That is why it had to be said that Kali is destruction. And yet, in another place, they also say that Kali is the best among the epochs. Why are both these things being said in the same breath - is it for you or for the world? Is it the job of these speakers to impose this ignorance? We neither know this by ourselves nor can we ask and find out.

[GV4:P0780] How long will we live on this earth? Can we live until the end of the Kali Yuga? But the actions required to live in the Kali Yuga have not been taught to us. Nor has it been told how to overcome the Kali Yuga through our lives.

[GV4:P0781] The Satya Yuga has passed away truthfully itself. We saw what happened in the Treta Yuga and the Dvapara Yuga. What was spoken to Kamsa as an unembodied voice (oracle) is itself the proof. What was said? - You should not look up. Work for whatever you see in front of you. But Kamsa did not understand that. Because he happened to belong to the Shudra class. Except for those belonging to the Brahmana and Kshatriya classes who became kings in recent times, no one understood it. In Kerala, the majority of kings have come from the Shudra class. What buffoonery was shown to make them Kshatriyas! *Hiranyagarbham*! - Making a golden cow and making them pass through its belly without bending. And then, if a sacred thread is given, it is all settled! And before that, a *Murajapam* too. If asked why this is done, no one knows. If one becomes a king like this, who will get what benefit? Will the rulers or the ruled get any benefit? What is the knowledge gained along with the kingdom? Ages have passed by, caught in this kind of ignorance. Authority was converted into money, power, and wealth, and rejoicing upon it, growing arrogant, it transformed into the absolute form of absurdity. Who can change this situation, and how? You, who move forward considering caste, religion, and class as virtues, will become the owners of actions that degrade you to the level of the god of death (*Kala*). This will never set you right. If the Brahmana is playing by creating Mantras, and through Mantras Tantras, and through Tantras Yantras, and is doing only that action today, how will he live? Today, he is even ready to go to Persia. For what, to perform *Rakta Pushpanjali* (floral offering with blood)? It is easy to take the blood of all those slaughtered there and perform the *Pushpanjali*, isn't it?

[GV4:P0782] Having cultivated vile tendencies in this manner, we have completely become warriors of destructive actions. We dance and play in the world as long as we have health. After that, we become unwanted by anyone. Do you think that by listening to the story of Ajamila and calling out to God at the very end, God will jump and arrive? Or do you think God will come if you get goosebumps listening to the story of bursting out of a pillar to scoop out and eat blood and intestines? God does not come like that at all. The life of one who has fallen into this kind of blindness and jumped around will simply evaporate after death, and the body will merely sprout grass.

[GV4:P0783] The traditional wealth of each of us consists of the ruined actions that have completely faded away behind the demons who died singing and playing, standing in the Dharma that spreads blindness behind the ignorance ruined by stories. It is not being said that nothing in the world exists. It certainly does exist. But what is it that we should attain? We should acquire the knowledge of whatever good can be done for the respective times through our birth and death. We must give respect to whichever era we are born into—an era that comes into being, exists, and perishes. And having lived here for ten or a hundred years, we must take with us the Karma that reaches us. However, it is an ignorance devoid of any such thought that we are imparting through worship. What is that?

[GV4:P0784] One group slaughters and sheds blood. Another group prepares and offers it as a sacrament. Along with that, wine and other things. We have understood this as the worship of God. The karmic flaw of this degraded method of worship is present in all religious doctrines. Do we need liberation from this or not?

[GV4:P0785] We have done whatever anyone has said. But we have not achieved what needs to be achieved through human life. We acquired everything that should not be acquired. Whatever caste or religion we belong to, the situation is the same. If it is said that something good has sprouted in any one religion or caste, it is contrary to the truth. A great person or a visionary at the beginning who created a religious doctrine might have told us the truth. But after that, if one gets caught up in actions based on the advice of an ignorant person, that action will remain. Yet, they will also teach and claim that there is no Karma.

[GV4:P0786] It is true, one who is free from Karma is the master of everything in this world. But how does Karma cease? We do not know about that. Having mistakenly assumed that there is no Karma, we are bankrupt regarding the consciousness of where Karma ceases. There is no such scenario where Karma ceases when the body ceases to exist. If there is, it must be achieved while being alive. We have evidence before us of many who have shown this while alive.

[GV4:P0787] What is the nature of our familiarity today? We divide ourselves by caste, religion, and class, engage in quarrels and brawls, and then proceed to worship God. Does God reside within caste and religion? You are incapable of even contemplating this. Apart from indulging in activities like playing Balagokulam, enacting Sree Rama Pattabhishekam, performing Gurusi, Kuthiyottam, Theyyam, and offering blood to Kali—and whatever other reprehensible practices exist—what do you genuinely understand about worshipping God? Wherever a vacant field is found, you assemble these grotesque effigies, conduct spectacles and ceremonial baths (*Aaraattu*), and invoke God!

[GV4:P0788] You also say that the Guru is Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, and the Supreme Brahman. How does a Guru attain such glory? If you merely invoke the Guru in principle like this, will the Guru actually manifest? The Guru is not someone who comes to bestow blessings or inflict punishments upon you. You do not require such blessings or punishments. While you exist in the awareness of the states of wakefulness (*Jagrat*), dream (*Swapna*), and deep sleep (*Sushupti*), this realization must dawn upon you. That conviction must remain until karma is entirely severed from the soul. You yourselves must be able to witness how that karma is severed.

[GV4:P0789] You possess absolutely no knowledge regarding this path. All that you have heard are mere fragments of Vedanta. Or else, a Gayatri Mantra that combines everything. Do you believe that by going beyond that and repeatedly chanting the Brahmasutra, you will become a Vedantin? What have you assumed? Merely reciting the names of a pile of timber will not make a house. For it to become a house, the timber must be assembled according to its proper width and design.

[GV4:P0790] That is not what is being said. Rather, it is demonstrating right before you what Dharma is in the karma that connects the life and the soul. For that, the spiritual merit (*Sukrita*) pertaining to the soul is required. If you divide yourselves by caste, religion, or class, you will not be able to imbibe this. The period during which a Guru lives and the place where the Guru resides—that is the most sacred and filled with merit. It is often said that a place without a sage (*Sadhu*) is a ruined place.

[GV4:P0791] How have we understood the term 'Sadhu'? You have understood that 'Sadhus' mean poor people. That is not so. A Sadhu is a Sanyasin (ascetic). But a Sanyasin is not a wandering beggar. We boast about all sorts of wandering ascetics by pointing at them! If you are taught such corrupted notions, what will you do? Every religion teaches the exact same thing regarding a Sanyasin. This is another manifestation of the calamity that has befallen the world.

[GV4:P0792] What is the karma of Kali (the present age)? It is a karma that must be handled as meticulously as a gooseberry held in the palm of one's hand. The Guru demonstrates to you that very karma through the transitional states of gross (*Sthula*), subtle (*Sukshma*), effect (*Karya*), and cause (*Karana*). The Guru then reveals how your life interacts with the Guru, or the Guru lineage (*Guru Parampara*), what the position of that lineage is, and its significance within the worldly karma that has existed to this day. Otherwise, if anyone convinces you that karma does not exist, that what is called karma is an illusion, and that karma does not apply to a Yogi, a Jnani (wise one), a Karmi (man of action), or a Bhakta (devotee), then that is entirely a misguided doctrine. To convincingly impart this in all its glory, there must be a competent Guru, and a Guru lineage qualified to function at all times for that Guru. For this, there must always be a Guru who is as enlightened as the primordial Guru (*Adi Guru*) of that lineage. The unique distinction attained by Santhigiri in this regard is divinely ordained by Brahman.
