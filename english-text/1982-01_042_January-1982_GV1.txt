[GV1:P1130] January 1982

[GV1:P1131] Many variations occur in the evolution of faith and trust. Starting with the trust we have in our parents, we maintain different forms of trust in our children, family members, neighbors, and even the animals we rear. How many attitudinal shifts occur within this? If we reflect upon whether we have perfected this, we realize that we have not been able to achieve absolute trust in any of the aforementioned. Even concerning a mere garment, our trust in it lasts only as long as its utility. Thus, trust has its limits. Initially, there is a conviction that one cannot survive even for a moment apart from one's father or mother. However, this later shifts towards one's spouse and children. When some people place their faith in alcohol, they forget everything else.

[GV1:P1132] Above all this lies faith in God. This ranges from deities like Madan, Kali, Bhadra, and Chathan, all the way up to the Guru. Love is deeply intertwined with faith. A father's love for his children resides profoundly in his heart; it is invisible to others. The trust and love a woman feels for her husband differ from what she feels for her father. A mother's love is of an entirely different nature. Even if she lacks formal knowledge, she harbors immense love for her children within. Love is glorious. In today's times, even the love between siblings has its limits. How can we sustain this love beyond these boundaries? Nurturing any of these forms of love is easier said than done. Putting it into practice is where the difficulty lies.

[GV1:P1133] At the very least, we must preserve the depth of this love. This love is the result of humanity's boundless endeavors over countless ages. Today, we acquire a piece of clothing effortlessly. But what was our condition before its invention? Similarly, to bring each subject to our knowledge, our ancestors or we ourselves must have struggled through lifetimes. To prevent this love from breaking apart here and there, the parents are the first ones who can impart teachings. It is the parents who initially teach a child to bow before a lamp or a stone. Even without the child's conscious awareness, the glory of love blossoms within. The child then begins to express the same back to them. Unknowingly, knowledge awakens within the child.

[GV1:P1134] We roll the dung of the cow we rear and envision Lord Ganesha in it. Why? That is a form of faith. How did faith develop in this foul-smelling substance? A person without faith might find this ridiculous. How much must our ancestors have struggled to instill love within us even for cow dung? After bowing before the cow dung, people began shaping idols to solidify their faith. Even an onlooker would unknowingly bow down in reverence. When Tamils sculpt an idol of God, it often has a mustache. Both the one who sculpted it and the one who worships it do so with God in their thoughts.

[GV1:P1135] Some people remain neutral, not denying anything, thinking they do not know whether all these exist or not, and that it is all just people's beliefs. Such a person lives with understanding. Christians, Muslims, and Buddhists all establish their own respective constructs and attempt to instill faith in people. How can this faith be sustained in life? How can it be imparted to those around us? We are able to exert our own will by forgetting both those whom we nurture and those who nurture us. How is this possible?

[GV1:P1136] Consider what happens if the source of love flows elsewhere. What is the implication if it is said that a woman with four children eloped with another man? This can only be changed by your own mind. This is a path of washing away other vile thoughts by firmly establishing faith and love. Those of you who desire this should contemplate how to gather the strength for it.

[GV1:P1137] This is a subject where knowledge must be imparted without making a distinction between men and women. Just as those who teach in primary classes that the sun rises in the east later explain the reality as the students grow older, Vedic scholars will also reverse the order of worship. By fostering something called faith, they bring you to a certain stage, then detach that faith from one object and direct it towards reality. Gunangudi Masthan is an example of this. He says that there is no use in merely murmuring mantras. He says this only after reaching that elevated state. However, until then, they were the ones who taught people to murmur mantras.

[GV1:P1138] The majority of believers are those who desire to work towards an entirely better tomorrow through some form of disciplined practice or their faith. What was described above is a characteristic of that faith crumbling when it could not be sustained. What you can gain from this description is the insight to observe a certain state of the world. Only a Guru can provide that insight. Those belonging to the Trimurti tradition can perceive and accomplish things to some extent. That is what has been proclaimed in our Puranas and Vedas.

[GV1:P1139] However, beyond this, we can perceive it scientifically. When any science reaches a certain limit, it becomes impossible for them to handle it. The reason is that they understand all this by keenly observing nature. If a disturbance occurs in that nature, it will all come to an end. The evolution of the mind is not like that. It is a purity of nature that surpasses even the physical world. It is something acquired through life. We can perceive for ourselves how many lifetimes we have had. Even among those present here, there are quite a few who have carefully realized this truth. However, they have not been able to bring it forth into action. Yet, these believers are experiencing it. Why are they unable to bring it forth? How is it that we fail to attain its glory? If we indeed prepare ourselves to closely understand this glory, certain karmic courses will obstruct us. It will hinder us in life, not just in one way, but in every possible way. When one begins to act with a degree of sacrifice, some kind of shortcoming will inevitably attach itself to them. It will also cause them to act aimlessly.

[GV1:P1140] Both men and women will experience this. It is difficult for a person to understand this without the assistance of a Guru. There are numerous such subjects. It takes quite some time just to comprehend all of this. To impart this understanding, there must be a lineage of perfected Gurus who have regulated themselves through their own actions. When speaking of these Gurus, we typically only encounter those who fall under the Trimurti, those whose reach extends merely up to Vethala Nagaram, or, at best, Gurus who stand at the level of the Trimurti.

[GV1:P1141] Beyond this, there are *Kalanthara Gurus* (Gurus transcending time). This is said to be the lineage of Manu. You might ask why the lineage of Manu is being discussed again after stating that it had committed an error. Just because Manu erred, it cannot be deduced that it was not the era of Manu. A flaw in the lineage of a Manu cannot overturn this epoch. The errors that occurred over eons can only be rectified. The era of Manu cannot be falsified. Fourteen Manus were born and upheld its glory. It will be managed through them alone. However, since an error occurred in between, it is revealed that without granting complete autonomy to this, it will be fulfilled through a Guru who embodies it. This revelation is not something we can obtain simply by demanding it. Nor is it something we can bear in any manner. By divine decree and the design of Brahman, it arrives through the destined path. Beyond this, we have no further knowledge to impart.

[GV1:P1142] Guruvani
