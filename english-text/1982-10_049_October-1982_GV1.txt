[GV1:P1217] October 1982

[GV1:P1218] Our culture is at a very low level. Along with that, we were also born in an ignorant place. Knowledge has been lost in the culture of the Shudra. The time (Kali) has come for the Shudra. If this Kali is meant for the Shudra, this is a perfect opportunity to completely transform it. Through the growth of the Shudra, with the Vaishya, Kshatriya, and Brahmin mingling and living together so that everyone becomes alike, we have been given an aspect to systematically transition back to the Satya Yuga.

[GV1:P1219] This is a notification given to us by God. It signifies that the growth of this Shudra is the growth of the world. When we say the growth of the world, it can only grow through a particular individual, a specific region, or a specific country. Otherwise, everyone cannot grow together simultaneously. The notification is that such an absolute individuality will certainly come to us in Kerala.

[GV1:P1220] It has been explained to those in this lineage how to carry forward their faith. Merely imparting it to the ignorant will not enable them to assimilate it quickly. However, you (the knowledgeable), at least a few among you—men and women, elders and children—must come together, assimilate it, and consciously engage in the act of diligently reclaiming the prudent knowledge required to pass it on to your lineage. To facilitate this, the culture of the preceptors that has been handed down to us thus far has been presented as a medium. Subsequently, the errors that have occurred within their lineage will also be highlighted. How we can rectify these errors now—this too will be demonstrated and explained.

[GV1:P1221] Today, the communities that are the most difficult to bring to the forefront are the Kuravas, Paravas, and Mukkuvas. Uplifting these groups is considered to be a formidable challenge. This knowledge has been imparted by the masters. Henceforth, you must carefully comprehend this. One must always wait for such communities with utmost vigilance. There are numerous shortcomings within these communities. Their rejection of the masters is cited as the reason for this. No matter how much we refine the Kuravas, some form of deficiency invariably manifests in them, without their own realization. When they assimilate that culture from a particular place, they tend to reject it shortly thereafter. Alternatively, they might remain aloof without absorbing it at all.

[GV1:P1222] It has been impossible to uplift those engaged in occupations such as burning lime, climbing coconut trees, crafting cane products, and cobbling shoes. Regardless of the affection with which they are kept close, they eventually drift away. The Mukkuvas exhibit a similar tendency. Sage Agasthya had thoroughly understood and articulated this. Agasthya is the greatest sage the world has ever seen. He expressed it almost as a lament: "They cannot be brought forward." This clearly indicates his underlying desire to uplift them. However, it was not realized. Therefore, this effort must be integrated into our culture. They must be reformed and elevated. The divine will must also align with this endeavor. Let us pray for the same.

[GV1:P1223] Beyond the three communities mentioned earlier, there may be others. Nevertheless, it is these three groups that are specifically highlighted. They can be reformed only by keeping them close. If alienated, they will never improve. Furthermore, they can only be guided forward through faith. However, even today, they retain the tendency to place their faith in and worship 'forest deities'. The situation is identical among the Mukkuvas, compounded by their ingrained tendencies. It is said that they can be reformed only if these ingrained tendencies are eradicated. It appears that many have attempted to change this. They also possess considerable knowledge. Yet, it does not become a lasting tradition for them. It is understood that the underlying cause for this is indeed a curse.

[GV1:P1224] While we endeavor to establish this culture, we must not retreat upon witnessing the arrogance of the Nair. The term 'Nair' implies 'Na-Ayyar' (Not an Ayyar). Upon attaining this 'Ayyar' status, he became consumed by arrogance and deteriorated. The Ezhava also harbors a similar attitude. They are either exceedingly arrogant, or otherwise, foolish. One must not step back upon witnessing their arrogance and foolishness. As we attempt to instill this faith, we will have to endure their opposition, regardless of its magnitude. They will undoubtedly resist. Even on subjects they are ignorant about, they will speak with great eloquence. All of this is merely babble stemming from the epitome of their ignorance. Do not be swayed or intimidated by any of this. In order for them to be reformed, we must undertake any sacrifice with unwavering vigilance.

[GV1:P1225] To reform the individuals belonging to the Kurava, Mukkuva, and Parava communities, we must be vigilant in ensuring that they live intermingled with others. We should create a system, even at the governmental level, to facilitate such intermingling and thereby transform their culture. There is a profound purpose behind this. Only when this occurs will the people be able to envision a unified tomorrow. Towards this end, contributions are being made to a particular cause. The headquarters of this fund is located in Dharwad. Subsequently, a new institution named the Mysore Lingayat Education Fund came into existence. Its objective, too, was the educational advancement among the Lingayats. The Mysore Lingayat Education Fund was established during a conference of Lingayats held in Bangalore in 1905 to discuss religious and secular issues.

[GV1:P1226] In 1913, the Lingayats convened again in Belgaum. There were differences of opinion among the Lingayats on various matters, particularly regarding religious affairs. Consequently, the assembly decided to focus solely on educational, secular, and economic matters, and to relegate religious issues to the 'Shivayoga Mandir', which was under the control of the Jangamas.

[GV1:P1227] The Lingayats did not neglect the literary branch. The commentary authored by Sripati Panditacharya on the *Vedanta Sutras*, titled *Srikara Bhashya*, serves as an example of this. From a philosophical perspective, this has been characterized as *Shakti Vishishtadvaita*. Although it is said that Renukacharya and Nilakanthacharya had written commentaries prior to this, their manuscripts have not yet been discovered.

[GV1:P1228] Guruvani
