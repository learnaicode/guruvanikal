[GV1:P1535] August 1985

[GV1:P1536] In Kerala, a special importance is given to the month of Chingam. Similarly, to the first day of the month of Medam. That can be seen all over India. If you look at the topography of Kerala, it is springtime itself. According to astrology, those born in the month of Chingam also deserve importance. Without relying on any other scientific rule, we celebrate Onam on an auspicious day in the month of Chingam. Referring to it as the 'Golden Chingam' (*Ponnin Chingam*), more importance is given to the month of Chingam than to all other months. It is the month sanctified by the birth of great souls for the awakening and upliftment of the *Avarna* (lower-caste) community. The time when the suppressed Asura emperor 'Maveli' steps out to visit his land. A month when Keralites do not hesitate to celebrate Onam for twenty-eight days. The time when the whole world jumps with joy and celebrates, waiting for the arrival of that great emperor who conducted a rule where hardship and sorrow never occurred. The month in which great souls take birth, imparting philosophical teachings to people who were subjected to untouchability through 'distance and touch' (*theendal and thodeel*), enabling them to attain prosperity and progress by forgetting caste and religious differences. In every way, an auspicious time. On this occasion, may we attain the knowledge and wisdom to view, in accordance with the times, the great souls who gave us the opportunity to think about what our land, our people, and the world need.

[GV1:P1537] The time today is Kali (Kali Yuga). History and science help us understand that the Satya, Treta, and Dvapara yugas had their own unique conditions. Then we must know that Kali also has a designated state. The announcement of the ancestors is also the same. Nature is proclaiming to us that it is absolutely true. From the time of Lord Krishna, the land of Bharath has witnessed the birth of numerous great souls who imparted noble knowledge, sacrifice, and purity of action to the Shudra class as a whole and to the believing public in particular. But all this has been disrupted by the Avarna-Savarna distinction and the *Varnashrama Dharma*. The temple system, which brought Vedic dharma through rituals, incorporating worship like the Shaktheya tradition, its associated mantras, tantras, festivals, and various arts extending up to the Devadasi system, navigated us on a path that introduced us to the abyss from the heights. From this, caste was created, and from caste, sub-castes were created. An administrative system that properly enforced untouchability by prescribing distance and touch onto the illiterate poor, along with the kings required for it, existed all over Bharath. Through this, the Avarna system persisted. They lived a life with no duty towards themselves, their home, or their country. That life, over time, completed the system of slavery. That is why our country came to be ruled and made to be ruled by another group.

[GV1:P1538] Although it fostered education, wealth, and enlightenment, the excessive infatuation towards Western lands by administrators who tried to bring about changes over time did not consider that it would weaken the people to the point of stifling the voice of life crying out in distress, becoming debilitated like a patient afflicted by inescapable karmic faults in life. This is a time highly suitable for the resolution of karma (*karma-nivaranam*) by coming together in accordance with the guidance of great souls to view this with value, with the determination and vigor that the goal of life is to impart peace and tranquility, providing a time for not just the Avarnas but also the Savarnas to correct their mistakes and move forward, and to show a 'better tomorrow' where there is no Avarna-Savarna divide by including the Savarnas alongside the Avarnas. For example, it has shown us Ambedkar, who rose up in creation itself through his karma to write the Constitution from a position where the administrative sphere could not even be peeked into under any circumstance. Similarly, being born in this era, where our politics—advancing with socialism and other ideological codes without anyone realizing it—was blended with spirituality and portrayed like a historical truth to gain an administrative experience that does not consider gender differences, is a great fulfillment indeed. Praying to the Lord of the Universe (Jagatprabhu) that everyone may utilize their logic and intellect for actions that pave the way for the liberation of the soul, for the fulfillment of their material and spiritual life,

[GV1:P1539] Your Swami

[GV1:P1540] Guru Vani

[GV1:P1541] September 1985

[GV1:P1542] Because an error occurred in the previous lineage, it has not been possible to rectify it even after all this time. Today, each of us frequently says this. Similarly, Vedantins and scholars often use the phrase '*Aham Brahmasmi*'—meaning, 'I am Brahman'. However, it is worthwhile to consider whether we are truly entitled to say this about ourselves. Upon logical reflection, it is evident that it is untenable. What authority do we have to proclaim that we are Brahman? This is a fundamental flaw that crept into Hindu Vedanta. There is a widespread tendency among us to invoke Vedanta for everything. If you visit southern Travancore, even the pan-shop vendors will speak of Vedanta. This is not the case in the north. People have no hesitation in making casual remarks like, "That is how it should be, and it will happen." They say this not out of profound knowledge, but from mere hearsay. Repeating this incessantly, we eventually arrived at the phrase '*Aham Brahmasmi*'. None of us possesses the authority to declare this. We respect our father, but we do not show respect by addressing him by his name. Nor do we claim that we are our father ourselves. Our primary shortcoming is that we have not accorded even that basic level of reverence to this concept. Our Vedantins have taught us to address God as "You". That is permissible. This practice is most prevalently used in the Islamic faith. Prayers are offered by addressing the divine as "You". In recent times, it was Sree Narayana Guru who addressed the divine as "You". He stated, "You are Truth, Knowledge, and Bliss." Thus, one can pray by addressing God as "You". At the same time, it must be a prayer infused with a profound sense of dedication when using "You". Otherwise, it is inappropriate for individuals of a lower spiritual standing to do so. Is it not a misfortune if we fail to cultivate a capacity for true reverence? When the eloquence of prayer is replaced by mere logic, even if it is technically correct, does it not lose its profound humility? However, when a sense of absolute dedication arises, we can certainly practice it. There is nothing wrong in doing so.

[GV1:P1543] Here, even that has not been accomplished. We are actively being corrected. First, it was explicitly stated that we cannot use this term 'Brahman' so casually. Besides this, a statement was also provided to correct how we should properly perceive the concept of Brahman. We have long suffered from an inherent inability to foster true reverence. This deficiency has existed since the era of the *Trimurti*. We mold a piece of cow dung to represent Lord Ganesha. We place a flower on top of it. After a short while, we simply discard it. When women in the south perform the *Pongala* ritual, they consecrate Ganesha in such a remarkably trivial manner. This is not practiced in the north. In the north, they perform a ritual called *Udaya Pooja* once a year. Furthermore, it is not conducted in individual households. An entire agricultural field in a specific region is prepared for this purpose, and the people of that area gather to perform it collectively. It is executed with the utmost purity. These are practices established by our ancestors. The knowledge we have inherited suggests that none of this is entirely correct. Along with '*Aham Brahmasmi*', there also emerged the notion that the Shudra class is strictly forbidden from acquiring the knowledge of Brahman (*Brahmajnana*). Consequently, we find ourselves in this current predicament today. The mythological story of Vamana illustrates how a monarch who meticulously upheld truth and righteousness was trampled down into the netherworld simply because he was not a Brahmin. If a deity can trample down an emperor in such a manner, what prevents them from trampling us? Was it not a deity who descended and stomped on him? This fate befell Mahabali solely because he lacked the label of a Brahmin. His only crime was that someone who was supposedly prohibited from practicing truth and righteousness actually successfully upheld them. This reveals the deeply degraded and prejudiced history preserved by our ancestors. The writers of that era were merely mercenaries who penned such narratives for financial gain.

[GV1:P1544] In a similar vein, we have managed to persistently worship the exact opposite of whatever constituted the truth. Suchindram serves as a prime example. That is the very place where the *Trimurti* were utterly defeated and humiliated. Yet, today, it is the *Trimurti* who are worshipped there. What exactly does this defeat entail? The myth states that when the wife of a sage sprinkled the holy water used to wash her husband's feet, the *Trimurti* were instantly transformed into mere infants. This narrative is formally inscribed and illustrated there. It is also claimed that Devendra attained purification (*shuchi*) from that sage. That is precisely how the name Suchindram originated. Subsequently, Devendra is worshipped there. Whose fabrication is this? Are you not familiar with that story? When the *Trimurti* were turned into infants, their respective wives purportedly came to beg for their marital bliss and, by sprinkling holy water once again, restored them to their original forms. However, because the infants looked identical, they had to undertake severe penance just to identify their own husbands. Devendra, upon learning of this, came to the exact spot where Sage Atri had meditated, performed penance himself, and attained purity. Whose lie is it to continue worshipping Devendra there? They invent elaborate stories for this exact purpose. Vishnu worships Shiva. Shiva worships Vishnu. Both of them together worship Shakti. Besides these convoluted and repetitive tales, have these Vedantins or the worshippers of the *Trimurti* ever managed to articulate anything with absolute conceptual clarity? Every single one of us must critically ask this question. Since that time, Brahman has been attempting to reform them. The *Trimurti* did not manifest on their own. They were created specifically to rectify the fundamental errors of Manu. The *Devas* are inherently hedonistic. There is not a single narrative about a *Deva* that does not involve romantic or sexual exploits. The *Deva* is far more worldly than an ordinary human being. Thus, does a *Deva* even possess the time or inclination to attain liberation (*mukti*) or achieve purity of action (*karmashuddhi*)? There is merely a momentary blessing. Then, an immediate curse. That is all there is to it. For a long time, neither has been effectively occurring. We have unfortunately fallen for this mythological narrative. We must be capable of fundamentally altering this perspective. It is in this precise context that we have received the sacred word from Brahman.

[GV1:P1545] There have been individuals in the past who successfully transcended the state of the *Trimurti*. Consider the *Parayi Petta Panthirukulam* (the twelve children born to a Pariah woman)—there is only one female among them. That is Avvaiyar. All twelve of these individuals surpassed the elevated status of the *Trimurti*. They conclusively demonstrated that they could achieve far more in this earthly realm than the *Trimurti* themselves. Poets have traditionally elevated the *Trimurti* and enshrined them at the absolute pinnacle. However, the children of the Pariah woman treated this concept almost as a jest. For instance, the Vedic priests of Ambalappuzha failed miserably in their attempt to consecrate the goddess. The idol simply would not firmly set. It was only when Pakkanar entered, spat the remnants of his chewed betel leaves (creating a *thambalam* or spittoon), and performed the consecration that it firmly took hold. That is exactly how Thambalappuzha eventually became Ambalappuzha. A remarkably similar incident occurred at the Cherthala Karthyayani temple. Naranath Bhranthan caught a prawn, tied it securely with a string, dangled it, and was wandering along the road babbling like a madman. The Vedic priests had been desperately trying for three days, but the consecration would not fix firmly. He arrived, hung that very prawn right in front of the temple, went inside, and commanded the deity, "Sit here, woman," firmly establishing her presence. He even uttered two expletives. The consecration was perfectly secured.

[GV1:P1546] No *Deva* holds any significance before a true *Jnani* (an enlightened sage). An individual named Gunangudi Masthan has also performed similar feats. Just like Naranath Bhranthan, the other eleven also logically and comprehensively rejected the conventional worship of *Devas*. They did not claim that God does not exist. Rather, they profoundly corrected us on who we should become and how we should conduct ourselves. Unfortunately, we were utterly unable to follow that enlightened path. That is the absolute truth. The exact same realization occurred in my own personal experience. When it powerfully manifested in my experience, a necessary correction was consequently received.

[GV1:P1547] If you observe this vow (*vratam*) with strict discipline, you will attain a certain merit after completing fifty vows. Let us resolve to perform this properly for five years. If done for ninety days in the manner of an ascetic (*sanyasi*) within these five years, the result of five years will be achieved. Afterwards, it should not be done in that exact manner. Do it by changing the pattern. The Monday vows and Sunday vows of the past are no longer necessary. God has provided an opportunity to do only this for everything. What does this mean? In order to correct the mistakes made by our predecessors, we, who know nothing, have been brought to the forefront and made to undertake that karma. It is not the wise or the learned who are coming forward. It is for you and your children at the grassroots level to create the fruits of your karma as your own. Understand that this karma is what improves you. Since the time of Lord Krishna, karma has held a significant place. No god or goddess can bestow anything through blessings other than the merit of the karma performed by a human being. We used to believe they would. However, we have not been able to see it so far. You have received an opportunity for this. That is it. We celebrate the Pournami Vratam (full moon vow) here. But you must change your perspective a little. It is a vow for liberation (*mukti*) that supersedes all other vows. Yet, it is completely worldly as well. Why is it said that it grants the right to liberation and the right to complete worldliness? Both the sun and the moon have a role in the creation of all living beings. The sun brings about evolution in everything, just like a hen incubating its eggs. At the same time, the cosmic dust falling from the moon also has its own significance. The earth also has a role.

[GV1:P1548] It is said that the growth and decline of this earth stem from our karma. India (Bharatam) is considered a good land. The Puranas state that being born in Bharatam is itself a great glory. The righteousness of Bharata's rule still exists here. Even though we have had the privilege of obtaining the best country on earth, blessed by the lives of the people of those times, the shortcoming that remains with us is our inability to imbibe its goodness. This must be changed. The divine will (*Brahmanischayam*) itself has brought us to the forefront to change this. To correct this aforementioned mistake, great souls were created initially. They were born here and there, imparted their teachings, and departed. Our ancestors, comprising Vedic scholars, pundits, and poets, have concocted several lame stories for us. Hearing those stories, we stand bewildered—wondering what to do next. You must understand this, act upon it, and come forward.
