[GV1:P2335] April 1989

[GV1:P2336] Everything has been brought forth by imposing a specific limit within which every reality grows and organizes itself. This can be observed if one looks at Christianity, Islam, and Buddhism. When peering into the traditional history of the Hindus, it is continually compared with the concepts of various sages (*Rishis*). The *Shakteya* will claim that Shaktism is the greatest. The *Shaiva* will claim that Shaivism is the greatest. The *Vaishnava* will claim that Vaishnavism is the greatest. It is never stated that these three are fundamentally identical and correct. They are also coupled with other karmas possessing contradictory attributes. All that has been said is indeed true. But have we looked deeply enough into the light of time?

[GV1:P2337] A specific history suitable for each era was brought forth. Since world history had not traveled so extensively back then. Why?

[GV1:P2338] Disciple: It was not written down.

[GV1:P2339] Guru: It is not that it was not written down. It was done in their own manner. The Hindus have a history spanning a vast period. Writing existed in those times. Writing of some form or another. Or rather, there was a certain concept. Otherwise, how could such a monumental historical truth emerge? If one examines history from a Western perspective, one finds records of fifteen hundred years; four thousand years; or at most, eight thousand years; a Western history extending beyond this is not seen. The history written in the modern method is only that much. Even before this, life existed everywhere.

[GV1:P2340] How was this life for humanity? Or what were they? It is not easy to reflect upon and comprehend such things about that era. Therefore, it must be left at that. There is no point in becoming exhausted by thinking about it. Regarding what exists today, one must write about India keeping in the forefront the life of the recent prophet, that is, from the time of Moses, or the life of Jesus or the Prophet (Muhammad), or the life of Buddha or Krishna. Otherwise, you will go astray.

[GV1:P2341] Disciple: Hinduism emerged through various paths, namely the paths of Hindu prophets—the *Shaiva*, *Shakteya*, and *Vaishnava* traditions, as well as the paths of numerous other Hindu preceptors. How can this be reconciled with Judaism and the religious traditions of Moses, Jesus, the Prophet, and others? These are prophetic religions. Here too, there were sages (*Rishis*) who resembled prophets. However, it does not clearly appear as a continuous tradition. What does the Guru have to say about this?

[GV1:P2342] Guru: It has appeared clearly. But we lacked the familiarity to elucidate it. What intruded in the meantime is an ignorance akin to a demonic nature. For example, how do you understand Vaishnavism?

[GV1:P2343] Disciple: As a Hindu, I harbored a traditional understanding. All the ten incarnations (*Dashavatara*) are incarnations of Lord Vishnu. Furthermore, Buddha is also included among those incarnations. It was the understanding that *Brahma*, *Vishnu*, and *Maheshwara* are the deities responsible for creation, preservation, and destruction.

[GV1:P2344] Guru: Therefore, because such imaginative *karmas* became ingrained among us, the *karma* that reveals reality became obscured, as if hidden away. Later, upon coming into that *karma* of 'imaginative-karmic-mind' (*Sankalpakarmamanasa*)—consider it merely as a *Shakteya* karma—when observing its primordial root, in order to sustain a certain concept or interest within nature, what man discovered from nature was how to influence or utilize the truths of nature. When the zenith (intensity) of human evolution occurs, man transforms into a deity (*Deva*), a sage (*Rishi*), or an ascetic (*Sannyasin*). After cultivating human intensity, compassion and empathy must be brought back. For example, when peering into the domain of a small insect, what operates there are their respective bonds of affection. If one examines how that bond of affection develops, one can see that it reflects certain phenomena occurring today across this earth, the sea, and the sky. We are in a helpless state where we cannot determine how many celestial bodies exist within this celestial sphere. Now, the Earth is a sphere. The Moon is a sphere, the Sun is a sphere—these three are visible to our eyes. However, it has been scientifically discovered today that there are stars much larger than all these spheres. What must exist within this universe (*Andakadaha*)? What must not exist? This must be determined by a visionary. Or, by a holy personage who can be measured alongside an era. Without reflecting on what the conception of that holy personage is or from where that holy personage obtained it, we simply believe in him, and continue to believe. Or, believing in something, we revolve around it and attempt to develop all aspects of life through it.

[GV1:P2345] Disciple: Were Buddha, Mahavira (Jain), Christ, and the Prophet all such holy personages?

[GV1:P2346] Guru: They were. But it cannot be said that they were all global traditionalists. However, they can be made so. Because if an idea can be imparted to all people—imparting even the smallest idea is sufficient. For example, the Christian ideology.

[GV1:P2347] Disciple: Is the Guru suggesting that it is small?

[GV1:P2348] Guru: Yes.

[GV1:P2349] Disciple: In what way is it small?

[GV1:P2350] Guru: It is small in proportion to its conceptual *karma*.

[GV1:P2351] Disciple: It would be better if this were clarified further.

[GV1:P2352] Guru: It is the sacrifice of Christ that has accorded Christ such a preeminent position. When considering the magnitude of its concept, it is small.

[GV1:P2353] Disciple: Is not sacrifice a great concept?

[GV1:P2354] Guru: Sacrifice becomes great only to the extent of that individual's sacrifice, does it not? Is sacrifice as great as the magnitude of the concept?

[GV1:P2355] Disciple: Do you mean that sacrifice is only one part?

[GV1:P2356] Guru: Yes.
