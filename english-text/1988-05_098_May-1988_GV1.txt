[GV1:P1939] May 1988

[GV1:P1940] It is hoped that the unexpected displacement at Sivagiri will serve as an occasion not only for Sivagiri but also for the believers of Narayana Guru and the lineage that loves the acharyas to accept it as a model, and for the members of the Dharma Sangham to attain complete strength. It is known that it is not right to judge the past leaders of the Dharma Sangham for anything other than a good purpose. However, it is painful that a Secretary worked for seventeen years as if he were everything for the Dharma Sangham on behalf of that SNDP, for which the Guru had to write that he resigns from the SNDP Yogam in thought, word, and deed. In other words, their actions only succeeded in creating an impression among other community members that the Dharma Sangham belongs not to Narayana Guru but to the SNDP, and that the Swami worked for the community.

[GV1:P1941] I remember advocate P. Madhavan, who belonged to the same community, writing an article in Kerala Kaumudi when such speeches and actions were taking place, purportedly for the sake of the community. That article made it clear that Narayana Guru was not someone who thought about eradicating caste in a trivial manner. Similarly, although many people were deeply hurt, they were in a helpless situation where they could not speak out or act because they lacked the means to do so. The intelligent and capable people within the community caused considerable pain to the Swami while he was alive, in their attempt to make him the property of the SNDP. The ignorance of the community leaders of that time distressed the Swami at the felicitation meeting for his sixtieth birthday, held at the Alappuzha Kidangamparambu temple ground. The harassment that began that day continued until the time of his Samadhi. Even three days prior to his Samadhi, without giving him any peace, they troubled him by approaching him with a will to bequeath assets for the community. Having worked for this cause for eleven years, when the Swami realized that they would only stand contrary to his ideals, he was heartbroken and wrote, "I resign from the SNDP in thought, word, and deed." On his way to Madras for Dr. Pandala's treatment, he gathered others and registered the Dharma Sangham, consisting of fourteen members, at Koorkanchery in Thrissur. It was because Thrissur was under British rule at that time that the Swami accomplished this there itself. The present-day Dharma Sangham must be able to deeply comprehend this pain of the Guru and rectify the errors. It is for this purpose that all this is being documented at this juncture.

[GV1:P1942] Nataraja Guru was also included among the fourteen members of the Dharma Sangham at that time. He was excluded from the Dharma Sangham only because he affixed one signature on the bylaw but refrained from putting the second signature. However, the members of the Dharma Sangham did not show any lack of affection towards Nataraja Guru. The evidence for this is that Nataraja Guru was selected as the advisor to the Dharma Sangham during the tenure of Swami Achyuthananda. When the Swami slapped a young man named G.N. Das, who served as Swami Achyuthananda's cook, for some mistake, he took him along, departed to the Gurukulam, and resigned from the position of advisor. After that incident, he did not behave affectionately towards Sivagiri. He would visit Sivagiri, pay his respects, and leave, but maintained no other relationship. On that occasion, Swami Brahmananda and I went to the Gurukulam. At that time, Swami Brahmananda was known as Narayanan. Having joined the army and returned during the time of Swami Achyuthananda, he had left in displeasure because he was not conferred the name Brahmanandan. It was when he returned to Sivagiri again and was working as a clerk there that the two of us visited the Gurukulam. While discussing various matters concerning Narayana Guru there, Nataraja Guru made a remark: "Yes, the Swami had to act in such a manner back then." Upon hearing this, we did not stay much longer; we had to leave after briefly discussing this very matter.

[GV1:P1943] Within two months of saying this, he prepared to depart for America. I was performing pooja at the Samadhi at that time. Nataraja Guru arrived at the Samadhi accompanied by a saffron-clad individual who went by the name Paramanandan. He asked Nataraja Guru, "What should we expect regarding your ability to make Narayana Guru known to the outside world once you go to America?" The reply was, "If the circumstances are favorable, and if God provides the opportunity, I hope to be able to do what is necessary." A year and a half after his departure, a letter arrived for the Madathipathi Swami at Sivagiri, which read as follows: "I am reminded of the proverb that the jasmine in the front yard has no fragrance—because, sometime after arriving there, I was able to reside on top of a mountain, secured accommodation in a room next to the British Government Library, and was able to refer to British Philosophy. Thus, I was able to read clearly about Islam, Hinduism, the Parsis, the propagation of Christianity by the Portuguese, and their period of governance. I have also been able to evaluate Jesus." This is how the first letter proceeded. Two more letters arrived subsequently. Each letter would typically arrive after an interval of ten or fifteen months.

[GV1:P1944] "It is only now that I have completely understood Narayana Guru. I feel distressed when I realize that I received a first-class merely for writing down two lines from Narayana Guru's *Darshanamala*." Nataraja Guru, who until then had not believed in touching the Guru's feet or in the Indian monastic traditions, wrote in his final letter, "When I return there, I will certainly proclaim the Guru's greatness and customs to the people." As a result of instructing Swami Sankarananda that all three of these letters should be read in the prayer meeting, they were indeed read aloud. The time of his return to Varkala from America and the specific train he would arrive on were also communicated.

[GV1:P1945] The locals and Sivagiri jointly organized a reception at the railway station. That was a period when the Dharma Sangham was split into two factions, and the conflict was at its peak. A year prior to that—in the Malayalam year 1125—Swami Nijananda was standing ousted and excommunicated. He approached me and said, "I have no money, you must string a garland for me." As per his request to conduct the worship by adorning the Guru's portrait, I strung together Rajamalli flowers and Ashoka leaves and provided it to him. While handing it over after the worship, I had advised, "You should attempt to place our garland first." I had crafted the garland with the intention that each flower should represent a garland. Amidst the list of various organizations and individuals, the garlanding by Swami Nijananda inadvertently became the ninth. Out of curiosity to witness this, I too had gone. However, even as he was being helped down from the train, Nijananda had already placed the garland. Although a major altercation ensued over this, it yielded results. At the conference held at Vasu Talkies the following week, Sadana Mathavu Narayani Amma of Kollam stated, "Does not everyone refer to the Swami as Sree Narayana Guru? Similarly, it is my request that he be addressed as Nataraja Guru, and everyone should accept this." The conference passed the resolution unanimously. At least some individuals in Varkala are aware of this matter.

[GV1:P1946] After his return from America, wherever he sat, tender coconut, bananas, and puffed rice would be placed on a leaf nearby. This was intended as an offering in reverence to the Guru. Many might not have noticed the purpose behind it. Furthermore, he strictly mandated others to perform prostration at his feet. Prior to his departure to America, Nataraja Guru was an individual who rejected such customs. Once he realized his error, he corrected it and ensured that others corrected it as well.

[GV1:P1947] A faction of Dharma Sangham members—nine of them, from Krishnanandan to Ramanandan—objected to the idea of handing over the land near Varkala College to the SNDP. It was Kumaraswami and Swami Ramananda who played the prominent role in conducting the litigation that spanned ten years between the SNDP and the Dharma Sangham. When these nine individuals were expelled from Sivagiri, I was consecutively transferred to seven branch ashrams solely for serving coffee to Kumaraswami, who was one among them. Later, when he won the case and returned, a complaint was raised that I did not accord him proper respect. During the course of this litigation, this Kumaraswami safeguarded the monks by burying the keys and standing guard with a machete for seven years. The ousted Saswathikananda is a relative of that Kumaraswami. Yet, he could not even recall that sorrow. To this day, Sivagiri has not been able to even compile a factual history of these events.

[GV1:P1948] Swami Anandatheerthan primarily handled the legal aspects of the case, and upon its conclusion, he was installed as the President. However, not only was he removed from his position by fabricating false cases, but the then Secretary also had the cunningness to execute a document stating that the thirty acres of land belonging to the Dharma Sangham would automatically become the property of the SNDP if taxes were paid for twelve years.

[GV1:P1949] At that time, Nataraja Guru, Mangalanandan, and Anandatheerthan jointly raised a claim through the *Gurukulam* magazine, questioning why the Dharma Sangham could not manage such a college. In response, some thugs from Varkala gathered at the gates of the Gurukulam and hurled abuses, and since Dayanandan, who had recently joined the Dharma Sangham, directed unmentionable remarks at Nataraja Guru, they withdrew from the initiative.

[GV1:P1950] However, when Anandatheerthan stayed back at Sivagiri and advocated for this cause, these individuals hid in Viswambharan's shop and orchestrated an attack on Anandatheerthan when he stepped out. I am also aware of the history where that case was settled for two thousand rupees. At the time, no one knew who the assailant was. Yet, that very person later cried out from a hospital bed, confessing that his leg had to be amputated because he had assaulted that particular individual. All these facts are known to the current President, Swami Madhavananda. Therefore, I pray for the Guru's blessings and the benevolent wishes of the people so that the Dharma Sangham may continue to exist truly as the Dharma Sangham, and to make the public realize that it is not merely a communal organization.
