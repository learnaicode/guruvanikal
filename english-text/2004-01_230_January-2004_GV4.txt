[GV4:P0961] January 2004

[GV4:P0962] In this concept referred to as Manvantara, the matter of Brahma is not evident. It is only in the seventh Manvantara that the concept of Brahma is observed. You might even question whether God is unaware of why such a concept arose. Whenever a kingdom or a position is conferred, when that position is assumed, it is taken up in accordance with the respective era. Our ancestors lacked the experience to assume this position. If dharma in accordance with the times is not enforced, it will result in a flaw within this evolutionary theory. Therefore, it was introduced without allowing such an error. However, this is perceived only up to the heavenly realms by certain seers and a few other individuals. The reality of the rest is different. It ultimately comes down to the calculations of a solar system. It will have to be articulated how many Brahmas exist for the solar system, what their nature is, what they are, and why they came into being. Living a life devoid of this knowledge, or when the greatest scholar rewrites following the texts written until then, and attains success to the extent of their fortune, it is reduced to mere literal interpretation. Consequently, the meaning of the word changes. When it changes in this manner, numerous kinds of confusions arise among us. Readers of the Ramayana will exaggerate and state it emphatically. How so? 'Ra' must change. When asked what that implies, they assert that likes and dislikes (*Raga-Dvesha*) must change. Most people fail to consider whether altering these likes and dislikes is something anyone can practically achieve while alive. Then emerges the theory of scholars who chase after the poetic imagination which dictates that until 'Ra' changes, 'Ma' cannot be grasped. In order to demonstrate who was the greater scholar in the royal court of their respective times, if scholars competitively wrote and recited their works, the king did not necessarily need to comprehend south, north, east, or west. He merely needed to endorse it. The scholars have thus documented that all these accomplishments were brought about during this particular ruler's reign. With that, the matter was deemed settled.

[GV4:P0963] Many such instances have occurred among us. Maha Vishnu, who trampled righteous rulers down to the netherworld (*Patala*) under the pretext that they lacked absolute decorum, was born here. If one asks why he was trampled into the netherworld in this manner, it is because he was classified as an Asura there. Thus, because he happened to be an Asura emperor, they instituted their law stipulating that he was not permitted to rule. Therefore, to establish that a Brahmin rules during that Sudra era within the Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, and Sudra systemic framework according to their laws, this individual had to be eliminated by fabricating a narrative about the emperor. However, the story propagated is highly illogical. Three paces of land were requested; when that was deemed insufficient, his head was also trampled down. You need not harbor any doubt that this is merely the logic of a poet. Just as it is narrated regarding Brahma, so too about Narada, or concerning numerous Brahmarakshasas, or in the accounts of countless angels, or certain celestial beings and the so-called djinns; by presenting various such accounts and leading humanity astray through literal interpretations, divine will must inevitably intervene at some point. Otherwise, by miscalculating the Manvantaras, it will be impossible to usher in a new Manvantara. Therefore, a resolution is only possible if a wealth of preceptorship (*Acharya Sampath*) emerges, capable of conveying to the people what must be contributed to the Manvantara and explaining why errors occurred within it.

[GV4:P0964] The subsequent thought is regarding what kind of person that Acharya (preceptor) should be. This cannot be altered by an ordinary path of a Guru, or a path of renunciation (Sanyasa), or a minor prophetic path. Only someone who possesses a long-term vision spanning the duration of a Manvantara can change it. Such a long-term vision means understanding the past—how, after six Manvantaras, this came to be in the seventh Manvantara, where its cause lies, and what the reason behind that cause is; only a Mahatma capable of explaining this can set it right. This is because it is a mistake that occurred when a great soul of this Manvantara did the right thing. The fact is that there were no experienced individuals to hand over to us the perfect path of how to perform the right action. Speaking of experienced ones, there are modes of worship ranging from a deity and a demon up to numerous Brahmarakshasas. What has happened in the mode of worship, who all are involved in it, and if there is a duty to grant them birth or liberation by reaching their level in the matter of their existence, the Acharya can move forward only after fulfilling that. This is something that not many can achieve. However, reaching the position of what is called the virtue of the soul (Atmasukrutham)—of gods and goddesses—is also Atmasukrutham itself. After that, what are the positions of the Ganas, and what all Ganas are there—reaching their position is also possible only through Atmasukrutham. But beyond this, there is another state. When all these beings reach their respective places, they attain a celestial realm (Akashamandalam) commensurate with their position, according to their knowledge. That celestial realm, whether they call it the vast expanse (Vettaveli) or as mentioned in the verses of the Ishavasya Upanishad, extends even from such a pinnacle. It is not known how or in what manner one should be guided to that pinnacle. In this, when each demon and each deity appears, one must explain what was initially available for worship in the lineage of the primordial Manu (Aadimanuvu). After explaining that and accustoming them to it, corrections should be made at the stage involving gods, goddesses, sages (Rishis), and ascetics (Sanyasis). A disciple must grow to that level after this correction. There is a possibility that it could be a male or a female. What is that male and female? There are two divisions in that as well. If the male and female stand bound together, masculinity cannot be imparted to the female. For that, they themselves state a name—Napumsakam (gender-neutral). It cannot be given in that state either. Then, how many are the states of the Goddess, how many states exist from this demon to the deity, what are the transitional steps one passes through, and what that karma is—showing these to a disciple and having that disciple impart it to the higher realms of this world is the true aspect. Instead of that, if we go back to the written concepts of Manvantara and try to bring back the original nature of Manu, how do we correct the false narratives that portrayed him as the son of Brahma? Where is the Vedic knowledge located to reclaim the lineage of Manu alongside the lineage of the Trimurti? When viewed this way, numerous things will have to be corrected and rewritten. But this will not happen in one lifetime or even ten lifetimes. Then what is the use of setting out to do it? It is impossible. Then, based on what was seen, they bundled it up and created texts, created letters for the text, and created interpretations for the letters. Thus, assigning sound, form, and shape, they will arrive at an action embellished with ornaments in every note. This embellished action is what we artists call a musical concert. That is an ornamentation. The task is to assign sound, give form to that sound, merge into that form, give meaning to the sound that emerges from it—from within—and study it. Well, if all this could lead us to God, we would not have any other afflictions. It is our Mahatmas themselves who have proven that none of this is what is required. For that, many Gurus from the Parayi Petta Panthirukulam (the twelve clans born to the Pariah woman) and those raised in the Brahmin clan will write down the logic of the Brahmin. Those born in the clan of Shaivites will write down that Shaivism is everything. Otherwise, if one is a Shaktheyan, that is everything for them. These interpreters of letters have brought us to a point where we cannot know which is greater or which is smaller. We have become so corrupted that we are unable to show any sincerity of our own to move away from this. It is not our fault. We flowed along in a course of karma that respected the obstacles encountered along the path of passing times, without analyzing what those obstacles were. It will not be possible in one or two generations to correct this, rewrite it, and convince the people about the actual paths traversed by numerous figures who commonly appear among us now, such as Agastya, Bhogar, or others like Bhishma. People will repeatedly cling to the old and forget the story of the new again. This is a habituation we have been undergoing for so many ages.

[GV4:P0965] Guruvani
