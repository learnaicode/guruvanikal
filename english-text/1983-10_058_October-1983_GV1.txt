[GV1:P1343] October 1983

[GV1:P1344] How have we brought about the value of human life? We have been entrusted with the task of correcting this value from the wrong path and bringing it back to the right path. First, we must learn how to perform this duty. That is what was meant earlier when it was said that we are starting at the very beginning. What has been done now is the beginning of every part. You must not make mistakes in such a beginning. If a mistake is made, it will also become subject to divine punishment.

[GV1:P1345] You are merely rolling things around in your intellect without understanding any of this. Intellect should not be used in such a manner. Mistakes will occur. Whether right or wrong, things must be reported to the Guru exactly as they are. It was mentioned earlier that the Guru might even ask one to commit a mistake. Why is this so? To dislodge a karma that has become firmly rooted through errors, the disciple himself must be made to commit a mistake. For this purpose, the Guru, knowingly or unknowingly, may utter a word that leads to that mistake. Then the disciple will be shaken, thinking, "Alas! I have done this," and subsequently correct it by reversing the action. For instance, it is like something revolving from one to another. What if one fails to rotate? Both (right and wrong) must inevitably rotate.

[GV1:P1346] You should at least understand this much before initiating the karma. Otherwise, the karma will not be perfected. There are those who argue, "That is wrong; if he is a Guru, would he do such a thing or make someone do it?" It is unknown what knowledge prompts them to speak in this manner. Their understanding remains at such a rudimentary level. The objective is to teach the disciple the subtle aspects of the committed error and the reasons behind its occurrence, thereby correcting him. Only then does he attain perfection in it.

[GV1:P1347] What we require today is to identify where our karma has gone wrong, where our ancestors have erred, and what must be done to prevent future generations from making mistakes—these constitute our objectives. When correcting the mistakes that have occurred in karma, we must endure significant hardships. Thus, the karma is redirected into an entirely different course.

[GV1:P1348] It is impossible not to make mistakes; errors will happen. Only if a mistake occurs in karma can it be corrected. Therefore, there is no need to fear when a mistake happens. However, one should not intentionally commit errors. It does not mean that since mistakes do not matter, one can allow them to happen. If there is even the slightest speck of doubt in our conscious mind, we must halt right there. Further action should only be taken after seeking clarification. Otherwise, it will lead to an error, necessitating a backward journey to rectify it. That situation should be avoided.

[GV1:P1349] When we examine the lives of Vyasa, Vasishta, and Viswamitra, we observe that they committed numerous mistakes and subsequently corrected them. Countless errors can be seen in their lives. Why has this been demonstrated? Could they not have proceeded without making mistakes? If we consider all the Gurus, we can observe this pattern. Therefore, understanding this, we must purify our karma. In this manner, we must advance by questioning, discussing, and acting upon every single aspect. This is what we are required to do now.

[GV1:P1350] Guruvani
