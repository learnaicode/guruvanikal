[GV1:P0379] November 1978

[GV1:P0380] Question: What is the initial nature of devotion?

[GV1:P0381] Answer: Attentiveness. There will be a nature of paying attention to whatever is seen. According to the Hindu tradition, there are numerous deities. In the beginning of devotion, the devotion towards gods and goddesses will keep changing. We might feel that we are good. In reality, if we observe closely, we will understand if there is anyone worse than us. A Guru is there to change the course of karma from previous births.

[GV1:P0382] Question: When devotion arises, will there be any flaws in it?

[GV1:P0383] Answer: When devotion arises, although great love and glory may come at first, there are some flaws in it too. That is—those living in the next house might have base qualities. Looking at them, one might say, "Alas, they are wretched people." They will also retort, "Oh! Such devotion—chanting starts as soon as it dawns." When all this devotion comes to us, some among lust, anger, greed, delusion, arrogance, pride, and jealousy will disappear, while some others will surge up.

[GV1:P0384] Question: What is the reason for that?

[GV1:P0385] Answer: Didn't I say that desire (*Kama*) stands at the forefront in devotion? It will eagerly jump forward for a good color, a good object, and everything else.

[GV1:P0386] Question: You mentioned that three things can be understood through philosophical vision, didn't you? How can this be understood?

[GV1:P0387] Answer: One can know it by paying special attention to its color. However, it is not the same for a devotee, a yogi, a wise man (jnani), and a man of action (karmi). If it is a devotee, they see everything through the Guru. If it is a yogi, they might wander off the path. If the Guru is there to guide closely, then let them wander. If it is a jnani, they can see with discrimination. However, for a karmi, it can be seen only when combined with their own effort.

[GV1:P0388] Question: O Guru! What is the reason for attaining it in such various natures?

[GV1:P0389] Answer: I shall explain. If it is a devotee, there is no sense of 'I'. They will surrender everything to the Guru. If it is a yogi, it will be in the manner of 'I am everything'. They will give priority to their own affairs. They will speak of everything trivially. No matter what thoughts arise, not denying anything and not blaming anything is one of the greatest fortunes.

[GV1:P0390] Guruvani
