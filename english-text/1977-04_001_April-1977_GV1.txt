[GV1:P0001] Guruvani

[GV1:P0002] April 1977

[GV1:P0003] Question: When there is devotion, many extraneous thoughts that are incompatible with devotion also tend to arise. What is the reason for this?

[GV1:P0004] Answer: No matter how extraneous thoughts arise, they will not be able to overcome true devotion. Devotion is the perfection of love. The nature of true devotion is to view one's chosen deity or guru as God. Above all, a person who desires the love of God may have to experience extreme sorrows from the deity or the trusted guru. On such occasions, one should find peace in the thought that all this is meant to cleanse the impurities arising from one's sinful karma, and must remain steadfast in love again and again. If one observes the sacrifice of such an individual, it will not even appear as a sacrifice. This is because he is able to perceive all sorrows as full of love and as nectarine. The love he develops towards all living beings will depend on the nature of his surrender to God.

[GV1:P0005] Question: Why do such people experience sorrow from others?

[GV1:P0006] Answer: Sorrow must occur. It is meant to come. Even if he is convinced that everything he does is good, if the people around him readily accept it, he would never know what sorrow is.

[GV1:P0007] Question: He is only doing such good deeds, isn't he? Then why does he have to experience sorrow?

[GV1:P0008] Answer: That is true. Firstly, without experiencing some sorrow, he cannot understand what sacrifice is. If he did not have sorrow, the very concept of sacrifice could not exist in this human world. Or suppose it happens to him without any sorrow; would he be able to understand the sorrow of another person? In that case, he can neither learn through his own experience nor understand the sorrow of others.

[GV1:P0009] Question: What exactly should be the complete nature of a true devotee?

[GV1:P0010] Answer: The nature of a true devotee is exactly what was mentioned above. However, let me elaborate further. His greatest asset is maintaining the purity of character to hate nothing and to perceive everything with love. Acknowledging any sorrow, behaving affectionately, and transforming it into one's own experience by deeply understanding it. This implies that he himself becomes the seat of both sorrow and happiness. Perceiving the earth as a bed of flowers. Viewing deep forests filled with stones and thorns as beautiful gardens. The profound experience he gets upon reaching such a place—he will then relish all the beauty of the world. Through that, he might even perceive God. Thus, the fulfilled mind that sees God in everything can be summed up in a single term: supreme wisdom.

[GV1:P0011] Question: If so, what do you say is the nature of true devotion?

[GV1:P0012] Answer: Everything else is merely for the fulfillment of various desires. All of this will ultimately lead to disappointment.

[GV1:P0013] Question: Just because devotion has been somewhat compromised, why should it lead to sorrow?

[GV1:P0014] Answer: Sorrow did not arise because there was a compromise in devotion. It is because the mountain of their desires has collapsed.

[GV1:P0015] Question: He might not have desired it; isn't it merely that a deficiency occurred in his devotion?

[GV1:P0016] Answer: That is true. Haven't you seen it? The kind of devotion where people stand in front of temples and burst into tears. Do you know in how many different ways they cry?

[GV1:P0017] Question: How does a devotee experience numerous such outbursts of crying?

[GV1:P0018] Answer: Excellent. It is unlikely that anyone else cries as much as a devotee does. Even a true devotee experiences a great deal of crying; then, for a devotee who compromises, it will be a multitude of tears.

[GV1:P0019] Question: If that is the case, devotion is not good, is it? Won't devotion only leave time for crying?

[GV1:P0020] Answer: I told you earlier that a true devotee does not cry.

[GV1:P0021] Question: But you also mentioned that a true devotee experiences a lot of crying. You spoke in a contradictory manner at the same time. It would be good if you could explain this.

[GV1:P0022] Answer: A true devotee beholds the form of his chosen deity in all animate and inanimate beings with utmost joy. Then, unknowingly, he is moved to tears by the love he feels upon experiencing the glory of God. Thus, a devotee who has realized this in various ways will cry out of love. Despite doing all this, when those who lack the wisdom to comprehend this love sadden him by spreading false tales about him, he may also weep thinking, "They are hurting me out of ignorance; doesn't God see this?"
