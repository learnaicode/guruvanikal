[GV4:P0671] October 2002

[GV4:P0672] In what all ways have our ancestors provided guidance for our sake, or for the sake of this worldly knowledge, or in the financial and knowledge-related fields of the world! They have shown us by finding and applying every matter suitable for guidance. One of the most important among them is Ayurveda. It is the Veda that holds the secret of life.

[GV4:P0673] According to the topography of our land, harsh administration of medicines should not be done. Ingredients like mercury, sulphur, and arsenic should not be applied as they are. However, they can be processed, brought to the practice called Gurukalpa, and used in the Gurukalpa Seva system. But the Western method of application is not like that. Today, there is a speed there akin to boiling rice in five minutes. The medicines given to us are those prepared using ashes (bhasmas) and various other such materials. They quickly affect the body. This will harm the body.

[GV4:P0674] There are reasons why the way Westerners produce and apply medicine is different from ours. One is the difference in topography. Another is the lack of availability of medicinal plants. It is difficult to get plant varieties there. But we do not have that difficulty. Any kind of plant that grows in our forests is medicinal. However, there are also difficulties in utilizing these medicinal plants. Our ancestors have also shown us the ways to overcome those difficulties. The same systems that have existed since very ancient times are still in existence today. But today, that is not enough. Why?

[GV4:P0675] Let us assume that someone manifested a god, someone manifested a goddess—or rather, that they even manifested and fulfilled the great sage Dhanvantari himself, who is considered the most complete figure in medical science. They might have prescribed two or four medicines to this worshipper. Taking the medicine obtained in this way, considering it together with his ascetic power and the theory he has been following, he will apply it to some people. And the disease will be cured. Does that mean we should assume that the diseases of the entire world are cured?

[GV4:P0676] The disease will be cured. All those are truthful aspects. But this is not enough. What we need is a precept to act by seeing and knowing in the action that is changing momentarily. Kali is the best epoch suited to create such a thing. This is God-given. If we continue to burn and distill as was done in past times, the Dharma of this Kali will not be fulfilled. Everything that exists is true. But for which time is this to be applied? We must think about this time. That is Kali. Kali is the time we have been given to be born.

[GV4:P0677] It is said that even some gods have wished to be born as at least a blade of grass in Kali. Why? That desire is because if they were born as at least a blade of grass in this Bharata, they could fulfill this karma in the growth and glory of this nature. But we have suffered some kind of loss to the extent that we are unable to be born and grow in that Bharata and honor the Dharma that has imparted vigilance and truth.

[GV4:P0678] During the time of Sree Rama, Bharadwaja brought forth the science of aeronautics (Vimana Shastra). His Guru had told Bharadwaja that such a science was not needed for that era. If the flying machine (Vimana) was built, it would lead to everyone's destruction. But Bharadwaja did not obey. He went to Kubera's kingdom and had the king there build it. The war occurred because that flying machine was used to come here and abduct Sita. We all presume this to be a war between Rama and Ravana. But it was entirely a struggle between a Guru and his disciple. Consequently, the kingdom itself was destroyed. The calamities resulting from not doing what is necessary at the appropriate time have still not left us today. After that, we have never been able to regain the sanctity of that Guru-disciple relationship.

[GV4:P0679] Let someone else make the goods. We will get them too. But that is not equal to the Dharma (duty/nature) of our life (Jeeva). Our birth is meant for thinking and making others think about the Dharma of life. Our soul is confined within an instinctual origin (Vasanas), having seen, heard, known, and acted upon good and bad across many births. We must absolutely possess a state of mind capable of contemplating what system is required to understand that soul.

[GV4:P0680] If a scholar sees a poor man doing something, he will only treat it with contempt. The scholar has the attitude that he knows everything. But what is this poor man doing? He takes a fallen man onto his lap, nurses him, and speaks to him about good and bad. But the scholar does not know how to do this. He [the poor man] does not have the scholar's attire or education. Without these two, what else can be done!

[GV4:P0681] What is this nursing? It is a task that depletes one's merit (Punya). At the same time, the other person's merit increases. There is a Karma (action) that can reclaim this lost merit. What is that dedicated work? You will not attain it by simply setting out somewhere. For that, one goes and knocks on the doors of a deity whose eyes remain closed, crying, "Oh Lord, didn't you see, haven't I been calling and doing all this for so long?" But the Lord will open His eyes. There is a way for that. That path can only be known while your soul is still residing within its cage (the body).

[GV4:P0682] If we have an educated scholar, an officer, and a doctor who knows the body by dissecting it—thus various kinds of people—in our house, the children of that house will walk around with quite a bit of arrogance. Does carrying this arrogance mean that they know about their home, their land, and their country just because they have all this? By instilling this arrogance, this ego, into these children, they have become spoiled to the extent that they cannot distinguish between wealth, capability, and the actions through which these are applied. This is our situation today. By composing, fabricating, or studying Puranas, how much knowledge can we really gain to change this situation?

[GV4:P0683] There lies a Karma here, taken up through immense effort over a long period, grown logically, discovered, honored with love, humbled, struggled, and sacrificed for. We have not seen the doctrine of this Karma. We have not acquired that knowledge. Without knowing whether that knowledge exists or not, what else are we doing other than amusing ourselves by arguing back and forth that it exists or it does not? What exists is not going to vanish, and what does not exist is not going to materialize. This is a matter from my own experience.

[GV4:P0684] Now, regarding ghosts (Pishacha). If asked whether ghosts exist, the answer is yes. If anyone says they don't, I ask them, then why do people perform sorcery, tantric rituals, mantra worship, and death rituals invoking ghosts? Can those who perform these actions abandon the ghost? It is easy to say it does not exist, and it is easy to say it does. It was the former Acharyas who stated that everything from ghosts to the realm of vampires (Vethala Nagaram) is agony. They spoke after seeing it. It is only when one reaches the realm of vampires that one understands the special nature of the sorrow there. Beyond that, when one reaches the actions of Shaktheyam, Shaivam, and Vaishnavam, one will see the positions where gods, goddesses, sages, and ascetics stand. Even if seen, to bring it into one's experience, there must be a Guru to instruct, saying, "Do only this in this specific way," or "Manifest it thus"—"Do not do that, do this instead"; he imparts it by making it part of your experience and your fortune.

[GV4:P0685] In the truth of life that comes according to our Karma and Dharma, everyone is equal. There is no other justification in this. However, if there are five children in a family, all five will have slight differences in their course of Karma. They are all indeed the children of the same father and mother. But one will not be like the other. One might have more compassion for the poor. One might want to marry from a poor family. People end up poor due to the lack of merit (Punya) in their soul. Not everyone can take that upon themselves. It will only increase the pain of the soul.

[GV4:P0686] When we say 'Sadhukkal' (the gentle/poor), it should not be understood as the poor. It means 'Sathukkal' (the virtuous). Do not try to remove these flaws using your logic and intellect. What you need is a life that examines the flaws of the soul and transforms them. There is no point in showing off other justifications and arrogance. Whatever weight (arrogance) we have is ours alone. No one can steal it. Similarly, our good and bad are right in our own hands. Our children are the ones who transform it by suffering little by little, imbibing Dharma through the experience of that pain, and realizing merit. There is no point in preaching Vedanta for this. There is also no point in trying to correct children by scolding them. Without knowing the purpose for which you are getting married, what will the children born from such a marriage become? What is the Karma that correctly sustains the affairs of a household? How should our virtues be situated in that Karma, and how should this virtuous Karma be given to the land and the home? Without contemplating any of this, have we been able to show our children any action that provides satisfaction, other than bragging and recounting the magnitude of education, the magnitude of properties and possessions, and the magnitude of prosperity? This is our shortcoming.

[GV4:P0687] You do not need to teach children Vedanta. It is enough to see children as children. If you find children with natural aptitudes (Vasanas), you should guide them along that path. There is no point in applying aptitude in unwanted ways; it must be applied in the right direction. This approach is needed for every one of our homes. You should not distance yourselves from believing, loving, knowing, and letting others know about this. This should be the goal of each one of you. The goal of an ascetic, a wise person, a worker, and a devotee. The path of Santhigiri is the one that unites all these paths. It is a path capable of discovering what the union of life (Jeeva) and the soul (Atma) is. This path will absolutely help you to move forward by understanding what the agony of the vampire realm (Vethala Nagaram) is.

[GV4:P0688] In our habit of thinking categorized into Shaivism, Shaktism, and Vaishnavism, or in the realms of gods, goddesses, sages, ascetics, the wise, and devotees who have fulfilled the course of karma brought about by their compassionate nature, where should we position our ancestors? This must be our goal. If you cannot achieve this, at least your children must be able to. You have the absolute authority to reflect upon who you are within that karma. You should not go seeking anyone's blessings (*anugraha*) or wrath (*nigraha*). People come to me here as well, asking for blessings. The knowledge of these poor souls is an ignorance that does not comprehend what this blessing or curse truly means. It is not blessings or wrath that you should seek. Then what should you seek? The affliction in your life—in that course of karma experienced by the body and mind, what is the *vasana* (inherent tendency) of the karmic course residing deep within the mind, and how shall we eradicate this *vasana*? That *vasana* remains rooted in dharma. As long as the *vasana* remains active, you will unknowingly usurp various things. Everyone usurps in different ways; that is all. Some like their father, some like their mother, some like their elder sister, some like their elder brother—this liking itself is a form of usurpation. What is the point of criticizing our neighbours while holding on to this theft ourselves? "That girl ruined my son; otherwise, my son would have been well-behaved." What a falsehood is being uttered here? What justice will the future generations learn from the absolute form of this injustice that is practiced by speaking in such a manner?

[GV4:P0689] No one ruins anyone else. That origin, which is replete with *vasana*, has manifested on its own. We lack the karmic consciousness to instill good *vasanas* in them from childhood in order to transform that innate nature. Instead, we have reached a stage of action where we curse ourselves, claiming that we are inherently bad. What is the use of such people like us finding fault with those around us?

[GV4:P0690] It might be true that the girl or boy in the neighboring house has flaws. What should we do about that? Should we wish that they somehow perish in those flaws? That is a course of karma acquired from birth. We should attempt to transform that karmic course in a way known to us. What is the action that helps shift them from that mindset and bring them into another mindset? They can be transformed through that action. I received a reward for bringing about such a transformation. I shall tell you about it.

[GV4:P0691] Guruvani
