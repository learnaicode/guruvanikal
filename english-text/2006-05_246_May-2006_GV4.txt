[GV4:P1272] May 2006

[GV4:P1273] It is situated slightly below the Padmanabhapuram Palace, towards the northern side. Have you heard of Peerukannu Sahib? You certainly would know of him. Are you familiar with Thuckalay? Do you know the Thuckalay Mosque? Are you aware that a multitude of people visit this mosque? You must comprehend it in this context. The individual known as Peerukannu Sahib was an eminent scholar. He even authored a treatise on Vedanta in the Tamil language. His guru was Kattuva Sahib. One must be cognizant of all these historical details. It was there that this guru attained *Samadhi*. It was impossible to transport his physical remains back. To do so, one would have had to traverse the distant mountains. Standing there, one can observe a fort-like structure near the Padmanabhapuram Palace. The site is located to the northeast of that structure. What he did there was gather stones of significant size and place them over the remains, ensuring that the body would not be exhumed or carried away by animals. Should you visit the site today, it is visible; stones are amassed to the height of a person. It spans lengthwise in its entirety. Presently, devotees visit the site to prepare gruel and perform *Annadanam* (the sacred offering of food). This practice occurs on most days. If you visit and observe the site, you can witness this firsthand.

[GV4:P1274] Did you realize that all of this is an entanglement encountered on the path of searching for God? In the place where this person called Kattuva Sahib is worshipped or venerated, inside the aforementioned mosque, there is a *Manjanathi* (Morinda coreia) tree. Isn't it called *Manjanathi*? Do you know? Only the people of South Travancore would know the term *Manjanathi*. He attained *Samadhi* (spiritual liberation) resting at the base of this tree. They have buried him there. The said *Manjanathi* tree stands there. There are stones on all four of its sides. If we go there before dusk, individuals will offer at least ten to sixteen bananas each. Some will bring jaggery. Some will bring salt. Some will bring black pepper. These are simply scattered around. This is what occurs. What is being narrated here is merely a fraction of what we have witnessed upon visiting various places in this manner. All of this constitutes a path taken in the quest for God. How will you attain this?

[GV4:P1275] Now, Beemapally is a place where a mother and son attained *Samadhi*. This is a narrative unfolding in a context where Islam dictates that graves should not be worshipped. Similarly, Hindus are people who install deities wherever an *Alstonia* (Pala) tree sprouts. What can be done? Should we believe whether this is a right or a wrong practice that has emerged from our antiquity, or should we not? It could be wrong. It could be right. Should we or shouldn't we? How can we possibly eradicate this right and wrong? How is it possible?

[GV4:P1276] Disciple: It becomes possible through a Guru.

[GV4:P1277] Guru: That is correct. Must we not find such a Guru? What are you saying? This concept of a Guru is not merely what we have been taught to recite. You will not comprehend it in that manner. Who do you assume all these aforementioned great souls are? What kind of renunciates do you assume they are? Even now, I strive to redirect everyone who comes here away from this, to the best of my abilities. When I say I am trying to redirect them, it is not merely about walking away pointlessly. One does not simply leave by declaring "it does not exist." There are specific detriments in it; you must eliminate them and then step away. Otherwise, they will entangle your children in this. This is what I have to tell you. If you need your children, you must change this. Is there any utility in my wandering the land delivering speeches, instead of stating this to you—to the mother, the children, and the father? It is my personal experience. Currently, there is such an awareness within you. If asked how that awareness arose, would you be able to articulate it? That is the phenomenon. In this state, ignorance befalls us. The question is whether we should recognize it or not. Is it acceptable to merely state that we are ignorant? We cannot possibly tell our children that we lack knowledge, can we? Would that suffice? Therefore, a systemic action has been executed to crush and bury us within this. Similarly, back then, regarding a condition that defied treatment by any doctor, it was instructed to apply holy ash in a specific manner, and it was done. If you do not understand this and act appropriately in the required context, there is no point in claiming you have visited here and there. You approached regarding each of these for a specific purpose. Do not assume that everything concludes with that. What currently exists within these children, what resides within us, what all we have to accomplish, and what we must do—if a clear comprehension of these matters does not solidify within you, what will you gain despite my performing all these actions? If you fail to understand this, what can I achieve after you come here and articulate a few things? Nothing can be accomplished. I am neither a sorcerer, nor a physician, nor an astrologer. I am a believer. I can share the experience of that. I can explain how you may attain it. What is your understanding of the term '*Grihasthashrami*' (householder)? You should only speak if you are a *Grihasthashrami*. Otherwise, you should not speak. For a '*Grihasthashrami*', there are five principal duties (*Dharmas*): *Pithru Dharma* (duty to ancestors), *Deva Dharma* (duty to deities), *Athma Dharma* (duty to the self), *Dana Dharma* (duty of charity), and *Jnana Dharma* (duty of knowledge). How does one become the rightful authority for all these five *Dharmas*? I am not a person who will ask questions like whether you arrived in a particular car when you went there. I know nothing about cars, nor do I know anything else. I am not discussing matters like whether you came in a white car or a black car, or if a chicken jumped in front of you when you reached a certain place. Have these five *Dharmas* been prescribed for you or not? How will you obey and adopt these five *Dharmas*? Nothing more than this is required to elicit anger towards you.

[GV4:P1278] A '*Grihadharmi*' (practitioner of household duties) must fulfill all these five *Dharmas*. What we require is a lineage possessing the functional capability to perform this, to cause it to be performed by us, and to sustain it. Consequently, do we not need to acquire such a distinction of functional capability? Merely acquiring it is insufficient. We must actively adopt it. We will pay any price for that. In that regard, there is no discrimination based on whether one is male or female, young or old. *Grihasthashrama* is the *Dharma* of aligning with one's respective role to facilitate taking, to actively take, to give, and to receive all of that. It is not merely for idle amusement. If it is said that such a *Grihasthashrami* is incapable of this, what can be done? I am stating this to you in brief.
