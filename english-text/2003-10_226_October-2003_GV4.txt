[GV4:P0942] October 2003

[GV4:P0943] I have visited on numerous occasions. Each time, I would come as far as Olassery and then return. I had not ventured into other nearby localities, having visited merely two or four places. Never before have I come here during this summer season. I was told that this is a remarkable place. Somehow, a synthesis of the land's ancient and modern knowledge has merged here. However, we have not been able to assimilate it in the proper manner, nor have we had the opportunity to do so. If one asks whether it is better elsewhere, the answer is no. The reason for this lies in our archaic knowledge and familiarities—what constitutes the distinction between the lineage of the Trimurti and that of Manu, what Manu represents, and how Manu came to be. We have not been corrected or enlightened regarding such profound knowledge. There is another reason to state for this. We acquire complete knowledge of the divine will only from certain transcendent Gurus of different eras and their contemporary counterparts. However, the prevailing understanding is that this knowledge slipped from our grasp approximately twenty-five *Chaturyugas* ago. This cannot be comprehended through the words of ordinary mortals, nor can it be realized through the words of deities. I acquired this knowledge much like one learns through an unforeseen ordeal. If you ask how, we once possessed a certain knowledge—a knowledge belonging to the lineage of the Trimurti. Customarily, it elaborates on the aspects of *Parashakti* and then concludes there. Or, to express it in a slightly more refined manner, we have only witnessed the practice of narrating the tale of Devendra and then casually dismissing it as the divine play of the Lord. In all those instances, we are presented with acts that merely evoke astonishment. We are left with no choice but to blindly believe. Why? Because we were unaware of where the error lay and where it required correction. I indicated to you earlier that this was realized through being caught in such a misconception. This so-called misconception is evident in the fact that we observe a deeply entrenched familiarity with vehemently worshipping actions up to the stature of the Trimurti. Yet, this essentially halts with forceful narrations of the heavenly realms. To reconcile this, we are appeased with descriptions of the 'netherworld' (*Pathala*), accompanied by its specific attributes. When patched together with aspects of the dharma of *Kali Yuga*, it never truly aligns logically. As they narrate this, we are taught and made to recite tales of realms that could merely be rationalized as the other side of our earth. Nevertheless, this does not elevate us to a genuine state of profound knowledge. It fails to transcend the boundaries of a merely geographically expansive understanding. The reason it fails to transcend is because it is a narrative constructed by referencing the positions of Devendra and Parashakti, and writing further based solely upon them. It neither progresses beyond that point, nor is it possible to even reach thus far. Subsequently, this is how we perceive it. We note that certain *Puranas* delineate transitional states of existence. However, they have been unable to conclusively explain them. What does this mean? When we speak of a transitional state, it is essentially a vision or revelation. The definitive conclusion of this vision is stated precisely in terms of seven thousand, seven thousand four hundred and forty-four, and so on—incrementing by seventy to reach seventy thousand seven hundred and forty-three. Consequently, this implies that such a multitude of transitional states exist within a specialized framework of completion. How can we demonstrate this? In what manner can it be articulated? However, this vision is exceedingly rare for an ordinary human to attain. Even if attained, we are scarcely permitted to fully immerse ourselves in it. The reason is that Gurus who have transcended this fourth state are exceedingly rare. The designation of *Hamsa* has been appended—many Gurus are referenced with this title—yet we have not been informed of the various categories of this *Hamsa* state. Everything is simply stated by attaching the term *Hamsa*. To summarize this *Hamsa* state, once we began discussing it, it was postulated that, if necessary, all animals serve as the vehicles for these incarnate beings. How many can realistically believe this? Similarly, we remain stagnated at a point where we are merely told of the vehicle of Subrahmanya or the vehicle of Garuda. This is a domain where they have meticulously managed the convergence of the material and the spiritual. Yet, upon reaching that juncture, they find themselves with nothing substantive to impart. This is my personal experience. The reason they lack anything to impart is that some of us have heard up to what extent this status of Devendra reaches, though not everyone has. The entity known as Devendra is one who has surpassed this status of *Deva*. If it is concluded with the tale of a *Yaksha*, and we are told this is not it, we would fail to comprehend it. Because we have neither witnessed it, nor has it been explained to us. And if asked what has been explained, we remain ignorant of that as well. It is only after being trapped and wandering for an immeasurable epoch in the deeds where the *Puranas* and epics ultimately culminated, that we are finally granted an opportunity to emerge from it. Regarding this, the epochs articulated by the author of the *Gita* are indeed the accurate ones.

[GV4:P0944] In that position referred to as 'Kali Shudrankal' (Kali in the Shudra), it is the Shudra class. When we speak of the Shudra class, in our Kerala itself, it has been divided into seventy-four castes. These seventy-four castes, when they reach North India, transform into more than a hundred. We cannot even imagine where this sense of dharma—which subsequently turned caste into a deity and rendered it devoid of karma—will take us, how it will culminate, and how we will be able to proceed. The reality is that no Purana, Itihasa (epic), or Shastra has emerged to explain how we should overcome such karmic defects. Why did it not emerge? It is because of not acting with the knowledge of the times (Yugas). When we speak of times, we are thinking about the four epochs: Satyayuga or Kritayuga, Tretayuga, Dwapara, and Kali. We have arrived at the point where the author of the Gita has stated 'Kali Shudrankal'. Thus, standing here, like those who are unable to comprehend the interpretation concerning us without a completely theoretical knowledge or without learning a language, which guru—who is the guru of these times, why are the primordial gurus mentioned in this way to merely gloss over things, who should come as the followers of this guru, in which lineage should it culminate, which part should it reach—this is what we do not know. So, what is the dharma of Kali? We ourselves must observe the rights and wrongs that have occurred up to this day. Having seen that, we should fulfill it within the dharma of Kali. It is not about running away and abandoning Kali. We have been made to believe that the dharma of Kali is entirely evil. Because Kali has been depicted as a defect in this manner, we do not become thinkers in every way. Then, one should not think about it. It is said what state is achieved in that experience by placing atomic science in it through an atmospheric revolution. But beyond that, no one can do anything there. Knowing how this atom originated is the path. What this path implies is, how does the vibration of an atom exist within us? How many billions of vibrations exist together? It means we must perceive this ourselves. There is no other justification. Discovering it in this manner, if we say male and female, it is sufficient to view it merely as an origin. Be it male or female, or any other prophetic religion, or any other kind of guru across times (Kalanthara Guru), they cannot just depart after merely receiving vibrations corresponding to that epoch. But even if they receive it, they do not become a Kalanthara Guru. The description of an epoch is illustrated through twenty-one gurus. So, in reality, we have not been able to see such gurus anywhere recently. If there are any, it is the misfortune of not being able to transition through there. That is what is called a Yogi. So, what has the Yogi done? He will not touch anyone. Without touching anyone, proceeding along a path, mastering the laws of this nature in whatever ways possible, and considering this law as his life, he preserved it as Yoga.
