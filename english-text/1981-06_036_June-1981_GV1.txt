[GV1:P0735] June 1981

[GV1:P0736] Question: You started talking about Sree Krishna earlier and stopped, didn't you? I am interested to know what that is.

[GV1:P0737] Guru: According to the laws of Manu, five gurus emerge during a single epoch (*Yuga*). When two epochs are combined, eleven gurus appear. Sri Krishna is the eleventh guru in the combined span of the *Treta Yuga* and *Dvapara Yuga*.

[GV1:P0738] Question: Is it not the case that Sri Krishna is traditionally referred to as a deity (*Deva*)?

[GV1:P0739] Guru: Generally, conferring the status of a deity was the prevailing practice. Elevating him to the status of a deity was a necessity for the survival of the Brahmins. They perpetually maintained a specific form of worship exclusively for themselves. They remain the sole officiants of rituals in any temple. Furthermore, they reserve certain specialized rituals strictly for their own households. Consider the great Lord Venkateshwara—the idol lacks a distinct nose and eyes. The adorning of sacred ornaments and the complete coating with sandalwood paste (*muzhukkappu*) are conducted in a highly specialized manner. A figure is drawn by concealing the facial features such as the eyes.

[GV1:P0740] Appropriating anything of value as their own was an integral part of their strategy. They have consistently preserved this approach throughout history. Even today, regardless of the political party or organization one examines, they can be found positioning themselves as the authoritative figures over any non-caste-based entity. They operate as the dominant elites in society, securing a position that sustains their influence. Following the lifetimes of the gurus from the *Parayi Petta Panthirukulam*, they authored verses retrospectively assimilating these figures into the Brahmin fold. Historical records indicate that during the lifetimes of these gurus, there was a profound reluctance to either associate with them or to accord any canonical status to their poetic works.

[GV1:P0742] Historical accounts suggest that even prior to Sri Krishna's birth, anticipating the emergence of such a profound force, efforts were made to thwart it. Eventually, they themselves developed the conviction that Sri Krishna was perfected by his very birth. It was also established that he was a profoundly wise being. When his preeminence became evident through his birth and maturation, they began to disseminate writings that, while perhaps not overtly abusive in tone, subtly undermined him through the citation of specific narratives. The advent of Sri Krishna provided the world with a magnificent figure who upheld the highest standards of morality and sanctity within the prevailing socio-cultural system of that era. He accomplished this by assimilating all the pre-existing virtues, honoring and cementing them, thereby fulfilling the ultimate purpose of his incarnation. A singular incident narrated in the epic account of the eighteen-day war serves as conclusive evidence for this. Since he aligned himself with truth, it is evident that he possessed absolute clarity regarding the purpose of his mission. He relinquished his entire army and armaments to the Kauravas, choosing to stand entirely alone alongside Arjuna. Assuming the role of Arjuna's aide, he guided him through profound discourse and impelled him to wage the war. Had he been a mere deity, he would have directly engaged in the conflict himself. To a guru, all individuals are fundamentally equal; however, desiring the ultimate victory of truth, a guru invariably aligns with it.

[GV1:P0743] Following the advent of Krishna, they invoke names such as Krishna, Kalki, and Janardhana. These concepts have largely transformed into subsequent interpolations within the Hindu tradition. There have been concerted efforts to deify figures like Sri Ramakrishna, Sri Narayana Guru, and various eminent personalities from North India as divine incarnations (*Avataras*). A similar narrative is propagated by some concerning Chattampi Swami. Nevertheless, one might plausibly contend that the manifestation of the Kalki *Avatara* has already occurred. Elements such as the sword and shield are notably prominent within the lineage of Guru Nanak. Aside from the traditions of Christianity and Islam, they represent a distinct lineage adhering strictly to the structural discipline of a guru tradition or prophethood. Mainstream orthodoxy refuses to acknowledge this, dismissing it on the grounds that it effectively established a separate religion. Regardless of these contentions, it remains an undeniable historical reality that this phenomenon occurred within India itself.

[GV1:P0744] Many religions, such as Jainism and Buddhism, have originated in India. Among them, they are reluctant even to admit that this occurred in India. The reason for this is that it possesses a character distinct from Hinduism. Even upon joining the military, they have been granted the right to retain their traditional headgear and swords. Although they depicted Krishna as a deity and added many other elements while writing his story, they circulated a mere fabricated myth among the common people—the myth of him being the husband of sixteen thousand and eight women.

[GV1:P0745] If such a narrative is presented to the common masses, how would these innocent people perceive this individual? Those with limited knowledge will merely remain entangled within this orchestrated scheme, unsure of what to do. For them to acquire knowledge, all aspects of wisdom have been compiled and restricted to the recitation of the *Gita*. Others are strictly forbidden from even viewing it. They have guarded the Vedas in a similar manner, and the same applies to the Upanishads.

[GV1:P0746] Question: There is no prohibition against reading the *Gita*, is there?

[GV1:P0747] Guru: There is no explicit prohibition against reading it. However, you are aware that those in the lower strata of society were historically denied even the right to basic literacy. In a context where even basic literacy was denied to a *Shudra*, perhaps only one in a hundred thousand people from other communities would have possessed knowledge of epics like the *Mahabharata* and the *Ramayana*, or profound texts like the *Gita*.

[GV1:P0748] Today, the general assumption is that everyone is familiar with the *Gita*. But how many people actually read the *Gita*? A few individuals might attend and listen to discourses on the *Gita* where they are held, and that is the extent of it. One should reflect on how extensively the Sanskrit *Gita* has been mastered in languages like Malayalam, Tamil, Telugu, or Tulu. It has gained some traction in Hindi, primarily due to the lack of significant differences in the script.

[GV1:P0749] If one observes closely, they will realize whether such widespread propagation of the *Gita* existed prior to the last sixty years. However, until recently, there was widespread familiarity with texts like the *Narayaneeyam*. That is no longer the case; the *Gita* has now been brought to the forefront. What was the underlying motive behind circulating a degrading fabrication among the poor regarding a great personality who possessed the rights to such a monumental text? It should not be naively dismissed as an act done out of ignorance. This remains one of the most monumental farces perpetrated through the cunning manipulation of knowledge.

[GV1:P0750] Certain historical accounts state that Krishna married eight women. That might also have been due to the societal conditions of that era, which dictated that unmarried girls should not interact too closely with men. Similarly, the history of the Prophet mentions that he married ten women. This is why they are referred to as the luminaries of their respective epochs. Whatever is required for that epoch, be it good or bad, they must demonstrate it before their departure. It is for this precise reason that such marriages were demonstrated. Krishna was one who attained eight such states. Similarly, it is stated that the Prophet had to marry to fulfill this specific state. This is an act compelled by nature itself—perhaps implying that they had a specific cosmic mandate to fulfill. Merely because such a perspective exists in Islam, a local *beedi* roller or a fishmonger attempts to equate himself with the Prophet and marries ten times. Similarly, citing the example of Panchali, people in southern Travancore can be seen marrying up to five times. They fail to emulate or even glance at any of the other profound actions demonstrated by these great figures.

[GV1:P0751] This group of people has not displayed even the slightest inclination to contemplate why these great figures acted in such ways. Whether dealing with a king or a divine incarnation, unless the label of 'Brahminism' is attached to them, some form of deceit will inevitably be concealed within their historical narratives. The narrative surrounding Maveli serves as a prime example of this. By examining the extensive efforts made to confer *Kshatriya* status upon rulers from other castes at this end of the country, one can comprehend the extent of the manipulations they have orchestrated. To prevent the loss of their divine status, they intellectually engineered structures like the *Amarakosha*, beginning with 'Amara Nirjara Deva', completely consolidating their narrative. They even managed to subtly integrate texts like the *Siddharoopam*, which cost a mere eight *kashu* in Travancore at the time, into this grand scheme. Consequently, irrespective of the context, anyone operating within this linguistic framework is inextricably bound to the concept of the deity.

[GV1:P0752] In order to prevent the loss of such Vedic authority, the *Varnashrama Dharma* has been preserved to this day. The *Gita* itself states that in the *Kali Yuga*, there will be an intermingling of castes (*Sankara*). Why, then, should one adhere to the *Varnashrama Dharma* today? Today, poetry is hybridized. When even a plant growing in the courtyard is experienced as a hybrid, what exactly are we trying to achieve with this *Varnashrama Dharma*? If one examines this meticulously, the trajectory up to the present day demonstrates that throughout the extensive duration of this *Kali Yuga*, the lifespans of all human beings will be guided toward an ultimate reality where only liberation (*Mukti*) and the divine (*Deva*) hold authority.

[GV1:P0753] Question: What did you mean by stating "only liberation and the divine"?

[GV1:P0754] Guru: That is to say, it has been elucidated thus far that those who follow the absolute Guru lineage attain liberation, whereas those who follow alternative paths are granted multiple incarnations, elevating them at least to the stature of a deity. As an illustration of this, even if the realization that human rights are universal for all individuals has not been entirely achieved, the conviction has been firmly established.

[GV1:P0755] It has become government legislation that education is accessible to all human beings. The government has facilitated this by providing financial assistance. Consequently, there is no doubt that over time, all forms of knowledge will become universally accessible. Once knowledge, wealth, and intellectual awakening have been comprehensively attained, humanity will reflect, "Despite possessing all these, a state of tranquil peace has not been achieved; let us investigate this." Once knowledge, wealth, and intellect are equitably attained, human endeavor will be directed toward peace. There is absolute certainty that they will attain and perfect it unconsciously. Should these aspirations remain unfulfilled over ten or twelve generations, the world may descend into profound chaos in ten to two hundred years. If such a state of stagnation occurs, nature might manifest an opportunity, akin to the immediate influx of water following the introduction of salt, resulting in the birth of exalted souls capable of inherently transforming the situation. Hoping that, instead of compelling such an intervention through divine glory, all sections of our society may be inspired to utilize our endowed intellect and capabilities to earnestly comprehend and act in accordance with the divine will, I shall conclude for the present.

[GV1:P0756] Guruvani
