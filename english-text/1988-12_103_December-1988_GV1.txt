[GV1:P2311] December 1988

[GV1:P2312] We must be cautious of the nature that manifests in various forms such as mischief, glory, love, devotion, and action. We must safeguard our minds. It will take a considerable amount of time just to understand this. This is precisely because our parents or ancestors were not perfect. Their actions and moral principles will affect us to some extent. This is because nature cultivates blood, marrow, and flesh not just in them and us, but in a way that sustains all living beings in the world. It is transmitted to all living beings in their respective natures. There are also transformations for all these. Every thinker has a distinct perspective on all this. This grass—what is it! How many varieties, and similarly, how many species of birds and animals! All of these possess stomachs, functional capacities, and blood vessels. What is this system that operates everything in perfect equilibrium? Similarly, through the magnitude of what great scientists have discovered, it is possible to fly to many places. Even if all that is true, there will still remain millions and billions of things yet to be known in this world, even if countless discoveries are made. If there is a change in this atmospheric nature or the nature of the solar system, how will we know? We are only capable of acknowledging what we already know as good, known, or heard.

[GV1:P2313] The world is like this. How does it permeate within us? This is what we must consider next. When reflecting thus, among those we consider father, mother, husband, or wife—what lineage have they upheld in this? Under the influence of what inherent tendencies (*vasanas*) have they presented themselves? We must clearly explain this to every child, especially the children studying here. You are not making them understand in that manner. The reason is that you lack such inclinations. The first thing to instill in these children is that you yourself must convince them of the flaws and shortcomings of their parents. Then, what are the circumstances of our home and the circumstances of our ancestors? First, they must be made to understand that. Following that, one must be prepared to confess one's own faults. Who should be prepared? The teachers. I too have several faults. But they are not appropriate to be mentioned now; hence, I am not stating them; they must be told someday. Only then will they comprehend what you teach. Thus, the teacher was able to admit that their father, mother, themselves, and their lineage have faults! That mind is expansive. Therefore, the teacher is worthy of respect. This is being communicated to them for the purpose of cultivating a practice of teaching them in this manner. Otherwise, they will mimic and magnify the antics displayed before them by men and women. No matter how much is said, they will exhibit it. Make this a practice here—our primary task is to encourage them to confess their faults, to observe the misfortunes of their lineage, to repent, and to instill a determination in them to resolve it. The realization should not merely be that one's father, mother, and lineage are at fault and how one can resolve it, but that there are collective flaws spanning from all living beings to the human race. First, make them contemplate how we should perceive this fault. Thus, it should be imparted to children as small, digestible topics. It must also be established that these are the distinct qualities inherent in life, and stopped there. Otherwise, it will veer into Vedanta, and if it moves towards Vedanta, these children will also delve into Vedanta. Vedanta is entirely rationalism. Although some may argue that rationalism is good, it is not. Why? Through rationalism, using intellect, things beyond comprehension will be introduced. Secondly, one might attempt to analyze expansively, thinking, 'Given my parents' condition, I am unlikely to possess this much intellect—yet I have this much intellect.'

[GV1:P2314] This must be communicated to the children first. Only then will a contemplation about themselves be beneficial to them. If this is instilled early on in students, they will subsequently begin to study about something or someone. They will also study about writers. When learning about writers, the children will realize that what they have written is not derived from practical experience, but is imagination. That is a significant achievement. Subsequently, they will learn about certain great figures. Whatever they state are historical truths.

[GV1:P2315] Why historical truth? Providing a semblance of an indication about the impending future, a definitive word is articulated in some part of it. There are children capable of comprehending the timeframe specified for the rest to materialize. They will understand it and execute it through themselves. They themselves might have stated it. There are certain brief lines in the *Bhagavata*. These should be read. Certain sections must be read with deep attention. If one says that the sage's advice is good; that it is Vedanta; it implies that several good things have been articulated in it. What needs to be conveyed is about the concepts that thinkers have comprehended through reflection and practically applied. Otherwise, thought and action will bifurcate into two distinct entities—which is never beneficial.

[GV1:P2316] Guruvani
