[GV1:P1273] April 1983

[GV1:P1274] Where does human life originate? Where does it culminate? What must one acquire through human life? Or, what level of understanding had we reached before commencing this life? What level of understanding have we reached today? First, conceptualize and comprehend this knowledge. If one lacks the capacity to do so, one must find a capable person (a Guru), stand behind him, observe, familiarize oneself, and seek to understand.

[GV1:P1275] When attempting to find such a person, one must discern whether he is a Yogi, a Karmi, a Jnani, or a Bhakta. Those seeking his acquaintance must understand this before approaching him. The one thus found must be devoted and capable of destroying his own *karma*. When we speak of *karma*, the *karma* accumulated across countless lifetimes spanning multiple reincarnations up to this day remains inextricably entangled and stagnant within this soul. The Guru we seek must be someone capable of taking upon himself the *karma* of those who approach him, utilizing his own actions, intellect, and the power of penance. We may not be able to accept this readily. This is because today we stand before those who preach and practice the ideology of absolute egalitarianism. That mindset has penetrated us, and we too have become a part of it. Rather, one must prepare for this path by observing and learning from the life of an individual who has attained a distinct persona—one who has realized the destined source of existence, acted upon it, facilitated action in others, and acquired blessedness through a life lived in surrender to it.

[GV1:P1276] If we observe our society today, we often see the highly educated incapable of accomplishing any meaningful work, while those with no formal education operate at a very high level. This depends on the *karmic* capacity of each individual. Similarly, we see individuals who have transitioned from running a small betel leaf shop to a wholesale business, as well as those who are incapable of running even a betel leaf shop or rolling a single beedi. What we witness there is the purity of *karma* formed by the alignment of his fortune, blessedness, and aptitude for action. Those who acquire such *karmic* capacity eventually become public leaders and administrators. For example, if there is an era where someone who merely acts in movies can take over governance, all this is possible. You know who rules today. That is a distinct characteristic of *Kali Yuga*. Even though he wandered into cinema due to a diversion in his path, he reached the seat of governance through his *karmic* capacity and intellectual power. Whether rightly or wrongly, he is also able to control a considerable number of people. Yogis are also capable of this.

[GV1:P1277] But there is a Karma Yoga that discerns who the preceptor is, who the disciple is, who the subject is, and who the ruler is. If one does not perceive the collective totality from the standpoint of that Karma Yoga, it will collapse like the Western concept of the collective and, in the frenzied dance of distortion, proceed along a path that destroys both the people and oneself. Today, they have reached that stage. Seeing this, we too are leaping into it. Today, we are like dog feces wrapped in silk and labeled as a pot of nectar. What resides within us is the rampant play of distortion and the frenzied dance of the grotesque. However, if one realizes that within us lies a precious gem, and within that, a pot of nectar, no one will throw dog feces into it. But instead of dog feces wrapped in silk, we must truly have that precious gem and the pot of nectar within us.

[GV1:P1278] Karma is what you cultivate for this purpose. Behold the difference between following a blessed soul whose life is enriched by the performance of that Karma, and following one who dances grotesquely, reinforcing collectivism! A body or action nurtured in that distorted nature can achieve nothing here. Nor can it discover anything. They will only depart from here after destroying what has been discovered, turning it into a legacy of distortion, and sowing destruction. What is needed here is a mother to envision this and a father to observe and act with restraint. For this, each of you must be prepared to begin a new life. What is required here is a Karma Yogi who absorbs only the good on that path of life, eliminates the bad, and bestows a creative life that ascends once again to that exalted position. You must not deviate until you assign this its true worth and stature, enrich your Karma, attain eligibility, and integrate it as a collective totality, much like oil permeating within. You must initiate such a life. You cannot achieve this except through a life that imbibes and assimilates its primordial and ultimate values, blending it into the purity of character like the oil that fills every sesame seed, and elevating it to a state of blessedness. Your Karma must be dedicated to that purpose.

[GV1:P1279] Understanding this, we must be able to fulfill Dharma, attain liberation through action (Karma Nivritti), and completely eradicate destruction in order to lead our lives to the realization of their ultimate goal. For this, God has given us intellect and a suitable nature. We have been provided with paths of action suitable for all states of health. The era of lacking food and clothing, and not knowing how to use them, has passed; today, having taught us how to refine and utilize food and clothing and how to lead our lives, God is guiding us towards a path that redirects our intellect toward spiritual contemplation and secures the right to the liberation of the soul. At that time, both punishment and protection will come equally from the same source. If one considers that as a hunt, the purpose will not be served. No practitioner of Karma who fails to understand that by accepting that punishment, they are embracing another spiritually fruitful action can achieve anything here. There is no meaning in your saying that you will accomplish everything through this Karma without understanding this.

[GV1:P1280] The aforementioned Yoga and the path of Dharma constitute a way of performing Karma with a sense of dedication, without abandoning adherence to righteousness. No one should utter even a single word aimed at destroying that path. If that happens, it will forever lead you to ruin. When you look at it, it might seem like a temporary deception or a betrayal. But it will manifest somewhere and emerge as a means of exhausting one's Karma (Karma Nivritti). That will harm you; you will also have to suffer. Therefore, instead of turning towards that destruction, you must work for the spiritual welfare of your life or soul.

[GV1:P1281] Therefore, you must conduct yourselves with at least the sense of dedication that our present actions, thoughts, and humility should all harmoniously blend together to build a better tomorrow, so that virtuous progeny, endowed with humility, purity of action, and constant vigilance, may emerge as a foundational support for the world. That should be your goal. Instead of idly talking about Vedanta and the like, wasting time without achieving the goal, leading a foolish life, and corrupting Karma, you must pray that our lineage is spared from such Sanyasa, Karma, or Yoga, or even any act resembling the nature of one fallen from Yoga (Yoga Bhrashta). You must pray for all good qualities and virtues to be present for all people yet to be born and the great souls yet to come, and perform your duties with the dedication that this goodness should benefit the world, the nation, each individual among us, and every blade of grass. Only then will we reach our intended destination.

[GV1:P1282] Guruvani
