[GV1:P0081] June 1977

[GV1:P0082] Question: It is said that he is a realized soul (*Siddha*) and a wise one (*Jnani*). Then how did it remain unchanged?

[GV1:P0083] Answer: There are certain residual karmic effects required to expel some ingrained tendencies (*Vasanas*). No matter how wise a person may be, it might not be efficacious for everyone.

[GV1:P0084] Question: What is the reason it did not prove efficacious for him? Can you explain?

[GV1:P0085] Answer: Some things can be explained, but not everything. A true devotee does not focus on destroying his vigor or preserving it like a precious jewel. Let whatever is meant to happen, happen. For him, devotion is the only refuge—thus, the glory of God is his sole sanctuary.

[GV1:P0086] Question: Are you implying that figures like Bhogar and Narada are not genuine devotees?

[GV1:P0087] Answer: Consider a father with ten children. They all address him as 'Father.' Their mother is also the same. The ten individuals act with ten distinct characteristics. Their innate inclination towards their father is identical. However, true love may only be evident in two or three of them. Only the father and mother can comprehend that; outsiders cannot. Similarly, there are those who practice Yoga through devotion. Some ritualists exhibit bodily purity and follow ritualistic practices according to Vedic traditions. A wise person observes all these with moderation and understands each individual's course of action. Regardless of the action performed, a devotee constantly perceives God alone as a sweet, pervasive presence.

[GV1:P0088] Then, irrespective of the devotee's greatness, God behaves with them like an ignorant child, embodying the attitude, "I know nothing." At that time, God is consistently perceived acting within them, akin to a mother fondling an ignorant child. A karma yogi (ritualist) and a yogi might speak of their operational capabilities. They may discuss the specific qualities of medicine or the qualities of actions. God continuously fondles the devotee, much like a mother who does not allow her child to cry. A beloved child will eventually have to do certain things on their own. However, all are indeed children.

[GV1:P0089] Question: Are you suggesting that a devotee lacks knowledge?

[GV1:P0090] Answer: Does it imply that? It merely means they are at ease because they are devoid of the attitude of having acquired knowledge, or the ego of believing they are everything.

[GV1:P0091] Question: Then, are you stating that Bhogar preserved his vitality by transmuting it into a bead using medicine?

[GV1:P0092] Answer: Yes, it is preserved.

[GV1:P0093] Question: At the Santhigiri Ashram, which propagates monotheism, certain framed pictures are worshipped. Is this appropriate?

[GV1:P0094] Answer: Initially, humanity adopted various mediums to assign forms. All concepts prevailing to this day originated in this manner. Even the use of lamps is analogous. Meditation practices encompass numerous natures, such as formless meditation and meditation with form. The individuals who discovered these methods might have initiated a philosophical discipline—it may not be immediately comprehensible. Until one grasps it, by remaining steadfast and fixing upon it, and until the essence of other concepts is understood, if one stays firm in their belief, the mind's wavering will cease, and this can be discarded once the nature of wisdom dawns.

[GV1:P0095] Question: A devotee was heard mentioning the term '*Trikalajnana*' to a friend. I wish to know more about *Trikalajnana*.

[GV1:P0096] Answer: *Trikalajnana* is experienced by all individuals. It goes unnoticed simply because it is an unrecognized experience. Only the wise comprehend its true nature. Firstly, one must learn what its true form is. Subsequently, it can be known and understood.

[GV1:P0097] Question: Why is the final phase of the lives of great souls fraught with sorrow?

[GV1:P0098] Answer: Great souls do not attain greatness based on their physical constitution. The fundamental question is what they stood for. Recall the words of Manu. One should not ponder the origin and the fall of a river—similarly, do not contemplate the birth and death of great souls.

[GV1:P0099] Question: It is considered that Monday is auspicious for prayer. Furthermore, prayers are conducted every month on the day of the full moon (*Pournami*). What is the rationale behind this?

[GV1:P0100] Answer: It is a common belief among all people, not just ordinary Hindus, that Mondays and Thursdays are auspicious. Adherents of the *Shakteya* tradition consider Fridays and Tuesdays to be auspicious. For self-knowledge (*Atma Vidya*), Mondays and Thursdays are deemed auspicious across all religions. Sunday is also considered favorable. For sacrifice, Wednesdays and Saturdays are regarded as auspicious. This is calculated based on the spheres—specifically, planetary positions viewed as spheres. The planetary positions are determined based on the nine planets (*Navagrahas*). Days are deemed auspicious in reverence to the periods when they orbit and approach the earth.

[GV1:P0101] The special significance attributed to the day of *Pournami* (full moon) stems from the division of a month into two lunar fortnights: the dark fortnight (*Karuthavavu*) and the bright fortnight (*Veluthavavu*). The bright fortnight culminates on the day the moon rises completely. The bright fortnight is characterized by the light directly transmitted from the solar sphere to the lunar sphere without any obstruction. Sages have deemed the *Pournami* vow auspicious based on the microscopic particles that reach the earth via the moonlight. These particles descend upon all animate and inanimate entities. This phenomenon gives rise to two distinct outcomes: firstly, it grants liberation to those who lead lives guided by *sattvic* thoughts, vows, and prayers; secondly, it is postulated that those engrossed in worldly thoughts on that day become entangled in worldly existence. It is based on this principle that the sages have instructed us that this day is conducive to prayer.
